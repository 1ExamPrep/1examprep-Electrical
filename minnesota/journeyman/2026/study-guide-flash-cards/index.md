## Exam Details

Minnesota electrician licensing is handled through the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry. The department administers personal electrician licenses, including the Class A Journeyworker Electrician license, and provides the electrical license examination guide used by applicants preparing for examination.

The Minnesota Class A Journeyworker Electrician examination consists of 80 questions. The passing score for Minnesota electrical license examinations is 70%. The time allowed to complete the examination is 5½ hours. Examination results are sent to applicants after the examination, and passing result letters include directions for obtaining the license.

Minnesota’s electrical license examinations are administered by the Department of Labor and Industry. Before an applicant may schedule an examination date, the examination application must be approved by the department. Walk-ins are not permitted. Applicants who fail an examination must submit a new application and examination fee before being approved for reexamination.

The Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry’s electrical license examination guide states that the examination question format, degree of difficulty, and length of examination have been in effect since July 1, 2026. ## Open Book Test

The Minnesota Class A Journeyworker Electrician exam is an open-book examination in the sense that the Department of Labor and Industry provides reference materials for use during the exam. The department provides a soft-cover National Electrical Code, a Laws and Rules Booklet, and an electronic calculator. Applicants are allowed to use those materials during the entire examination.

Candidates do not bring their own NEC, calculator, tabs, notes, study guides, flash cards, or practice exams into the examination. All reference materials and the calculator are provided by the department. No other materials or electronic devices, including cell phones, are allowed in the building.

Open book does not mean easy. Minnesota’s exam guide explains that although reference materials are available during the entire examination, applicants should be adequately prepared and should not rely on the provided references to answer all questions. The majority of questions are intended to be answered without the applicant needing to refer to reference materials.

Students should use this study guide, practice exams, final exams, and flash cards before test day. During the actual exam, candidates should follow Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry rules for identification, scheduling, exam materials, calculators, reference use, and test-site procedures.

A strong Minnesota exam strategy includes understanding NEC structure before the exam. Students should practice with the table of contents, index, article organization, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and common code sections. They should also review Minnesota laws and rules, electrical theory, algebra, Ohm’s Law, Watt’s Law, series circuits, parallel circuits, volt-amperes, transformer calculations, voltage drop, box fill, conduit fill, conductor ampacity, motor calculations, and short-circuit current concepts.

## Licensing Steps

Minnesota journeyman electrician licensing begins with meeting the qualifications for the Class A Journeyworker Electrician license. Applicants must document qualifying electrical experience under the rules administered by the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry. The practical experience requirements are addressed in Minnesota Rules 3800.3520, which identifies allowable experience credit, acceptable situations for experience, and experience requirements for electrical license categories.

A typical Minnesota Class A Journeyworker Electrician pathway involves registering as an unlicensed individual when required, gaining qualifying supervised electrical experience, maintaining accurate work records, completing approved technical training when applicable, submitting the examination application to the Department of Labor and Industry, receiving written approval to schedule the exam, taking the examination, and following the license instructions provided after a passing result.

Applicants for the Class A Journeyworker Electrician license who successfully complete a two-year technical college program approved by the department may receive 12 months of experience credit toward the experience required for the license. Technical training credit is only granted for programs approved by the department prior to completion by the applicant.

Applicants may also receive experience credit for electrical work performed in the military. Applicants with military experience should include the required military documentation with their examination application materials. Minnesota also has reciprocal licensing agreements with several states for journeyworker and master electrician licenses when the applicant obtained the license by examination and meets the applicable reciprocity requirements.

Before anyone may schedule an examination date, the individual’s examination application must be approved by the department. Dates scheduled without written approval are rejected. Approved applicants receive instructions for scheduling and appearing for the examination.

This study guide and flash card combo supports the preparation stage of the licensing process. Students can use it while completing supervised experience, while documenting work hours, before applying for exam approval, after receiving examination approval, or during final review before the scheduled exam date.
