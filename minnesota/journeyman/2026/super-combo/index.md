Minnesota’s electrical license examination guide explains the basic qualifications, knowledge areas, examination criteria, and format used for personal electrician licenses administered by the department.

The Minnesota Class A journeyworker electrician examination consists of 80 questions. ## Open Book Test

The Minnesota Class A journeyworker electrician examination is an open book test. Minnesota’s electrical license examination guide states that applicants are allowed to use the National Electrical Code, a Laws and Rules Booklet, and an electronic calculator during the entire examination. The department provides the reference materials and calculator used for the examination.

Because Minnesota provides the examination reference materials, candidates should not plan to bring personal books, tabs, notes, devices, or other materials into the building unless current DLI instructions specifically allow an item. The National Electrical Code copy provided for the exam is in soft-cover format and does not include tabs or other aids.

## Licensing Steps

The Minnesota journeyworker electrician licensing path begins with registration and supervised electrical work experience. A person performing electrical work without a personal electrical license must be registered with DLI as a registered unlicensed electrician, work within the scope authorized by the employer, and be directly supervised by a licensed journeyworker or master electrician. The registered unlicensed electrician and the supervising licensed electrician must be employed by the same licensed electrical contractor or registered electrical employer.

To become licensed as a journeyworker electrician in Minnesota, an applicant must pass the written examination before applying for the journeyworker electrician license. To take the journeyworker examination, the applicant must provide evidence of sufficient electrical work experience. Minnesota grants credit for qualifying work experience when it meets the department’s requirements, including proper registration, qualifying employment, and acceptable supervision.

For the Class A journeyworker electrician license, Minnesota’s minimum experience schedule identifies a minimum total of 48 months of acceptable experience. The schedule includes a minimum of 24 months in wiring for and installing electrical wiring, apparatus, and equipment, with additional allowable experience categories such as maintaining and repairing electrical wiring, apparatus, and equipment. A maximum of one year of experience credit may be allowed for successful completion of a two-year post-high-school electrical course approved by the Board.

After meeting the experience requirements, the candidate applies to take the examination through DLI. The exam application is reviewed before the candidate may schedule an examination date. After approval, the candidate schedules the exam, studies the knowledge areas, prepares with code and calculation practice, and sits for the examination administered by DLI.

After passing the examination, the candidate follows the instructions provided with the passing result letter to obtain the journeyworker electrician license. A practical preparation path includes registering properly, gaining acceptable supervised experience, maintaining work experience records, applying for the exam, reviewing the examination guide, studying the NEC, practicing calculations, building reference navigation skills, using flash cards for recall, passing the examination, and completing the licensing process.

## State Requirements

Minnesota regulates personal electrician licenses through the Department of Labor and Industry. A person may perform electrical work in Minnesota when the person is at least 17 years old, is licensed by DLI or registered with DLI as a registered unlicensed electrician, and is a W-2 employee of a licensed electrical contractor or registered electrical employer, or is the owner of a licensed electrical contractor.

A registered unlicensed electrician may perform electrical work without a personal license only when the work falls within the scope authorized by the employer and the work is directly supervised at all times by a licensed journeyworker or master electrician. Minnesota also limits supervision by stating that a licensed electrician may not supervise more than two unlicensed individuals.

For the journeyworker electrician license, Minnesota requires applicants to pass a written examination before applying for licensure. Applicants must provide evidence of sufficient electrical work experience before taking the examination. Journeyworker electrician licenses expire every two years at the end of the month in which the license was originally issued.

To renew a Minnesota journeyworker electrician license, the license holder must complete 16 hours of DLI-approved continuing education, with at least 12 hours related to the National Electrical Code, and pay the renewal fee. Licensees should maintain continuing education records and follow DLI renewal procedures so the license remains active.

Minnesota has reciprocal licensing agreements at the journeyworker electrician level with Alaska, Arkansas, Colorado, Iowa, Montana, Nebraska, North Dakota, South Dakota, and Wyoming. Reciprocity requirements include qualifying conditions and are handled through DLI’s licensing process.
