## Exam Details

The Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry administers personal electrician licenses and provides the electrical license examination guide for applicants. The Class A journeyworker electrician examination is intended to measure whether the applicant has the competence needed to practice within the scope of the license without causing harm to the public. The exam focuses on practical code knowledge, electrical theory, and the ability to apply electrical rules in real job conditions.

The Minnesota electrical license examination guide states that the 2026 National Electrical Code became effective July 1, 2026, and is the code edition used for questions in electrical license examinations. The guide also explains that the Class A journeyworker electrician examination consists of 80 questions, has a passing score of 70%, and allows 5½ hours for completion.

Minnesota examination materials are provided by the Department during the test. The examination guide states that applicants are allowed to use the National Electrical Code, a Laws and Rules Booklet, and an electronic calculator during the examination. The NEC book provided for the examination is in soft-cover format and does not include tabs or other aids. No other materials or electronic devices are allowed in the building.

## Open Book Test

The Minnesota Class A journeyworker electrician examination is an open book, reference-based test. During the examination, applicants may use the National Electrical Code, a Laws and Rules Booklet, and an electronic calculator provided by the Department. Personal reference materials, personal electronics, and other outside materials are not allowed.

An open book electrical exam still requires serious preparation. Having access to reference material does not mean every answer will be easy to find. Minnesota’s examination guide states that although reference materials are available for the entire examination, applicants should be adequately prepared and should not rely on provided reference materials to answer all questions. The majority of questions are intended to be answered without needing to refer to reference materials.

## Licensing Steps

The Minnesota Class A journeyworker electrician licensing path begins with qualifying electrical work experience. A person may perform electrical work in Minnesota if they are licensed by DLI or registered with DLI as a registered unlicensed electrician, are at least 17 years old, and work under the required employment and supervision structure. A registered unlicensed electrician must work within the scope authorized for the employer and be directly supervised by a licensed journeyworker or master electrician.

To take the Class A journeyworker electrician examination, the applicant must provide evidence of sufficient electrical work experience. Minnesota identifies a minimum total of 48 months for the Class A journeyworker electrician license. Experience categories and maximum credit allowances are defined by Minnesota rules. The required experience is focused heavily on wiring for and installing electrical wiring, apparatus, and equipment for light, heat, and power, while certain other categories have limited credit allowances.

Applicants who successfully complete a two-year post-high-school electrical course approved by the Board may receive up to one year of experience credit toward the required experience. Minnesota also provides rules for acceptable experience gained while working for licensed contractors, registered employers, out-of-state employers, or through qualifying military electrical work.

After the candidate has sufficient experience, the next step is to apply online to take the exam and submit required experience documentation. Once the Department approves the examination application, the candidate receives instructions for scheduling the examination. Minnesota’s examination guide states that applicants may not schedule an exam date before receiving written approval.

After passing the examination, the applicant receives directions about how to obtain the license. Journeyworker licenses expire every two years at the end of the month in which the license was originally issued. Renewal requires completing required continuing education and paying the renewal fee according to DLI instructions.

## State Requirements

Minnesota regulates electrical licensing through the Department of Labor and Industry. A Class A journeyworker electrician license is an individual license for electrical workers who have met the state’s experience and examination requirements. This credential is different from a Class A master electrician license and different from an electrical contractor license.

Minnesota requires unlicensed individuals performing electrical work to be registered with DLI and properly supervised, unless an exemption applies. A registered unlicensed electrician must be supervised by a licensed journeyworker or master electrician and must be employed by the same licensed electrical contractor or registered electrical employer as the supervising electrician. A licensed electrician may not supervise more than two unlicensed individuals.

For the Class A journeyworker electrician license, Minnesota identifies a minimum total of 48 months of acceptable experience. The experience must fit the categories and credit limits recognized by the state. Candidates should keep organized records of registration, employers, supervision, type of work performed, dates, and hours because work experience documentation is central to exam eligibility.

Minnesota also requires continuing education for renewal. Journeyworker electricians must complete 16 hours of DLI-approved continuing education before renewal, and at least 12 of those hours must relate to the National Electrical Code. This helps licensed electricians stay current with code changes and technical requirements after the license is issued.

Minnesota has reciprocal licensing agreements with several states at the journeyworker level. Reciprocity has specific conditions, including holding a qualifying license obtained by examination and meeting the requirements of the state where application is made. Candidates pursuing reciprocity should follow DLI’s current reciprocity instructions rather than assuming that a license automatically transfers.
