Exam Details

New York electrical licensing is local rather than statewide. This means the exam details depend on the jurisdiction where the candidate is applying. A master electrician exam in New York City is not the same as a master electrician or electrical contractor exam used by another city, county, town, or village. Candidates preparing for New York master-level electrical licensing should study the NEC while also learning the specific rules of the local licensing authority.

New York City is the most prominent example. The New York City Department of Buildings issues Master Electrician and Special Electrician licenses. A New York City Master Electrician is licensed to perform electrical work as an independent contractor in or on any buildings, lots, or parts of buildings in New York City. A Special Electrician license is tied to electrical work in or on specific buildings, lots, or parts of buildings.

The New York City Master and Special Electrician written examination is administered through the city’s examination process and is listed as a closed book exam with 80 multiple-choice questions. The content outline includes New York City Electrical Code topics, Rules of the City of New York, and electrical trade knowledge. The full New York City licensing process also includes practical examination requirements and license application review after examination steps are completed.

Outside New York City, electrical licensing requirements vary by municipality. Cities and counties may use different experience requirements, exam providers, license names, approved references, renewal cycles, insurance requirements, local code editions, and permit rules. Some jurisdictions may use NEC-based open book exams, while others may use local examinations or additional business requirements.

Licensing Steps

The New York master electrician licensing path begins with identifying the local licensing authority. Because New York does not issue one statewide master electrician license, candidates should begin with the city, county, town, or village where they plan to work, pull permits, supervise electrical work, or operate an electrical business.

For New York City, candidates apply through the Department of Buildings process for the Master Electrician or Special Electrician license. The process includes applying for the examination, completing the required examination steps, satisfying experience and background requirements, and completing the licensing application process through DOB NOW: Licensing. New York City requires electrician license applications to be submitted through the DOB NOW licensing system.

New York City Master Electrician candidates must follow the Department of Buildings requirements for experience documentation, examination, investigation, and license issuance. The licensing process includes written and practical examination components, followed by application review and submission of required documentation. The Master Electrician license is associated with the authority to perform electrical work as an independent contractor within New York City.

For jurisdictions outside New York City, candidates follow the local licensing process where the work will be performed. A local authority may require documented trade experience, proof of prior licensing, a local master electrician exam, business registration, insurance documentation, bonding, permit registration, renewal fees, continuing education, or local code compliance requirements.

After passing the required exam and completing the local application process, the candidate receives the license or registration issued by that jurisdiction. Because New York licensing is local, a license issued in one municipality may not automatically authorize work in another. Electricians and contractors should maintain compliance with the requirements of each locality where they perform work.

State Requirements

New York does not have one statewide master electrician license that applies uniformly across the entire state. Electrical licensing authority is commonly held by local governments, including cities, counties, towns, and villages. Local jurisdictions set their own licensing procedures and control who may perform or contract for electrical work within their boundaries.

New York City licenses Master Electricians and Special Electricians through the Department of Buildings. Master Electricians are licensed to perform electrical work as independent contractors in or on buildings, lots, or parts of buildings in New York City. Special Electricians are licensed for electrical work tied to specific buildings or premises.

Other New York jurisdictions may issue their own master electrician, journeyman electrician, electrical contractor, limited electrician, or specialty electrical licenses. Requirements may include experience, examination, insurance, local registration, permit authorization, renewal, and local code compliance. A candidate preparing for electrical work outside New York City should follow the rules of the specific jurisdiction where the license is needed.

New York also has state-level rules related to certain alarm installer work through the Department of State. Master electricians licensed by a municipality before certain statutory dates may qualify for specific waivers or registrations related to security or fire alarm systems. These rules are separate from ordinary local master electrician licensing and apply to specific alarm-related circumstances.
