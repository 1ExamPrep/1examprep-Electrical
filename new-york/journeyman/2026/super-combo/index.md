## Exam Details

New York electrical exam details depend on the local licensing authority. There is no single statewide New York journeyman electrician exam that applies to every city, town, and county. Local jurisdictions may set their own license categories, experience requirements, application steps, exam providers, passing scores, fees, approved references, renewal rules, and work authorization requirements.

In New York City, the Department of Buildings licenses Master Electricians and Special Electricians rather than issuing a journeyman electrician license. New York City’s licensing process is separate from local journeyman licensing used in some other municipalities. The New York City Master and Special Electrician written examination is a closed-book exam with 80 multiple-choice questions and is based on the New York City Electrical Code, applicable rules, and trade-specific knowledge.

Outside New York City, some jurisdictions recognize or issue journeyman electrician credentials. Buffalo, for example, has a journeyman electrician license application path that references a passing ICC exam score and documented relevant work experience, while also allowing the local board discretion to accept other qualifying proof. Other New York municipalities may use their own local examinations, local licensing boards, or locally adopted requirements.

For candidates preparing at the journeyman level, the ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician exam is a common exam model used by various authorities. The ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician examination has 80 multiple-choice questions and a 4-hour time limit. It is open book and tests general knowledge, services and service equipment, feeders, branch circuits and conductors, wiring methods and materials, equipment and devices, control devices, motors and generators, and special occupancies, equipment, and conditions.

Journeyman-level electrical study should include NEC definitions, electrical theory, services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor ampacity, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, raceways, boxes, cables, panelboards, motors, transformers, equipment for general use, low-voltage systems, safety, special occupancies, special equipment, and electrical calculations. These areas support local New York journeyman-level exams and provide a strong foundation for electricians who may later pursue master electrician, special electrician, or electrical contractor credentials.

## Open Book Test

Many New York journeyman-level electrical exams are prepared for as open-book, NEC-based exams, especially when the local authority or testing provider uses an ICC-style journeyman electrician examination or allows approved code references. The ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician examination is open book and uses the National Electrical Code as the primary reference.

Open-book exam rules depend on the local jurisdiction and testing provider. Candidates should follow the exact rules for the exam they are taking, including the approved NEC edition, allowed reference books, tab rules, calculator rules, highlighting rules, note restrictions, and prohibited materials. Some exam providers allow permanent tabs while prohibiting temporary tabs, loose notes, handwritten inserts, photocopied references, or unapproved study materials.

Open book does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates still need to know how to read the question, identify the subject, locate the correct reference, interpret the code language, and answer within the time limit. A candidate who is familiar with the NEC can spend less time searching and more time solving the question.

## Licensing Steps

New York candidates should begin by identifying the jurisdiction where they plan to work. Because electrician licensing is local, the correct license path depends on the city, county, town, or local board that regulates electrical work in that area. A candidate working in Buffalo may follow a different process than a candidate working in New York City, Rochester, Syracuse, Albany, Yonkers, Binghamton, Nassau County, Suffolk County, or another municipality.

A practical path begins with confirming the local license classification. Candidates should determine whether the jurisdiction requires a journeyman electrician license, master electrician license, special electrician license, electrical contractor license, limited electrician license, registration, or another local credential. The license title matters because each classification may have different experience requirements, exam rules, work scope, and renewal requirements.

After identifying the correct local license, candidates should gather required documentation. This may include proof of work experience, apprenticeship records, employer verification, education or classroom training records, examination results, identification, insurance information, business documentation, or local application forms. Requirements vary by jurisdiction.

Once the application is complete, the local authority may approve the candidate to test, require additional documentation, or direct the candidate to a testing provider. Candidates should schedule the correct exam, prepare with the approved reference list and content outline, complete the examination, and submit any passing score documentation required by the local licensing office.

Passing an exam is an important step, but it does not automatically authorize electrical work everywhere in New York. A license issued by one local jurisdiction may not authorize work in another. Candidates who plan to work across multiple areas should confirm the requirements for each jurisdiction where they will perform electrical work.

Flash cards are useful for shorter study sessions.
