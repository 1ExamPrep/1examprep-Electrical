Exam Details

The New Jersey Electrical Contractor licensure examination is administered through PSI after approval from the New Jersey State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. Candidates must receive authorization from the Board before scheduling their initial examination. Once approved, the candidate receives an Examination Eligibility Notice with instructions for paying for and scheduling the exam through PSI.

To qualify for licensure as a New Jersey Electrical Contractor, candidates must pass the Electrical Contractor, Alarm Systems Electrical Contractor, and Business and Law examinations. Candidates approved on or after March 16, 2015, must take all three examinations on the first attempt on the same day. If one or more sections are failed, only the failed section or sections must be retaken. Candidates must pass all three required examinations within the allowed attempt and eligibility limits established by the Board and PSI.

The New Jersey Electrical Contractor exam contains 100 questions, requires a 70% score to pass, and allows 255 minutes of testing time under the current PSI bulletin. The exam content outline includes General Electrical Knowledge, Raceways and Enclosures, Services, Feeders, and Branch Circuits, Overcurrent Protection, Conductors and Cables, Grounding and Bonding, Equipment for General Use, Special Occupancies, Special Equipment and Conditions, Motors and Controls, Low Voltage and Communications Circuits, and Safety.

The Business and Law exam contains 50 questions, requires a 70% score to pass, and allows 130 minutes of testing time. The Alarm Systems Contractor exam contains 50 questions, requires a 70% score to pass, and allows 165 minutes of testing time. Electrical Contractor candidates should prepare for the technical trade exam while also understanding that the full New Jersey licensing process includes the other required examination sections.

Open Book Test

The New Jersey Electrical Contractor exam is an open book test. The current PSI bulletin states that the Electrical Contractor examination is open book and that the allowed reference is the NFPA 70 National Electrical Code, 2023 Edition. That NEC reference is provided at the test center. Candidates may not use their own copy of the NEC during the Electrical Contractor exam, and candidates may not write, highlight, underline, or index the test-center-provided NEC reference.

Licensing Steps

The New Jersey licensing path begins with reviewing the requirements of the New Jersey State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. Candidates seeking the Electrical Contractor license must apply to the Board and receive authorization before testing. PSI does not approve the candidate for the initial examination; the Board determines exam eligibility.

After the application is reviewed and approved, the Board sends an Examination Eligibility Notice with instructions for paying and scheduling through PSI. The candidate then schedules the required examinations. Candidates approved under the current testing rules take the Electrical Contractor, Alarm Systems Electrical Contractor, and Business and Law examinations as part of the licensing examination process.

On exam day, candidates must report to the PSI testing center with proper identification. The candidate’s name on the government-issued identification must match the name used for exam registration. PSI provides an online calculator, scratch paper, and pencil during the exam. Only approved items may be taken into the testing room.

After testing, candidates receive results upon completion, and results are transmitted to the Board. Candidates who pass all required portions continue the licensing process through the Board. Candidates who do not pass one or more sections must follow the retesting rules, including waiting periods and attempt limits, before retaking failed sections.

Students should begin technical preparation well before scheduling. State Requirements

New Jersey electrical licensing is regulated by the New Jersey State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors within the Division of Consumer Affairs. The Board licenses and regulates electrical contractors, registers qualified journeyman electricians, and handles exemptions from licensure for certain telecommunications wiring activities.

New Jersey’s primary statewide contracting credential is the Electrical Contractor license. A licensed electrical contractor may engage in the business of contracting to install, erect, repair, or alter electrical equipment used for the generation, transmission, or utilization of electrical energy. Candidates pursuing this license must satisfy Board requirements, receive examination authorization, pass the required examinations, and complete the licensing process.

The New Jersey exam process includes the technical Electrical Contractor exam, the Alarm Systems Electrical Contractor exam, and the Business and Law exam. This structure means students should prepare not only for NEC-based electrical knowledge, but also for alarm systems content and business law concepts connected to contractor licensing.

New Jersey also recognizes qualified journeyman electrician registration through the Board. The qualified journeyman path is different from electrical contractor licensure. Candidates should understand which credential they need based on whether they are seeking individual recognition, employment qualification, or authority to engage in electrical contracting as a licensed business.
