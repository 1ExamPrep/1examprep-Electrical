## Exam Details

New Jersey electrical licensing examinations are administered through PSI for the New Jersey State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. Candidates must receive authorization from the Board before scheduling the initial examination. After approval, the candidate receives an Examination Eligibility Notice with instructions for paying and scheduling the examination through PSI.

The Electrical Contractor examination is listed with 100 questions, a 70 percent passing score, and 260 minutes of testing time. The published content outline includes General Electrical Knowledge, Raceways and Enclosures, Services, Feeders, and Branch Circuits, Overcurrent Protection, Conductors and Cables, Grounding and Bonding, Equipment for General Use, Special Occupancies, Special Equipment and Conditions, Motors and Controls, Low Voltage and Communications Circuits, and Safety.

The New Jersey licensing examination process also includes the Business and Law examination and the Alarm Systems Contractor examination for candidates seeking to qualify as a New Jersey Electrical Contractor. The Business and Law examination is listed with 50 questions, a 70 percent passing score, and 130 minutes of testing time. Its content outline includes Business Organization, Licensing, Estimating and Bidding, Contract Management, Project Management, Risk Management, Safety, Labor Law, Financial Management, Tax Law, and Lien Law.

The Alarm Systems Contractor examination is listed with 50 questions, a 70 percent passing score, and 165 minutes of testing time. Its content outline includes General Electrical Knowledge, Application of Fire Alarm Systems, Application of Intrusion and Security Systems, Installation Requirements, Wiring Methods and Materials, and Special Occupancies and Conditions.

## Open Book Test

The New Jersey Electrical Contractor examination is identified as an open book examination. The current PSI bulletin lists NFPA 70 National Electrical Code, 2023 Edition, as the electrical reference used for the exam, and states that this code book is provided at the test center. Candidates may not use their own copy of the NEC reference book for the Electrical Contractor examination, and the provided reference may not be written in, highlighted, underlined, or indexed.

## Licensing Steps

New Jersey electrical licensing is handled through the New Jersey State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. The Board licenses and regulates electrical contractors, registers qualified journeyman electricians, and grants exemptions from licensure for certain telecommunications wiring work. A candidate pursuing New Jersey electrical contractor licensing should begin by confirming the correct license path and reviewing the Board’s application process.

A practical licensing path begins with meeting the Board’s qualification requirements, completing the application process, submitting required work experience certification forms and supporting documents, receiving Board approval to test, scheduling the PSI examinations, passing the required examinations, and completing the licensing process through the Board. The PSI bulletin states that candidates approved for initial examination must pass the Electrical Contractor, Alarm Systems Electrical Contractor, and Business and Law examinations to qualify for New Jersey Electrical Contractor licensure.

New Jersey’s examination procedure requires candidates approved on or after March 16, 2015, who are taking the examination for the first time, to take all three examinations on the same day. The bulletin also states that candidates must take the first attempt within two years from the eligibility date and must pass all three required examinations within three attempts. Candidates who do not pass the three required examinations within five years from the eligibility date must reapply with the Board.

The study portion of the licensing path should be treated as a structured project. Candidates should review the exam content outlines, study the 2026 NEC, practice calculations, work through code navigation, review safety and trade knowledge areas, and use flash cards for repeated reinforcement. Consistent preparation helps candidates approach New Jersey electrical contractor and master-level electrical study with more organization and confidence.

The New Jersey Electrical Contractor examination scope covers engaging in the business of contracting to install, erect, repair, or alter electrical equipment for the generation, transmission, or utilization of electrical energy. Work experience certification forms and required documentation must be submitted as part of the application process. Candidates should follow the Board’s current application instructions and PSI’s current candidate bulletin for examination scheduling, required identification, testing rules, scoring, and retesting procedures.
