Exam Details

Ohio does not operate one single statewide journeyman electrician license that applies uniformly to every individual electrician in every city. The Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board, commonly known as OCILB, issues state licenses for electrical contractors who perform commercial work. Individual journeyman electrician licensing and local credential requirements may be handled by municipalities or local authorities.

Because of that structure, exam details can vary depending on the path being followed. A candidate preparing for an Ohio local journeyman electrician exam should review the requirements issued by the applicable city or local licensing authority. A candidate preparing for an Ohio electrical contractor license should follow OCILB instructions, including the examination application process and PSI exam requirements.

For state electrical contractor licensing through OCILB, applicants must be approved before taking the licensing examination. OCILB identifies electrical, HVAC, refrigeration, plumbing, and hydronics contractors as trades licensed by the Board for commercial work. The Board’s examination application materials state that applicants must be at least 18 years of age, be a U.S. citizen or legal alien, and have been a tradesperson in the type of licensed trade for which the application is filed for not less than five years immediately prior to the application date.

Ohio electrical contractor exams are administered through PSI after eligibility is determined by OCILB. The PSI candidate information for the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board explains that the Board determines eligibility and PSI conducts the examination program through computer examination centers. Candidates should follow the official PSI and OCILB instructions for scheduling, identification, examination rules, reference materials, scoring, and retesting.

Local journeyman electrician exams may use different application requirements, question counts, time limits, fees, and reference rules. For example, some Ohio municipalities maintain their own journeyman electrician application and examination process. This is why candidates should confirm the exact licensing path before test day. The study guide and NEC combo is designed to support NEC-based preparation, but the official requirements of the local authority or state Board control the actual exam process.

Open Book Test

Open-book electrical exams require candidates to do more than bring a code book. They require candidates to locate information quickly, interpret code language correctly, and apply the rule to the question being asked. Ohio electrical contractor testing through PSI is commonly structured around approved references, and some Ohio local journeyman electrician exams are also open book. The City of Middletown journeyman electrician examination application, for example, identifies the exam as open book.

Because Ohio journeyman licensing may be local, candidates should not assume that every local exam uses the same format, the same books, the same time limit, or the same reference rules. The safest study approach is to prepare with the NEC, learn the layout of the code book, and confirm the exam-room rules issued for the specific exam being taken.

An open book test does not mean the exam is simple. Many electrical questions require candidates to identify the correct subject, move to the right NEC article, read the applicable rule, check tables or exceptions, and complete calculations accurately. Candidates who are unfamiliar with the NEC can spend too much time searching, even when they understand the general electrical concept. Practicing with the 2026 NEC paperback and tabs helps build the speed needed for this kind of exam environment.

Licensing Steps

The Ohio licensing path depends on whether the candidate is pursuing a state electrical contractor license or a local journeyman electrician credential. For state electrical contractor licensing, candidates apply through the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board. OCILB reviews eligibility, and approved candidates proceed to the PSI examination process.

For an Ohio electrical contractor license, the candidate should review the OCILB license qualification process, complete the required examination application, submit required documentation, and wait for approval before scheduling the exam. The Board’s application materials identify baseline requirements, including age, citizenship or legal alien status, and trade experience. Candidates should also review any insurance, business, renewal, continuing education, or company-registration requirements connected to electrical contractor licensing.

For a local journeyman electrician credential, the candidate should begin with the city, municipality, or local building department where the license is required. Local authorities may require experience documentation, apprenticeship records, employer verification, application forms, fees, examination approval, identification, and compliance with local electrical rules.
