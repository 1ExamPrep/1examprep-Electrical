Exam Details

Ohio does not issue one single statewide journeyman electrician license that applies uniformly across every city and county. Journeyman electrician licensing is generally handled at the local level. Candidates may need to apply through the city, county, or municipal authority where they plan to work. Because of this local structure, exam details can vary by jurisdiction.

Ohio candidates may encounter local journeyman electrician exams through municipalities or local building departments. Some Ohio jurisdictions use local examination processes, while others may accept scores from a recognized testing provider or require locally approved documentation. The exact exam title, question count, time limit, passing score, reference list, code edition, application fee, and renewal requirement are controlled by the local authority.

For statewide electrical contracting, the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board issues electrical contractor licenses. That license is different from a local journeyman electrician credential. The electrical contractor licensing process is used for individuals seeking authority to contract for electrical work under the state contractor licensing system. Journeyman-level candidates should confirm whether they need a local journeyman license, a local registration, an electrical contractor license, or another credential based on their work role.

Open Book Test

Ohio journeyman electrician exam reference rules depend on the local jurisdiction and testing provider. Many journeyman-level electrical exams are prepared for as open-book, NEC-based exams, but candidates must follow the exact rules issued for the exam they are taking.

Local exam rules may specify the approved NEC edition, whether reference books are allowed, whether tabs must be permanent, whether highlighting or notes are permitted, what calculator may be used, and which materials are prohibited. Candidates should confirm whether their specific Ohio exam is open book, closed book, or divided into reference and no-reference sections.

Open book does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates still need to know how to read the question, identify the subject, locate the correct article or table, interpret the code language, and answer within the time limit. A candidate who is comfortable with the NEC can spend less time searching and more time solving the question.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs included in this Super Combo is intended for study and code navigation practice. Tabs help candidates become familiar with major NEC articles, definitions, tables, and commonly used sections. Licensing Steps

Ohio candidates should begin by identifying the jurisdiction where they plan to work. Because journeyman electrician licensing is generally local, the correct license path depends on the city, county, or municipal authority that regulates electrical work in that area.

A practical path begins with confirming the local license classification. Candidates should determine whether the jurisdiction requires a journeyman electrician license, electrical contractor license, registration, apprentice record, local exam, proof of experience, continuing education, insurance, bond, or another credential. The license title matters because each classification may have different requirements and work authority.

After identifying the correct local path, candidates should gather required documentation. This may include proof of supervised electrical work experience, apprenticeship records, employer verification, classroom instruction records, identification, local application forms, testing authorization, or examination score reports. Requirements vary by local authority.

Once the application is complete, the local jurisdiction may approve the candidate to test, require additional documentation, or direct the candidate to a testing provider. Candidates should schedule the correct exam, prepare with the approved references and content outline, complete the examination, and submit any passing score documentation required by the local licensing office.

Candidates pursuing statewide electrical contractor licensing follow the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board process instead of a local journeyman path. That process includes state application review and passing the required licensing examination before a contractor license is issued. Passing a local journeyman exam does not automatically issue a statewide electrical contractor license, and passing a contractor exam does not always replace local journeyman requirements for every work situation.

Test Information and Study Materials

The Ohio Journeyman Electrician Super Combo supports a complete preparation routine by combining structured study, code review, calculation practice, and quick recall. The Ohio Journeyman Electrician Study Guide helps candidates organize preparation around journeyman-level electrical knowledge. The Electrician Calculations Study Guide strengthens math-based preparation. The NEC paperback with tabs supports code navigation. The Journeyman Electrician Flash Cards reinforce important information through repetition.

Electrical calculations should be practiced consistently before the exam. Candidates should review Ohm’s Law, power formulas, amperage, voltage, resistance, watts, conductor sizing, branch circuit calculations, feeder concepts, service load concepts, box fill, conduit fill, motor calculations, transformer concepts, overcurrent protection, and voltage drop. Calculation questions can take more time than direct code questions, so repeated practice helps improve both speed and accuracy.

Code navigation should also be part of every study plan. Candidates should practice using the NEC table of contents, index, article numbers, definitions, tables, notes, and exceptions. The goal is to identify the topic quickly and move to the correct section without wasting time. A candidate who understands how the NEC is organized is better prepared for open-book electrical testing.

Services, feeders, and branch circuits should receive careful attention. Candidates should review service conductors, service disconnects, grounding at services, feeder sizing, branch circuit ratings, required outlets, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, and load calculations. These topics connect code knowledge with practical installation skill.

Grounding and bonding are important areas for journeyman exam preparation. Candidates should review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, raceway bonding, panel bonding, grounded conductors, separately derived systems, and related safety requirements. Grounding and bonding questions often require careful reading because similar terms can lead to different code requirements.

Wiring methods and materials should be studied consistently. Candidates should review conductors, cables, raceways, boxes, fittings, supports, conductor ampacity, insulation types, flexible wiring methods, underground wiring, and installation conditions. These topics appear frequently in electrical work and are central to NEC-based study.

Motors, transformers, controls, and equipment should also be part of the study routine. Candidates should review motor branch circuits, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, disconnecting means, controllers, transformer sizing, transformer protection, luminaires, receptacles, switches, appliances, panelboards, and related installation requirements.

Special occupancies, special equipment, and special conditions should not be ignored. Candidates should review hazardous locations, health care facilities, emergency systems, standby systems, swimming pools, signs, mobile homes, RV parks, data processing equipment, fire alarm systems, and low-voltage systems when these areas appear in the local exam outline.

Flash cards are useful for shorter study sessions. Candidates can use them to review formulas, definitions, electrical theory, safety concepts, NEC topics, and trade terms before work, after work, during breaks, or between longer study blocks. Flash cards work best when paired with deeper reading, calculation drills, and code lookup practice.

A strong study plan should include scheduled reading, NEC navigation, calculation drills, timed practice, and repeated review of weaker topics. Candidates should avoid studying only familiar material. Journeyman electrician preparation rewards steady practice, careful reading, and the ability to apply electrical knowledge under exam conditions.
