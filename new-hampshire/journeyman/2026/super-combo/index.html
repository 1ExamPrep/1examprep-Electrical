Exam Details

The New Hampshire Journeyman Electrician examination is administered by Prov for the New Hampshire Office of Professional Licensure and Certification and the State of New Hampshire Electrician Board. Candidates must first complete the appropriate application with the Board. After the state approves the candidate for testing, the candidate may schedule the examination through Prov.

The New Hampshire Journeyman Electrician examination has 110 questions and a time allowance of 4.5 hours. The exam is open book and timed. The exam fee listed in the current candidate bulletin is $90.00 for the Journeyman Electrician examination, and the fee is the same for first-time testing and retesting.

The exam subject areas include general electrical knowledge, equipment for general use, motors and generators, control devices, special conditions, special equipment, special occupancies, services and service equipment, branch circuits and conductors, feeders, wiring methods and materials, and administrative topics.

The subject breakdown lists 22 questions on general electrical knowledge, 9 questions on equipment for general use, 19 questions on motors and generators, 5 questions on control devices, 2 questions on special conditions, 2 questions on special equipment, 2 questions on special occupancies, 12 questions on services and service equipment, 6 questions on branch circuits and conductors, 8 questions on feeders, 14 questions on wiring methods and materials, and 9 questions on administrative content.

The current New Hampshire candidate bulletin lists the Journeyman Electrician exam references around the 2020 National Electrical Code, New Hampshire amendments, New Hampshire laws and rules, American Electrician’s Handbook, and Ugly’s Electrical References. Open Book Test

The New Hampshire Journeyman Electrician examination is open book. All New Hampshire electrician examinations listed in the current Prov bulletin are open book and timed. Candidates may use only authorized reference materials during the exam, and those references are checked before the candidate is permitted to enter the testing room.

Reference materials may be prepared by highlighting, underlining in pen, and attaching permanent tabs. Handwritten notes are not allowed in any portion of a reference book used for testing. Moveable tabs are never permitted. Reference books cannot contain Post-it notes, photocopied documents added to the book, photocopied versions of reference books, or unapproved study guides.

Test preparation study guides are not approved references and are not permitted in the testing room. Candidates may bring approved references and a simple 4-function calculator. PDAs, tablets, cell phones, special calculators, electronic devices, loose notes, unauthorized references, and similar materials are not allowed in the testing room.

Open book does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates still need to know how to read the question, identify the topic, locate the correct section, interpret the code language, and answer within the time limit. A candidate who is familiar with reference materials can spend less time searching and more time solving the question.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs included in this Super Combo helps candidates practice code navigation during study. Tabs can help candidates become more familiar with the layout of the NEC, including major chapters, articles, definitions, and tables. The strongest preparation comes from using the tabs as a navigation aid while also learning how the code is organized.

Licensing Steps

New Hampshire electrician licensing is handled through the Office of Professional Licensure and Certification and the Electricians’ Board. Candidates seeking licensure must complete the appropriate state application before they can take the required examination. Once the state approves the candidate, testing information is provided to Prov so the candidate can schedule the exam.

A practical licensing path begins with confirming that the Journeyman Electrician license is the correct license classification. Candidates then complete the required application, submit supporting documentation, receive state approval to test, schedule the examination through Prov, prepare using the exam subject areas and approved references, take the exam, and complete the remaining licensing process through the state.

New Hampshire offers electrician licensing classifications that include apprentice, journeyman, master, and high or medium voltage electrician categories. Candidates should apply under the classification that matches their experience, training, and work goals. The journeyman license is a major step for electricians who are moving beyond apprentice status and seeking recognition as licensed electrical professionals.

Prov provides unofficial written results by email shortly after exam completion, and official results are sent to the state within the timeframe listed in the candidate bulletin. Passing the examination is an important licensing milestone, but the license is issued through the New Hampshire licensing process after the state confirms that the applicant has satisfied the applicable requirements.

State Requirements

New Hampshire law requires state licensing for individuals who are electricians, who are engaged in electrical work, or who use a title, sign, card, advertisement, or similar device to indicate that they are engaged in electrical work. The Electricians’ Board develops and maintains professional and ethical standards for electrician licensure in the state.

Journeyman and master electrician applicants use the state’s application process through the Office of Professional Licensure and Certification. Applicants must submit the required application materials and payment to the state. The state determines examination eligibility and provides the approved candidate information to Prov for scheduling.

New Hampshire also provides an endorsement path for certain applicants. Applicants for endorsement must meet the state’s professional experience requirements in the licensure category sought. Candidates should follow the application route that matches their licensing background and documentation.

Because state licensing requirements are controlled by the New Hampshire Electricians’ Board and the Office of Professional Licensure and Certification, candidates should use the current state forms and examination instructions for their license type. Study materials help candidates prepare, but the state determines eligibility, exam approval, license issuance, renewal compliance, and enforcement of licensing requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

The New Hampshire Journeyman Electrician Super Combo supports a complete preparation routine by combining structured study, code review, calculation practice, and quick recall. General electrical knowledge is one of the largest areas on the New Hampshire Journeyman Electrician exam. Candidates should review electrical theory, basic circuit relationships, terminology, practical trade knowledge, installation concepts, safety principles, and NEC definitions. These questions may test both memory and the ability to apply concepts to real electrical work.

Motors and generators are heavily represented on the exam. Candidates should review motor circuits, motor load calculations, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, disconnecting means, controllers, motor conductors, generator concepts, and related NEC requirements. These topics often combine code knowledge with theory and calculation skills.

Services and service equipment should receive careful attention. Candidates should study service conductors, service disconnects, grounding at services, bonding, service load calculations, temporary services, available fault current, and service equipment installation requirements. These questions may require code lookup, calculation work, and practical installation understanding.

Wiring methods and materials are also important. Candidates should review raceways, conductors, cables, boxes, enclosures, cabinets, panelboards, switchboards, fittings, supports, flexible wiring methods, underground wiring methods, conductor ampacity, and protection requirements. This area rewards candidates who can move through the NEC efficiently and apply installation rules correctly.

Electrical calculations should be practiced consistently before the exam. Candidates should review Ohm’s Law, power formulas, amperage, voltage, resistance, watts, conductor sizing, branch circuit calculations, feeder calculations, box fill, conduit fill, motor calculations, transformer concepts, overcurrent protection, and voltage drop. Calculation questions can take more time than direct code lookup questions, so repeated practice helps improve both speed and accuracy.

Administrative topics are part of the New Hampshire Journeyman Electrician exam. Candidates should review New Hampshire laws and rules applicable to electrician licensing, state amendments, licensing responsibilities, and the administrative content listed for the exam. Administrative questions can affect the final score and should not be left for last-minute review.

Special conditions, special equipment, and special occupancies should also be part of the study plan. Candidates should review hazardous locations, emergency systems, standby systems, pools, signs, special equipment installations, and other NEC areas that may appear in journeyman-level preparation. Even a small number of questions can make a difference in a timed licensing exam.

Flash cards are useful for shorter study sessions. Candidates can use them to review formulas, definitions, electrical theory, safety concepts, NEC topics, and trade terms before work, after work, during breaks, or between longer study blocks. Flash cards work best when paired with deeper reading, calculation drills, and code lookup practice.

A strong study plan should include scheduled reading, NEC navigation, calculation drills, timed practice, and repeated review of weaker topics. Candidates should avoid studying only familiar material. Journeyman electrician preparation rewards steady practice, careful reading, and the ability to apply electrical knowledge under exam conditions.
