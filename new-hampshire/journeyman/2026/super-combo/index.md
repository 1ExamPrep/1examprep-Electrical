## Open Book Test

The New Hampshire Journeyman Electrician examination is open book. All New Hampshire electrician examinations listed in the current Prov bulletin are open book and timed. Candidates may use only authorized reference materials during the exam, and those references are checked before the candidate is permitted to enter the testing room.

Reference materials may be prepared by highlighting, underlining in pen, and attaching permanent tabs. Handwritten notes are not allowed in any portion of a reference book used for testing. Moveable tabs are never permitted. Reference books cannot contain Post-it notes, photocopied documents added to the book, photocopied versions of reference books, or unapproved study guides.

Test preparation study guides are not approved references and are not permitted in the testing room. Candidates may bring approved references and a simple 4-function calculator. PDAs, tablets, cell phones, special calculators, electronic devices, loose notes, unauthorized references, and similar materials are not allowed in the testing room.

Open book does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates still need to know how to read the question, identify the topic, locate the correct section, interpret the code language, and answer within the time limit. A candidate who is familiar with reference materials can spend less time searching and more time solving the question.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs included in this Super Combo helps candidates practice code navigation during study. Tabs can help candidates become more familiar with the layout of the NEC, including major chapters, articles, definitions, and tables. The strongest preparation comes from using the tabs as a navigation aid while also learning how the code is organized.

Once the state approves the candidate, testing information is provided to Prov so the candidate can schedule the exam.

A practical licensing path begins with confirming that the Journeyman Electrician license is the correct license classification. Candidates then complete the required application, submit supporting documentation, receive state approval to test, schedule the examination through Prov, prepare using the exam subject areas and approved references, take the exam, and complete the remaining licensing process through the state.

New Hampshire offers electrician licensing classifications that include apprentice, journeyman, master, and high or medium voltage electrician categories. Candidates should apply under the classification that matches their experience, training, and work goals. The journeyman license is a major step for electricians who are moving beyond apprentice status and seeking recognition as licensed electrical professionals.

Prov provides unofficial written results by email shortly after exam completion, and official results are sent to the state within the timeframe listed in the candidate bulletin. Passing the examination is an important licensing milestone, but the license is issued through the New Hampshire licensing process after the state confirms that the applicant has satisfied the applicable requirements.

## State Requirements

New Hampshire law requires state licensing for individuals who are electricians, who are engaged in electrical work, or who use a title, sign, card, advertisement, or similar device to indicate that they are engaged in electrical work. The Electricians’ Board develops and maintains professional and ethical standards for electrician licensure in the state.

Journeyman and master electrician applicants use the state’s application process through the Office of Professional Licensure and Certification. Applicants must submit the required application materials and payment to the state. The state determines examination eligibility and provides the approved candidate information to Prov for scheduling.

New Hampshire also provides an endorsement path for certain applicants. Applicants for endorsement must meet the state’s professional experience requirements in the licensure category sought. Candidates should follow the application route that matches their licensing background and documentation.

Because state licensing requirements are controlled by the New Hampshire Electricians’ Board and the Office of Professional Licensure and Certification, candidates should use the current state forms and examination instructions for their license type. Study materials help candidates prepare, but the state determines eligibility, exam approval, license issuance, renewal compliance, and enforcement of licensing requirements.
