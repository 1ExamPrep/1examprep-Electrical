The 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide supports the math portion of preparation. Electrical calculations can be difficult when students have not practiced them recently. Useful calculation study areas may include Ohm’s law, power formulas, conductor sizing, box fill, raceway fill, voltage drop concepts, service and feeder calculations, motor calculations, overcurrent protection, and load calculation principles.

Calculation preparation should be active. Students should work problems by hand, write out formulas, identify the known values, check units, and review mistakes carefully. Reading a solved example can help introduce a topic, but repeated practice is what builds speed and confidence. The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback should be used throughout the study process. Students should practice using the index, article headings, tables, and definitions. They should also learn how different code topics connect. For example, a conductor question may involve ampacity, adjustment factors, terminal limitations, overcurrent protection, or equipment requirements. A grounding question may involve grounding electrodes, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, services, or fault-current path concepts.

A balanced study schedule may rotate between trade review, calculation practice, and NEC navigation. One session might focus on grounding and bonding. Another might focus on conductor calculations. Another might involve locating raceway and enclosure rules in the NEC. This rotation helps students avoid over-studying one subject while leaving another area weak.

Students should also include timed practice. Since the Arkansas journeyman exam is timed and open book, pacing is an important part of preparation. Timed practice helps students learn when to look up an answer, when to move forward, and how to manage the exam without spending too long on one question.
