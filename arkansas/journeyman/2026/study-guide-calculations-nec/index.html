Arkansas electrician licensing examinations are administered through Prov for licensing through the Arkansas Department of Labor and Licensing. Candidates must complete the proper application process with the Arkansas Board of Electrical Examiners before testing. The state requires licensing for individuals who work as electricians or who are engaged in electrical work under the covered licensing categories.

The Arkansas Journeyman Electrical exam is listed as a timed, open book examination. The journeyman exam has been listed with 60 questions, a 3-hour testing time, and a required passing score of 70%. Candidates should prepare for both trade knowledge and code-based questions.

Licensing Steps

Arkansas journeyman electrician candidates begin by applying through the Arkansas Board of Electrical Examiners. The applicant must submit the required application materials and documentation for review before being approved to test. The Board determines whether the applicant satisfies the requirements for the license classification requested.

After the application is approved, the candidate may move forward with the examination process through Prov. The exam is scheduled according to the testing provider’s procedures. Candidates should review scheduling rules, testing location options, identification requirements, and approved reference rules before exam day.

On exam day, candidates should arrive prepared with the required identification and permitted reference materials. Because the exam is timed and open book, it is important to practice with the code book before testing. Candidates who are unfamiliar with reference navigation may lose time even when they understand the general electrical topic.

After testing, results are reported according to the state and testing provider procedures. A passing score allows the candidate to continue with the licensing process. Candidates who do not pass must follow the applicable retesting requirements before attempting the examination again.

A strong licensing plan should begin before the application is submitted. Candidates should organize experience documentation, confirm that their work history supports the license classification they are seeking, review the Board’s instructions, and create a regular study schedule. The best study plans include electrical trade review, code navigation, and calculation practice spread over time.

State Requirements

Arkansas journeyman electrician applicants must satisfy the experience and application requirements established for the license classification. A journeyman electrician applicant is commonly associated with four years of qualifying electrical work experience or 8,000 hours of documented training and experience under appropriate supervision.

Arkansas also recognizes apprentice registration as part of the electrical licensing structure. Apprentices gain supervised experience while building the trade knowledge needed for future journeyman-level work. Candidates moving toward the journeyman exam should keep accurate employment records, training documentation, and any supporting materials needed for the application process.

The Arkansas Board of Electrical Examiners administers licensing for several electrical classifications. Students should make sure they are applying for the correct license type. The journeyman electrician license is different from a master electrician license, residential journeyman license, residential master license, air conditioning electrician license, and industrial maintenance electrician license.

Arkansas electrical work must be performed under the proper licensing structure. The licensing program is intended to help ensure that individuals performing electrical work have the necessary skill, knowledge, and training to do the work safely and effectively. Candidates should follow the Board’s application instructions carefully and provide complete documentation before testing.

The 2026 Arkansas Journeyman Electrician Study Guide helps students organize these subjects into a more focused study path. Instead of studying random code sections without a plan, students can review major topic areas one at a time and connect each subject to the way electrical work is performed in the field.

The 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide supports the math portion of preparation. Electrical calculations can be difficult when students have not practiced them recently. Useful calculation study areas may include Ohm’s law, power formulas, conductor sizing, box fill, raceway fill, voltage drop concepts, service and feeder calculations, motor calculations, overcurrent protection, and load calculation principles.

Calculation preparation should be active. Students should work problems by hand, write out formulas, identify the known values, check units, and review mistakes carefully. Reading a solved example can help introduce a topic, but repeated practice is what builds speed and confidence. This is especially important for timed exams because even a familiar formula can become stressful when a candidate is working against the clock.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback should be used throughout the study process. Students should practice using the index, article headings, tables, and definitions. They should also learn how different code topics connect. For example, a conductor question may involve ampacity, adjustment factors, terminal limitations, overcurrent protection, or equipment requirements. A grounding question may involve grounding electrodes, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, services, or fault-current path concepts.

A balanced study schedule may rotate between trade review, calculation practice, and NEC navigation. One session might focus on grounding and bonding. Another might focus on conductor calculations. Another might involve locating raceway and enclosure rules in the NEC. This rotation helps students avoid over-studying one subject while leaving another area weak.

Students should also include timed practice. Since the Arkansas journeyman exam is timed and open book, pacing is an important part of preparation. Timed practice helps students learn when to look up an answer, when to move forward, and how to manage the exam without spending too long on one question.
