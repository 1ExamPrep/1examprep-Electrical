Exam Details

The Arkansas Department of Labor and Licensing oversees the licensing process for electricians, and the Board contracts with Prov, Inc. to develop and administer the electrical licensing examination program. Candidates must first complete the appropriate application with the Arkansas Board of Electrical Examiners. Once approved for testing, the Board notifies the candidate that they are eligible to contact Prov to schedule the examination.

The Arkansas Journeyman Electrician exam is listed by Prov as an examination available in English or Spanish. The official Arkansas electrical candidate information bulletin identifies the Arkansas Journeyman Electrician exam as a National Electrical Code-based exam. The current bulletin lists the Arkansas Journeyman Electrician examination as 60 questions with a 3-hour time allowance. The bulletin also states that all exams are graded against a cut score of 70%.

The Arkansas Journeyman Electrician exam content outline includes grounding and bonding, general knowledge of the electrical trade and calculations, service, feeders, branch feeders and overcurrent protection, raceways and enclosures, conductors, motors and controls, utilization and general use equipment, special occupancies and equipment, low voltage circuits including alarms and communications, and safety.

Because the exam covers several important trade areas, candidates should prepare with a balanced study plan. It is not enough to study only calculations or only code definitions. Candidates should review electrical theory, calculations, wiring methods, grounding, service equipment, branch circuits, motors, low voltage systems, safety, and general electrical trade knowledge. The strongest preparation routine combines reading, code lookup, calculation practice, flash card review, and timed practice questions.

This product is based on the 2026 National Electrical Code and is designed for candidates preparing with the 2026 code cycle. Arkansas examination references may be updated by the Board or testing provider as code cycles change, so candidates should use the exam bulletin and Board instructions connected to their approved testing window when scheduling.

Open Book Test

The Arkansas electrical candidate information bulletin states that all examinations administered through the program are open book and timed. For the Arkansas Journeyman Electrician exam, the listed reference is NFPA 70, the National Electrical Code. Open-book testing gives candidates access to an approved reference, but it does not remove the need for preparation.

Open-book electrical exams require speed and familiarity with the code book. A candidate who does not know how the National Electrical Code is organized may spend too much time searching for answers. A candidate who studies with the NEC regularly can move more efficiently between articles, sections, tables, definitions, and exceptions.

Prov’s reference rules allow candidates to prepare reference materials using highlighting, underlining in pen only, and permanent tabs. The rules do not allow handwritten notes in reference books, moveable tabs, Post-it notes, photocopied documents added to reference books, photocopied versions of reference books, or test preparation study guides in the testing center. Candidates may not highlight, underline, mark, or write in reference materials during the examination.

The included National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs is useful for building code-navigation habits during study. Use the code book while working through practice questions. Locate the rule, read the full section, check nearby exceptions, review table notes, and confirm that the answer matches the question. This type of practice helps candidates become more accurate and efficient under timed conditions.

Licensing Steps

The Arkansas journeyman electrician licensing process begins with identifying the correct license class and meeting the Board’s application requirements. Arkansas recognizes several electrical license classes, including journeyman electrician and residential journeyman electrician, so candidates should make sure they are applying for the correct classification before submitting an application or scheduling an examination.

Candidates must complete the appropriate application through the Arkansas Board of Electrical Examiners. The Board reviews the application and determines eligibility for testing. Prov is not authorized to determine which examination a candidate must take. Questions about the correct exam or license class must be resolved with the Board before scheduling.

After the Board approves a candidate for testing, the candidate is notified that they are eligible to contact Prov and schedule the examination. The candidate information bulletin states that approved candidates are eligible to take the exam for six months. Candidates should use that eligibility period wisely by scheduling the exam after they have had time to review the code book, work through calculations, and complete practice questions.

After completing the exam, candidates receive a score result at the testing center. Prov shares results with the Arkansas Department of Labor and Licensing Electrical Examining Board because the examination process is a licensing requirement. Candidates who pass should continue following Board instructions for license issuance. Candidates who do not pass should follow the Board and Prov retesting procedures.

State Requirements

The Arkansas Board of Electrical Examiners is responsible for administering Arkansas laws governing the licensing of electricians. The Board also registers electrical apprentices and establishes standards for the performance of electrical work in the state. The Director of Code Enforcement has responsibility for enforcing these electrical laws.

Arkansas law requires state licensing for individuals who are electricians, are engaged in electrical work, or use any title, sign, card, advertisement, or other device to indicate that they are engaged in electrical work. Because licensing is tied to the specific class of work, candidates should follow the requirements for the correct Arkansas electrical license class.

The Board’s available electrical license classes include master electrician, journeyman electrician, residential master electrician, residential journeyman electrician, air conditioning electrician, and industrial maintenance electrician. The Arkansas Journeyman Electrician classification is the focus of this product. Candidates pursuing a different classification should use the exam bulletin and licensing requirements for that specific category.

For the Arkansas Journeyman Electrician exam, the candidate information bulletin identifies the National Electrical Code as the listed reference. Candidates should be prepared to use the NEC efficiently and follow all testing provider reference rules. Test preparation study guides and practice examinations are not approved references for the testing room, even though they are useful study tools before exam day.

Arkansas also provides reciprocity information for certain electrical license classes. Reciprocity rules are separate from the exam preparation process and depend on the original license state and current Board requirements. Candidates seeking reciprocity should follow Arkansas Board instructions for reciprocal licensing rather than assuming that exam preparation materials alone satisfy the licensing process.
