Exam Details

The Arkansas Board of Electrical Examiners is responsible for the administration of Arkansas laws governing electrician licensing. The Arkansas Department of Labor and Licensing oversees the state electrical licensing process, and the state provides license classifications that include master electrician, journeyman electrician, residential master electrician, residential journeyman electrician, air conditioning electrician, and industrial maintenance electrician.

The Arkansas Department of Labor and Licensing has contracted with Prov, Inc. to develop and administer the electrician licensing examination program. The Candidate Information Bulletin explains the testing process, scheduling, examination rules, and exam requirements for applicants seeking electrical licensing in Arkansas.

The Arkansas journeyman electrician examination is designed to measure whether a candidate has the knowledge needed to perform journeyman-level electrical work safely and in accordance with applicable standards. Candidates should expect the exam to emphasize electrical code knowledge, trade practice, electrical theory, calculations, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, services, feeders, branch circuits, motors, transformers, and safety-related installation principles.

The Candidate Information Bulletin identifies the journeyman electrician examination as an open book examination. The bulletin also identifies the journeyman electrician examination as a timed exam with a passing score requirement. Candidates should prepare to use approved reference materials efficiently and should become comfortable locating information in the National Electrical Code before the examination.

This product is based on the 2026 National Electrical Code. Candidates preparing for an Arkansas electrical examination should match their study plan to the NEC edition required for their exam date. Licensing Steps

The Arkansas journeyman electrician licensing process begins with gaining qualifying electrical work experience. Arkansas journeyman electrician applicants are generally required to show four years, or 8,000 hours, of electrical work experience, or a four-year combination of board-approved training and experience. Maintenance electrical experience may be considered by the Board as part of the review process.

Applicants should keep accurate records of employment, training, and supervised electrical work. Documentation is important because the Board must be able to determine whether the applicant meets the experience requirements for the license classification. Candidates coming through an apprenticeship or training program should also follow the program’s procedures for release to test when applicable.

After meeting the experience requirement, the candidate submits the required application materials through the Arkansas electrical licensing process. The Board reviews the application and determines whether the applicant is eligible to test. Once eligible, the candidate follows the examination scheduling procedures provided through the testing vendor.

After passing the required examination, the applicant completes the remaining licensing steps required by the Arkansas Department of Labor and Licensing. Candidates should follow the state’s current instructions for applications, fees, documentation, and license issuance. Keeping copies of applications, exam notices, score reports, and experience records can help avoid delays.

Candidates should also stay aware of renewal and continuing education requirements after licensure. Electrical licensing is not only about passing the first exam. Licensed electricians must maintain their credential according to state rules and should continue to stay current with code changes as new NEC cycles are adopted.

State Requirements

Arkansas requires state licensing for electricians. The Board of Electrical Examiners administers the state’s electrician licensing laws, and the Arkansas Department of Labor and Licensing provides the electrical inspection and licensing program. The state recognizes multiple electrical license classifications, including journeyman electrician.

The journeyman electrician credential is intended for individuals who have developed the experience and knowledge needed to perform electrical work at the journeyman level. Arkansas requires applicants to document qualifying experience before becoming eligible for examination. The common experience requirement for journeyman electrician applicants is four years, or 8,000 hours, of electrical work experience, or an accepted combination of approved training and experience.

Electrical work experience should be relevant to the license classification and should support the applicant’s ability to perform electrical installations safely and in compliance with applicable code requirements. Candidates should make sure their experience documentation is complete, accurate, and submitted in the format required by the state.

Arkansas also registers electrical apprentices. Apprenticeship and supervised training are important parts of the licensing pathway because they help candidates build the real-world skills needed for safe electrical work. Candidates who are still accumulating hours should keep records throughout the process instead of waiting until the end of their training period.

The state electrical licensing process is tied to public safety. Licensing helps ensure that individuals performing electrical work have demonstrated the training, experience, and knowledge needed to work safely. The journeyman exam is one part of that process, and preparation should focus on both code knowledge and practical electrical understanding.

Test Information and Study Materials

Journeyman electrician preparation should include a combination of trade review, code study, calculation practice, and repeated self-testing. Candidates should review electrical theory, conductors, raceways, boxes, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, services, feeders, branch circuits, motors, transformers, special occupancies, special equipment, and general safety practices.

The National Electrical Code is one of the most important study tools for an electrical candidate. It contains the rules and structure used to support safe electrical installations. Candidates should become familiar with Article 100 definitions, wiring and protection requirements, wiring methods and materials, equipment rules, special occupancies, special equipment, and tables used for sizing, fill, adjustment, and correction.

When studying with this combo, begin with one topic in the Arkansas Journeyman Electrician Study Guide, then locate the related NEC sections in the 2026 code book. This approach helps connect study guide review with the actual code language. It also helps candidates avoid relying only on memorized answers.

For calculation topics, write out the full process. Electrical calculations often require identifying what the question is asking for, selecting the correct formula or table, applying the proper adjustment or correction, and checking whether the answer should be a minimum size, maximum rating, ampacity, load, conductor size, or overcurrent device rating. Writing each step helps reveal mistakes and builds accuracy.

Code lookup practice should be timed occasionally. Open book exams can feel easier in theory, but time pressure can make them difficult if the candidate has not practiced using the book. Set aside study sessions where you answer questions while using the NEC, but give yourself a reasonable time limit. This helps build speed without sacrificing accuracy.

Reviewing missed questions is one of the most valuable parts of preparation. When an answer is wrong, return to the study guide and the NEC. Identify the rule, read the surrounding code language, and understand why the correct answer applies. This habit helps you prepare for questions that test the same concept in a different way.

Because this product is based on the 2026 NEC, candidates should use it to build familiarity with the 2026 code cycle. Electrical code updates can affect terminology, article structure, and rule language. Studying with the code edition connected to your preparation helps make review more consistent and organized.
