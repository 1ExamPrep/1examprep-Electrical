## Open Book Test

The Arkansas Journeyman Electrician exam is an open-book test. Open book does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates still need to know how the National Electrical Code is organized, how to locate rules quickly, how to apply requirements correctly, and how to answer questions within the time allowed.

Open-book electrical exams reward preparation and reference familiarity. Students should practice using the table of contents, index, definitions, article structure, tables, notes, and exceptions before exam day. The more comfortable a student is with the code book, the less time is wasted searching during the exam.

The Arkansas candidate bulletin states that authorized references may be used during the exam and that reference materials are checked by the test administrator before candidates enter the testing room. Reference materials may be prepared with highlighting, underlining in pen, and permanent tabs. Handwritten notes are not allowed in any portion of a reference book. Moveable tabs, Post-it notes, photocopied documents added to a reference book, photocopied reference books, test preparation study guides, and practice examinations are not approved references.

Students should use this study guide, practice exams, final exams, and flash cards before test day. During the actual exam, candidates should bring only the approved reference materials allowed for the Arkansas Journeyman Electrician exam. A strong open-book strategy includes knowing the material, practicing code navigation, and using references efficiently instead of relying on the book for every answer.

## Licensing Steps

Arkansas law requires state licensing for individuals who are electricians, who are engaged in electrical work, or who use a title or other representation indicating that they are engaged in electrical work. The Arkansas Board of Electrical Examiners administers the state’s electrical licensing laws and issues license classes including journeyman electrician, master electrician, residential journeyman electrician, residential master electrician, air conditioning electrician, and industrial maintenance electrician.

To begin the Arkansas journeyman licensing process, candidates must complete the appropriate application with the Arkansas Board of Electrical Examiners. Applications are submitted through the Board’s process, and candidates must receive approval before scheduling the examination with Prov. Prov is not responsible for determining which exam a candidate must take; the Board determines the correct examination for the license being pursued.

Arkansas employment verification materials list the journeyman experience requirement as completion of a USDOL-certified apprenticeship with 8,000 hours of on-the-job training, or out-of-state qualifying experience in a licensed jurisdiction as described by the Board’s current rules. Hours must be verified on the required Arkansas employment verification form, and each employer must verify work experience separately.

After the candidate is approved for testing, the Board notifies the candidate that they are eligible to schedule the examination with Prov. The exam is administered by computer at Prov testing centers. Test results are provided after the examination, and the state is notified of the candidate’s result.

This study guide and flash card combo supports the exam preparation stage of the licensing process. Students can use it while gathering experience documentation, after submitting an application, after receiving testing approval, or during final review before their scheduled exam date.
