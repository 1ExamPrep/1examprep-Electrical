Exam Details

The Arkansas Department of Labor and Licensing oversees the licensing process for electricians, and the Arkansas Board of Electrical Examiners is responsible for the state electrical licensing program. The Board has contracted with Prov, Inc. to develop and administer the electrical licensing examination program. Applicants must complete the proper application with the Arkansas Board of Electrical Examiners before scheduling an examination.

After an applicant is approved for testing, the Board notifies the applicant that they are eligible to contact Prov to schedule the examination. The current Arkansas candidate bulletin states that candidates are eligible to take the exam for six months after approval. Prov is not authorized to determine which examination a candidate should take; candidates must resolve exam classification questions with the Board before scheduling.

The Arkansas Master Electrician exam is listed as an examination utilizing the 2023 National Electrical Code. The current candidate bulletin lists the Arkansas Master Electrician exam as 60 questions with a 3-hour time limit. All Arkansas electrical exams listed in the bulletin are timed and open book. The cut score for all exams is 70%, and candidates who achieve that score or higher receive a passing grade.

The Arkansas Master Electrician exam subject areas include Grounding and Bonding, General Knowledge of the Electrical Trade and Calculations, Service, Feeders, Branch Feeders and Overcurrent Protection, Raceways and Enclosures, Conductors, Motors and Controls, Utilization and General Use Equipment, Special Occupancies and Equipment, Low Voltage Circuits including Alarms and Communications, and Safety.

Licensing Steps

The Arkansas master electrician licensing path begins with meeting the qualification requirements established by the Arkansas Board of Electrical Examiners. Applicants should determine the correct license classification before applying. Arkansas recognizes several license classifications, including master electrician, journeyman electrician, residential master electrician, residential journeyman electrician, air conditioning electrician, and industrial maintenance electrician.

A master electrician applicant should gather the required documentation before submitting the application. Arkansas application materials require applicants to list work experience and trade-related education. Candidates should keep accurate records of electrical employment, supervision, license history, education, and any documentation needed to support the license application.

After preparing the required documentation, the applicant submits the proper application to the Arkansas Board of Electrical Examiners. Once the Board reviews and approves the applicant for testing, the applicant receives authorization to schedule the examination through Prov. The candidate then schedules the Arkansas Master Electrician exam, studies the approved reference list, follows the open-book rules, and sits for the timed examination.

After completing the exam, Prov’s scoring system grades the exam. The Arkansas candidate bulletin states that the Board contacts the candidate by email with a copy of the test score and the next steps needed to obtain the license. Candidates who do not pass may retake a failed examination as often as necessary until the end of the eligibility period, subject to the current retesting process.

A practical preparation path includes confirming the correct license classification, documenting qualifying work experience, submitting the application to the Board, receiving approval to test, reviewing the candidate bulletin, studying the NEC, practicing calculations, building reference navigation speed, using flash cards for quick recall, passing the examination, and completing the remaining licensing steps through the Arkansas electrical licensing program.

State Requirements

Arkansas regulates electrician licensing through the Arkansas Department of Labor and Licensing and the Arkansas Board of Electrical Examiners. The Board administers the state laws governing electrician licensing and establishes standards for electrical work in Arkansas. The Director of Code Enforcement is responsible for enforcing the state’s electrical laws.

Arkansas law requires state licensing for everyone who is an electrician, is engaged in electrical work, or uses any title, sign, card, advertisement, or other device to indicate that they are engaged in electrical work. Applicants must complete the proper application with the Arkansas Board of Electrical Examiners and receive approval before scheduling the examination.

Arkansas statutes define a master electrician as an individual who possesses the necessary qualifications, training, experience, and technical knowledge to plan, lay out, and supervise the installation, maintenance, and extension of electrical conductors and equipment. Master electrician preparation should therefore include code knowledge, field application, supervisory-level understanding, safety, calculations, and the ability to interpret electrical rules accurately.

Arkansas statutes also require journeyman electrician, residential journeyman electrician, master electrician, and residential master electrician licensees to complete at least eight hours of continuing education for the current National Electrical Code cycle in the first year of each new code cycle before renewing the license.

For exam preparation, students should understand that state qualification requirements and study preparation are related but separate. State requirements determine whether an applicant may test and become licensed. Study materials help the candidate prepare for the knowledge, code, calculation, and trade portion of the process.
