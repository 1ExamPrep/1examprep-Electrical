Exam Details

Arkansas electrician licensing exams are administered by Prov for the Arkansas Department of Labor and Licensing Electrical Examining Board. Candidates must first complete the appropriate application with the Arkansas Board of Electrical Examiners. After approval for testing, the Board notifies the candidate that they are eligible to schedule the examination with Prov. Prov is not responsible for determining which examination a candidate must take; the examination must match the license being pursued.

The Arkansas Master Electrician examination is available in English or Spanish. The current Arkansas Master Electrician exam outline lists 60 questions with 3 hours of allowed testing time. The passing score is 70 percent. Results are provided at the testing center after completion, and the State is notified of the candidate’s result.

The Arkansas Master Electrician exam outline includes grounding and bonding, general electrical trade knowledge and calculations, service, feeders, branch feeders and overcurrent protection, raceways and enclosures, conductors, motors and controls, utilization and general use equipment, special occupancies and equipment, low voltage circuits including alarms and communications, and safety. These subject areas make NEC familiarity and calculation practice especially important for successful preparation.

Arkansas also offers related electrical exams for other license classes, including journeyman electrician, residential master electrician, residential journeyman electrician, industrial maintenance electrician, master sign electrician, parking lot lighting endorsement, and electrical heating and air conditioning installation contractor. Candidates should study for the exam tied to the exact Arkansas license class they are pursuing.

The Arkansas Journeyman Electrician exam outline lists 60 questions and 3 hours. The Arkansas Residential Master Electrician exam outline lists 60 questions and 3 hours. The Arkansas Residential Journeyman Electrician exam outline lists 40 questions and 2 hours. Each license category has its own scope and subject emphasis, so students should build their study plan around the correct examination.

Open Book Test

Arkansas electrical licensing examinations administered by Prov are open book and timed. Candidates may use authorized references during the exam. Reference materials are checked by the test administrator before the candidate is allowed into the testing room. Approved references may be prepared only by highlighting, underlining in pen, and attaching permanent tabs.

Arkansas testing rules do not allow handwritten notes in any portion of a reference book. Moveable tabs are not permitted. Reference books may not contain Post-it notes, photocopied documents, or photocopied versions of reference books. Test preparation study guides and practice examinations are not approved references and are not permitted in the testing center.

Candidates may bring approved references, pencils, and a calculator into the testing center. Only simple four-function calculators are permitted. Tablets, cell phones, PDAs, and special calculators are not allowed. Candidates may not share references during testing and may not highlight, underline, mark, or write anything in their reference materials during the exam.

Open-book electrical exams still require strong preparation. The time limit does not allow candidates to slowly search the code book for every answer. Students need to know how the NEC is organized, how to use the index, how to recognize the correct article, and how to work through tables and calculations efficiently. Practicing with the NEC before test day helps reduce wasted time and improves confidence.

Licensing Steps

Arkansas electrician candidates begin by identifying the correct license class. The state offers multiple electrician license categories, including master electrician, journeyman electrician, residential master electrician, residential journeyman electrician, air conditioning electrician, and industrial maintenance electrician. The correct application and examination depend on the license class being pursued.

After selecting the appropriate license class, the candidate completes the required application with the Arkansas Board of Electrical Examiners. Applications are handled through the Board’s process. Once the Board approves the candidate for testing, the candidate receives eligibility to schedule the required exam through Prov.

After approval, candidates schedule their examination with Prov. Arkansas testing is available through Prov’s testing program, and the candidate schedules based on available testing locations and appointment times. Arkansas fixed testing locations listed in the candidate information include Conway, El Dorado, Fayetteville, Fort Smith, Jonesboro, Little Rock, Paragould, and Searcy.

On test day, candidates must follow Prov’s identification, check-in, reference-material, calculator, and test-room rules. After the exam, the test is scored and the candidate receives a score result. The State is notified of the result automatically. Candidates who achieve the required score receive a passing grade for that examination.

Students should keep licensing and studying as two connected but separate tasks. The state application process determines eligibility and licensing status. Study materials help the candidate prepare for the exam content, improve code navigation, and build calculation skills before the scheduled test date.

State Requirements

The Arkansas Board of Electrical Examiners administers Arkansas electrician licensing laws. The Board is responsible for license classes, apprentice registration, and standards for electrical work in the state. The Director of Code Enforcement is responsible for enforcing these laws.

Arkansas requires state licensing for individuals who work as electricians, engage in electrical work, or use a title, sign, card, advertisement, or other device to indicate that they are engaged in electrical work. Candidates must apply through the Board before scheduling the licensing examination. After approval, the candidate may schedule with Prov for the exam tied to the license class.

The Arkansas Master Electrician license is one of the state electrical license classes. Master electrician-level preparation generally requires strong NEC knowledge, practical trade understanding, and the ability to work through code-based questions across a wide range of subject areas. Candidates preparing for this level should give special attention to grounding and bonding, services and feeders, branch circuits, overcurrent protection, raceways and enclosures, conductors, motors, utilization equipment, special occupancies, low voltage circuits, safety, and calculations.

Arkansas also maintains reciprocal agreements for certain license categories with specific states. Reciprocity rules are separate from exam preparation and depend on the original license held by the applicant and the state listed by the Board. Candidates seeking licensing by examination should follow the application and testing process for the Arkansas license class they are pursuing.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Arkansas Master Electrician study plan should combine NEC reading, topic review, calculation practice, and timed reference navigation. The exam outline includes both code-heavy topics and practical electrical knowledge. Students should study each subject area separately and then practice mixed-topic review so they can shift between questions quickly.

Grounding and bonding deserve careful attention because the Arkansas Master Electrician exam outline places significant emphasis on this area. Students should understand grounding electrode systems, bonding paths, equipment grounding conductors, grounding electrode conductors, service grounding, separately derived systems, and common NEC rules that apply to safe fault-current paths.

Services, feeders, branch circuits, and overcurrent protection are also major exam areas. Candidates should practice identifying the load involved, determining conductor requirements, selecting overcurrent protection, and recognizing when a code rule applies to a service, feeder, or branch-circuit situation. These topics often connect directly to calculations and table usage.

Raceways, enclosures, and conductors require both code knowledge and table navigation. Students should review conductor types, insulation, ampacity, conductor fill, raceway fill, box fill, bending rules, installation methods, support requirements, and environmental conditions. Exam questions may require the candidate to recognize installation limitations or apply a table correctly.

Electrical calculations should be practiced repeatedly. Candidates should work on load calculations, conductor ampacity, voltage drop concepts, box fill, raceway fill, motor calculations, overcurrent protection, and service or feeder sizing. Good calculation preparation means learning the process, not just memorizing answers. Students should read the question carefully, identify the topic, locate the correct code rule or table, set up the calculation, solve it, and check the answer against the question’s wording.

The 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide supports this process by giving students focused calculation review. Calculation errors often happen because the candidate rushes, applies the wrong table, misses a condition in the question, or forgets whether the problem asks for a minimum or maximum value. Repeated practice helps build a more reliable method.
