Exam Details

Utah electrical licensing exams are connected to the Utah Division of Professional Licensing and the state’s electrician licensing program. Beginning August 1, 2025, Utah updated its electrician examination structure. Journeyman and residential electrician candidates take a combined exam covering theory and code, while master electrician and residential master electrician candidates take a Law and Rules exam. Master Electrician candidates may also need to complete the Utah Electrician Practical exam unless they already passed the applicable practical exam when earning the journeyman license.

Utah Master Electrician applicants must pass the Utah Master Electrician Law and Rule exam and the Utah Electrician Practical exam when the practical exam is required. The practical exam is not required again for candidates who previously passed the Utah Electrician Practical exam as part of their journeyman licensing process. The Utah exam process includes approval before testing. Candidates apply through DOPL, submit the required documentation, and follow the state’s examination instructions after approval. Licensing Steps

The Utah master electrician licensing path begins with meeting one of the state’s approved qualification routes. A common path requires 8,000 hours of experience as a licensed journeyman, followed by the Utah Master Electrician Law and Rule exam and the Utah Electrician Practical exam when required. This path is designed for experienced electricians who have already gained substantial licensed journeyman experience.

Utah also recognizes education-based routes. A candidate with a bachelor’s or master’s degree in an electrical engineering program accredited by EAC/ABET may qualify with 2,000 hours of work experience as a licensed apprentice electrician, along with the required Master Electrician Law and Rule exam and practical exam when required. A candidate with an associate degree in applied science from an electrical trade school course of study may qualify with 4,000 hours of work experience as a licensed journeyman electrician, along with the required exams.

After selecting the correct qualification route, the applicant submits the master electrician application through DOPL. The application process includes documenting experience, education when applicable, licensing history, identity information, and any required forms or fees. Applicants using education-based routes should make sure the program and experience documentation match the state’s requirements.

After DOPL reviews and approves the application for examination, the candidate follows the current scheduling instructions in the Candidate Information Bulletin. Candidates should prepare for the Utah Master Electrician Law and Rule exam and determine whether the practical exam is required based on prior journeyman licensing history. Candidates who previously passed the Utah Electrician Practical exam as a journeyman do not need to retake that practical exam for the master license.

After passing the required examination components and completing the licensing process, the applicant may be issued the Utah Master Electrician license. Licensees must continue to follow Utah licensing rules, renewal requirements, continuing education requirements, contractor requirements when applicable, permit procedures, inspection requirements, supervision responsibilities, and the scope of work authorized by the license held.

State Requirements

Utah electrician licensing is administered statewide by the Utah Division of Professional Licensing. Utah requires individuals performing regulated electrical work to hold the appropriate electrical license classification. The Electricians Licensing Board and DOPL licensing program support the state’s electrical licensing standards, examination process, and enforcement of licensing requirements.

The Utah Master Electrician license is an advanced individual credential. The standard experience route requires 8,000 hours of experience as a licensed journeyman. Education-based routes are also available for qualified applicants with approved electrical engineering or electrical trade school education, paired with the required licensed experience.

Utah distinguishes individual electrical licensure from contractor licensing. A Master Electrician license shows individual qualification at the master level, but contracting for electrical work may require the proper contractor license, qualifying individual relationship, business registration, insurance, bond, and other construction trade licensing requirements. Electricians who plan to operate or qualify an electrical contracting business should understand both the master electrician license and the contractor licensing path.
