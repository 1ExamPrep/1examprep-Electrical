## Exam Details

Utah electrician examinations are administered through the Utah electrical exam program used by DOPL. Current Utah electrical exam categories include Journeyman Examination, Residential Examination, Master Law and Rules Examination, Utah Electrician Practical, and Utah Residential Electrical Practical. Master Electrician candidates take the Utah Master Electrician Law and Rule Exam and the Utah Electrician Practical Exam, unless the practical exam was previously passed as part of the Journeyman Electrician path.

The current Utah Master Electrician Rule and Laws exam is listed as an 80-question exam with 240 minutes of testing time and a 75% passing score. The exam content includes Utah construction trades licensing law, DOPL licensing law, Utah electrician licensing rules, general DOPL rules, NEC amendments, NFPA 70E safety content, grounding and bonding, services, feeders, branch circuits, conductors, cables, voltage drop, overcurrent protection, special occupancies, special equipment, and other advanced electrical topics.

The Utah Electrician Practical Exam is a hands-on exam made up of six tasks with 110 minutes of testing time. Practical tasks include torque setting, transformer termination, conduit bending, motor control, three-way and four-way switch wiring, and schematic reading and wiring. Candidates who already passed the Utah Electrician Practical Exam while earning the Journeyman Electrician license do not need to retake it for the Master Electrician license.

## Licensing Steps

Utah Master Electrician licensing is handled through DOPL. A typical path includes confirming the correct license classification, meeting the education and experience requirements, taking the required Utah Master Electrician Law and Rule Exam, completing the Utah Electrician Practical Exam when required, submitting the license application, and completing final licensing steps through DOPL.

Utah offers several pathways for Master Electrician applicants. One pathway requires 8,000 hours of experience as a licensed Journeyman Electrician. Another pathway allows a bachelor’s or master’s degree in an electrical engineering program accredited by EAC/ABET with 2,000 hours of work experience as a licensed Apprentice Electrician. A third pathway allows an associate degree in applied science from an electrical trade school course of study with 4,000 hours of work experience as a licensed Journeyman Electrician.

Master Electrician applicants must pass the Utah Master Electrician Law and Rule Exam and the Utah Electrician Practical Exam. The practical exam is not required when the applicant previously passed it as a Journeyman Electrician. Candidates should keep organized records of license history, electrical experience, education, exam results, application materials, and DOPL correspondence.
