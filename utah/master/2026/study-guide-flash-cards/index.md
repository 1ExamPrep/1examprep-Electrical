## State Requirements

Utah electrical licensing is regulated by the Utah Division of Professional Licensing. The Division provides application instructions, exam information, licensing classifications, renewal information, laws and rules, and related resources for electrical professionals.

Utah Master Electrician applicants may qualify through several pathways. One pathway requires **8,000 hours of experience as a licensed journeyman electrician** and passing the Utah Master Electrician Law and Rule exam and the Utah Electrician Practical Exam. The practical exam is not required if it was previously passed as a journeyman.

Another pathway is available to applicants with a bachelor’s or master’s degree in an electrical engineering program accredited by EAC/ABET. Under that path, the applicant must also have **2,000 hours of work experience as a licensed apprentice electrician** and must pass the required Utah Master Electrician Law and Rule exam and practical exam when applicable.

A third pathway is available to applicants with an associate degree in applied science from an electrical trade school course of study. Under that path, the applicant must have **4,000 hours of work experience as a licensed journeyman electrician** and must pass the required Utah Master Electrician Law and Rule exam and practical exam when applicable.

Utah also identifies separate requirements for journeyman and residential licenses. Journeyman applicants may qualify through completion of a four-year electrical apprenticeship education program with required licensed apprentice experience, or through a larger number of hours of licensed apprentice experience. Residential journeyman and residential master applicants follow separate residential licensing requirements.

Utah announced electrical exam changes effective August 1, 2025. Under the updated structure, journeyman and residential electrician candidates take a single combined exam covering theory and code, while master electrician candidates take a new Law and Rule exam. Candidates should use current DOPL and Prov instructions when preparing, registering, and submitting documentation.
