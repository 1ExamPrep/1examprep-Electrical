## Exam Details

Utah electrician licensing is handled through the Utah Division of Professional Licensing under the Utah Department of Commerce. Utah has several electrician license classifications, including Apprentice, Journeyman, Master, Residential Journeyman, and Residential Master. Candidates pursuing the Utah Master Electrician license must meet the education and experience path required for the classification and pass the required examinations.

Utah’s electrical licensing examinations are administered through Prov. Beginning August 1, 2025, Utah changed the electrician licensing examination structure. Master Electrician candidates no longer take separate theory and code exams. Instead, Master Electrician candidates take the Utah Master Electrician Law and Rules examination. Master Electrician candidates must also pass the Utah Electrician Practical Exam unless the practical exam was previously passed as part of earning a Utah Journeyman Electrician license.

The Utah Master Electrician Rule and Laws examination is listed with 80 questions, 240 minutes of testing time, and a required passing score of 75 percent. The content outline includes communications systems, conductors and cables, the Utah Construction Trades Licensing Act, current, resistance, voltage drop, definitions and general requirements, the DOPL Licensing Act, the Electricians Licensing Act Rule, general DOPL licensing rules, grounding and bonding, lighting and general-use equipment, motors and controllers, NFPA 70E, overcurrent protection, raceways and enclosures, services, feeders, branch circuits, special conditions, special occupancies and equipment, Utah health and safety material, Utah labor code material, Utah occupational health and safety material, and Utah NEC amendments.

The Utah Electrician Practical Exam is a hands-on examination listed with 6 tasks and 110 minutes of testing time. The practical exam subjects include torque setting, transformer termination, conduit bending, commercial motor control using a ladder diagram, 4-way and 3-way switch wiring, and schematic reading and wiring. Candidates who previously passed the Utah Electrician Practical Exam when earning a Journeyman license are not required to retake that practical exam for the Master Electrician license.

## Open Book Test

The Utah Master Electrician Law and Rules examination is an open book examination. Open book testing does not make the exam easy. It means candidates must know how to use approved references efficiently, read code and rule language carefully, understand table notes and exceptions, and apply requirements under time pressure.

Utah’s exam reference rules allow candidates to use listed reference books during theory or code examinations. Current and previous year code books are permitted for use during the exam. The bulletin encourages candidates to prepare testing materials by highlighting text or placing permanent tabs on important pages. Candidates must follow the exam provider’s reference rules for exact titles, editions, permanent tabs, highlighting, underlining, handwritten notes, added documents, stickers, and marking in books during the exam.

Reference rules are strict. Moveable tabs such as repositionable tabs or Post-it notes are not permitted. Books may have highlighting and underlining in pen, but candidates may not mark in books during the test. Handwritten notes are not allowed in any portion of a reference book unless authorized by the licensing or certifying jurisdiction. Other documents, formula sheets, indexes, stickers, or attached items may not be added, glued, or taped into a reference book unless permitted by the exam details.

## Licensing Steps

A practical Utah licensing path begins by confirming that the Master Electrician license is the correct classification for the candidate’s goal. Utah’s Master Electrician classification is a higher-level individual electrical license, and candidates should understand the difference between an individual electrician license and any separate contractor or business licensing requirements that may apply to electrical contracting activity.

Utah applicants for the Master Electrician license may qualify through one of several paths. One path requires 8,000 hours of experience as a licensed Journeyman Electrician, passing the Utah Master Electrician Law and Rule exam, and passing the Utah Electrician Practical Exam unless the practical exam was previously passed as a Journeyman. Another path requires a bachelor’s or master’s degree in an electrical engineering program accredited by EAC/ABET, 2,000 hours of work experience as a licensed Apprentice Electrician, and the required exams. Another path requires an associate degree in applied science from an electrical trade school course of study, 4,000 hours of work experience as a licensed Journeyman Electrician, and the required exams.

Utah does not require preapproval before testing for the electrician exams. Candidates register and schedule examinations through Prov and must self-certify under penalty of perjury that they meet the necessary exam eligibility requirements before beginning the exam. Effective January 19, 2026, first-time candidates registering for licensing exams with Prov must submit the Verification of Electrician Experience form directly to Prov by electronically attaching it to the exam registration.

After meeting the testing path and passing the required examination components, the candidate completes the license application process through Utah DOPL. Utah provides online, manual, and in-person application options. Applicants should prepare required experience documentation, education documentation when applicable, exam results, personal identification information, and any other required application materials.

The study portion of the licensing path should be treated as a structured project. Candidates should review the Utah exam structure, study the NEC, practice calculations, review Utah law and rule material, strengthen safety knowledge, prepare for practical skill expectations when applicable, and use flash cards for repeated reinforcement. Consistent preparation helps candidates approach Utah master electrician study with more organization and confidence.
