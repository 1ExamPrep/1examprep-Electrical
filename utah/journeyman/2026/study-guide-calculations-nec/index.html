Exam Details

The Utah journeyman electrician licensing exam is administered through the Utah electrical exam program. Utah’s updated journeyman electrician written exam structure became effective after August 1, 2025, replacing the prior separate code and theory format with a single journeyman electrician examination covering both code and theory subject matter.

The Utah journeyman electrician written examination includes 100 questions, allows 240 minutes, and requires a 75% passing score. The exam content includes electrical code knowledge, theory, calculations, and state law and rule topics. Candidates should be prepared to answer both direct knowledge questions and application-based questions that require careful interpretation of electrical concepts and code provisions.

Published Utah journeyman electrician exam content areas include conductors and cables, current, definitions and general requirements, grounding and bonding, lighting and general-use equipment, motors and controllers, overcurrent protection, power, raceways and enclosures, resistance, services, feeders and branch circuits, special occupancies and equipment, state laws and rules, voltage, and voltage drop.

The exam places strong emphasis on real working knowledge. Grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, conductors, services, feeders, branch circuits, raceways, voltage drop, motors, and calculations can all require careful reading and problem-solving. Candidates should be comfortable moving between electrical theory, code structure, and jobsite-style application.

Utah also requires a practical electrician exam as part of the journeyman electrician licensing process. The practical exam is hands-on and is intended to evaluate technical knowledge and ability. Candidates pursuing licensure should prepare for both the written journeyman electrician examination and the Utah Electrician Practical exam requirements.

Exam fees, scheduling procedures, and candidate registration requirements are handled through the official exam program. Utah announced that, effective January 19, 2026, first-time candidates registering for licensing exams with Prov must submit the Verification of Electrician Experience form directly to Prov by electronically attaching it to the exam registration. Out-of-state applicants who attended a trade program must also attach official transcripts to their registration, and graduates of approved Utah trade programs may be placed in pending status until school authorization is completed.

Open Book Test

The Utah electrical exam program includes reference rules for open book exams. Candidates using reference materials must follow the testing provider’s rules for exact book titles, tabs, highlighting, underlining, loose materials, notes, and added documents. Permanent tabs are allowed, but movable tabs and Post-it notes are not allowed. Books may have highlighting and underlining in pen, but candidates may not mark in books during the exam. Handwritten notes are not allowed in any portion of a reference book unless specifically authorized by the licensing or certifying jurisdiction.

Open book does not mean easy. Many candidates lose time because they know the material generally but cannot find the exact article, table, definition, or calculation method quickly enough. A productive study plan should include code navigation practice, repeated review of major NEC chapters, and timed practice with electrical calculations. This combo helps candidates build those habits by combining code study with journeyman exam review and calculation-focused preparation.

The better a candidate understands the structure of the NEC, the more useful the code book becomes during a timed test.

Licensing Steps

Utah journeyman electrician applicants must meet Utah licensing requirements before receiving a license. For Utah applicants, the state lists a path that includes completion of a four-year electrical apprenticeship education program with 576 classroom hours, 8,000 hours of licensed electrical experience working under the supervision of a licensed journeyman or master electrician, and passing the Utah Journeyman Electrician Written exam and Utah Electrician Practical exam.

Utah also lists an alternate path for applicants with 16,000 hours of licensed electrical experience working under the supervision of a licensed journeyman or master electrician, along with passing the Utah Journeyman Electrician Written exam and Utah Electrician Practical exam.

A practical licensing path for candidates generally includes confirming eligibility, gathering experience documentation, completing required education or experience, registering for the required exams, preparing for the written and practical exams, passing the examinations, and submitting the license application through the Utah Division of Professional Licensing process. Candidates should make sure their experience records, education documentation, and exam registration materials match the requirements for their situation.

It is built to help candidates prepare academically and practically for the journeyman electrician exam topics they are expected to know.

State Requirements

Utah journeyman electrician licensure is regulated through the Utah Division of Professional Licensing. Utah applicants must satisfy the required education and electrical experience standards and pass the required examinations. The state’s journeyman electrician applicant information includes two main qualification routes: a four-year electrical apprenticeship education program with 576 classroom hours plus 8,000 hours of licensed supervised electrical experience, or 16,000 hours of licensed supervised electrical experience.

Experience must be electrical experience performed as a licensed apprentice electrician under the supervision of a licensed journeyman or master electrician. The state’s requirements are designed to confirm that applicants have both classroom preparation and field experience before being licensed at the journeyman level.

Utah’s written journeyman electrician examination includes state laws and rules as part of the content outline. Candidates should not focus only on calculations and NEC questions. State rules, licensing responsibilities, and Utah-specific requirements can also appear on the exam. A complete study plan should balance code knowledge, theory, calculations, and licensing law awareness.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Utah journeyman electrician study plan should include several types of preparation. Candidates should review NEC organization, practice electrical calculations, study state law and rule topics, and become familiar with the timing and structure of the written examination. The official journeyman electrician written exam includes 100 questions over 240 minutes, so pacing matters. Four hours may sound generous, but code lookup questions and calculations can consume time quickly when a candidate is not organized.

The Utah journeyman electrician content outline includes conductors and cables, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, services, feeders, branch circuits, raceways and enclosures, motors and controllers, lighting and general-use equipment, special occupancies and equipment, definitions, general requirements, current, resistance, voltage, power, and voltage drop. These areas should be studied in a repeated cycle rather than reviewed only once.

Calculation preparation is especially important because many electrician candidates are comfortable with field work but need extra repetition with exam-style math. Electrical calculations may require candidates to read the question carefully, identify the correct formula or code table, apply the correct units, and avoid common mistakes with rounding, conductor sizing, voltage drop, or load values. The 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide gives candidates a dedicated resource for this part of their preparation.

Code navigation is another key skill. Candidates should learn where major topics are located in the NEC and how articles are structured. Definitions, branch circuits, feeders, services, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, conductor rules, boxes, raceways, motors, transformers, special occupancies, special equipment, tables, and annex material should become familiar through repeated use. The goal is to reduce hesitation on exam day and improve accuracy under timed conditions.

For best results, candidates should create a weekly study schedule. A practical routine may include one session for Utah journeyman electrician trade review, one session for NEC article navigation, one session for calculations, and one session for mixed practice. Timed practice is useful because it helps candidates build the pace needed to complete a 100-question exam in four hours.
