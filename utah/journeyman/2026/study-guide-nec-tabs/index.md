## Exam Details

Utah journeyman electrician candidates are required to pass the Utah Journeyman Electrician Written Exam and the Utah Electrician Practical Exam. The written exam covers electrical knowledge related to code, theory, and calculations. The practical exam is hands-on and tests technical electrical knowledge and ability. Utah’s exam information states that preapproval for testing is not required, but candidates must meet the applicable eligibility requirements for the license classification.

For journeyman electrician testing eligibility, Utah lists two routes. One route requires completion of a 4-year electrical apprenticeship education program with at least 576 classroom hours, along with 6,000 hours of electrical experience as a licensed apprentice electrician. Another route requires 16,000 hours of electrical experience as a licensed apprentice electrician. For licensure as a Utah applicant, Utah lists completion of a 4-year, 576-classroom-hour electrical apprenticeship education program, 8,000 hours of licensed electrical experience under the supervision of a licensed journeyman or master electrician, and passing the Utah Journeyman Electrician Written Exam and Utah Electrician Practical Exam.

Utah announced important electrician exam changes that began August 1, 2025. Under the updated structure, journeyman and residential electricians take a single combined exam covering both Theory and Code. Utah also announced that, effective January 19, 2026, first-time candidates registering to take licensing exams with Prov must submit the Verification of Electrician Experience form directly to Prov by attaching it electronically to their exam registration. Out-of-state applicants who attended a trade program must also attach official transcripts to their registration, and applicants who graduated from an approved Utah trade program may have their application placed in pending status until the school authorizes them to proceed.

Because Utah’s electrical examination process includes both written and practical testing, students should prepare in more than one way. Written exam preparation should focus on code lookup, theory, calculations, and trade knowledge. Practical preparation should focus on hands-on electrical skills, safe work habits, technical accuracy, and familiarity with the type of tasks required for the practical modules.

## State Requirements

Utah issues several electrician license classifications, including Apprentice, Journeyman, Master, Residential Journeyman, and Residential Master. The Utah Division of Professional Licensing administers the licensing process and publishes exam and application requirements for each classification.

For Utah journeyman electrician applicants, the state lists the following requirements: completion of a 4-year electrical apprenticeship education program with 576 classroom hours, 8,000 hours of licensed electrical experience working under the supervision of a licensed journeyman or master electrician, and passing the Utah Journeyman Electrician Written Exam and Utah Electrician Practical Exam. Utah also lists an alternative route requiring 16,000 hours of licensed electrical experience working under the supervision of a licensed journeyman or master electrician, along with passing the written and practical exams.

Utah’s exam information also lists testing eligibility requirements for the journeyman written and practical exams. Those requirements include completion of a 4-year electrical apprenticeship education program or competency exam equivalence and 6,000 hours of electrical experience as a licensed apprentice electrician, or 16,000 hours of electrical experience as a licensed apprentice electrician.

Students should pay close attention to the difference between testing eligibility and final license application requirements. Utah’s public materials describe both exam eligibility and license application requirements, and candidates should make sure their education, experience, exam registration, and application documents match the path they are using.

Utah’s 2025 and 2026 exam updates also make documentation important. First-time candidates registering for licensing exams with Prov must attach the Verification of Electrician Experience form to the exam registration beginning January 19, 2026. Candidates with trade school backgrounds may also need school authorization or official transcripts depending on their situation.
