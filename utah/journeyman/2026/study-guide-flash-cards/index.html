Exam Details

Utah electrician licensing is handled through the Utah Division of Professional Licensing, commonly referred to as DOPL. Utah has statewide electrical licensing classifications, including Apprentice Electrician, Journeyman Electrician, Master Electrician, Residential Journeyman Electrician, and Residential Master Electrician. This product is focused on the Utah Journeyman Electrician license.

For the Utah Journeyman Electrician classification, the examination requirements include the Utah Journeyman Electrician Written Exam and the Utah Electrician Practical Exam. The practical exam for the journeyman classification includes six modules. Utah’s electrician licensing exams were updated beginning August 1, 2025, so journeyman and residential electrician candidates take a single combined written exam covering both Theory and Code.

The written exam is designed to measure minimum competency in electrical knowledge, including the National Electrical Code, electrical theory, and calculations. Students should prepare for code application, trade terminology, electrical formulas, safety concepts, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, conductor rules, raceway requirements, boxes, services, feeders, branch circuits, motors, controllers, lighting, equipment, special occupancies, special equipment, communication systems, and practical field scenarios.

Utah testing is handled through Prov for electrician licensing examinations. Effective January 19, 2026, first-time candidates registering to take licensing exams with Prov must attach the Verification of Electrician Experience form directly to their exam registration. Out-of-state applicants who attended a trade program must also attach official transcripts to their registration. Applicants who graduated from an approved Utah trade program may have their application placed in pending status until the school authorizes them to proceed.

Open Book Test

The Utah Journeyman Electrician written exam is an open-book examination. Candidates should prepare to use approved reference materials efficiently while still building strong knowledge of electrical theory, calculations, NEC structure, and journeyman-level trade concepts before test day.

Open book does not mean easy. Candidates still need to understand the material, recognize how questions are written, locate information quickly, apply code rules correctly, and manage exam time. A student who depends only on searching through the code book for every answer may lose valuable time and miss questions that require conceptual understanding or calculation skill.

Open-book electrical exams reward reference familiarity. Students should practice using the National Electrical Code table of contents, index, article layout, definitions, tables, exceptions, notes, and common code sections before exam day. Students should also practice calculations repeatedly so conductor sizing, load relationships, grounding and bonding rules, box fill, raceway fill, motor requirements, voltage drop concepts, and overcurrent protection rules become easier to recognize.

Reference rules are controlled by the current Utah candidate information bulletin and testing instructions. Candidates should follow the current rules for approved books, book condition, highlighting, tabs, notes, loose papers, calculators, electronic items, and exam-room conduct. Study guides, practice tests, flash cards, removable notes, and unauthorized materials should be used before test day and should not be treated as exam-room references.

A strong open-book strategy includes learning where major NEC topics are located before the exam. Students should practice identifying the correct article, checking definitions first, reading table notes carefully, reviewing exceptions, and confirming whether the question asks for a minimum, maximum, permitted condition, prohibited condition, or calculation result. Flash cards help students recall key concepts faster, while practice exams help build the habit of reading questions carefully and choosing the correct reference path.

Licensing Steps

Utah journeyman electrician licensing begins with apprentice licensing, approved education, supervised electrical experience, and exam preparation. A candidate following the standard Utah applicant pathway must complete a four-year electrical apprenticeship education program with at least 576 classroom hours, complete licensed electrical experience under the supervision of a licensed journeyman or master electrician, and pass the Utah Journeyman Electrician Written Exam and Utah Electrician Practical Exam.

Utah’s license application requirements identify completion of a four-year, 576-classroom-hour electrical apprenticeship education program, 8,000 hours of licensed electrical experience working under the supervision of a licensed journeyman or master electrician, and passing both required exams. Utah also provides an alternate pathway for candidates with 16,000 hours of licensed electrical experience under the supervision of a licensed journeyman or master electrician, along with passing the required exams.

Utah’s exam information also identifies testing eligibility requirements. For the journeyman electrician classification, candidates may qualify for testing with completion of a four-year electrical apprenticeship education program or competency exam equivalence and 6,000 hours of electrical experience as a licensed apprentice electrician, or with 16,000 hours of electrical experience as a licensed apprentice electrician. Candidates should track both testing eligibility and full license requirements so the exam process and licensure process stay aligned.

A typical Utah journeyman path includes registering as an apprentice electrician, completing approved electrical education, gaining supervised licensed electrical experience, documenting work history, preparing for the written and practical exams, registering for the required exams, attaching the Verification of Electrician Experience form when required, passing the Utah Journeyman Electrician Written Exam, passing the Utah Electrician Practical Exam, and completing the remaining DOPL licensing steps.
