Exam Details

North Carolina electrical exams are tied to the electrical contracting licensing process administered by the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. The Board’s licensing structure includes 10 electrical contracting classifications. The main license classifications include Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited, with additional Special Restricted classifications for limited phases of electrical contracting work.

The Limited license allows the licensee to engage in a single electrical contracting project within the value and voltage limits set by the Board. The Intermediate license allows the licensee to engage in a single electrical contracting project up to the Board’s stated project value limit. The Unlimited license allows the licensee to engage in electrical contracting projects regardless of value. Special Restricted classifications cover specific limited areas of electrical work, such as residential dwelling, fire alarm and low-voltage, electric sign, elevator, groundwater pump, swimming pool, and certain other restricted scopes.

North Carolina qualifying examinations are designed around the license classification being pursued. The exam scope includes technical and practical knowledge related to safe and proper electrical installation, along with permit and inspection requirements, business practices, and North Carolina laws and rules applicable to electrical contracting.

The Board requires applicants to apply for examination approval before scheduling an exam. After approval, candidates receive an eligibility notice and then schedule through the testing process provided for the Board’s exams. Current Board information states that exams are computer based and that candidates receive their examination score the same day.

The Board’s rules state that a minimum grade of 70 out of 100 is required to pass qualifying examinations administered by the Board. Candidates should review the official exam information for their selected classification before testing, because the approved references, exam authorization period, application requirements, and classification rules are part of the licensing process.

Open Book Test

North Carolina electrical contractor qualifying exams are open book according to the Board’s examination information. Candidates are allowed to refer to approved code and reference materials during the exam. Open book testing makes code navigation one of the most important study skills for electrical candidates.

An open book test still requires serious preparation. Candidates must know how to read the question, identify the subject, locate the correct article or table, interpret the code language, and apply the rule correctly. The ability to search the NEC quickly can make a major difference during timed testing. Licensing Steps

The North Carolina electrical licensing process begins with selecting the correct license classification. Candidates should review the scope of each classification and choose the exam that matches the type of electrical contracting work they intend to perform. The main classifications include Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited, along with Special Restricted classifications for narrower areas of electrical contracting.

After identifying the correct classification, applicants must complete the examination application process required by the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. The Board instructs applicants to read the Exam Information Handbook, choose the license classification, complete the application packet, submit the required fee and documents, and wait for the notice of approval or returned application instructions.

Once the application is approved, the applicant receives an exam approval notice. The Board’s exam information explains that the approval letter lists the exam classification and the exam authorization period. Candidates then use the testing provider’s scheduling process after receiving the required eligibility information.

After taking the exam, candidates receive their score. A passing score is one part of the licensing process. Candidates may still need to complete additional licensing steps, submit required documentation, satisfy name or business requirements, and follow the Board’s rules before a license is issued or activated.

Study preparation should be handled alongside the application process. This approach helps candidates build both conceptual understanding and reference-navigation speed.

State Requirements

North Carolina electrical contractor licensing is administered by the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. The Board’s licensing structure is based on classifications, and each classification has its own scope or limitation of work. Candidates should understand the difference between general journeyman-level study terminology and the licensing classifications used by the state Board.

The Board lists Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited license classifications, along with seven Special Restricted classifications. The Limited license is restricted by project value and voltage. The Intermediate license is restricted by project value. The Unlimited license allows electrical contracting projects regardless of value. Special Restricted classifications are limited to specific types of electrical contracting work.

North Carolina rules also identify types of experience that may be considered for exam qualification. Electrical experience may be categorized by the Board as primary, secondary, or other equivalent experience depending on the nature of the work or training. Examples listed in Board rules include experience as a journeyman electrician or electrician mechanic, electrical foreman, electrical superintendent, estimator for a licensed electrical contractor, recognized electrical inspector, instructor of NEC-related electrical courses, apprentice electrician training, helper experience, and other electrical-related categories.

Candidates should make sure their experience documentation aligns with the classification they are pursuing. The Board reviews application information and determines whether the applicant meets the requirements for examination. Submitting a complete application with accurate experience information is an important part of the process.
