## Exam Details

The North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors develops the electrical contracting licensing examinations and contracts with PSI to administer and score the exams. The regular electrical contracting license classifications include Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited. These examinations are for electrical contracting licensure and qualified individual certification, not a separate statewide journeyman electrician license.

The Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited electrical contracting examinations each consist of 100 multiple-choice questions. Candidates have a maximum of 6 hours to complete the examination. The examinations are designed to measure technical and practical knowledge related to the safe and proper installation of electrical work and equipment.

North Carolina electrical contractor examinations include content related to the National Electrical Code, electrical plans and specifications, estimating electrical installations, fundamentals of electrical work and equipment, permit and inspection requirements, business practices, North Carolina statutes, and Board rules applicable to electrical contracting.

The North Carolina exam process requires Board approval before scheduling. Candidates submit the examination application and required documents to the Board. After approval, the candidate receives a Notice of Approval with the information needed to register with PSI. The approval period is limited, and each approval includes one examination attempt. Candidates who fail must reapply before retesting and must follow the Board’s retest waiting rules.

For journeyman-level candidates, the exam preparation topics remain highly relevant even when the final license path is contractor-based. Candidates should review general electrical knowledge, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, conductors, cables, raceways, boxes, overcurrent protection, equipment for general use, motors, transformers, safety, plan reading, estimating concepts, and electrical calculations.

## Open Book Test

The North Carolina electrical contracting examinations are open book for the approved references listed in the examination handbook. Candidates are required to bring their own approved NEC reference for the examination. Candidates taking Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, SP-SFD, or Fire Alarm/Low Voltage classifications are also required to bring the listed National Fire Alarm Code reference.

Only the references listed in the examination handbook are allowed in the examination center. Reference materials must be softbound, spiral bound, or hardbound. Loose-leaf references are not allowed. Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and annotated before the examination session, but candidates may not write in the references during the exam.

References may be tabbed with permanent tabs only. Temporary tabs, such as Post-it notes or stapled tabs, are not allowed and must be removed before the examination begins. Divider tabs are also not allowed. Candidates may not bring additional papers, loose materials, or attached extra materials inside approved references.

Although the examination allows approved references, not every topic is open book. Questions concerning the Electrical Contractors Licensing Law, the Board’s Rules, and the NASCLA Contractors’ Guide to Business, Law, and Project Management: North Carolina Electrical Edition are closed-book questions. Candidates should study those areas before the exam because they may not refer to those documents during the examination.

Open book does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates still need to know how to read the question, identify the subject, locate the correct reference, interpret the code language, and answer within the time limit. A candidate who is familiar with the NEC can spend less time searching and more time solving the question. The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs included in this Super Combo is intended for study and code navigation practice.

## Licensing Steps

North Carolina candidates should begin by identifying the license classification that matches their goal. The Board issues electrical contracting licenses in Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and Special Restricted classifications. The appropriate classification depends on the type and scope of electrical contracting work the candidate or business intends to perform.

A practical path begins with reviewing the Board’s examination handbook and licensing requirements, selecting the correct classification, completing the examination application, submitting the required documents, receiving Board approval, scheduling the examination with PSI, preparing with the approved references and study topics, passing the examination, and then completing the license application process.

After passing the examination, the candidate must apply to obtain and activate the electrical contracting license. The licensing process includes completing the license application for the desired classification, submitting the license application with the required license fee, and meeting the Board’s requirements for that classification.

North Carolina’s structure includes qualified individuals and licensees. A qualified individual is someone who has passed the qualifying examination for a classification and has been certified by the Board. A listed qualified individual has the duty and authority to supervise and direct electrical contracting work done by or in the name of the licensee on whose license the individual is listed.

Passing the examination is an important milestone, but it does not automatically activate a license by itself. The license is issued through the Board after the applicant completes the required license application process and meets the applicable requirements.

North Carolina license classifications include Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and several Special Restricted classifications. The Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited classifications are the main electrical contracting classifications and are supported by the Board’s examination process.
