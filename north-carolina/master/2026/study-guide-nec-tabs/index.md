The Board’s examination process applies to candidates seeking North Carolina electrical contracting licenses in classifications such as Limited, Intermediate, or Unlimited.

North Carolina examinations are computer based. Candidates receive their examination score the same day. After approval, candidates schedule the exam through the testing process identified by the Board. The Board’s exam information states that candidates bring their books to the exam center and that the exam is open book. Calculators and other testing necessities are provided at the site.

## Licensing Steps

The North Carolina electrical contractor licensing path begins with determining the correct license classification. Candidates should decide whether the Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, or another approved classification fits the type of electrical contracting work they intend to perform. The classification matters because each category controls the scope and limitations of work that may be contracted.

The next step is applying for examination approval through the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. Candidates complete the required examination application and submit the required fee and documentation to the Board. The Board reviews the application and determines whether the applicant is approved to test.

After approval, the candidate receives examination scheduling information. The approval includes a specific authorization period, and candidates must schedule and take the exam within that period. If the candidate does not pass, misses the authorization window, or needs another attempt, a new application and fee may be required according to the Board’s rules.

Once the candidate passes the required examination, the candidate applies to obtain and activate the electrical contracting license. The licensing process includes submitting the new license application for the desired classification, paying the applicable license fee, and providing any required documentation. Passing the exam alone does not automatically activate a license; the license application process must be completed with the Board.

After licensure, electrical contractors must comply with renewal and continuing education requirements. North Carolina licensees should keep their license active, follow Board rules, meet continuing education requirements, and maintain compliance with electrical contracting statutes, permit rules, inspection procedures, and business requirements.
