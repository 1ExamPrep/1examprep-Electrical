Exam Details

North Carolina electrical contracting examinations are administered through PSI for the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. The Board develops the licensing examinations and determines passing standards, while PSI administers and scores the exams. Candidates apply through the Board and must receive approval before scheduling the examination through PSI.

The North Carolina Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited electrical contracting examinations each consist of 100 multiple-choice questions. Candidates are allowed a maximum of 6 hours to complete the examination. These classifications are the primary electrical contracting license classifications in North Carolina, with the Unlimited license allowing the licensee to engage in electrical contracting projects without a project-value limitation.

The examination may include questions based on the National Electrical Code as incorporated into the North Carolina State Building Code, analysis of electrical plans and specifications, estimating of electrical installations, and fundamentals of electrical work and equipment. In addition to NEC-based content, candidates should be prepared for closed-book questions related to North Carolina electrical contractors licensing law, the Board’s rules, and business, law, and project management topics used in the North Carolina electrical contracting licensing process.

Open Book Test

The North Carolina electrical contracting examinations are identified as open book examinations. Open book testing does not make the exam easy. It means candidates must know how to use approved references efficiently, read code language carefully, understand table notes and exceptions, and apply requirements under time pressure.

The North Carolina examination handbook requires candidates to bring their own approved references to the examination center. The handbook lists the National Electrical Code as a required reference and also lists the National Fire Alarm Code for candidates taking the Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, SP-SFD, or SP-FA/LV classifications. The handbook also states that the NEC and applicable fire alarm code references are the only references allowed in the examination center.

North Carolina reference rules allow references to be softbound, spiral, or hardbound. Loose-leaf references are not allowed. Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and annotated before the exam session, but references may not be written in during the examination. Additional loose or attached papers are not permitted. References may be tabbed with permanent tabs only. Temporary tabs, stapled tabs, and divider tabs are not allowed.

Licensing Steps

North Carolina electrical contractor licensing is handled through the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. A practical licensing path begins by identifying the correct classification for the work being performed. The main license classifications include Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited, along with special restricted classifications for specific types of electrical contracting work.

Candidates begin by selecting the desired classification and submitting the examination application to the Board with the required information. If approved, the Board processes the application and issues a Notice of Approval. PSI then sends the candidate an eligibility email with information needed to schedule the examination. Candidates may schedule only the examination for which they have been approved and must follow the authorization period listed by the Board.

After passing the qualifying examination, the candidate must complete the new license application process to obtain and activate the license. North Carolina distinguishes between a qualified individual and a licensee. A qualified individual is the person who has passed the qualifying examination in a classification, while the licensee may be an individual, partnership, firm, or corporation that has a listed qualified individual responsible for supervising and directing electrical contracting work.

Candidates who do not pass must follow the Board’s retesting rules. The published handbook states that a candidate who fails an exam is not eligible to take another examination in the same classification until three months after the failed examination date. A new approval and authorization are required before another attempt.

The study portion of the licensing path should be treated as a structured project. Candidates should review the exam handbook, study the 2026 NEC, practice calculations, work through code navigation, review licensing law and Board rules, and use flash cards for repeated reinforcement. Consistent preparation helps candidates approach North Carolina electrical contractor and master-level electrical study with more organization and confidence.

State Requirements

North Carolina regulates electrical contracting through the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. The Board’s purpose is to protect life, health, and property through examination of applicants, education of licensees, and discipline of individuals in order to promote quality electrical contracting under North Carolina law.

North Carolina law requires a license for persons, partnerships, firms, or corporations that engage or offer to engage in electrical contracting within the state. Electrical contracting includes installing, maintaining, altering, or repairing electric work, wiring, devices, appliances, or equipment. Each separate place of business operated by an electrical contractor must have at least one listed qualified individual on active duty with the authority to supervise and direct electrical wiring or installation work performed by that place of business.

The Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited classifications are central to North Carolina electrical contracting licensing. The Unlimited classification permits electrical contracting work without project-value limitation. Special restricted classifications apply to limited phases of electrical contracting work, such as fire alarm and low voltage, single-family detached residential dwelling, electric sign, elevator, groundwater pump, swimming pool bonding, and other restricted scopes listed by the Board.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong North Carolina electrical exam study plan should include NEC study, calculation practice, trade knowledge review, plan-reading practice, estimating review, licensing law review, Board rules review, reference navigation, and timed question work. Electrical exams can cover a wide range of subjects, so candidates should avoid spending all study time on only one topic. A balanced routine helps build confidence across technical, regulatory, and practical contractor subjects.

For NEC study, start by learning the structure of the code book. Review article organization, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and cross-references. Practice moving from a question to the correct article and reading the surrounding language carefully. Many electrical exam questions are missed because a candidate finds a related section but overlooks a condition, exception, table note, or specific wording.

For North Carolina law and rules, study the licensing law and Board rules as part of the exam routine. These items may appear as closed-book questions, so candidates should not depend only on reference lookup. A candidate should understand the role of the Board, license classifications, qualified individuals, licensees, supervision responsibilities, application steps, and the rules connected with electrical contracting in North Carolina.

For calculations, write out each step. Electrical calculation mistakes often happen when a candidate skips a conversion, uses the wrong table, applies the wrong value, or confuses branch circuit, feeder, service, motor, transformer, raceway fill, or box fill requirements. The 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide gives candidates a dedicated way to practice these skills repeatedly.

For open book preparation, use the NEC actively during study. Practice locating sections, reading tables, and confirming answers in the code book. Then practice some questions without immediately opening the code book so you can build recognition. The stronger your recall becomes, the more time you can save during questions that require deeper code lookup.

For flash card study, use short sessions throughout the week. Flash cards can help reinforce formulas, definitions, code concepts, and common electrical terms. They are useful before work, during breaks, after a longer study block, or as final review. Flash cards should not replace full study sessions, but they help strengthen recall and recognition.
