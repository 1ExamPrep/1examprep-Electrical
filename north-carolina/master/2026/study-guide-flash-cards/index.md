## Exam Details

The North Carolina electrical contractor examinations are administered through PSI for the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. Candidates must apply to the Board, submit the required examination application materials, and receive a Notice of Approval before scheduling the examination through PSI.

The Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited electrical contracting examinations each contain **100 multiple-choice questions** and allow a maximum of **6 hours** for completion. These classifications are the primary electrical contractor license classifications used by North Carolina for general electrical contracting work.

The examinations test technical and practical knowledge needed for safe and proper electrical work. Exam content includes appropriate provisions of the National Electrical Code as incorporated in the North Carolina State Building Code, analysis of electrical plans and specifications, estimating electrical installations, fundamentals of electrical work and equipment installation, permit and inspection requirements, business practices, and North Carolina statutes and rules applicable to electrical contracting.

North Carolina’s Special Restricted electrical classifications use different exam lengths and scopes. Most Special Restricted examinations contain 40 multiple-choice questions and allow 3 hours. The Fire Alarm/Low Voltage examination contains 50 multiple-choice questions and allows 3 hours. The Swimming Pool Bonding examination contains 25 questions and allows 1 hour. Candidates should prepare for the classification that matches the work they plan to perform.

The Unlimited license allows the licensee to engage in electrical contracting projects without a project-value limitation. ## Open Book Test

The North Carolina electrical contractor examinations are **open book tests**. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved reference materials to the examination center. The approved exam-room references include the National Electrical Code and, for applicable classifications, the National Fire Alarm Code.

Reference materials must be bound. Loose-leaf references are not allowed. Approved reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and annotated before the examination session. Notes on the pages of the reference materials are allowed when prepared according to exam rules. Candidates may not write in references during the examination session.

Permanent tabs are allowed. Temporary tabs such as Post-it notes or stapled tabs are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins. Divider tabs are not allowed. Candidates may not bring additional papers, whether loose or attached, inside approved references. Calculators, scratch paper, and pencils are provided at the test center.

Open-book testing still requires serious preparation. The exam is not simply about bringing a code book into the room. Candidates need to know how the NEC is organized, how to identify the subject being tested, how to locate the correct Article or table quickly, and how to apply the rule accurately within the time allowed.

## State Requirements

North Carolina electrical contractor licensing is regulated by the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. The Board examines applicants, certifies qualified individuals, issues electrical contracting licenses, administers rules, and disciplines licensees when required to protect the life, health, and property of the public.

North Carolina defines a qualified individual as a person who is qualified in a specific license classification as a result of passing the qualifying examination required for that classification and being certified by the Board. A listed qualified individual is a qualified individual whose name is listed on a license issued by the Board and who has the duty and authority to supervise and direct electrical contracting performed under that license.

Electrical contracting in North Carolina includes engaging or offering to engage in the business of installing, maintaining, altering, or repairing electrical work, wiring, devices, appliances, or equipment. A person, partnership, firm, or corporation may not engage or offer to engage in electrical contracting in North Carolina without receiving a license in the applicable classification from the Board.

The Unlimited license allows the licensee to engage in any electrical contracting project regardless of value. The Intermediate and Limited classifications have more restricted scopes and project-value limitations. North Carolina also offers Special Restricted classifications for limited phases of electrical contracting work.

After passing the qualifying examination, an applicant must complete the licensing process to obtain and activate the license. License applicants must submit the required application, license fee, and information for at least one person to serve as the listed qualified individual for the license. Licensees must also follow Board rules for supervision, license status, renewal, continuing education, and lawful electrical contracting practice.
