## Licensing Steps

Oklahoma journeyman electrician licensing begins with apprentice training. Before becoming a journeyman, a candidate must train as an apprentice and gain qualifying electrical construction trade experience under the direct supervision of a licensed journeyman or licensed contractor. Apprentice registration and proper documentation are important parts of the path toward journeyman eligibility.

For the Unlimited Electrical Journeyman category, Oklahoma requires 8,000 verifiable hours of on-the-job experience in the electrical construction trade as a registered apprentice under the direct supervision of a licensed journeyman or licensed contractor. Of those hours, 4,000 must be in commercial or industrial work. Oklahoma allows 2,000 hours to be satisfied by formal electrical education when the education meets the state’s requirements and is verified through classroom-hour documentation.

For the Residential Electrical Journeyman category, Oklahoma requires 4,000 verifiable hours of on-the-job experience in the electrical construction trade as a registered apprentice under the direct supervision of a licensed journeyman or licensed contractor. Oklahoma allows 1,000 hours to be satisfied by formal electrical education when properly documented. Educational experience must be actual classroom hours verified with a transcript.

A typical Oklahoma journeyman path includes registering and working as an apprentice, gaining the required supervised experience, completing approved education when applicable, documenting commercial, industrial, residential, or other qualifying work experience, submitting the journeyman application, receiving approval for examination, scheduling the PSI examination, passing the exam, and completing the remaining licensing steps through the Construction Industries Board.

Oklahoma also includes special categories and requirements that may affect certain candidates. A refinery electrical journeyman category is listed for individuals who are current and in good standing. Alarm endorsement requirements may apply before a person begins work that requires an alarm endorsement under Oklahoma electrical industry regulations. Candidates should select the proper category and follow the current application instructions for their intended work scope.
