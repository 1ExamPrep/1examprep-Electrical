Exam Details

The Oklahoma electrical licensing examinations are administered through PSI after the candidate is approved by the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board. Eligibility for examination is determined by the Board. Once approved, the candidate is responsible for scheduling the examination through PSI.

The Oklahoma Journeyman Electrician examination is a computer-based licensing exam for candidates pursuing journeyman-level electrical licensure. Current exam information commonly identifies the Oklahoma Journeyman Electrician exam as an 80-question examination with a 3.5-hour time allowance and a 70% passing score. Candidates should always follow the current PSI candidate bulletin and Construction Industries Board instructions for the exact exam code, reference list, code edition, and scheduling requirements for the exam they are approved to take.

The Oklahoma electrical exam program includes multiple electrical license classifications, including journeyman and contractor-level categories. Candidates should make sure they are applying for the correct classification. The journeyman electrical path is different from an electrical contractor path, and some classifications may have different examination requirements, reference lists, or business and law requirements.

Open Book Test

The Oklahoma Journeyman Electrician examination is open book under the reference rules established by PSI and the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board. Candidates should follow the current PSI candidate bulletin for the approved reference list, code edition, tab rules, calculator rules, and prohibited materials.

Open-book electrical exams still require serious preparation. Having a code book available does not guarantee that the answer will be easy to find. Candidates need to recognize the topic being tested, locate the right article or table, read the exact language, apply the rule to the question, and manage time. Slow reference navigation can make an open-book exam difficult, especially when calculation questions are included.

Reference rules may address which books are allowed, whether books must be bound, whether tabs must be permanent, whether notes are allowed, whether highlighting is permitted, and what materials are prohibited. Candidates should prepare their references according to the official exam rules before test day. Loose papers, unauthorized study guides, handwritten inserts, removable notes, or unapproved materials may not be permitted.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs included in this Super Combo is intended for study and code navigation practice. Tabs help candidates become familiar with major NEC articles, definitions, tables, and commonly used sections. Licensing Steps

Oklahoma candidates should begin by confirming the correct electrical license classification. The Oklahoma Construction Industries Board provides the path to licensing for electrical apprentices, journeyman electricians, contractors, and related license categories. Candidates preparing for the journeyman license should follow the journeyman application and examination process.

A practical licensing path begins with registering and working in the electrical trade under the applicable supervision, obtaining the required number of hours in the type of experience required for the category sought, completing the required application process, receiving approval to test from the Construction Industries Board, scheduling the examination through PSI, passing the required examination, and completing the licensing process with the Board.

Oklahoma recognizes different electrical work categories, and candidates should make sure their documented experience matches the license category they are pursuing. Experience may involve residential, commercial, or industrial electrical work depending on the classification. Candidates should maintain accurate records of employment, supervision, dates, hours, and the type of electrical work performed.

After the Board approves a candidate for examination, the candidate schedules the test with PSI. Candidates should review the PSI candidate bulletin, arrive with required identification, bring only approved references and materials, and follow all testing center rules. After passing the exam, the candidate completes the licensing process through the Construction Industries Board.

Passing the examination is an important milestone, but the license is issued through the Board process. The Board determines whether the applicant has met the requirements, submitted the proper documentation, passed the required examination, and satisfied the licensing rules for the requested category.

Electrical calculations should be practiced consistently before the exam. Candidates should review Ohm’s Law, power formulas, amperage, voltage, resistance, watts, conductor sizing, branch circuit calculations, feeder concepts, service load concepts, box fill, conduit fill, motor calculations, transformer concepts, overcurrent protection, and voltage drop. Calculation questions can take more time than direct code questions, so repeated practice helps improve both speed and accuracy.

Code navigation should also be part of every study plan. Candidates should practice using the NEC table of contents, index, article numbers, definitions, tables, notes, and exceptions. The goal is to identify the topic quickly and move to the correct section without wasting time. A candidate who understands how the NEC is organized is better prepared for open-book electrical testing.

Services, feeders, and branch circuits should receive careful attention. Candidates should review service conductors, service disconnects, grounding at services, feeder sizing, branch circuit ratings, required outlets, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, and load calculations. These topics connect code knowledge with practical installation skill.

Grounding and bonding are important areas for journeyman exam preparation. Candidates should review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, raceway bonding, panel bonding, grounded conductors, separately derived systems, and related safety requirements. Grounding and bonding questions often require careful reading because similar terms can lead to different code requirements.

Wiring methods and materials should be studied consistently. Candidates should review conductors, cables, raceways, boxes, fittings, supports, conductor ampacity, insulation types, flexible wiring methods, underground wiring, and installation conditions. These topics appear frequently in electrical work and are central to NEC-based study.

Motors, transformers, controls, and equipment should also be part of the study routine. Candidates should review motor branch circuits, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, disconnecting means, controllers, transformer sizing, transformer protection, luminaires, receptacles, switches, appliances, panelboards, and related installation requirements.

Safety should be reviewed as part of trade preparation. Candidates should understand electrical hazards, safe work practices, grounding and bonding safety, equipment protection, overcurrent protection, working clearances, personal protection concepts, and job-site awareness. Safety questions may connect directly to NEC requirements and practical field judgment.

Flash cards are useful for shorter study sessions. Candidates can use them to review formulas, definitions, electrical theory, safety concepts, NEC topics, and trade terms before work, after work, during breaks, or between longer study blocks. Flash cards work best when paired with deeper reading, calculation drills, and code lookup practice.

A strong study plan should include scheduled reading, NEC navigation, calculation drills, timed practice, and repeated review of weaker topics. Candidates should avoid studying only familiar material. Journeyman electrician preparation rewards steady practice, careful reading, and the ability to apply electrical knowledge under exam conditions.
