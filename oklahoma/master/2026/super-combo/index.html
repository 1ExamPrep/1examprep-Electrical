Exam Details

Oklahoma electrical examinations are administered through PSI for the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board. Eligibility for examination is determined by the State of Oklahoma Construction Industries Board. Once a candidate is approved by the state, the candidate is responsible for scheduling the examination through PSI.

The Oklahoma Unlimited Electrical Contractor examination is listed with 100 scored items, 240 minutes of testing time, and a 70 percent passing score. The examination tests a candidate’s knowledge of the design, plan, layout, installation, repair, and alteration of electrical conductors, fixtures, appliances, apparatus, raceways, conduit, and related equipment and fixtures that use electrical energy for light, heat, power, data, and communications.

The Unlimited Electrical Contractor content outline includes General Knowledge, General Electrical Knowledge, Electrical Installation Requirements, Services, Feeders, and Branch Circuits, Overcurrent Protection, Grounding and Bonding, Conductors and Cables, Raceways and Boxes, Special Occupancies and Equipment, Low Voltage, Alarms, Signaling Systems, and Communications, Lighting and Signs, Safety, and Motors and Transformers. Oklahoma also lists Limited Electrical Contractor and Residential Electrical Contractor exams. The Limited Electrical Contractor exam is listed with 100 scored items, 240 minutes, and a 70 percent passing score. Open Book Test

The Oklahoma Unlimited Electrical Contractor examination is identified as an open book examination. Open book testing does not make the exam easy. It means candidates must know how to use approved references efficiently, read code language carefully, understand table notes and exceptions, and apply requirements under time pressure.

The Oklahoma PSI bulletin lists the National Electrical Code as an allowed reference for the Unlimited Electrical Contractor examination. The bulletin currently lists NFPA 70 National Electrical Code, 2023 Edition, as revised and adopted by the Oklahoma Uniform Building Code Commission. NEC Handbooks and spiral-bound copies of the National Electrical Code are not allowed in the test center under the current bulletin. The bulletin also lists OSHA construction regulations and Ugly’s Electrical References as allowed materials for the exam.

Reference rules are strict. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own references to the examination center. Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination session. References may not be written in before or during the examination. Additional papers are not permitted with approved references. References may be tabbed or indexed with permanent tabs only, and temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not allowed.

Licensing Steps

A practical Oklahoma licensing path begins by confirming the correct license category. Oklahoma electrical contractor categories include Unlimited Electrical Contractor, Residential Electrical Contractor, and Limited Electrical Contractor. Candidates should match the license category to the type of work, business role, and electrical contracting scope they intend to perform.

Oklahoma electrical contractor licensing is handled through the Construction Industries Board. A candidate generally gains verifiable experience by working as an apprentice, then as a journeyman, and then meeting the requirements to become a contractor. The Construction Industries Board identifies electrical contracting as the highest ranking electrical license category, and all electrical contractors must be an owner, partner, or officer in the electrical firm.

For the Unlimited Electrical Contractor category, Oklahoma lists 12,000 verifiable hours of on-the-job experience in electrical construction work. The requirements include proof of 4,000 hours of experience as a licensed Unlimited Electrical Journeyman, and 6,000 hours must be in commercial industrial work obtained while employed by a licensed electrical contractor. Oklahoma also identifies that up to 2,000 hours may be satisfied by formal electrical education.

After the candidate completes the application process and the state approves the candidate for examination, the candidate schedules the required examination through PSI. Oklahoma’s PSI bulletin states that there is no expiration on eligibility. If a candidate fails the first time, the candidate must wait 30 days before retesting, and electrical exams have a 30-day waiting period for additional retests.

After passing the required examination and meeting the remaining Board requirements, the candidate completes the licensing process through the Construction Industries Board. Active electrical contractors must also meet applicable bond, insurance, workers’ compensation, and license activation requirements. Candidates should follow the current Board instructions for their specific license category.

State Requirements

Oklahoma regulates electrical licensing through the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board. The Board handles electrical licensing, examination eligibility, industry rules, forms, continuing education, and related licensing resources for electrical contractors, journeymen, apprentices, and other regulated electrical categories.

The Unlimited Electrical Contractor license is the broadest electrical contractor category listed by the Board. Unlimited Electrical Contractors are unlimited in the type of electrical contracting they can perform, subject to the state licensing rules and the contractor’s compliance responsibilities. Residential Electrical Contractors are limited to wiring buildings defined in the NEC for one- and two-family dwellings. Limited Electrical Contractors must be an owner, partner, or officer in an electrical firm and are prohibited from engaging in the work of an apprentice or journeyman electrician.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Oklahoma electrical exam study plan should include NEC study, calculation practice, trade knowledge review, safety review, Oklahoma regulation review, reference navigation, and timed question work. Electrical exams can cover a wide range of subjects, so candidates should avoid spending all study time on only one topic. A balanced routine helps build confidence across general electrical knowledge, installation requirements, services, feeders, branch circuits, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, conductors, cables, raceways, boxes, special occupancies, low-voltage systems, lighting, signs, motors, transformers, and calculations.

For NEC study, start by learning the structure of the code book. Review article organization, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and cross-references. Practice moving from a question to the correct article and reading the surrounding language carefully. Many electrical exam questions are missed because a candidate finds a related section but overlooks a condition, exception, table note, or specific wording.

For calculations, write out each step. Electrical calculation mistakes often happen when a candidate skips a conversion, uses the wrong table, applies the wrong value, or confuses branch circuit, feeder, service, motor, transformer, raceway fill, or box fill requirements. The 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide gives candidates a dedicated way to practice these skills repeatedly.

For Oklahoma regulatory preparation, review the electrical licensing and administrative subjects connected with the Construction Industries Board. Contractor-level electrical preparation is not only about trade knowledge. Candidates should understand the licensing structure, contractor responsibilities, experience path, regulatory references, and exam rules that apply to the Oklahoma electrical industry.

For open book preparation, use the NEC actively during study. Practice locating sections, reading tables, and confirming answers in the code book. Then practice some questions without immediately opening the code book so you can build recognition. The stronger your recall becomes, the more time you can save during questions that require deeper code lookup.

For flash card study, use short sessions throughout the week. Flash cards can help reinforce formulas, definitions, code concepts, and common electrical terms. They are useful before work, during breaks, after a longer study block, or as final review. Flash cards should not replace full study sessions, but they help strengthen recall and recognition.
