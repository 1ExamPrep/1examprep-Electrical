Exam Details

Oklahoma electrical examinations are administered through PSI for the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board. Candidates must be approved by the State before scheduling an examination. Once approved, the candidate schedules through PSI and takes the appropriate examination for the license category being pursued.

The Unlimited Electrical Contractor examination tests a candidate’s knowledge of the design, plan, layout, installation, repair, and alteration of electrical conductors, fixtures, appliances, apparatus, raceways, conduit, related equipment, and fixtures that use electrical energy for light, heat, power, data, and communications. The exam contains 100 scored items, allows 240 minutes, and requires 70% to pass.

The Unlimited Electrical Contractor exam outline includes general knowledge, general electrical knowledge, electrical installation requirements, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special occupancies, special equipment, motors, transformers, generators, controls, utilization equipment, communications, safety, and Oklahoma-specific requirements. Candidates should prepare for both technical NEC questions and trade-practice questions.

Oklahoma also offers Residential Electrical Contractor and Limited Electrical Contractor categories. Residential Electrical Contractors are limited to contracting for wiring buildings defined in the NEC for one- and two-family dwellings. Limited Electrical Contractors are tied to qualifying experience or engineering-related requirements and are restricted by the scope established by Oklahoma law and regulation. Candidates should study for the exact category they are approved to test for.

Open Book Test

The Oklahoma electrical examinations are open book tests. Candidates may bring approved references into the examination center according to PSI and Oklahoma Construction Industries Board rules. The exam reference rules allow approved materials to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the exam session, but references may not contain writing before or during the examination.

Oklahoma testing rules allow permanent tabs only. Temporary tabs, including Post-it notes, are not allowed and must be removed before the examination begins. Candidates may not bring additional loose papers with approved references. Downloaded references must be properly bound, hole-punched in a binder, or stapled in the left-hand corner when allowed by the testing rules.

The current Oklahoma electrical exam reference list includes the NFPA 70 National Electrical Code, 2023 Edition, as revised and adopted by the Oklahoma Uniform Building Code Commission, Code of Federal Regulations 29 CFR Part 1926 or PSI’s OSHA selections, and Ugly’s Electrical References. NEC Handbooks and spiral-bound copies of the NEC are not allowed in the test center under the current bulletin.

Licensing Steps

The Oklahoma electrical licensing path begins with identifying the correct credential. A person entering the trade typically registers as an apprentice and gains supervised experience under a licensed journeyman or licensed contractor. After meeting journeyman experience requirements, the applicant applies for the appropriate journeyman examination. After working as a journeyman and meeting additional contractor requirements, the electrician may pursue an electrical contractor license.

For journeyman licensing, Oklahoma recognizes categories such as Unlimited Electrical Journeyman and Residential Electrical Journeyman. An Unlimited Electrical Journeyman applicant must document 8,000 verifiable hours of on-the-job electrical construction experience as a registered apprentice under proper supervision, with 4,000 hours in commercial or industrial work. Formal electrical education may satisfy up to 2,000 hours when it meets the Board’s requirements.

For contractor licensing, Oklahoma requires Electrical Contractors to be an owner, partner, or officer in the electrical firm. Unlimited Electrical Contractors must document 12,000 verifiable hours of electrical construction experience, including proof of 4,000 hours as a licensed Unlimited Electrical Journeyman. The experience must also include 6,000 hours in commercial or industrial work obtained while employed by a licensed Electrical Contractor. Up to 2,000 hours may be satisfied by formal electrical education.

Residential Electrical Contractors must document 8,000 verifiable hours total, including 4,000 hours as a Residential or Unlimited Journeyman Electrician under the employment and supervision of an Electrical Contractor. The remaining experience may be obtained as a registered apprentice, with limited educational credit allowed under Board rules.

After meeting the experience requirement, the applicant submits the required application to the Construction Industries Board. Once approved, the candidate receives testing information and schedules the exam through PSI. After passing the required exam, the applicant completes the licensing process with the Board, including contractor activation requirements when applicable. Active electrical contractors must maintain bond and insurance requirements and provide workers’ compensation coverage documentation or an approved exemption when required.

State Requirements

Oklahoma electrical licensing is regulated statewide by the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board. The Board’s mission is to protect life and property through licensing and inspection of related trades for the health, safety, and welfare of the public. Electrical licensing, electrical testing, apprentice registration, contractor requirements, continuing education, and enforcement are handled under the state’s electrical laws and regulations.

Oklahoma’s electrical contractor categories include Unlimited Electrical Contractor, Residential Electrical Contractor, and Limited Electrical Contractor. The Unlimited Electrical Contractor category is the broadest electrical contracting category and is not limited by the type of electrical contracting work performed. Residential Electrical Contractors are limited to wiring buildings defined by the NEC for one- and two-family dwellings. Limited Electrical Contractors are restricted by the scope and qualifications established by Oklahoma rules.

Oklahoma also licenses journeyman electricians, including Unlimited Electrical Journeyman and Residential Electrical Journeyman categories. Journeymen work for active licensed Electrical Contractors and continue gaining experience toward higher qualification. Oklahoma’s path generally moves from apprentice registration, to journeyman licensure, to contractor qualification.

Electrical contractors must maintain active license status and meet continuing responsibilities after licensure. Oklahoma electrical journeymen and electrical contractors must complete 12 hours of continuing education every three years or 36 months preceding the expiration date of the license or registration certificate. Continuing education includes code updates and other approved electrical topics such as circuit theory, calculations, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, transformers, motors, controls, alternative energy systems, NFPA 70E safety, equipment installation, and the Electrical License Act.
