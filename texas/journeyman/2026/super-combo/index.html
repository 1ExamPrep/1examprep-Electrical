Exam Details

The Texas Journeyman Electrician examination is administered through PSI for the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. Candidates must apply through TDLR, meet the eligibility requirements, and receive authorization before scheduling the examination. PSI provides the candidate information bulletin, scheduling process, test center rules, and examination instructions.

The Texas Journeyman Electrician exam is a computer-based licensing exam. The exam has been reformatted into two portions: NEC Knowledge and Calculations. The examination content remains focused on journeyman-level electrical competency, but the calculation questions are separated from the NEC knowledge questions. Candidates should prepare for both portions because calculation skill and code knowledge are both important to exam performance.

Texas journeyman electrician exam preparation should include general electrical knowledge, definitions, services, service equipment, feeders, branch circuits, conductors, wiring methods and materials, raceways, boxes, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, equipment for general use, control devices, motors, transformers, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, safety, and electrical calculations.

Calculation preparation is especially important for the Texas exam because the exam format separates calculation questions into their own portion. Candidates should practice Ohm’s Law, power formulas, conductor sizing, voltage drop, branch circuit calculations, feeder calculations, service load concepts, box fill, conduit fill, motor calculations, transformer concepts, and overcurrent protection calculations. The included Electrician Calculations Study Guide is designed to support this part of preparation.

The Texas exam program uses the National Electrical Code as a central reference for electrical licensing exams. This Super Combo is based on the 2026 National Electrical Code for candidates who want current-code study materials, updated code navigation practice, and a complete preparation package. Candidates should follow the current PSI and TDLR instructions for the exact code edition and exam rules that apply to their scheduled examination.

Because the exam is timed, candidates should practice answering questions efficiently. Code lookup questions can take time when the candidate is unfamiliar with the NEC layout. Calculation questions can also slow candidates down if formulas are not practiced before exam day. A balanced study plan should include reading, code navigation, calculation drills, flash card review, and timed practice.

Open Book Test

The Texas Journeyman Electrician examination is open book under PSI and TDLR reference rules. Candidates should follow the current candidate information bulletin for the approved reference list, code edition, tab rules, calculator rules, markings, and prohibited materials.

Open-book electrical exams still require serious preparation. Having the NEC available does not mean every answer will be easy to find. Candidates must recognize the topic being tested, locate the correct article or table, read the rule carefully, apply the code language to the question, and manage the time allowed for the examination.

Reference rules may address whether books must be bound, whether tabs are allowed, whether tabs must be permanent, whether highlighting or underlining is permitted, whether written notes are allowed, and whether materials may be marked during the exam. Candidates should prepare reference materials according to the current PSI rules before test day and avoid bringing unauthorized loose papers, inserts, removable notes, photocopies, or unapproved study materials.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs included in this Super Combo is intended for study and code navigation practice. The strongest preparation comes from using the tabs as a navigation aid while also learning how the NEC is organized and how common exam topics connect to specific code sections.

Licensing Steps

Texas candidates should begin by confirming that the Journeyman Electrician license is the correct license classification for their work goals. Texas offers several individual electrical license types, including apprentice, residential wireman, journeyman electrician, master electrician, maintenance electrician, and related classifications. The correct license depends on the candidate’s experience, work scope, and career path.

To apply for the Texas Journeyman Electrician license, a candidate must have completed at least 7,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Master Electrician licensed in Texas. Submitting at least 7,000 hours allows a candidate to take the examination before reaching the full 8,000 hours required for licensure. A candidate must complete the full 8,000 hours before the journeyman license can be issued.

Candidates must obtain experience verification from each Texas-licensed Master Electrician who supervised the work. Experience records should be accurate, complete, and consistent with the work performed. Candidates should keep documentation of employment dates, hours, supervisor information, company information, and the type of electrical work performed.

After meeting the application requirements, the candidate applies through TDLR. Once approved, the candidate schedules the examination through PSI. Candidates should review the PSI candidate information bulletin, prepare their approved materials, bring required identification, follow test center rules, and take the examination as scheduled.

After passing the required examination and completing the full experience requirement, the candidate completes the remaining licensing process through TDLR. Passing the exam is an important milestone, but the license is issued through TDLR after the applicant satisfies the state requirements.

State Requirements

The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation regulates electricians and electrical contractors in Texas. Electrical license holders must follow TDLR requirements, state laws, administrative rules, and the scope of work allowed by their license classification.

The Texas Journeyman Electrician pathway requires supervised on-the-job training under a Texas-licensed Master Electrician. A candidate may apply to take the journeyman exam with at least 7,000 hours of qualifying training, but 8,000 hours are required before the Journeyman Electrician license can be issued. This structure allows eligible candidates to test before the final hours are completed while still requiring the full experience total for licensure.

A Texas Journeyman Electrician license is different from a Texas Master Electrician license. The master electrician path requires additional experience as a licensed journeyman before a candidate can qualify for the master-level examination. Candidates planning long-term advancement should understand how journeyman licensure fits into the progression toward master-level responsibility.

Texas also requires license holders to maintain their licenses according to TDLR renewal and continuing education rules. Active license holders should follow renewal deadlines, complete required education, and keep contact and license information current through TDLR.

Study materials help candidates prepare, but licensing decisions are made by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation based on the applicant’s qualifications, documentation, examination result, and compliance with Texas requirements.

Electrical calculations should be practiced consistently before the exam. Candidates should review Ohm’s Law, power formulas, amperage, voltage, resistance, watts, conductor sizing, branch circuit calculations, feeder concepts, service load concepts, box fill, conduit fill, motor calculations, transformer concepts, overcurrent protection, and voltage drop. Calculation questions can take more time than direct code questions, so repeated practice helps improve both speed and accuracy.

Code navigation should also be part of every study plan. Candidates should practice using the NEC table of contents, index, article numbers, definitions, tables, notes, and exceptions. The goal is to identify the topic quickly and move to the correct section without wasting time. A candidate who understands how the NEC is organized is better prepared for open-book electrical testing.

Services, feeders, and branch circuits should receive careful attention. Candidates should review service conductors, service disconnects, grounding at services, feeder sizing, branch circuit ratings, required outlets, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, and load calculations. These topics connect code knowledge with practical installation skill.

Grounding and bonding are important areas for journeyman exam preparation. Candidates should review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, raceway bonding, panel bonding, grounded conductors, separately derived systems, and related safety requirements. Grounding and bonding questions often require careful reading because similar terms can lead to different code requirements.

Wiring methods and materials should be studied consistently. Candidates should review conductors, cables, raceways, boxes, fittings, supports, conductor ampacity, insulation types, flexible wiring methods, underground wiring, cabinets, panelboards, switchboards, and installation conditions. These topics appear frequently in electrical work and are central to NEC-based study.

Motors, transformers, controls, and equipment should also be part of the study routine. Candidates should review motor branch circuits, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, disconnecting means, controllers, transformer sizing, transformer protection, luminaires, receptacles, switches, appliances, panelboards, and related installation requirements.

Flash cards are useful for shorter study sessions. Candidates can use them to review formulas, definitions, electrical theory, safety concepts, NEC topics, and trade terms before work, after work, during breaks, or between longer study blocks. Flash cards work best when paired with deeper reading, calculation drills, and code lookup practice.

A strong study plan should include scheduled reading, NEC navigation, calculation drills, timed practice, and repeated review of weaker topics. Candidates should avoid studying only familiar material. Texas journeyman electrician preparation rewards steady practice, careful reading, and the ability to apply electrical knowledge under exam conditions.
