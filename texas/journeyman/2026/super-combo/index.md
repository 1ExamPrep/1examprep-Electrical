## Exam Details

The Texas Journeyman Electrician examination is administered through PSI for the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. Candidates must apply through TDLR, meet the eligibility requirements, and receive authorization before scheduling the examination. PSI provides the candidate information bulletin, scheduling process, test center rules, and examination instructions.

The Texas Journeyman Electrician exam is a computer-based licensing exam. The exam has been reformatted into two portions: NEC Knowledge and Calculations. The examination content remains focused on journeyman-level electrical competency, but the calculation questions are separated from the NEC knowledge questions. Candidates should prepare for both portions because calculation skill and code knowledge are both important to exam performance.

## Licensing Steps

Texas candidates should begin by confirming that the Journeyman Electrician license is the correct license classification for their work goals. Texas offers several individual electrical license types, including apprentice, residential wireman, journeyman electrician, master electrician, maintenance electrician, and related classifications. The correct license depends on the candidate’s experience, work scope, and career path.

To apply for the Texas Journeyman Electrician license, a candidate must have completed at least 7,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Master Electrician licensed in Texas. Submitting at least 7,000 hours allows a candidate to take the examination before reaching the full 8,000 hours required for licensure. A candidate must complete the full 8,000 hours before the journeyman license can be issued.

Candidates must obtain experience verification from each Texas-licensed Master Electrician who supervised the work. Experience records should be accurate, complete, and consistent with the work performed. Candidates should keep documentation of employment dates, hours, supervisor information, company information, and the type of electrical work performed.

After meeting the application requirements, the candidate applies through TDLR. Once approved, the candidate schedules the examination through PSI. Candidates should review the PSI candidate information bulletin, prepare their approved materials, bring required identification, follow test center rules, and take the examination as scheduled.

After passing the required examination and completing the full experience requirement, the candidate completes the remaining licensing process through TDLR. Passing the exam is an important milestone, but the license is issued through TDLR after the applicant satisfies the state requirements.

## State Requirements

The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation regulates electricians and electrical contractors in Texas. Electrical license holders must follow TDLR requirements, state laws, administrative rules, and the scope of work allowed by their license classification.

The Texas Journeyman Electrician pathway requires supervised on-the-job training under a Texas-licensed Master Electrician. A candidate may apply to take the journeyman exam with at least 7,000 hours of qualifying training, but 8,000 hours are required before the Journeyman Electrician license can be issued. This structure allows eligible candidates to test before the final hours are completed while still requiring the full experience total for licensure.

A Texas Journeyman Electrician license is different from a Texas Master Electrician license. The master electrician path requires additional experience as a licensed journeyman before a candidate can qualify for the master-level examination. Candidates planning long-term advancement should understand how journeyman licensure fits into the progression toward master-level responsibility.

Texas also requires license holders to maintain their licenses according to TDLR renewal and continuing education rules. Active license holders should follow renewal deadlines, complete required education, and keep contact and license information current through TDLR.

Study materials help candidates prepare, but licensing decisions are made by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation based on the applicant’s qualifications, documentation, examination result, and compliance with Texas requirements.
