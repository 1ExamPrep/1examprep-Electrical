## Exam Details

The Texas Journeyman Electrician examination is administered through PSI for the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. TDLR states that, starting March 11, 2025, the Journeyman Electrician exam consists of two separate parts: a knowledge portion and a calculations portion. Both portions are multiple choice, and candidates must pass each portion with a minimum score of 70%.

The **Texas Journeyman Electrician NEC Knowledge Portion** includes 59 items, including 3 non-scored items. The time allowed for this portion is 130 minutes, and the minimum passing score is 70%. The content outline includes definitions, theory, plans, electrical services, service equipment, separately derived systems, feeders, branch circuits, conductors, wiring methods, electrical materials, equipment, devices, motors, generators, control devices, disconnecting means, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, photovoltaic systems, and electric vehicle chargers.

The **Texas Journeyman Electrician Calculations Portion** includes 26 items, including 2 non-scored items. The time allowed for this portion is 110 minutes, and the minimum passing score is 70%. The calculation outline includes electrical theory, service load calculations, electrical service equipment, grounding and bonding, transformers, feeder load calculations, feeder overcurrent protection, branch-circuit calculations, conductor calculations, raceways, air conditioning equipment, refrigeration equipment, heating equipment, appliances, motors, generators, control devices, disconnecting means, mobile homes, park trailers, marinas, and electric vehicle chargers.

The examination fee listed in the PSI bulletin is $78 for both journeyman portions. If a candidate fails one or both portions, the retake examination fee is listed as $78. Time does not roll over from one portion to the next, so candidates should practice pacing separately for NEC knowledge questions and calculation questions.

Texas exam candidates receive approval after TDLR reviews the completed license application and receives the application fee. After approval, candidates may schedule through PSI. TDLR also states that applicants have one year from the date the application was received by TDLR to satisfy all requirements to obtain the license, including passing the examination.

## Open Book Test

The Texas electrician examination is an open book test. TDLR and PSI list the National Electrical Code as the allowed reference material. Texas currently lists the **National Electrical Code, 2023 Edition** for the examination, and candidates are responsible for bringing their own NEC book to the examination site.

PSI’s rules state that any soft-bound edition of the NEC may be used for the examination, but the examination questions are based on the current TDLR-adopted bound edition. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies of the NEC are not allowed in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is also not allowed.

Texas allows the candidate’s NEC book to be highlighted, underlined, and contain notes before the examination session. NEC books may be tabbed with permanent index tabs manufactured or provided by the NEC book publisher. Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, index, or mark the NEC during the examination session. Additional paper, whether loose, taped, adhered, stapled, or attached, is not allowed in the examination room.

Because the exam is open book and timed, preparation should include repeated code lookup practice. Students should learn how to move through Article 100 definitions, service rules, feeder requirements, branch-circuit rules, conductor ampacity tables, grounding and bonding provisions, raceway fill, box fill, wiring method articles, equipment rules, special occupancies, and calculation sections. The **National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs** helps students practice these skills during study, while candidates should follow current TDLR and PSI rules for the exact reference material allowed on exam day.

## Licensing Steps

1. **Register and gain supervised electrical experience.** Texas journeyman candidates must document on-the-job training under the supervision of a Master Electrician licensed in Texas.
2. **Complete at least 7,000 hours before applying to test.** TDLR states that 7,000 hours allows an applicant to take the examination before reaching the full 8,000 hours required for licensure.
3. **Obtain experience verification.** Candidates must obtain verification from each supervising Master Electrician. The experience must be documented and signed on the required Experience Verification Form.
4. **Submit the journeyman license application.** Texas requires the initial Journeyman Electrician license application to be submitted by mail with the non-refundable application fee.
5. **Receive examination approval.** After the application is reviewed and verified, TDLR notifies the applicant that they are approved to sit for the licensing exam.
6. **Schedule with PSI.** Approved candidates schedule the exam through PSI and follow the testing instructions provided in the candidate information bulletin.
7. **Pass both exam portions.** Candidates must pass both the NEC Knowledge Portion and the Calculations Portion with a minimum score of 70% on each portion.
8. **Complete the full licensure requirement.** Texas requires the full 8,000 hours of qualifying on-the-job training before the journeyman license is issued.
9. **Renew the license annually.** Texas electrician licenses are valid for one year from the date of issuance and must be renewed annually.

## State Requirements

Texas electrician licensing is regulated by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. A Texas Journeyman Electrician is licensed to perform electrical work under the general supervision of a Master Electrician. With limited exemptions, anyone who performs electrical work in Texas must be licensed.

TDLR defines electrical work broadly, including labor or material used in installing, maintaining, or extending an electrical wiring system and related appurtenances, apparatus, or equipment used with electrical energy in, on, outside, or attached to a building, residence, structure, property, or premises. Service entrance conductors, as defined by the National Electrical Code, are also included.

To apply for a Texas Journeyman Electrician license, an applicant must have completed at least 7,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Master Electrician licensed in Texas. The 7,000-hour threshold allows the applicant to take the examination before reaching the full 8,000 hours required for licensure. The applicant must obtain signed verification from each supervising Master Electrician, and TDLR may contact individuals to verify the reported experience.

The initial application must be submitted by mail with the required non-refundable application fee. After the application has been received and verified, the applicant receives notification of approval to take the licensing examination. The examination is administered by PSI, and TDLR receives exam results electronically from the testing provider.

Texas electrician licenses are valid for one year from the date of issuance and must be renewed annually. Licensees should keep their contact information current with TDLR and follow all renewal and continuing education requirements that apply to their license classification.
