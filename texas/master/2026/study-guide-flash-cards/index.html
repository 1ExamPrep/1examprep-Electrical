Exam Details

The Texas Master Electrician examination is administered by PSI for the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. After TDLR receives and verifies the completed license application and experience documentation, the candidate receives notification of approval to take the licensing exam and may schedule testing through PSI.

The Texas Master Electrician written examination consists of two portions: the NEC Knowledge Portion and the Calculations Portion. Both portions are multiple choice. Candidates must pass each portion with a minimum score of 70%. Time from one portion does not roll over into the other portion.

The Texas Master Electrician NEC Knowledge Portion contains 75 items, including 5 non-scored items. Candidates are allowed 150 minutes, and a score of 70% correct is required to pass.

The NEC Knowledge Portion content outline includes:

  • Definitions, Theory, and Plans: 7 items
  • Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems: 11 items
  • Electrical Feeders: 4 items
  • Branch Circuit Conductors: 11 items
  • Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials: 7 items
  • Electrical Equipment and Devices: 8 items
  • Motors and Generators: 7 items
  • Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: 3 items
  • Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions: 8 items
  • Renewable Energy Technologies: 4 items

The Texas Master Electrician Calculations Portion contains 33 items, including 3 non-scored items. Candidates are allowed 170 minutes, and a score of 70% correct is required to pass.

The Calculations Portion content outline includes:

  • Calculations and Theory: 2 items
  • Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems: 8 items
  • Electrical Feeders: 3 items
  • Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: 4 items
  • Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials: 2 items
  • Electrical Equipment and Devices: 2 items
  • Motors and Generators: 6 items
  • Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: 1 item
  • Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions: 1 item
  • Renewable Energy Technologies: 1 item

Open Book Test

The Texas Master Electrician examination is an open book test. Candidates are allowed to reference the National Electrical Code during the examination and are responsible for bringing their own NEC book to the examination site. Electronic notepads are provided during the examination.

Texas examination rules allow any soft-bound edition of the National Electrical Code to be used for the examination, but exam questions are based on the current TDLR-adopted bound edition. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies of the NEC are not allowed in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is not allowed in the examination room.

A candidate’s NEC book may be highlighted, underlined, and may contain notes before the examination session. NEC books may be tabbed only with permanent index tabs manufactured or provided by the NEC book publisher. Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, index, or mark the NEC book during the examination session. Homemade, do-it-yourself, or non-NEC tabs are not permitted. Additional paper, whether loose, taped, adhered, or stapled, is not allowed in the examination room.

Open-book testing still requires serious preparation. The exam is not simply about having a code book available. Candidates need to understand how the NEC is organized, how definitions are used, how tables apply, how exceptions are written, and how to identify the correct rule for the question being asked.

Because the Texas Master Electrician exam separates NEC knowledge and calculations into two portions, candidates should prepare for both reference navigation and calculation accuracy. The practice exams help you recognize the subject area being tested. The full final exams help you work through longer exam-style sessions. The flash cards help strengthen recall so you do not have to search for every basic concept during the test.

Licensing Steps

  1. Confirm the Texas Master Electrician license path. Texas Master Electrician licensing is regulated by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.
  2. Hold a qualifying journeyman license. Applicants must have held a Journeyman Electrician license for at least 2 years. Journeyman Industrial Electrician experience may not be used to qualify for a Master Electrician license.
  3. Document required work experience. Applicants must show at least 12,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician.
  4. Obtain experience verification. Candidates must obtain verification from each Master Electrician who supervised their qualifying work experience, using the required experience verification documentation.
  5. Submit the license application. The initial Texas Master Electrician license application is submitted by mail to TDLR with the required non-refundable application fee.
  6. Receive approval to test. After TDLR receives and verifies the application and experience documentation, the candidate receives notification of approval to take the licensing exam.
  7. Schedule the exam through PSI. After approval, the candidate schedules the Texas Master Electrician examination through PSI.
  8. Prepare with structured study materials. Use the study guide, flash cards, 12 practice exams, 2 full final exams, NEC review, reference-navigation practice, and calculation review to build readiness.
  9. Pass both exam portions. The NEC Knowledge Portion and Calculations Portion must each be passed with a minimum score of 70%.
  10. Maintain the license. Texas electrician licenses are valid for one year from the date of issuance and must be renewed annually.

State Requirements

The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation regulates electrician licensing in Texas. With limited exemptions, anyone who performs electrical work in Texas must be licensed. Electrical work includes labor or material used in installing, maintaining, or extending electrical wiring systems and related appurtenances, apparatus, or equipment connected with the use of electrical energy, including service entrance conductors as defined by the National Electrical Code.

A Texas Master Electrician is licensed to install, maintain, or extend electrical wiring systems and related appurtenances, apparatus, or equipment used in connection with electrical energy in, on, outside, or attached to buildings, residences, structures, property, or premises. This license represents an advanced individual credential in the Texas electrical licensing structure.

To apply for a Texas Master Electrician license, the applicant must have held a Journeyman Electrician license for at least 2 years. The applicant must also show completion of at least 12,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician.

Each supervising Master Electrician must verify the applicant’s experience. If the applicant held a municipal or regional master electrician license, the applicant may verify their own on-the-job training for the years they held that license. TDLR may contact individuals to verify the experience listed in the application materials.

The initial application must be submitted by mail with the required fee. After the completed application has been received and verified, the candidate receives approval to take the licensing examination. Test results are sent electronically to TDLR by the testing provider, so candidates do not need to send their exam results to TDLR.

Texas electrician licenses are valid for one year from the date of issuance and must be renewed annually. Licensed electricians should follow TDLR renewal, continuing education, supervision, local permit, inspection, and compliance requirements.
