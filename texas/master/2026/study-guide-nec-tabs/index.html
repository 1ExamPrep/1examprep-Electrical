Exam Details

The Texas Master Electrician examination is administered by PSI for the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. Candidates must first apply through TDLR and receive eligibility approval before scheduling the examination with PSI. After TDLR approves eligibility, the candidate receives instructions for scheduling the exam.

The Texas Master Electrician exam consists of two separate multiple-choice portions: NEC Knowledge and Calculations. Candidates must pass each portion with a minimum score of 70%. Time from one portion does not roll over into the next portion, so students should prepare for each portion separately and practice pacing for both sections.

The NEC Knowledge Portion includes 75 items, including 5 non-scored items, and allows 150 minutes. The content outline includes definitions, theory, plans, electrical services, service equipment, separately derived systems, feeders, branch circuit conductors, wiring methods and materials, equipment and devices, motors and generators, control devices and disconnecting means, special occupancies, equipment, conditions, and renewable energy technologies.

The Calculations Portion includes 33 items, including 3 non-scored items, and allows 170 minutes. The calculation outline includes electrical theory, services, service equipment, separately derived systems, load calculations, grounding and bonding, transformers, feeders, branch circuit calculations, conductors, raceways, equipment, appliances, motors, generators, control devices, disconnecting means, special occupancies, and renewable energy technology questions.

The current Texas PSI bulletin lists exam questions as based on the National Electrical Code, 2023 Edition, which is the current TDLR-adopted bound edition for exam purposes. This product is based on the 2026 NEC, making it useful for updated code-cycle study, long-term code development, and reference-navigation practice. Candidates should use the official NEC edition required for the exam they are scheduled to take.

Open Book Test

The Texas Master Electrician exam is an open book test. PSI’s Texas electrician candidate bulletin identifies the National Electrical Code as the allowed examination reference. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved NEC book to the examination site.

The Texas exam reference rules are specific. A soft-bound edition of the National Electrical Code may be used for the exam. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies of the NEC are not allowed in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is not allowed. Electronic notepads are provided during the exam.

A candidate’s NEC book may be highlighted, underlined, and may contain notes before the examination session. NEC books may be tabbed with permanent index tabs manufactured or provided by the NEC book publisher. Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, index, or mark the NEC book during the examination session. Homemade, DIY, or non-NEC tabs are not allowed. Additional paper, whether loose, taped, adhered, or stapled, is not allowed in the examination room.

This combo includes the National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs. It is designed for updated NEC study and code-navigation practice. Because the current Texas exam is based on the 2023 NEC, candidates should use the official code edition and compliant reference setup required by TDLR and PSI for exam day.

Open book testing still requires serious preparation. The NEC is large, detailed, and technical. Students who have not practiced with the code book may lose time searching for basic sections, using the wrong table, overlooking an exception, or missing an article-specific requirement. The strongest candidates understand how to move from a question topic to the correct article, read the code language carefully, check the proper table, and apply the rule accurately.

Licensing Steps

The Texas master electrician licensing path begins with holding a Texas Journeyman Electrician license for at least two years. Texas does not allow journeyman industrial electrician experience to be used to qualify for the Master Electrician license. The master path is intended for electricians who have already progressed through journeyman licensing and gained extensive supervised experience.

The applicant must also document at least 12,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Master Electrician licensed in Texas. The applicant must contact and obtain verification from each Master Electrician who supervised the qualifying work. The employment history portion of the application must include the full qualifying experience.

After organizing experience documentation, the applicant submits the Texas Master Electrician license application to TDLR with the required information and fee. TDLR reviews the application and experience. Once the experience has been approved, the candidate becomes eligible to schedule the exam with PSI.

After receiving eligibility approval, the candidate schedules the Texas Master Electrician exam through PSI. The candidate takes both required exam portions and must pass each portion with at least 70%. Score results are provided immediately after completion of the examination, and candidates who do not pass receive a diagnostic report showing strengths and weaknesses by examination type.

After passing the required examination and completing the licensing process, the applicant may be issued the Texas Master Electrician license. Licensees must continue to follow Texas electrical licensing laws, TDLR rules, renewal requirements, continuing education requirements, electrical contractor requirements, local permitting procedures, and the scope of work authorized by the license held.

State Requirements

Texas electrical licensing is administered by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. Texas requires state-issued electrical licenses for individuals performing non-exempt electrical work. Electrical work must also be performed through a licensed electrical contractor unless an exemption applies.

To qualify for the Texas Master Electrician license, an applicant must have held a Journeyman Electrician license for at least two years, must document 12,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician, and must pass the master electrician examination. This makes the Texas Master Electrician license an advanced credential built on journeyman licensing and supervised master-level experience.

Texas also issues other electrical license types, including apprentice electrician, residential wireman, journeyman electrician, maintenance electrician, master sign electrician, journeyman sign electrician, journeyman industrial electrician, journeyman lineman, residential appliance installer, and contractor-related licenses. Candidates should pursue the license type that matches the electrical work they intend to perform.

A Texas Master Electrician may perform electrical work, including electrical work performed by journeyman electricians and other electrician license types within the scope allowed by law. However, operating as an electrical contracting business requires the proper contractor license and compliance with TDLR requirements. Individual licensure and business contractor authority are related but separate responsibilities.

Test Information and Study Materials

Texas Master Electrician preparation should begin with the two-part exam structure. Students should study NEC knowledge separately from calculation work because each portion has its own question count, time limit, and passing requirement. A balanced study plan should include code lookup practice, timed calculation drills, missed-answer review, and repeated practice with NEC tables.

The NEC Knowledge portion includes definitions, theory, and plans. Students should review Article 100 definitions, electrical theory fundamentals, plan-reading concepts, symbols, schedules, service layouts, equipment locations, conductor information, panel schedules, and code-compliance details shown in drawings or specifications.

Services, service equipment, and separately derived systems are major study areas. Students should practice service load knowledge, fault current concepts, service disconnect rules, grounding and bonding at services, temporary wiring, transformers, separately derived system grounding, service conductor sizing, and overcurrent protection.

Feeders, branch circuits, and conductors should receive steady attention. Students should review feeder grounding, feeder bonding, feeder overcurrent protection, branch-circuit conductor rules, GFCI and AFCI protection, conductor ampacity, adjustment factors, correction factors, neutral conductor rules, continuous loads, noncontinuous loads, and required outlets.

Wiring methods and materials are central to Texas master electrician preparation. Students should review raceways, cable trays, boxes, enclosures, cabinets, panels, cables, cords, underground wiring, conduit fill, box fill, conductor protection, support rules, wet-location requirements, and Chapter 9 table use.

Electrical equipment and devices should include receptacles, switches, luminaires, appliances, air conditioning equipment, refrigeration equipment, heating equipment, workspace clearance, panelboards, disconnects, and utilization equipment. Motors and generators should include conductor sizing, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, disconnecting means, controllers, generator installation rules, and related calculations.

Special occupancies, equipment, conditions, and renewable energy technologies should also be included in the study schedule. Students should review swimming pools, fountains, RVs, mobile homes, park trailers, marinas, data processing equipment, communications equipment, emergency systems, miscellaneous equipment, photovoltaic systems, and electric vehicle chargers.

The included 12 Open Book Practice Exams help students apply what they study. Practice exams should be reviewed carefully after completion. Students should locate the supporting code section for missed questions, understand why the correct answer applies, and return to weak subjects before moving on. The 2 Complete Final Exams with Answers & Analysis help students measure readiness and practice pacing closer to exam conditions.
