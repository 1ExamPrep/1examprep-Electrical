Exam Details

Texas electrician licensing is handled through the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. The Texas Master Electrician examination is administered by PSI after the applicant’s eligibility has been approved by TDLR. The exam is designed to measure competency in master-level electrical knowledge, NEC application, and calculation skills.

The Texas Master Electrician written examination consists of two separate multiple-choice portions: the NEC Knowledge Portion and the Calculations Portion. Candidates must pass each portion with a minimum score of 70 percent. Time from one portion does not roll over into the other portion, so candidates should prepare for each section as its own timed exam event.

The NEC Knowledge Portion is listed with 75 items, including 5 non-scored items, and 150 minutes of testing time. The content outline includes Definitions, Theory, and Plans; Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems; Electrical Feeders; Branch Circuit Conductors; Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials; Electrical Equipment and Devices; Motors and Generators; Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means; Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions; and Renewable Energy Technologies.

The Calculations Portion is listed with 33 items, including 3 non-scored items, and 170 minutes of testing time. The content outline includes Calculations and Theory; Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems; Electrical Feeders; Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors; Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials; Electrical Equipment and Devices; Motors and Generators; Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means; Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions; and Renewable Energy Technologies.

The Texas exam structure shows why candidates should prepare with both code study and dedicated calculation practice. The NEC Knowledge Portion requires broad code familiarity, careful reading, and strong reference navigation. The Calculations Portion requires step-by-step accuracy, formula recognition, and the ability to apply NEC requirements under time pressure. Open Book Test

The Texas electrician examinations are identified as open book examinations. For the current Texas exam reference rules, candidates are allowed to reference the National Electrical Code edition listed by TDLR and PSI for the examination. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own NEC book to the examination site.

Texas reference rules are strict. The allowed NEC must be a soft-bound edition. Loose-leaf, spiralbound, or ring-bound copies are not allowed in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is not allowed. The NEC book may be highlighted, underlined, and may contain notes before the examination session. Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, index, or mark the NEC during the examination session.

Texas also limits tabs. NEC books may be tabbed with permanent index tabs manufactured or provided by the NEC book publisher. Homemade, DIY, or non-NEC tabs are not permitted. Additional paper that is loose, taped, adhered, stapled, or otherwise attached is not allowed in the examination room. Candidates who arrive with a non-compliant code book may be required to test without it, remove non-compliant tabs, or forfeit the examination fee and test another day with a compliant NEC book.

Licensing Steps

A practical Texas licensing path begins by confirming that the Master Electrician license is the correct license for the work and responsibility the candidate intends to perform. Texas requires a license for non-exempt electrical work, and electrical work must be performed through a licensed electrical contractor when required by law.

To apply for a Texas Master Electrician license, the applicant must have held a Journeyman Electrician license for at least two years. TDLR notes that Journeyman Industrial Electrician experience may not be used to qualify for a Master Electrician license. The applicant must also show completion of 12,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Master Electrician licensed in Texas.

The applicant must obtain verification from each Master Electrician who supervised the qualifying work. The experience must be documented and signed by each supervisor on the required Experience Verification Form. If the applicant has held a municipal or regional master electrician license, the applicant may be able to verify on-the-job training for the years that the municipal or regional master license was held.

After preparing the experience documentation, the applicant submits the Master Electrician License Application Form and application fee to TDLR. The initial Texas Master Electrician application is submitted by mail. After the completed application has been received and verified, TDLR notifies the applicant of approval to take the licensing examination.

Once approved for testing, the candidate schedules the examination through PSI. The Texas Master Electrician exam includes the NEC Knowledge Portion and the Calculations Portion, and both portions must be passed with a minimum score of 70 percent. After the examination is completed, PSI sends results to TDLR electronically. The candidate does not need to send exam results to TDLR separately.

After the candidate satisfies the examination and remaining licensing requirements, TDLR completes the license process. Texas electrician licenses are valid for one year from the date of issuance and must be renewed annually. Renewal is completed through TDLR, and licensees should follow the current continuing education and renewal rules that apply to their license category.

State Requirements

Texas regulates electricians through the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. TDLR states that, with limited exemptions, anyone who performs electrical work in Texas must be licensed. Electrical work includes labor or material used in installing, maintaining, or extending an electrical wiring system and related appurtenances, apparatus, or equipment used in connection with electrical energy in, on, outside, or attached to a building, residence, structure, property, or premises, including service entrance conductors.

The Texas Master Electrician license is a high-level individual electrical license. A Master Electrician is licensed to install, maintain, or extend an electrical wiring system and the appurtenances, apparatus, or equipment used in connection with the use of electrical energy in, on, outside, or attached to a building, residence, structure, property, or premises, including service entrance conductors.

Texas Master Electrician applicants must have held a Journeyman Electrician license for at least two years and must document 12,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician. The employment history portion of the application must include the full qualifying experience, and TDLR may contact individuals to verify the experience.

Texas also regulates electrical contractors separately from individual electricians. A Master Electrician license is an important credential, but contracting activity must also meet the applicable electrical contractor licensing requirements when a person or company performs, offers to perform, or contracts to perform electrical work. Candidates planning to operate a business should understand the difference between an individual master license and an electrical contractor license.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Texas Master Electrician study plan should include NEC study, calculation practice, trade knowledge review, reference navigation, recall practice, and timed question work. Because the exam is divided into NEC Knowledge and Calculations, candidates should build a study schedule that gives each portion focused attention. Strong code navigation helps on the NEC Knowledge Portion, while careful math practice helps on the Calculations Portion.

For NEC study, start by learning the structure of the code book. Review article organization, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and cross-references. Practice moving from a question to the correct article and reading the surrounding language carefully. Many electrical exam questions are missed because a candidate finds a related section but overlooks a condition, exception, table note, or specific wording.

For the NEC Knowledge Portion, focus on definitions, theory, plans, services, service equipment, separately derived systems, feeders, branch circuits, wiring methods, wiring materials, equipment, devices, motors, generators, controls, disconnecting means, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, and renewable energy technologies. Study both common rules and less familiar areas so you are not surprised by a subject you rarely encounter in daily work.

For the Calculations Portion, write out each step. Electrical calculation mistakes often happen when a candidate skips a conversion, uses the wrong table, applies the wrong value, or confuses branch circuit, feeder, service, motor, generator, transformer, raceway fill, box fill, voltage drop, or overcurrent protection requirements. The 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide gives candidates a dedicated way to practice these skills repeatedly.

For open book preparation, use the NEC actively during study. Practice locating sections, reading tables, and confirming answers in the code book. Then practice some questions without immediately opening the code book so you can build recognition. The stronger your recall becomes, the more time you can save during questions that require deeper code lookup.

For flash card study, use short sessions throughout the week. Flash cards can help reinforce formulas, definitions, code concepts, calculation cues, and common electrical terms. They are useful before work, during breaks, after a longer study block, or as final review. Flash cards should not replace full study sessions, but they help strengthen recall and recognition.
