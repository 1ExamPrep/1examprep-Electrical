Exam Details

Rhode Island gives trade license examinations for electricians through the Department of Labor and Training’s Professional Regulation Unit. The Department’s electrician application packet identifies the Journeyperson Electrician B classification and explains the application requirements for electrician exams. Candidates must submit the required application materials and receive approval through the state process before testing.

The Rhode Island electrician application materials state that electrician journeyperson tests require verification of at least four years of experience in the respective trade and 576 hours of related instruction approved by the Department of Labor and Training. Rhode Island indentured apprenticeship candidates applying for electrician journeyperson exams must submit a Certificate of Completion of Apprenticeship issued by the Rhode Island Department of Labor and Training Apprenticeship Office.

The application packet also states that individuals who achieve a passing score of 70 or greater will be invoiced for the license fee. The application processing fee listed for the Journeyperson Electrician B exam is $75, and the two-year license fee listed for the Journeyperson Electrician B license is $72.

Rhode Island’s recommended study material for electrician exams includes the current National Electrical Code book, using the latest state-approved edition, and general trade knowledge. Because the Rhode Island exam process is tied to state licensing requirements, candidates should prepare for both trade knowledge and code-based understanding. Closed Book Test

The Rhode Island Journeyperson Electrician examination should be prepared for as a closed-book, knowledge-based licensing exam. Candidates should study the National Electrical Code, general trade knowledge, formulas, electrical theory, and Rhode Island licensing requirements before the examination rather than relying on exam-room reference lookup.

Closed-book preparation requires a strong command of key concepts. Candidates should be able to recognize common NEC topics, understand electrical formulas, remember important definitions, and apply trade knowledge without stopping to search through reference material. The goal is not to memorize the entire NEC word for word. The goal is to build enough familiarity with code structure, calculations, and electrical principles that questions can be answered accurately and efficiently.

Licensing Steps

Rhode Island candidates should begin by confirming that the Journeyperson Electrician B license is the correct license classification for their work goals. The state application materials list multiple electrician classifications, including electrical contractor, journeyperson electrician, limited maintenance journeyperson, fire alarm, sign, burner, and lightning protection categories. Candidates should apply under the classification that matches their experience and intended work.

A practical licensing path begins with completing the required supervised experience and related instruction. Journeyperson electrician applicants must verify at least four years of experience in the trade and 576 hours of related instruction approved by the Department of Labor and Training. Rhode Island indentured apprenticeship candidates must submit the required apprenticeship completion documentation from the Department’s Apprenticeship Office.

After meeting the experience and education requirements, candidates complete the electrician application for examination and apprenticeship, provide a government-issued identification copy, submit the notarized application, include the non-refundable application processing fee, and provide required employer documentation. Employer statements should include work-related experience and job descriptions that support the license classification requested.

Once the state reviews and approves the application, the candidate follows the Department’s exam scheduling process. After testing, individuals who achieve a passing score of 70 or greater are invoiced for the applicable license fee. The license is issued through the Rhode Island Department of Labor and Training after the candidate completes the required licensing steps.

Passing the examination is an important milestone, but the state licensing process determines when the license is issued. Candidates should keep copies of their application materials, apprenticeship completion documentation, employer verification, related instruction records, exam information, and license payment records.

State Requirements

Rhode Island requires electrician licensing through the Department of Labor and Training’s Professional Regulations Unit. The Board of Examiners of Electricians is part of the state professional regulation structure, and the Department reviews and licenses applicants for electrician classifications.

For a Journeyperson Electrician B license, Rhode Island application materials require verification of at least four years of trade experience and 576 hours of related instruction approved by the Department of Labor and Training. The state regulations identify the journeyperson Class B requirement as at least 8,000 hours, or four years, of experience as a registered apprentice.

Rhode Island electrical contractor applicants have separate requirements. The application materials state that electrical contractor tests require at least six years of trade experience and possession of a Rhode Island journeyperson license that has been valid for at least two years before applying for a contractor examination. Candidates preparing for the journeyperson license should understand that the journeyperson step can be part of a longer pathway toward contractor-level licensing.

Out-of-state applicants may need to provide a valid copy of an out-of-state electrician trade license and official license verification from the issuing state. The state application packet notes that computer printouts of trade license verification are not accepted as proof. Candidates applying through an out-of-state path should prepare the official documentation required by Rhode Island.

Study materials help candidates prepare, but licensing decisions are made by the Rhode Island Department of Labor and Training based on the applicant’s qualifications, documentation, examination result, and compliance with state requirements.

Calculation questions can take more time than direct knowledge questions, so repeated practice helps improve both speed and accuracy.

Code familiarity should also be part of every study plan. Candidates should practice using the NEC table of contents, index, article numbers, definitions, tables, notes, and exceptions. Even when preparing for a closed-book exam, code navigation during study helps candidates understand how subjects are organized and how one rule connects to another.

Services, feeders, and branch circuits should receive careful attention. Candidates should review service conductors, service disconnects, grounding at services, feeder sizing, branch circuit ratings, required outlets, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, and load calculations. These topics connect code knowledge with practical installation skill.

Grounding and bonding are important areas for journeyperson exam preparation. Candidates should review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, raceway bonding, panel bonding, grounded conductors, separately derived systems, and related safety requirements. Grounding and bonding questions often require careful reading because similar terms can lead to different code requirements.

Wiring methods and materials should be studied consistently. Candidates should review conductors, cables, raceways, boxes, fittings, supports, conductor ampacity, insulation types, flexible wiring methods, underground wiring, and installation conditions. These topics appear frequently in electrical work and are central to NEC-based study.

Motors, transformers, controls, and equipment should also be part of the study routine. Candidates should review motor branch circuits, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, disconnecting means, controllers, transformer sizing, transformer protection, luminaires, receptacles, switches, appliances, panelboards, and related installation requirements.

Flash cards are useful for shorter study sessions. Candidates can use them to review formulas, definitions, electrical theory, safety concepts, NEC topics, and trade terms before work, after work, during breaks, or between longer study blocks. Flash cards work best when paired with deeper reading, calculation drills, and code review.

A strong study plan should include scheduled reading, NEC familiarity, calculation drills, closed-book review, timed practice, and repeated review of weaker topics. Candidates should avoid studying only familiar material. Journeyperson electrician preparation rewards steady practice, careful reading, and the ability to apply electrical knowledge under exam conditions.
