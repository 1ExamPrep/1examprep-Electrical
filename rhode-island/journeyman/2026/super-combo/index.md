## Licensing Steps

Rhode Island candidates should begin by confirming that the Journeyperson Electrician B license is the correct license classification for their work goals. The state application materials list multiple electrician classifications, including electrical contractor, journeyperson electrician, limited maintenance journeyperson, fire alarm, sign, burner, and lightning protection categories. Candidates should apply under the classification that matches their experience and intended work.

A practical licensing path begins with completing the required supervised experience and related instruction. Journeyperson electrician applicants must verify at least four years of experience in the trade and 576 hours of related instruction approved by the Department of Labor and Training. Rhode Island indentured apprenticeship candidates must submit the required apprenticeship completion documentation from the Department’s Apprenticeship Office.

After meeting the experience and education requirements, candidates complete the electrician application for examination and apprenticeship, provide a government-issued identification copy, submit the notarized application, include the non-refundable application processing fee, and provide required employer documentation. Employer statements should include work-related experience and job descriptions that support the license classification requested.

Once the state reviews and approves the application, the candidate follows the Department’s exam scheduling process. After testing, individuals who achieve a passing score of 70 or greater are invoiced for the applicable license fee. The license is issued through the Rhode Island Department of Labor and Training after the candidate completes the required licensing steps.

Passing the examination is an important milestone, but the state licensing process determines when the license is issued. Candidates should keep copies of their application materials, apprenticeship completion documentation, employer verification, related instruction records, exam information, and license payment records.

## State Requirements

Rhode Island requires electrician licensing through the Department of Labor and Training’s Professional Regulations Unit. The Board of Examiners of Electricians is part of the state professional regulation structure, and the Department reviews and licenses applicants for electrician classifications.

For a Journeyperson Electrician B license, Rhode Island application materials require verification of at least four years of trade experience and 576 hours of related instruction approved by the Department of Labor and Training. The state regulations identify the journeyperson Class B requirement as at least 8,000 hours, or four years, of experience as a registered apprentice.

Rhode Island electrical contractor applicants have separate requirements. The application materials state that electrical contractor tests require at least six years of trade experience and possession of a Rhode Island journeyperson license that has been valid for at least two years before applying for a contractor examination. Candidates preparing for the journeyperson license should understand that the journeyperson step can be part of a longer pathway toward contractor-level licensing.

Out-of-state applicants may need to provide a valid copy of an out-of-state electrician trade license and official license verification from the issuing state. The state application packet notes that computer printouts of trade license verification are not accepted as proof. Candidates applying through an out-of-state path should prepare the official documentation required by Rhode Island.

Study materials help candidates prepare, but licensing decisions are made by the Rhode Island Department of Labor and Training based on the applicant’s qualifications, documentation, examination result, and compliance with state requirements.
