Exam Details

The Rhode Island Journeyperson Electrician examination is administered under the Rhode Island Department of Labor and Training, Division of Professional Regulation, and the Board of Examiners of Electricians. Applicants must submit the required application materials and receive approval before being admitted to the examination. Only applicants approved to take the examination may sit for the test.

Rhode Island electrician examinations are based on the edition of the National Electrical Code adopted by the Rhode Island State Building Code Standards Committee at the time of examination, along with Rhode Island electrical laws, rules, and code amendments. The Rhode Island rules require applicants to obtain an average score of at least 70% in order to obtain a license.

Rhode Island’s application instructions state that all electrical journeyperson tests require verification of at least four years of experience in the respective trade and 576 hours of related instruction approved by the Department of Labor and Training. Rhode Island indentured apprenticeship applicants must provide a Certificate of Completion of Apprenticeship issued by the Rhode Island Department of Labor and Training Apprenticeship Office when applying for journeyperson testing.

Journeyperson exam preparation should include the National Electrical Code, Rhode Island electrical rules, electrical theory, general electrical knowledge, services, feeders, branch circuits, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment for general use, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, motors and controls, transformers, lighting, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, communication systems, safety, and calculations.

This exam requires both electrical understanding and careful test management. A candidate may need to answer direct theory questions, apply NEC concepts, interpret installation rules, understand Rhode Island-specific requirements, or work math-based electrical problems. Because licensing exams are timed and detailed, candidates should practice not only the content but also pacing, question reading, calculation accuracy, and decision-making under exam conditions.

Closed Book Test

Rhode Island’s Board of Examiners of Electricians regulations state that a person found referring to notes or books during an examination, except by permission of the Board, will be disqualified from that examination. Candidates should prepare for the Rhode Island Journeyperson Electrician examination as a closed book test unless they have specific exam-day instructions from the Board permitting approved references.

Closed book testing requires a different kind of preparation than a fully open book exam. Candidates should still study with the National Electrical Code, but they should not depend on being able to search the code book during the examination. Instead, students should focus on understanding electrical concepts, remembering major code requirements, recognizing where common rules are located, and building enough practice to answer questions with confidence.

Licensing Steps

Rhode Island journeyperson electrician candidates typically begin by entering electrical apprenticeship or supervised electrical employment. Apprentices should work under proper supervision, complete required related instruction, and maintain documentation of job duties, dates of employment, and work experience.

The candidate must build at least four years of qualifying experience in the electrical trade and complete 576 hours of related instruction approved by the Rhode Island Department of Labor and Training. Rhode Island indentured apprentices applying for journeyperson testing must provide the Certificate of Completion of Apprenticeship issued by the Department of Labor and Training Apprenticeship Office.

After meeting the experience and instruction requirements, the candidate submits the electrician application materials to the Rhode Island Department of Labor and Training, Division of Professional Regulation. The application instructions require supporting documentation, including employer statements on company letterhead describing work-related experience. Applicants should make sure the application is complete, signed, notarized where required, and submitted with the required processing fee.

After the application is reviewed and approved, the candidate receives examination instructions. The candidate should review the current Rhode Island exam information, study material guidance, identification requirements, exam location instructions, and any rules issued by the Board before the test date.

After passing the examination, the candidate completes the remaining licensing requirements through the Rhode Island Department of Labor and Training. Passing the exam is an important step, but the licensing process is not complete until all state requirements are satisfied and the journeyperson electrician license is issued.

State Requirements

Rhode Island requires electricians to be licensed through the Department of Labor and Training, Division of Professional Regulation. The Board of Examiners of Electricians oversees electrician licensing rules and examination procedures. The state’s licensing structure includes electrical contractor and journeyperson electrician classifications, as well as several specialty electrical classifications.

The Rhode Island journeyperson electrician license is commonly associated with Certificate B. Electrical contractor applicants must have additional experience and must have held a Rhode Island journeyperson license for the required period before applying for contractor-level examination. This makes the journeyperson license an important step for electricians who plan to advance toward contractor-level licensing later.

Rhode Island’s electrical journeyperson testing requirements include at least four years of experience in the respective electrical trade and 576 hours of related instruction approved by the Department of Labor and Training. Candidates who completed a Rhode Island indentured apprenticeship must provide a Certificate of Completion of Apprenticeship issued by the state Apprenticeship Office.

Rhode Island rules state that electrician examinations are based on the National Electrical Code edition adopted by the Rhode Island State Building Code Standards Committee at the time of examination. The Division of Professional Regulation adopts the NEC provisions adopted by the State Building Code Standards Committee as the official rules and requirements for electrical work.

Rhode Island electrician licenses are renewable under state rules. Certificate A and Certificate B electricians must complete at least 15 hours of approved continuing education as a condition of renewal. Continuing education helps electricians stay current with code updates, Rhode Island amendments, and safe installation practices.
