# Failed the Electrician Exam? What to Change for the Retake

How to read the score report, why most second attempts fail for the same reason as the first, and a four-week plan that changes the thing that actually went wrong.
[study tips](https://passyourmasterelectricianexam.com/blog/tag/study-tips/) · [journeyman](https://passyourmasterelectricianexam.com/blog/tag/journeyman/) · [master](https://passyourmasterelectricianexam.com/blog/tag/master/)

## Key takeaways

- Read the score report's breakdown by content area and let it set the order of your study, not your instincts about what felt hard.
- Most retakes fail on time, not knowledge: if you did not finish or rushed the last third, the fix is pacing and code navigation, not more reading.
- Rebuild your study with timed drills from day one and an error log by code section.
- Most states allow a retake after 30 days with a new fee; use the waiting period in full rather than rushing back.

A fail is a data point, and a surprisingly precise one. The score report that came with it tells you which areas were weak, and the way the exam went tells you whether the problem was knowledge, the book or the clock. The candidates who pass on the second attempt are the ones who change something specific. Here is how to decide what.

## Read the score report

The report shows a pass or fail, your percentage, and a breakdown by content area: general requirements, branch circuits and feeders, grounding, wiring methods, calculations and so on. Write the areas down in order from weakest to strongest. That is the order of your study for the next month, and it will not match your instincts. Candidates usually feel that grounding was the hard part when the report says it was calculations.

If the exam has separately scored sections and you passed one, check whether your state lets you retake only the failed section. Several do, and it halves the work.

## Work out what actually went wrong

Three questions, answered honestly.

**Did you finish?** If you ran out of time or guessed the last fifteen questions, the problem is pace. More knowledge will not fix it; faster lookups and a stricter time budget will.

**Did you find the sections and still miss?** If you remember reading the right section and getting the wrong answer, the problem is reading: exceptions, conditions, the wrong table row. The fix is slower, more careful reading on the second pass, and highlighting exceptions during study.

**Did you not know where to look?** If you spent minutes in the index and never found the rule, the problem is the book. The fix is the index drill and re-tabbing, every day, until lookups are automatic.

Most candidates have one dominant cause. Fix that one first.

## The four-week retake plan

Most states set a 30 day waiting period before a retake. Use all of it.

**Week 1: the weakest two areas from the report.** Study them by article with the practice questions for those articles only. Start the error log on day one: every miss by code section and cause. If the cause from above was the book, add the index drill daily from this week.

**Week 2: the next two areas, plus calculations every day.** Whatever the report said, calculations get a session every day in week 2, because they are the fastest points to recover and the easiest to lose under pressure. Keep the log.

**Week 3: timed work.** Two full-length practice exams under exam conditions, three days apart, with the log updated after each. Between them, thirty-question drills in forty-five minutes to make the pace feel normal.

**Week 4: the log, then taper.** Spend the first four days on the sections the log names most often. Then stop testing, rewrite the formula sheet from memory each morning, do the index drill, and rest. Nothing new in the last 48 hours.

## What to change on the day

Whatever beat you last time, build a rule against it. Ran out of time: a hard checkpoint every twenty questions and a two-minute limit on lookups. Missed exceptions: read every section to the end before choosing. Lost in the index: table of contents first, index last. Arithmetic slips: write the formula before the numbers, every time.

## The things that do not help

Reading the whole study guide again from page one. Buying a different book because the first one "did not work". Studying for a week and sitting the exam the day the waiting period ends. Changing nothing and hoping the questions are easier.

## Fees, limits and paperwork

Each attempt costs the exam fee again, and some states cap attempts per year or require additional training after three or four fails. Your eligibility usually stays valid for a year from the board's approval; check the letter so you do not have to reapply. Schedule the retake as soon as you can see the date, because seats fill, and work backwards from it.

A second attempt with a specific fix behind it passes far more often than a first attempt did. You know what the room is like now, and you know where you lost the points. That is most of the battle.
