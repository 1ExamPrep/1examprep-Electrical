Master Electrician Exam: 15 Questions People Ask, Answered

How hard is the master electrician exam, how many questions are on it, how long it takes and how many hours you need first. Straight answers to the 15 questions electricians search most. Each answer is short, plain and checked against the official board and exam sources.

The master electrician exam is an open-book, multiple-choice test on the National Electrical Code edition your state has adopted. Most states require a journeyman license and one to four more years of experience before you can sit for it. The pass mark is usually 70 to 75 percent. Here are the 15 questions electricians search most.

What is the master electrician exam?

The master exam is the licensing test that moves you from journeyman to master. A journeyman works under supervision. A master can plan and lay out installations, supervise journeymen and apprentices, and in many states pull permits or qualify an electrical contractor business. Because of that, the master exam goes deeper than the journeyman test. It adds services, load calculations, transformers and more design questions, and some states add a business and law section. The state board sets the rules, and a testing vendor such as PSI, Prometric or Pearson VUE runs the exam.

How many hours do you need for a master electrician license?

Most states ask for about 8,000 hours, or four years, of supervised work before the journeyman exam. The master exam then adds one to four years of work as a licensed journeyman, so the total often lands around six to eight years in the trade. Some states let an electrical engineering degree or apprenticeship classroom hours replace part of the field time. The board's application lists exactly what counts and what proof it wants, such as employer letters, pay records or a sworn affidavit. Track your hours as you go so the paperwork is ready the day you qualify.

How do you apply for the master electrician exam?

You apply to your state licensing board, not to the testing company. Send the application, proof of experience and the fee. The board reviews it and, once you are approved, sends you an eligibility letter or tells the vendor you can test. Only then can you book a seat. Buying a study guide does not start this process. If you are a few months from finishing your hours, get the application ready early so you lose no time once you qualify.

How do you schedule the master electrician exam?

After board approval, schedule with the vendor named in your approval letter. Most vendors let you book online or by phone, and you pick a test center and date from what is open. From a complete application to an exam date is often four to eight weeks, depending on how fast the board works and how busy the center is. Book a date that leaves you six to eight weeks of study, and check the reschedule window in your confirmation email in case something comes up.

How do you book and take the exam on test day?

Booking is the same step as scheduling: you choose a center and time with the vendor. On the day, arrive 30 minutes early with a government photo ID that matches your registration exactly. Bring the approved code book in the right edition and two silent, non-programmable calculators. Phones, smart watches, bags and notes stay in your car or a locker. Most centers give you a preliminary pass or fail before you leave, and the official result comes from the board in a few weeks.

How hard is the master electrician exam?

The master exam is hard, mainly because of the clock. Most candidates know the trade well. What trips them up is finding the right section fast enough and working long calculations under pressure. The master exam leans harder on load calculations, services, motors and transformers than the journeyman test, and questions are written to make you read every word. People who have done timed practice with a tabbed code book find it fair. People who walk in expecting to rely on field experience alone often run out of time.

How many questions are on the master electrician exam?

Question counts vary by state. A typical journeyman exam has 80 to 100 questions, and master exams tend to be longer. Some states split the master exam into separately scored sections, such as code knowledge, calculations and business and law, and you must pass each one. Your state's candidate bulletin gives the exact count, the time limit and the percentage of questions in each content area. Read it before you start studying so your hours match what is actually on the test.

How long is the master electrician exam?

Plan for a long day. Journeyman exams usually run three to four hours, and master exams often take longer, especially in states that add a separate business and law part. Breaks are usually allowed, but the clock keeps running. Divide the time by the number of questions to get your pace, then set a checkpoint every 20 questions. A single question that takes more than three minutes is costing you two easier ones somewhere else.

What is on the master electrician exam?

The heavy areas are the same in most states:

  • General rules and definitions (Articles 90 to 110)
  • Branch circuits, feeders and services (210 to 230)
  • Overcurrent protection and grounding and bonding (240 and 250)
  • Wiring methods, conductors and boxes (300 to 399, 314)
  • Motors (430) and transformers (450)
  • Load calculations from Article 220 and Chapter 9

Masters also see more questions on special occupancies, hazardous locations and design. The content outline in your bulletin shows how many questions come from each area.

Is the master electrician exam open book?

Yes, in nearly every state for the code portion. You bring the National Electrical Code in the exact edition your state names, which is not always the newest one. A different year can change article numbers and answers. Tabs and highlighting are usually allowed, but loose papers and long handwritten notes are not, and some states inspect the book. The NEC Handbook with commentary is great for studying but is rarely allowed in the room, so practice with the plain code book you will carry in.

How do you study for the master electrician exam?

Six to eight weeks at about 90 minutes a day works for most working electricians. Split the time three ways:

FocusWhat to do
Code layoutLearn the chapters and drill the index until a lookup takes under 30 seconds
CalculationsWork loads, motors, voltage drop and transformers until they are automatic
Timed examsSit full-length practice tests in the last two weeks

Keep an error log by code section. The sections that keep showing up are your priority list. Flash cards help with definitions, ampacities and motor currents you should know without looking.

How do you pass the master electrician exam?

Pass by managing time and the book. Answer what you know first, flag anything that needs a long lookup and come back in a second pass. Never leave a question blank, since there is no penalty for guessing. Put the tabs in yourself so you know where every table lives. Write your formula list from memory at the start of each study session. And take at least two full-length timed exams before test day so the pace feels normal.

What score do you need to pass?

Most states set the pass mark at 70 to 75 percent. If your exam has separately scored sections, you usually need to pass each one on its own, so a strong code score will not cover a weak calculations score. Your score report shows pass or fail and a breakdown by content area, not the exact questions you missed. If you fail, that breakdown tells you where to spend the next round of study. Most states allow a retake after a short wait, often 30 days, with a new fee.

How do you get your master electrician license after the exam?

The board issues the license after the vendor reports your pass. In some states that happens on its own, and in others you send a final application and fee. Expect two to six weeks. You cannot work as a master until the license number is issued. If you want to bid jobs under your own business, most states also require an electrical contractor license, which usually adds insurance, bonding and sometimes a business and law exam.

How do you renew a master electrician license?

Most states renew every one to three years and require continuing education, commonly four to 16 hours per cycle. When a state adopts a new NEC edition, the hours usually include a code update course. Missing a renewal means late fees and, after a grace period, you may have to test again. Put the renewal date on your calendar the day the license arrives, and take your hours early in the cycle so a busy season does not push you past the deadline.

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers to a few more questions people ask about the exam and license.

Can I take the master exam without a journeyman license?

In most states, no. You need a journeyman license and extra years of experience first. A few states accept an engineering degree for part of the time, so check your board's rules.

Which NEC edition is on the master electrician exam?

The edition your state has adopted, as named in the candidate bulletin. It is not always the newest one, so check before you buy a code book.

Can I bring a calculator to the master electrician exam?

Yes. A silent, non-programmable calculator is allowed in nearly every state. Bring two and practice on the same model.

What topics are heaviest on the master electrician exam?

Branch circuits, feeders, services, grounding and bonding, motors, transformers and load calculations from Article 220.

Is the master electrician exam multiple choice?

Yes. Almost every state uses four-option multiple choice on a computer, with no penalty for a wrong answer.

How often can I retake the master electrician exam?

Most states allow a retake after a short wait, often 30 days, with a new exam fee each time.
