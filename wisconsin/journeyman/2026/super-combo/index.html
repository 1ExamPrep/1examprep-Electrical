The Wisconsin Journeyman Electrician exam is administered through the Wisconsin Department of Safety and Professional Services examination process. DSPS provides the licensing application process, exam information, approved reference rules, renewal information, and administrative requirements for Wisconsin electrical credentials.

The Wisconsin Journeyman Electrician examination is open book, and the passing score is 70%. Candidates should use the current DSPS trades examination information for exam dates, scheduling instructions, current reference material rules, and any updates to testing procedures.

Wisconsin exam information identifies reference materials that may be used during the exam. The exam covers Wisconsin administrative code material, National Electrical Code material, printed notes that meet DSPS requirements, and approved bound reference books. Candidates should prepare for questions based on Wisconsin Administrative Code chapters related to trades licensing and electrical requirements, along with NEC-based trade knowledge.

Open Book Test

The Wisconsin Journeyman Electrician examination is open book under DSPS exam reference material rules. Candidates should follow current DSPS instructions for approved references, code edition, printed notes, tabs, calculators, and prohibited materials.

Wisconsin exam reference rules allow printed notes when they are three-hole punched and placed in a binder. Printed code may be brought into the exam only when it is bound together in a three-ring binder. Practice exams and previous exams do not qualify as notes and are not allowed. Loose papers, removable tabs, sticky notes, and paperclips are not allowed.

Tabs are acceptable when they come with a bound code book. Three-ring paper dividers with tabs are also permitted. Candidates should prepare their materials before exam day and make sure the references comply with DSPS rules. Reference preparation matters because noncompliant materials can create problems before testing begins.

Wisconsin exam information allows specific reference items into the exam. These include Wisconsin Administrative Code material and printed notes in one binder, the National Electrical Code or NEC Handbook edition identified by DSPS, and up to two printed, bound reference books. Candidates should use the current DSPS exam information for the exact list and edition requirements that apply to their examination.

Open book does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates still need to recognize what the question is asking, identify whether the answer is in Wisconsin rules or the NEC, locate the correct section, interpret the language, solve calculations when required, and answer within the testing period. A candidate who is slow with references can lose valuable time even with books available.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs included in this Super Combo is intended for study and code navigation practice. Tabs help candidates become familiar with major NEC articles, definitions, tables, and commonly used sections. Candidates should also practice organizing Wisconsin administrative code materials and notes according to exam rules so they can use them efficiently during study and testing.

Licensing Steps

Wisconsin candidates should begin by confirming that the Journeyman Electrician license is the correct credential for their work goals. Wisconsin also recognizes other electrical credentials, including apprentice and master-level credentials. The correct license depends on the candidate’s experience, supervision, work scope, and long-term career goal.

A practical licensing path begins with completing qualifying electrical construction apprenticeship or approved equivalent experience. Candidates preparing for a Wisconsin journeyman license should maintain accurate records of apprenticeship completion, supervised work, dates of employment, job duties, and related instruction. Documentation matters because DSPS reviews the applicant’s qualifications as part of the licensing and examination process.

After meeting the applicable education and experience requirements, candidates apply through the DSPS LicensE platform or the current DSPS licensing process. DSPS uses LicensE for initial trades credentials, reinstatement, and renewal applications. Candidates should submit the correct application, provide required documentation, and follow any instructions issued by DSPS.

Once the candidate is eligible for examination, the candidate follows the current DSPS trades exam instructions. This includes reviewing the exam information page, preparing approved reference materials, organizing notes in the allowed format, confirming the code edition and Wisconsin administrative code material, and taking the examination as scheduled.

After passing the examination and completing the licensing process, the candidate receives the Wisconsin Journeyman Electrician license through DSPS. Passing the exam is an important milestone, but the license is issued only after the applicant satisfies Wisconsin requirements, submits required documentation, and completes the state process.

State Requirements

Wisconsin requires individuals performing electrical wiring activities to be properly licensed or registered unless an exception applies. Wisconsin law states that a person may not install, repair, or maintain electrical wiring unless licensed as an electrician by the Department or enrolled as a registered electrician by the Department.

A Wisconsin licensed Journeyman Electrician performs electrical wiring activities under the general supervision of a licensed Master Electrician or a Registered Master Electrician. Wisconsin rules also allow a licensed Journeyman Electrician or registered Electrical Apprentice to perform electrical wiring activities under the general supervision of a licensed Residential Master Electrician when the work is associated with dwellings, dwelling units, and detached accessory buildings or structures serving those dwellings or dwelling units.

The Wisconsin Journeyman Electrician license is different from the Wisconsin Master Electrician credential. A master-level credential carries a different role in supervision, work authority, and professional responsibility. Candidates planning to advance beyond the journeyman level should understand how journeyman licensure fits into Wisconsin’s broader electrical licensing structure.

Wisconsin candidates should also pay attention to renewal and continuing education requirements. Electrical credentials must be maintained according to DSPS renewal rules. License holders should keep their credential active, complete required continuing education when applicable, and stay current with updates to Wisconsin rules and electrical code requirements.

Study materials help candidates prepare, but licensing decisions are made by the Wisconsin Department of Safety and Professional Services based on the applicant’s qualifications, documentation, examination result, and compliance with Wisconsin requirements.
