## Open Book Test

The Wisconsin Journeyman Electrician exam is an open book test. DSPS allows reference materials under specific rules. Printed notes are allowed when they are three-hole punched and placed in a binder. Printed code may only be brought into the exam when bound together in a three-ring binder. Practice exams and previous exams do not qualify as notes and are not allowed.

Tabs are acceptable when they come with a bound code book. Three-ring paper dividers with tabs are permitted. Loose papers, removable tabs, sticky notes, and paperclips are not allowed. DSPS and Pearson exam locations only allow the listed reference items into the exam, including the Wisconsin administrative code materials, printed notes, the accepted NEC or Handbook, and up to two printed, bound reference books.

Open book testing rewards candidates who know how to use their references. A candidate who has not practiced with the code book may lose time searching for basic topics. Strong preparation includes learning the NEC layout, becoming familiar with the index, reviewing definitions, finding common tables, and practicing with article sections that appear often in electrical work. Wisconsin-specific materials should also be studied because state administrative code rules are part of the exam reference structure.

## Licensing Steps

Wisconsin journeyman electrician licensing is handled by the Wisconsin Department of Safety and Professional Services. A candidate may obtain a Wisconsin Journeyman Electrician credential by meeting the state’s apprenticeship or experience requirements and passing the Journeyman Electrician license examination, or through a qualifying reciprocity pathway.

One qualification path is completion of an electrical construction apprenticeship program recognized under Wisconsin Statutes Chapter 106 or the Federal Department of Labor. Applicants using this pathway provide documentation showing completion of the recognized electrical construction apprenticeship program. This path is commonly used by candidates who have completed formal apprenticeship training and are ready to move from apprentice or registered status toward journeyman licensure.

Another qualification path is experience in installing, repairing, and maintaining electrical wiring during a period of at least 48 months, with at least 8,000 hours of experience over that period, or at least 1,000 hours per year for at least five years. Wisconsin also allows qualifying education credit for full-time semesters in an electrical-related program at an accredited college, university, technical school, or vocational school. Each full-time semester may count as 500 hours of experience, with limits on the amount of education credit that may be applied.

Wisconsin also has a reciprocity pathway for applicants who hold a valid, unexpired Journeyman Electrician license acquired through a state examination in Iowa or New Hampshire. Reciprocity applicants must meet the specific conditions listed by DSPS for the state where the license was issued.

A practical licensing path includes organizing apprenticeship or experience records, submitting the required application through the DSPS licensing process, receiving eligibility to schedule the exam, preparing for the open book examination, passing the exam with the required score, and completing any remaining credential fee or licensing steps required by DSPS. Candidates should keep documentation organized, including apprenticeship completion records, experience records, transcripts, and reciprocity documentation when applicable.

## State Requirements

Wisconsin law requires individuals who install, repair, or maintain electrical wiring to be licensed as electricians by DSPS or enrolled as registered electricians. The Journeyman Electrician license allows a person to perform electrical wiring activities under the general supervision of a licensed Master Electrician or Registered Master Electrician. For wiring associated with dwellings, dwelling units, and detached accessory buildings and structures serving those dwellings or dwelling units, a licensed Journeyman Electrician or registered Electrical Apprentice may perform wiring activities under the general supervision of a licensed Residential Master Electrician.

Wisconsin’s licensing structure distinguishes between different electrical credentials and scopes of work. Journeyman Electrician licensing is separate from master electrician licensing, residential electrician licensing, registered electrician status, and other electrical credential categories. Candidates preparing for the Wisconsin Journeyman Electrician exam should make sure their study plan matches the journeyman electrician credential rather than a residential, industrial, or master-level credential.

The Wisconsin Journeyman Electrician credential requires proof of qualifying apprenticeship, experience, education credit, examination, or reciprocity. Applicants using experience must document work involving electrical wiring installation, repair, and maintenance. Applicants using education credit must provide appropriate transcripts when applicable. Applicants using a recognized apprenticeship must provide proof of completion.

Because the exam is open book and includes Wisconsin-specific reference materials, candidates should study both the National Electrical Code and Wisconsin administrative code provisions. Wisconsin Administrative Code SPS 305 relates to licenses, certifications, and registrations, while SPS 316 relates to electrical standards. A balanced study plan should include national code topics, Wisconsin code provisions, trade terminology, electrical theory, and calculation-based practice.
