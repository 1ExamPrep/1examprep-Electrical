Exam Details

The Wisconsin Journeyman Electrician license is regulated by the Wisconsin Department of Safety and Professional Services. Wisconsin uses Pearson VUE to administer DSPS trades examinations. Candidates must receive exam approval from DSPS through the LicensE system before scheduling the examination with Pearson VUE. After approval is sent to Pearson VUE, the candidate receives authorization information and can make an exam reservation.

The Wisconsin Journeyman Electrician exam is open book, and the passing score is 70%. The exam is part of the DSPS trades examination program, and candidates receive official score information after completing the computer-based exam. A passing result is used as part of the licensing process, but candidates must still follow the remaining DSPS license application instructions after passing.

Wisconsin’s DSPS Journeyman Electrician exam information identifies the permitted exam references for the journeyman electrician exam. The reference rules include Wisconsin Administrative Code materials, the National Electrical Code, and up to two printed, bound reference books. Printed notes are allowed only when three-hole punched and placed in a binder. Practice exams and previous exams are not allowed.

Because Wisconsin’s official exam reference information has listed the 2017 National Electrical Code as an allowed exam-room reference, candidates should match their exam-day materials to the current DSPS and Pearson VUE instructions for the exam they are scheduled to take. Open Book Test

The Wisconsin Journeyman Electrician exam is open book. Open-book testing does not mean the exam is simple. It means candidates must know how to use references quickly, accurately, and within the rules of the testing program. A candidate who has not practiced finding code sections, using tables, reading definitions, and applying exceptions can lose valuable time during the exam.

Wisconsin’s DSPS exam information allows printed notes when they are three-hole punched and placed in a binder. Printed code can be brought into the exam only when bound together in a three-ring binder. Tabs are acceptable when they come with a bound code book, and three-ring paper dividers with tabs are permitted. Loose papers, removable tabs, sticky notes, and paperclips are not allowed. Practice exams and previous exams are not allowed as notes.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs included in this combo helps candidates build code-navigation skill during preparation. Students should practice moving through the NEC in a clean, organized way, locating articles, reviewing tables, reading definitions, and applying rules to realistic questions. The goal is to create a repeatable process: read the question carefully, identify the topic, locate the correct rule, apply the requirement, and choose the best answer.

The Wisconsin Journeyman Electrician Study Guide, Electrician Calculations Study Guide, flash cards, and online test success kit support open-book preparation from different angles. Some questions require code lookup. Others require trade knowledge, calculation setup, or the ability to apply a rule correctly. A strong preparation plan combines reading, lookup practice, calculation drills, and repeated question review.

Licensing Steps

Wisconsin journeyman electrician candidates begin by reviewing the current DSPS licensing requirements and using Wisconsin’s LicensE system for the examination and license process. Candidates must request exam approval through LicensE. DSPS sends exam eligibility confirmation to Pearson VUE, and Pearson VUE then sends the candidate authorization information with scheduling instructions.

After receiving authorization, candidates schedule the Wisconsin Journeyman Electrician exam with Pearson VUE. Reservations are made online or by phone, and candidates must schedule in advance. Walk-in examinations are not available. Candidates should arrive at the test center with the required identification and any permitted exam materials that comply with the current DSPS and Pearson VUE rules.

After the exam, candidates receive their official score information. Passing results are valid for one year. After passing, candidates must wait two days before submitting the license application through LicensE. Candidates who do not pass must obtain new exam eligibility from Wisconsin DSPS before scheduling a retake.

State Requirements

Wisconsin electrical licensing is administered by the Department of Safety and Professional Services. Wisconsin law provides that a person may not install, repair, or maintain electrical wiring unless the person is licensed as an electrician by the Department or enrolled as a registered electrician by the Department. The journeyman electrician credential is part of Wisconsin’s regulated electrical licensing structure.

Under Wisconsin’s administrative rules, a licensed Journeyman Electrician or a registered Electrical Apprentice performs electrical wiring activities under the general supervision of a licensed Master Electrician or Registered Master Electrician. A licensed Journeyman Electrician or registered Electrical Apprentice may also perform certain dwelling-related wiring activities under the general supervision of a licensed Residential Master Electrician when the work is associated with dwellings, dwelling units, and detached accessory buildings or structures serving those dwellings.

Candidates pursuing the Wisconsin Journeyman Electrician credential should focus on the individual trades license process and the exam requirements for the journeyman electrician classification. The licensing process is handled through DSPS, while Pearson VUE administers the trades examination program. Proper application submission, exam authorization, scheduling, and post-exam licensing steps are all part of the path.

Because electrician licensing supports public safety, candidates should be prepared to demonstrate knowledge of safe electrical installation practices, code requirements, and trade principles. Study should include national code navigation, Wisconsin administrative code review, electrical theory, calculations, wiring methods, conductor sizing, grounding and bonding, services, feeders, branch circuits, motors, equipment, and general jobsite safety concepts.
