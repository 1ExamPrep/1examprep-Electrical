Exam Details

Wisconsin Master Electrician licensing is administered by the Wisconsin Department of Safety and Professional Services. DSPS regulates trades credentials and provides exam information for the Master Electrician license. The Wisconsin Master Electrician exam is open book, and DSPS lists the passing score as 70%.

Wisconsin law provides several qualification pathways for a master electrician applicant. An applicant may qualify with a bachelor’s degree or master’s degree in electrical engineering followed by passage of the required examination. Another pathway is 12 months of experience installing, repairing, and maintaining electrical wiring while licensed as a journeyman electrician, followed by passage of the required examination. A third pathway is experience installing, repairing, and maintaining electrical wiring during a period of not less than 60 months, with at least 10,000 hours of experience over that period, followed by passage of the required examination.

DSPS states that no person may install, repair, or maintain electrical wiring unless licensed as an electrician by the department or enrolled as a registered electrician by the department. A licensed Master Electrician who is responsible for the installation, repair, or maintenance of electrical wiring must use appropriately licensed or registered individuals to construct, install, repair, or maintain the electrical wiring.

The Master Electrician exam is intended to measure the knowledge needed to perform and supervise electrical work in compliance with Wisconsin requirements. Preparation should include National Electrical Code topics, electrical theory, wiring methods, services, feeders, branch circuits, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, conductor sizing, motor requirements, equipment rules, calculations, and Wisconsin administrative code material related to electrical work.

DSPS exam materials currently list specific reference rules for the Master Electrician exam. The department’s exam page lists Wisconsin Administrative Code SPS 305, Wisconsin Administrative Code SPS 316, printed notes placed in one binder, the listed NEC reference, and up to two printed, bound reference books. This product includes the National Electrical Code 2026 paperback for students who want current-code study materials as part of their preparation package.

Students preparing with this combo should use the Wisconsin Master Electrician Study Guide to organize the larger subject areas, the Electrician Calculations Study Guide to practice math-based questions, and the NEC 2026 paperback to develop code familiarity. Even when an exam is open book, preparation is strongest when the student already knows where common subjects are located and how to move through the code efficiently.

Open Book Test

The Wisconsin Master Electrician exam is open book. DSPS states that reference materials are permitted according to the list provided on the profession’s exam information page. The department also states that printed notes are allowed for all exams when they are three-hole punched and placed in the binder.

DSPS reference rules state that printed code can only be brought into the exam if it is bound together in a three-ring binder. Practice exams and previous exams do not qualify as notes and are not allowed. Tabs are acceptable if they come with a bound code book, and three-ring paper dividers with tabs are permitted. Items that are not allowed include loose papers, removable tabs, sticky notes, and paperclips.

Open book does not mean simple. Master electrician exams still require strong reading skills, code interpretation, and accurate calculations. Candidates must be able to recognize the subject of a question, identify the correct code area, apply the rule properly, and manage time. A candidate who spends too long searching for every answer can lose valuable time, even with references available.

The study guides in this combo are intended for preparation before the exam. They help students build familiarity with common exam topics, practice calculation steps, and develop a stronger study routine. Exam-room acceptance of any specific reference material is controlled by DSPS and the current exam instructions for the credential being tested.

Licensing Steps

The Wisconsin Master Electrician licensing path begins with meeting one of the qualification options established for master electrician applicants. Candidates should confirm that their education, journeyman experience, or electrical work experience satisfies the pathway they intend to use.

After meeting the qualification basis, the applicant submits the Master Electrician license application through the Wisconsin Department of Safety and Professional Services process. DSPS reviews the application and related information for the credential. Once the candidate is approved for examination, the candidate follows the scheduling instructions provided through the Wisconsin trades examination process.

On exam day, the candidate must bring the required identification, exam confirmation information, permitted reference materials, and an allowed calculator. DSPS states that no programmable calculators or cell phone calculators are allowed. Candidates taking the examination at DSPS locations receive rules and time requirements from the proctors, and examinees receive a 15-minute warning before the end of the exam.

After the exam, DSPS states that candidates receive an exam result letter by mail or email within 21 calendar days of the exam date. Candidates who do not pass receive instructions in the result letter for scheduling an exam retake through their LicensE account. Only one exam session at a time may be scheduled for each credential type.

A practical study plan should begin well before the exam date. Students can review the license requirements, gather approved study materials, review the Wisconsin Master Electrician Study Guide, practice calculations, and use the NEC paperback to build reference navigation skills. A steady schedule usually works better than last-minute review because master electrician topics cover a wide range of electrical knowledge.

State Requirements

Wisconsin master electrician licensing is tied to the state’s regulation of electrical wiring work. Wisconsin law requires individuals who install, repair, or maintain electrical wiring to be properly licensed or registered, unless an exception applies. The Master Electrician credential carries responsibility because the licensed individual may be responsible for the installation, repair, or maintenance of electrical wiring and must use properly licensed or registered individuals for the work.

For certain installations requiring a uniform building permit, Wisconsin rules state that a Master Electrician may not commence installation of electrical wiring until a permit is issued for the installation. This reinforces the importance of understanding both electrical code requirements and state regulatory responsibilities.

Wisconsin’s Master Electrician qualification pathways include electrical engineering education followed by examination, journeyman electrician experience followed by examination, or a longer practical experience pathway followed by examination. These routes recognize that candidates may reach master-level preparation through different professional backgrounds.

For exam preparation, students should treat Wisconsin requirements and NEC knowledge as connected subjects. A master electrician must be able to understand electrical installation rules, follow state requirements, supervise work responsibly, and apply code requirements to real electrical systems.
