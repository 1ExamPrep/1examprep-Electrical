Exam Details

Wisconsin electrician licensing is handled through the Wisconsin Department of Safety and Professional Services. DSPS issues credentials for master electricians, journeyman electricians, registered electricians, electrical contractors, residential electrician categories, and related trade credentials. Candidates preparing for the Wisconsin Master Electrician license should understand the state’s experience requirements, exam reference rules, license application process, continuing education requirements, and the relationship between individual licensure and electrical contracting.

The Wisconsin Master Electrician examination is a state licensing exam connected with master-level electrical responsibility. Master electrician preparation generally includes NEC knowledge, Wisconsin Administrative Code SPS 305 licensing requirements, Wisconsin Administrative Code SPS 316 electrical standards, electrical calculations, services, feeders, branch circuits, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, conductors, raceways, boxes, equipment installation, motors, transformers, special occupancies, safety, and practical electrical application.

Wisconsin DSPS identifies the master electrician exam as open book and lists a 70 percent passing score. The DSPS master electrician exam reference information identifies three categories of materials allowed into the exam: one binder containing Wisconsin Administrative Code SPS 305, Wisconsin Administrative Code SPS 316, and printed notes; the National Electrical Code book or NEC Handbook edition identified by DSPS; and up to two printed, bound reference books such as electrical reference or code-navigation books. Candidates should use the current DSPS exam information for the exact reference edition and allowed materials for the exam date they schedule.

The Wisconsin master electrician exam is not only a code lookup exercise. Candidates should be prepared to apply NEC requirements, understand Wisconsin-specific rules, solve calculations, interpret common electrical installation requirements, and recognize how licensing standards affect master-level responsibility. Wisconsin candidates should also understand the difference between a Master Electrician license and an Electrical Contractor license. A master electrician is an individual credential connected with responsibility for electrical wiring work and proper use of licensed or registered workers. An electrical contractor license applies to the business or entity performing electrical contracting work and carries its own responsibilities. Candidates planning to operate a business should review the contractor credential in addition to the individual master electrician license.

Open Book Test

The Wisconsin Master Electrician examination is an open book examination. Open book testing does not make the exam easy. It means candidates must know how to use approved references efficiently, read code language carefully, understand table notes and exceptions, and apply requirements under exam pressure.

Wisconsin DSPS reference rules allow printed notes if they are three-hole punched and placed in the approved binder. Printed code material may be brought into the exam only if it is bound together in a three-ring binder. Practice exams and previous exams do not qualify as notes and are not allowed. Tabs are acceptable when they come with a bound code book, and three-ring paper dividers with tabs are permitted. Loose papers, removable tabs, sticky notes, and paperclips are not allowed.

The Wisconsin DSPS master electrician exam reference list identifies Wisconsin Administrative Code SPS 305, Wisconsin Administrative Code SPS 316, printed notes, the National Electrical Code book or NEC Handbook edition identified by DSPS, and up to two printed, bound reference books. Candidates should prepare references before exam day, organize the binder carefully, and avoid bringing unapproved materials into the exam room.

Licensing Steps

A practical Wisconsin licensing path begins by confirming that the Master Electrician license is the correct credential for the candidate’s goal. A master electrician license is connected with higher-level responsibility for the installation, repair, or maintenance of electrical wiring. Candidates who plan to supervise work, take responsibility for electrical wiring activity, or support electrical contractor operations should understand the master electrician credential and the related electrical contractor credential.

The next step is reviewing the Wisconsin DSPS master electrician application requirements. Wisconsin identifies qualifying experience for the Master Electrician license as experience in installing, repairing, and maintaining electrical wiring during a period of at least 60 months with at least 10,000 hours of experience over that period, or at least 1,000 hours per year for at least seven years. Education in an electrical-related program may be credited toward part of the required experience under DSPS rules, with semester-based credit limits applied by the state.

Candidates should prepare the required application materials and experience documentation through the Wisconsin DSPS process. Documentation may include the Master Electrician License Application Information form, the experience table, transcripts when education credit is claimed, and any additional forms required by DSPS. If DSPS requests additional documents, candidates should provide the requested materials within the required timeframe to keep the application moving.

After the application and experience documentation are submitted, the candidate follows the Wisconsin DSPS examination process. Candidates should review the current exam information, approved reference materials, exam location options, identification rules, reference preparation rules, and passing score requirements. Wisconsin identifies the Master Electrician exam as open book with a 70 percent passing score, so candidates should prepare both the reference materials and the knowledge needed to use them efficiently.

After passing the required examination and satisfying the remaining DSPS requirements, the candidate completes the license issuance process. Once licensed, a Wisconsin master electrician should maintain the credential through timely renewal and required continuing education. Candidates who plan to perform electrical contracting work should also review Wisconsin electrical contractor licensing requirements, because the individual master electrician license and the contractor license are different credentials.

State Requirements

Wisconsin regulates electrician licensing through the Wisconsin Department of Safety and Professional Services. DSPS administers trade credentials, application forms, exam information, renewal information, administrative code requirements, and continuing education rules for licensed electricians and electrical contractors.

Wisconsin’s master electrician application requirements focus on qualifying experience and examination. The DSPS application information identifies experience in installing, repairing, and maintaining electrical wiring during a period of at least 60 months with at least 10,000 hours of experience, or at least 1,000 hours per year for at least seven years. A Wisconsin master electrician who is responsible for the installation, repair, or maintenance of electrical wiring must utilize appropriately licensed or registered individuals to construct, install, repair, or maintain electrical wiring. Wisconsin also regulates electrical contractors. A licensed electrical contractor must use appropriately licensed or registered individuals for electrical wiring work, and electrical installations that require permits must follow the applicable Wisconsin Administrative Code and local requirements. A master electrician license supports individual competency and responsibility, while the contractor license supports the business side of electrical contracting.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Wisconsin Master Electrician study plan should include NEC study, calculation practice, Wisconsin Administrative Code review, trade knowledge review, reference navigation, recall practice, and timed question work. Electrical exams can cover a wide range of subjects, so candidates should avoid spending all study time on only one topic. A balanced routine helps build confidence across services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, conductors, wiring methods, raceways, boxes, motors, transformers, special occupancies, special equipment, safety, licensing rules, and calculations.

For NEC study, start by learning the structure of the code book. Review article organization, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and cross-references. Practice moving from a question to the correct article and reading the surrounding language carefully. Many electrical exam questions are missed because a candidate finds a related section but overlooks a condition, exception, table note, or specific wording.

For Wisconsin-specific preparation, review Wisconsin Administrative Code SPS 305 and SPS 316 topics connected with electrical licensing, credential responsibilities, state electrical standards, and Wisconsin amendments or state requirements. The Wisconsin exam reference list includes these administrative code chapters, so candidates should treat state code review as an important part of the preparation process.

For calculations, write out each step. Electrical calculation mistakes often happen when a candidate skips a conversion, uses the wrong table, applies the wrong value, or confuses branch circuit, feeder, service, motor, transformer, raceway fill, box fill, voltage drop, or overcurrent protection requirements. The 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide gives candidates a dedicated way to practice these skills repeatedly.

For open book preparation, use the NEC and Wisconsin references actively during study. Practice locating sections, reading tables, and confirming answers in the reference material. Then practice some questions without immediately opening the book so you can build recognition. The stronger your recall becomes, the more time you can save during questions that require deeper lookup.

For flash card study, use short sessions throughout the week. Flash cards can help reinforce formulas, definitions, code concepts, licensing terms, calculation cues, and common electrical terminology. They are useful before work, during breaks, after a longer study block, or as final review. Flash cards should not replace full study sessions, but they help strengthen recall and recognition.
