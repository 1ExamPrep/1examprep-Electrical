Exam Details

Tennessee electrical examinations are connected to the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors. Contractor applicants are required to take the Tennessee Business and Law exam, and a trade exam is required for certain license classifications, including the CE Electrical Contractor classification. Beginning July 1, 2026, Prov begins administering exams for the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors, while existing PSI appointments scheduled during the transition may continue under the state’s posted transition rules.

The Tennessee CE Electrical Contractor exam is an open book trade examination. The current PSI contractor bulletin lists the CE Electrical Contractor trade exam with 100 questions, a required passing score of 73%, and 260 minutes of testing time. The exam outline includes General Knowledge and Electrical Installation Requirements, Building Code Requirements, Services, Feeders, and Branch Circuits, Overcurrent Protection, Grounding and Bonding, Conductors and Cables, Raceways and Boxes, Hazardous Locations, Special Occupancies, and Special Equipment, Low Voltage, Alarms, Signaling Systems, and Communications, Lighting, Signs and General Use Equipment, OSHA and NFPA 70E Safety, and Motors, Transformers, and Generators.

Tennessee also has the Limited Licensed Electrician exam for individuals performing covered electrical work in municipalities that use the Division of Fire Prevention for permits or inspections. The LLE license applies to work less than $25,000 per project and is issued to individuals who pass the LLE trade exam. The current LLE bulletin lists the exam with 40 questions, a required passing score of 72.5%, and 130 minutes of testing time.

The LLE exam outline includes general knowledge and electrical installation requirements, services, feeders, branch circuits, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, hazardous locations, special occupancies, special equipment, low-voltage systems, alarms, signaling systems, communications, lighting, signs, and general-use equipment. Students preparing for Tennessee electrical work should understand whether the CE Electrical Contractor classification or LLE license fits the work they plan to perform.

Open Book Test

The Tennessee CE Electrical Contractor exam is an open book test. The current exam bulletin identifies approved references and states that candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references to the examination center. Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the exam, but references may not be written in. Permanent tabs are allowed. Temporary tabs, including Post-it notes, are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins.

The Tennessee LLE exam is also an open book test. The LLE bulletin identifies approved references and the same type of reference rules: candidates bring their own approved materials, may highlight, underline, or index references before the exam, and may not write in references. Candidates may not bring loose or attached additional papers with approved references.

Open book testing still requires serious preparation. The NEC is large, detailed, and technical. Students who have not practiced with the code book may lose time searching for basic sections, using the wrong table, overlooking an exception, or missing an article-specific requirement. The strongest candidates understand how to move from a question topic to the correct article, read the code language carefully, check the proper table, and apply the rule accurately.

Licensing Steps

The Tennessee electrical licensing path begins with identifying the correct credential. A person or business contracting for projects of $25,000 or more generally needs a Tennessee contractor license before bidding, negotiating, or contracting. Electrical contracting falls under the CE Electrical Contractor classification. Smaller electrical work may fall under the LLE license in municipalities that use the Division of Fire Prevention for permits or inspections.

For the CE Electrical Contractor classification, contractor applicants follow the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors process. Contractor applicants are required to take the Tennessee Business and Law exam, and the CE Electrical Contractor trade exam is required for the electrical classification unless a valid trade exam waiver or accepted exam route applies. The qualifying agent is the person designated to take the exam for the entity seeking licensure and may be an owner, full-time employee, or officer.

After the testing requirement is met, the contractor application process continues through the Board. Contractor applicants complete the required application, submit financial information, provide required insurance information, identify the classification and monetary limit, and satisfy all state licensing requirements. Exam scores are typically valid for two years by the Board.

For the Limited Licensed Electrician license, the applicant completes the Initial Limited Licensed Electrician application through Tennessee’s online licensing system, provides photo identification and Social Security number information, pays the required application fee, and passes the LLE trade exam. Tennessee law states that a person may not act as a contractor with only the LLE license.

After passing the required exam and completing the licensing process, the licensee must continue to follow Tennessee licensing rules, permit requirements, inspection requirements, local jurisdiction rules, renewal requirements, and the scope of work authorized by the credential held.

Test Information and Study Materials

Tennessee electrical exam preparation should begin with the license category. Students preparing for the CE Electrical Contractor exam should focus on broader contractor-level electrical topics, building code requirements, safety, motors, transformers, generators, low-voltage systems, communication systems, special occupancies, and contractor responsibilities. Students preparing for the LLE exam should emphasize practical electrical installation requirements and NEC-based limited electrical work.

NEC study should include Article 90, Article 100 definitions, Article 110 general requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, communication systems, and Chapter 9 tables. Students should learn where topics are located and how the code book is organized before the exam.

Important areas include grounding electrode systems, grounding electrode conductor sizing, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, service bonding, separately derived systems, bonding of raceways and enclosures, and special grounding rules.

Wiring methods and materials are central to Tennessee electrical exam preparation. Students should review raceways, cables, boxes, cabinets, fittings, conduit fill, box fill, conductor protection, support rules, wet-location requirements, underground installations, pull boxes, junction boxes, cable assemblies, and Chapter 9 table use.
