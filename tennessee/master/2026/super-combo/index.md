## Exam Details

Tennessee contractor licensing is handled through the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors under the Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance. Electrical contractor candidates commonly prepare for the CE – Electrical Contractor classification. Tennessee contractor applicants are required to take the Tennessee Business and Law exam, and a trade exam is required for certain license classifications, including Electrical.

The Tennessee CE – Electrical Contractor examination is listed with 100 questions, a required passing score of 73 percent, which equals 73 correct items, and 260 minutes of testing time. The examination may also include a small number of experimental questions that are not scored but do count against the allowed testing time. Candidates should prepare for both technical accuracy and time management.

The CE – Electrical Contractor content outline includes General Knowledge and Electrical Installation Requirements, Building Code Requirements, Services, Feeders, and Branch Circuits, Overcurrent Protection, Grounding and Bonding, Conductors and Cables, Raceways and Boxes, Hazardous Locations, Special Occupancies, and Special Equipment, Low Voltage, Alarms, Signaling Systems, and Communications, Lighting, Signs and General Use Equipment, OSHA and NFPA 70E Safety, and Motors, Transformers, and Generators.

Tennessee also requires the Business and Law Management examination for contractor licensing. The Business and Law Management exam is listed with 50 questions, a required passing score of 73 percent, which equals 37 correct items, and 140 minutes of testing time. Its content outline includes licensing requirements, estimating and bidding, business organization and financial management, tax laws, labor laws, project management and lien law, contracts, risk management, and environmental and safety topics.

The same person is not required to take both the Business and Law exam and the trade exam for a contractor license application. Tennessee allows the qualifying agent process to be used for the entity obtaining the license, and up to two people may take each exam for the company. The qualifying agent may be an owner, full-time employee, or officer, with additional authorization required when the person does not have ownership.

## Open Book Test

The Tennessee CE – Electrical Contractor examination is identified as an open book examination. Open book testing does not make the exam easy. It means candidates must know how to use approved references efficiently, read code language carefully, understand table notes and exceptions, and apply requirements under time pressure.

Reference rules are strict. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references to the examination center. Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination session, but references may not be written in. Candidates are not permitted to bring additional loose or attached papers with approved references. References may be tabbed or indexed with permanent tabs only. Temporary tabs, including Post-it style tabs, are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins. Scratch paper is provided for calculations, and written material may not be removed from the examination room.

The Tennessee CE – Electrical Contractor reference list includes the National Electrical Code or National Electrical Code Handbook, NFPA 70E or NFPA 70E Handbook, International Building Code, OSHA construction regulations, and Ugly’s Electrical References. For code questions, the exam is based only on the code edition listed in the exam bulletin for the scheduled exam. Candidates should align final exam-room preparation with the current Tennessee exam provider instructions and the code edition listed for their examination date.

The Tennessee Business and Law Management examination is also identified as open book. The listed reference for that exam is the Contractors’ Guide to Business, Law and Project Management – Tennessee Edition. Business and law candidates must also follow reference rules for highlighting, underlining, indexing, permanent tabs, written notes, and loose or attached papers.

## Licensing Steps

A practical Tennessee licensing path begins by confirming the correct license classification and project scope. Tennessee contractor licensing applies to many types of work, including electrical work. The CE – Electrical Contractor classification is the key statewide contractor classification for electrical contracting. Tennessee also has a Limited Licensed Electrician program, but Tennessee law states that a person may not act as a contractor with the LLE license.

The contractor licensing process begins with examination planning. Contractor applicants are required to take the Tennessee Business and Law exam, and the trade exam is required for classifications that require technical testing. Electrical candidates preparing for the CE classification should plan for both the electrical trade exam and the Business and Law exam unless they qualify for an accepted waiver route.

After the required exam path is identified, the qualifying agent registers for the applicable examinations through the current exam provider. Beginning July 1, 2026, new contractor exam scheduling is handled through Prov, while PSI continues to administer previously scheduled appointments during the transition period. Candidates who are already scheduled with PSI for an exam date after July 1 may keep the existing PSI appointment or schedule through Prov instead.

After passing the required examinations, the applicant completes the Tennessee contractor license application process through the Board. Tennessee contractor licensing involves the business entity as well as the qualifying agent. The qualifying agent is the person designated to take the examination for the entity obtaining the license and may be an owner, full-time employee, or officer. If the qualifying agent does not have ownership, the Power of Attorney form is used as part of the process.

Applicants should be prepared to complete the Board’s application requirements, provide the required business and financial documentation, select the proper classification, identify the qualifying agent, and follow the Board’s instructions for license issuance. Tennessee also has trade exam waiver agreements with several states for contractor applicants who are actively licensed in participating jurisdictions, but a license from another state does not by itself authorize operation in Tennessee.

The study portion of the licensing path should be treated as a structured project. Candidates should review the content outline, study the 2026 NEC, practice calculations, work through electrical trade topics, review business and law subjects, and use flash cards for repeated reinforcement. Consistent preparation helps candidates approach Tennessee electrical contractor and master-level electrical study with more organization and confidence.

## State Requirements

Tennessee regulates contractor licensing through the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors. The Board’s mission is connected with quality construction practices and the protection of public safety and welfare. Electrical contractor applicants commonly pursue the CE – Electrical Contractor classification when the scope of work requires state contractor licensing.

Tennessee contractor applicants must satisfy the examination and application process for the classification requested. The Business and Law exam is required for contractor applicants, and a trade exam is required for certain classifications, including Electrical. Tennessee also accepts the NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors for certain electrical contractor licensing purposes, and candidates using NASCLA should still complete the Tennessee licensing process required by the Board.

Tennessee’s licensing structure separates contractor authority from limited electrician licensing. The Limited Licensed Electrician program is administered through the Board’s Limited Licensed Electrician Division, but the LLE license does not authorize a person to act as a contractor. Candidates should choose the licensing path that matches the value, scope, and legal responsibility of the work they plan to perform.

Tennessee also maintains trade exam waiver agreements with several states for contractor applicants. These agreements apply only to contractors licensed with the Board and do not apply to other license programs such as Home Improvement, LLE, or LLP. An out-of-state license does not allow a contractor to operate in Tennessee unless the Tennessee licensing process is completed.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Tennessee electrical contractor study plan should include NEC study, calculation practice, trade knowledge review, business and law review, safety review, reference navigation, and timed question work. Electrical exams can cover a wide range of subjects, so candidates should avoid spending all study time on only one topic. A balanced routine helps build confidence across installation requirements, building code topics, services, feeders, branch circuits, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, conductors, cables, raceways, boxes, hazardous locations, special occupancies, low voltage, alarms, signaling systems, communications, lighting, signs, safety, motors, transformers, generators, and calculations.

For NEC study, start by learning the structure of the code book. Review article organization, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and cross-references. Practice moving from a question to the correct article and reading the surrounding language carefully. Many electrical exam questions are missed because a candidate finds a related section but overlooks a condition, exception, table note, or specific wording.

For calculations, write out each step. Electrical calculation mistakes often happen when a candidate skips a conversion, uses the wrong table, applies the wrong value, or confuses branch circuit, feeder, service, motor, transformer, generator, raceway fill, box fill, voltage drop, or overcurrent protection requirements. The 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide gives candidates a dedicated way to practice these skills repeatedly.

For safety preparation, review OSHA construction safety topics and NFPA 70E workplace electrical safety concepts. The Tennessee CE – Electrical Contractor content outline includes OSHA and NFPA 70E safety, so candidates should include safety review in the study schedule instead of focusing only on NEC lookup questions.

For business and law preparation, review licensing requirements, estimating and bidding, business organization, financial management, tax laws, labor laws, project management, lien law, contracts, risk management, environmental topics, and safety. Contractor licensing requires both trade competence and business responsibility, so the Business and Law exam deserves its own study time.

For open book preparation, use the NEC actively during study. Practice locating sections, reading tables, and confirming answers in the code book. Then practice some questions without immediately opening the code book so you can build recognition. The stronger your recall becomes, the more time you can save during questions that require deeper code lookup.

For flash card study, use short sessions throughout the week. Flash cards can help reinforce formulas, definitions, code concepts, safety topics, and common electrical terms. They are useful before work, during breaks, after a longer study block, or as final review. Flash cards should not replace full study sessions, but they help strengthen recall and recognition.
