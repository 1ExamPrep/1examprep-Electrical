Exam Details

Tennessee electrical exam details depend on the credential being pursued. The Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors administers the state Limited Licensed Electrician license through PSI. The LLE license applies to electricians performing work in a municipality using the Division of Fire Prevention for permits or inspections and covers work less than $25,000 per project. Tennessee state materials also instruct applicants to check with the local government first because local governments may accept the LLE license or may require a local license for permits and inspections.

The Tennessee Limited Licensed Electrician examination is administered by PSI. The candidate information bulletin identifies the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors, Limited Licensed Electrician Division, as the licensing authority connected to the exam program. PSI provides computer-based examinations through examination centers, and candidates schedule directly through PSI after meeting the applicable licensing and exam requirements.

For larger electrical contracting work, Tennessee has electrical contractor classifications through the Board for Licensing Contractors. The CE Electrical Contractor exam is separate from journeyman-level or LLE preparation. Current PSI contractor exam information lists the CE Electrical Contractor trade examination as 100 questions with 260 minutes allowed and a required passing score of 73%. That contractor-level exam includes general knowledge and electrical installation requirements, building code requirements, services, feeders, branch circuits, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, hazardous locations, low voltage, lighting, OSHA and NFPA 70E safety, motors, transformers, and generators.

Local journeyman electrician exams in Tennessee may vary by city or county. Some local jurisdictions use their own requirements for journeyman or master electrician licensing, while others may rely on state credentials, local permits, or local registration rules. Candidates preparing for a Tennessee journeyman electrician credential should confirm the exact exam title, application requirements, reference list, code edition, passing score, question count, time limit, and renewal rules with the local licensing authority.

Journeyman-level preparation should include general electrical knowledge, services, service equipment, feeders, branch circuits, conductors, wiring methods and materials, raceways, boxes, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, equipment for general use, motors, transformers, control devices, special occupancies, special equipment, safety, and electrical calculations.

Open Book Test

Tennessee electrical exams administered through PSI are open book when the current PSI bulletin identifies the exam as open book. Candidates should follow the exact PSI and licensing authority rules for the credential they are taking, including the approved reference list, code edition, calculator rules, tab rules, highlighting rules, and prohibited materials.

For Tennessee PSI electrical exams, reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, or indexed before the examination, but they may not be written in. Candidates may not bring additional papers, whether loose or attached, with approved references. References may be tabbed or indexed with permanent tabs only. Temporary tabs, including Post-it style tabs, are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins. Scratch paper is provided for calculations, and candidates may use a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator when allowed by the exam rules.

Open book does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates still need to recognize what the question is asking, identify the code topic, locate the correct article or table, read the language carefully, apply the rule, and answer within the time limit. Slow code navigation can make an open-book electrical exam difficult, especially when calculation questions are included.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs included in this Super Combo is intended for study and code navigation practice. Tabs help candidates become familiar with major NEC articles, definitions, tables, and commonly used sections. The strongest preparation comes from using the tabs as a navigation aid while also learning how the code is organized and how common exam topics connect to specific NEC articles.

Licensing Steps

Tennessee candidates should begin by identifying the correct credential for their work goals. A local journeyman electrician license, a Tennessee Limited Licensed Electrician license, and a Tennessee Electrical Contractor license are not the same credential. The correct path depends on the type of work, project value, permitting authority, local acceptance, and whether the candidate plans to work as an individual electrician or operate as a contractor.

For the Tennessee Limited Licensed Electrician license, candidates complete the state application process through the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors and pass the LLE trade examination administered by PSI. The LLE license is issued to individuals who pass the required trade exam and meet the state application requirements. Tennessee state materials identify photo identification and Social Security number information as part of the licensing requirements, and the initial application fee is listed by the state.

Candidates planning to work under local journeyman or master electrician rules should contact the local permitting or licensing office before applying. Local governments may require documented work experience, apprenticeship completion, employer verification, an examination, local registration, continuing education, insurance, bonding, or permit-related qualifications. Requirements can differ from one Tennessee jurisdiction to another.

For electrical contractor licensing, candidates follow the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors process for the proper contractor classification. Contractor licensing is different from holding an LLE license. Tennessee state law materials explain that a person may not act as a contractor with only the LLE license. Candidates who plan to contract for electrical work should review the contractor licensing requirements, classification rules, exam requirements, and project value limits.

After identifying the correct path, candidates should gather documentation, prepare for the proper exam, schedule testing through the approved provider, take the required examination, and complete the licensing or local registration process. Passing an exam is an important step, but licensing authority comes from the state board or local jurisdiction after the full process is completed.

State Requirements

The Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors regulates Limited Licensed Electricians, electrical contractors, home improvement contractors, and related contractor licensing categories. The LLE license applies only in the situations covered by state law and local acceptance. It is used for work less than $25,000 per project in municipalities that use the Division of Fire Prevention for permits or inspections, but candidates must always check local government rules first.

Local governments in Tennessee may have their own electrician licensing requirements for projects, permits, inspections, and local work authority. A local jurisdiction may require a journeyman electrician credential, master electrician credential, electrical registration, contractor credential, or other local approval. This makes local verification important before bidding, performing work, or applying for a license.

Electrical contractor work is handled through the contractor licensing system. The contractor path is separate from local journeyman licensing and from the Limited Licensed Electrician license. Candidates planning to operate as a contractor should review the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors requirements for the correct electrical classification.

For journeyman-level preparation, candidates should be ready to demonstrate knowledge of electrical theory, National Electrical Code application, wiring methods, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors, overcurrent protection, motors, transformers, equipment, special installations, safety practices, and electrical calculations. Candidates should also understand the licensing path that applies to the work they plan to perform.

Study materials help candidates prepare, but licensing decisions are made by the applicable Tennessee board or local authority based on the applicant’s qualifications, documentation, exam results, and compliance with state or local requirements.

Code navigation should also be part of every study plan. Candidates should practice using the NEC table of contents, index, article numbers, definitions, tables, notes, and exceptions. The goal is to identify the topic quickly and move to the correct section without wasting time. A candidate who understands how the NEC is organized is better prepared for open-book electrical testing.

Services, feeders, and branch circuits should receive careful attention. Candidates should review service conductors, service disconnects, grounding at services, feeder sizing, branch circuit ratings, required outlets, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, and load calculations. These topics connect code knowledge with practical installation skill.

Grounding and bonding are important areas for journeyman exam preparation. Candidates should review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, raceway bonding, panel bonding, grounded conductors, separately derived systems, and related safety requirements. Grounding and bonding questions often require careful reading because similar terms can lead to different code requirements.

Wiring methods and materials should be studied consistently. Candidates should review conductors, cables, raceways, boxes, fittings, supports, conductor ampacity, insulation types, flexible wiring methods, underground wiring, cabinets, panelboards, switchboards, and installation conditions. Motors, transformers, controls, and equipment should also be part of the study routine. Candidates should review motor branch circuits, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, disconnecting means, controllers, transformer sizing, transformer protection, luminaires, receptacles, switches, appliances, panelboards, and related installation requirements.

Safety should be reviewed as part of Tennessee electrical preparation. Candidates should understand electrical hazards, safe work practices, grounding and bonding safety, overcurrent protection, working clearances, personal protection concepts, OSHA-related construction safety, and NFPA 70E electrical safety concepts when included in the exam outline.

Flash cards are useful for shorter study sessions. Candidates can use them to review formulas, definitions, electrical theory, safety concepts, NEC topics, and trade terms before work, after work, during breaks, or between longer study blocks. Flash cards work best when paired with deeper reading, calculation drills, and code lookup practice.

A strong study plan should include scheduled reading, NEC navigation, calculation drills, timed practice, and repeated review of weaker topics. Candidates should avoid studying only familiar material. Journeyman electrician preparation rewards steady practice, careful reading, and the ability to apply electrical knowledge under exam conditions.
