Exam Details

The Tennessee Limited Licensed Electrician examination is administered by PSI for the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors, Limited Licensed Electrician Division. The exam is computer-based and is designed to test basic electrical principles, code knowledge, and trade application. PSI provides examination scheduling, testing center administration, score reporting, and candidate instructions for the LLE exam.

The Tennessee LLE exam contains 40 questions. Candidates must answer 29 items correctly, or 72.5%, to pass. The time allowed is 130 minutes. PSI provides an introductory computer tutorial before the examination begins, and that tutorial time does not count against the examination time. Scores are displayed on screen at the end of the exam, and a score report is emailed to the candidate.

The published Tennessee LLE content outline includes general knowledge and electrical installation requirements, services, feeders, branch circuits, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, hazardous locations, special occupancies, special equipment, low voltage, alarms, signaling systems, communications, lighting, signs, and general-use equipment. These topics reflect the type of code knowledge and trade understanding candidates should be prepared to review.

For students using this 2026 Tennessee Journeyman Electrician Study Guide & National Electrical Code Combo with Tabs, the best study approach is to work through the study guide and then use the NEC to verify answers and strengthen lookup skills. Electrical exams often require careful reading. A small change in wording can shift the question from a branch-circuit rule to a feeder rule, from a grounding conductor rule to a grounding electrode conductor rule, or from a general installation requirement to an exception. Regular NEC practice helps students slow down, identify the correct topic, and locate the proper section.

Candidates should bring proper identification to the examination site and follow PSI’s check-in and security procedures. PSI allows only approved items into the examination room. Candidates may use a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator in the examination center. Scratch paper is provided for calculations, and candidates are not permitted to remove written materials from the exam room.

Open Book Test

The Tennessee Limited Licensed Electrician examination is an open book test. PSI’s bulletin states that candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references to the examination center. Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination, but they may not be written in. Candidates may not bring additional loose or attached papers with approved references.

Tabs must be permanent tabs. Temporary tabs, such as Post-it notes, are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins. This makes preparation with a properly organized code book especially important. The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs helps students build the habit of moving through the NEC quickly and consistently during study.

Open book does not mean easy. A timed electrical exam still requires knowledge, accuracy, and speed. Students need to know where to find NEC definitions, how to use tables, how to follow exceptions, and how to apply rules to real questions. Many candidates lose time because they know the answer is somewhere in the code book but do not know where to begin. A study routine that combines practice questions with active NEC lookup can reduce that problem.

Students should practice locating Article 100 definitions, service rules, feeder requirements, branch-circuit provisions, grounding and bonding sections, conductor ampacity tables, raceway and box requirements, wiring method rules, and special occupancy sections. The more familiar the NEC becomes, the more useful it is during exam preparation.

Licensing Steps

  1. Identify the correct Tennessee electrical licensing path. Tennessee’s Limited Licensed Electrician license applies to electricians performing work in jurisdictions that accept the LLE license and covers work below the project limit established for that license.
  2. Check local permit and licensing rules. Tennessee advises electricians to check with local government first because some municipalities and counties may have local licensing requirements for permits and inspections.
  3. Prepare for the trade exam. Use structured study materials, practice electrical calculations, review the exam content outline, and build NEC lookup skills with the code book.
  4. Take the PSI-administered LLE exam. The LLE trade exam is administered through PSI for the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors.
  5. Complete the online application. Applicants complete the Initial Limited Licensed Electrician application online through Tennessee’s licensing system.
  6. Submit the required application information. Tennessee lists photo identification and a Social Security number among licensing requirements for the LLE application.
  7. Follow post-exam licensing instructions. After passing the trade exam, applicants must complete the state application process and comply with all instructions from the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors.

The study guide and NEC combo supports the preparation portion of the process. It does not replace the application, local permit review, licensing approval, examination registration, identification requirements, or jurisdiction-specific requirements that may apply in Tennessee.

State Requirements

The Tennessee Limited Licensed Electrician license is issued by the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors. Tennessee states that the LLE license is only required by electricians performing work in a municipality utilizing the Division of Fire Prevention for permits or inspections and covers work less than $25,000 per project. Tennessee also directs electricians to check with local government first because the LLE license is not accepted by every local agency.

The state lists the LLE licensing requirements as photo identification, Social Security number, and passing the trade exam administered through PSI. The initial application is completed online through Tennessee’s licensing system, and Tennessee lists the initial application fee as $50.

Tennessee law states that a person may not act as a contractor with only the LLE license. Students who plan to contract for larger projects, operate as an electrical contractor, or perform work outside the scope of the LLE should review the contractor licensing requirements that apply to their business activity and project size. Electrical licensing can depend on the type of work, project amount, local acceptance, permit rules, and whether the work is performed as an individual licensee or as a contractor.

Because Tennessee has both state-level and local considerations, students should treat exam preparation and licensing compliance as separate responsibilities. This combo helps with electrical study and NEC practice, while the candidate must follow the correct licensing route for the type of work they plan to perform.
