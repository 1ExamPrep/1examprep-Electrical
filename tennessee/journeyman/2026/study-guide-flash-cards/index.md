## Exam Details

The Tennessee Limited Licensed Electrician examination is administered by PSI for the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors, Limited Licensed Electrician Division. The LLE exam is a computer-based exam administered through PSI testing centers. The exam is used for the Tennessee Limited Licensed Electrician credential and is not the same as a full electrical contractor license or every local journeyman credential issued by a city or county.

The Tennessee LLE exam is listed as 40 questions with 130 minutes allowed. The passing score is 72.5%, which equals 29 correct items. The exam content outline includes General Knowledge and Electrical Installation Requirements; Services, Feeders, and Branch Circuits; Overcurrent Protection; Grounding and Bonding; Conductors and Cables; Raceways and Boxes; Hazardous Locations, Special Occupancies, and Special Equipment; Low Voltage, Alarms, Signaling Systems, and Communications; and Lighting, Signs, and General Use Equipment.

The LLE exam is open book and uses approved references. It may also include questions based on trade knowledge or general industry practices. For code questions, the exam is based only on the code edition listed in the current PSI candidate information bulletin. Candidates should use the reference list and exam rules connected to the exam they are taking.

Tennessee local journeyman-level exams may vary by jurisdiction. Some cities or counties may use different credential names, local rules, testing providers, experience requirements, renewal rules, permit requirements, or local electrical code requirements. A candidate preparing for a local Tennessee journeyman-level exam should follow the candidate bulletin, local board instructions, or licensing office requirements for that specific jurisdiction.

## Open Book Test

The Tennessee Limited Licensed Electrician exam is an open-book examination. Open book does not mean easy. Candidates still need to understand electrical concepts, recognize how questions are written, locate information quickly, apply code rules correctly, and manage time. A student who depends only on searching through references for every answer can lose valuable time during the exam.

Open-book electrical exams reward reference familiarity. Students should practice using the National Electrical Code table of contents, index, article structure, definitions, tables, exceptions, notes, and common code sections before exam day. Students should also practice calculations repeatedly so conductor sizing, load relationships, grounding and bonding rules, motor requirements, box fill, raceway fill, voltage drop concepts, and overcurrent protection requirements become easier to recognize.

For the Tennessee LLE exam, approved reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. References may not be written in during the examination. Additional loose or attached papers are not permitted with approved references. References may be tabbed or indexed with permanent tabs only, and temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not allowed.

Candidates may use a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator in the examination center. Scratch paper is provided for calculations. Candidates should use this study guide, the 12 practice exams, the 2 full final exams, and the flash cards before test day. During the official exam, candidates should bring only the references and materials allowed by the current PSI and Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors instructions.

A strong open-book strategy includes learning where major NEC topics are located before the exam. Students should practice identifying the correct article, checking definitions first, reading table notes carefully, reviewing exceptions, and confirming whether the question asks for a minimum, maximum, permitted condition, prohibited condition, or calculation result. Flash cards help students recall key concepts faster, while practice exams help build the habit of reading questions carefully and choosing the correct reference path.

## Licensing Steps

Tennessee electrical licensing steps depend on the credential being pursued and the location where the work will be performed. Candidates should distinguish between the Tennessee Limited Licensed Electrician license, local journeyman or master electrician requirements, and Tennessee electrical contractor licensing. Each credential has a different purpose and may be tied to a different work scope, project value, jurisdiction, or permit requirement.

A typical Tennessee journeyman-level study path begins with entering the electrical trade, gaining supervised electrical work experience, learning electrical theory and code application, completing apprenticeship or trade training when required, identifying the credential needed for the jurisdiction, submitting the required application or registration, scheduling the required exam, passing the exam, and completing any final license, registration, or local credential steps.

For the Tennessee Limited Licensed Electrician pathway, applicants prepare for the LLE trade examination and follow the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors and PSI process. The LLE credential applies to individuals and is not the same as acting as a contractor. It is also important to confirm whether the municipality or local government where the work will be performed accepts the LLE license or requires a different local credential.

Electrical contractor licensing is a separate path. Tennessee contractor licensing applies to contractor-level work and is tied to the Board for Licensing Contractors. Electrical contractor applicants may need to complete a separate trade exam, business and law requirements, financial documentation, insurance or bonding requirements, and board approval depending on the classification and monetary limit involved.
