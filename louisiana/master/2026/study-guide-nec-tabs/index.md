## Open Book Test

The Louisiana Electrical Statewide examination is an **open book test**. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references to the examination center. The exam rules allow approved references to be highlighted, underlined, annotated, and indexed before the examination session. References may not be written in during the examination session. Additional loose or attached papers are not permitted with approved references.

Reference materials may be tabbed or indexed with permanent tabs only. Temporary tabs, including removable notes such as Post-it notes, are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins. This makes permanent, study-friendly tabbing important for candidates who plan to practice with their code book and improve reference speed before test day.

The Louisiana Electrical Statewide exam reference list includes **NFPA 70, National Electrical Code**, **Ugly’s Electrical References**, and **Code of Federal Regulations, 29 CFR Part 1926** or the PSI selections version of that OSHA reference. The listed exam reference materials identify the edition used for code questions, and candidates should bring the approved edition required for the examination they are scheduled to take.

## Licensing Steps

The Louisiana licensing path begins with identifying the credential being pursued. Individual master electrician and journeyman electrician credentials are commonly handled by local cities or parishes. Electrical contractor licensing, including the Electrical Statewide classification, is handled through the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors.

For statewide contractor licensing, the applicant submits an application for licensure or a request to add a classification or qualifying party to an existing license. After the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors approves the applicant for testing, the candidate receives confirmation from PSI and may schedule the examination. Exam eligibility is valid for one year. If the candidate does not pass within the one-year eligibility period, the candidate must reapply with the Board.

After approval, the candidate schedules the exam through PSI. PSI offers internet and telephone scheduling, and candidates select an available testing location and exam date. On exam day, candidates must arrive with the required identification and approved reference materials. The name used for scheduling must match the candidate’s identification.

After the examination, the score is displayed on screen and a score report is emailed. Candidates who fail receive diagnostic information showing strengths and weaknesses by exam type. That information can be used to adjust study plans before another attempt.

For local Louisiana master electrician licensing, candidates should follow the requirements of the city or parish where they plan to work. Local requirements may include experience documentation, examination, local registration, business documentation, insurance, renewal, or additional local rules. Students should prepare technically with the NEC while also following the licensing steps required by the local authority.

## State Requirements

Louisiana electrical licensing includes both statewide contractor licensing and local electrician licensing. The Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors regulates contractor licensing and classifications, including Electrical Statewide. Local municipalities and parishes may regulate individual electrician credentials such as journeyman and master electrician licenses.

The Electrical Statewide classification applies to electrical contracting work within the scope defined by the Board. The classification covers electrical work involving installation, construction, alteration, improvement, movement, maintenance, repair, or demolition of wiring, cabling, fixtures, appliances, equipment, or hardware for the supply of electricity to a structure, building, or project when the work falls within the licensing threshold and classification rules.

Because individual master electrician licensing is commonly local, Louisiana candidates should not assume that one city’s credential automatically satisfies another city or parish. Local authorities may set their own requirements for experience, examination, registration, license renewal, and work authorization. A contractor license may also be required depending on the type, value, and scope of work being performed.

Contractor applicants should understand that passing the trade exam is only one part of the licensing process. Application approval, classification approval, qualifying party requirements, business documentation, financial or insurance requirements, and licensing issuance remain under the control of the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors.
