Exam Details

The Alabama Electrical Contractors Board administers the statewide electrical licensing examination process for electrical contractor and journeyman classifications. Alabama’s master-level electrical preparation is commonly connected with the electrical contractor examination pathway because the state examination covers supervisory electrical construction knowledge, code application, business-related licensing readiness, and advanced trade competency.

The Alabama electrical contractor examination application requires qualifying supervisory electrical construction experience. The work experience must demonstrate that the applicant has designed, planned, laid out, and directly supervised electrical construction activities and the installation of electrical components. This makes the master-level exam different from a basic entry-level trade exam. Candidates should be ready for questions that require code knowledge, field judgment, calculations, and familiarity with the responsibilities of supervising electrical work.

The Alabama Electrical Contractors Board examination bulletin identifies the electrical exams as timed and open book. Candidates should expect to use approved reference material during the examination while still working efficiently under time limits. Open-book testing requires strong code-navigation skills because there is not enough time to learn the layout of the NEC during the exam.

Alabama’s published candidate information for the electrical contractor examination has identified key content areas such as general electrical knowledge, services, feeders, branch circuits, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, motors, special occupancies, special equipment, lighting, illuminated signs, fire detection and alarm systems, safety, low voltage, and electrical power. Candidates preparing for a master electrician exam should study these areas carefully and practice applying code rules to question-based scenarios.

A strong study plan should include repeated review of the National Electrical Code, active calculation practice, timed question practice, and review of missed questions. The goal is not just to recognize electrical terms. The goal is to understand how the code is organized, how to locate requirements quickly, how to read the full question, and how to select the answer that best matches the code and the facts given.

Open Book Test

The Alabama electrical examination bulletin identifies the Board’s exams as open book and timed. Open book testing allows candidates to use authorized references, but it does not make the exam simple. Candidates must still know how to find information quickly, apply the correct code rule, and avoid losing time searching through unfamiliar sections.

The National Electrical Code is a large technical document with articles, parts, definitions, tables, exceptions, informational notes, and cross-references. A candidate who has not practiced using the NEC may struggle even when the book is available. Master-level electrical candidates should study with the code book from the beginning of their preparation, not only during the final week before testing.

The included National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs is useful for open-book preparation because it supports faster navigation during study. Tabs can help candidates locate commonly used articles and sections, but they work best when paired with repeated practice. Candidates should use the tabbed NEC while answering practice questions, checking calculations, reviewing grounding and bonding rules, and locating tables for conductor and overcurrent protection questions.

During preparation, do not simply answer a practice question and move on. Look up the rule that supports the answer. Read nearby exceptions. Check the table notes. Review definitions that may affect the meaning of the question. This type of study helps candidates build the code-navigation habits needed for a timed open-book exam.

Licensing Steps

The Alabama master-level electrical licensing path begins with meeting the experience requirements established by the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board. Applicants for the statewide electrical contractor examination must document supervisory electrical construction experience. The Board’s application materials identify a requirement of four years or 8,000 hours of supervisory electrical construction experience.

The required experience must be in commercial, industrial, or residential new construction fields. The applicant must have held a supervisory or managerial role for the required time. The work experience should show involvement in designing, planning, laying out, and directly supervising electrical construction activities and the installation of electrical components.

Applicants must complete the proper Alabama Electrical Contractors Board examination application and provide the required supporting documentation. Work affidavits must be completed by an appropriate person who can attest to the applicant’s qualifying work, such as a licensed electrical contractor, electrical inspector, building inspector, or electrical project manager. Application materials must be complete and submitted according to the Board’s current instructions.

After the application is reviewed and approved, the candidate may move forward with the examination process. Candidates should prepare before scheduling the exam so they are ready for code lookup, calculations, trade knowledge, and supervisory-level electrical questions. After the exam is completed, the Board receives the examination results and handles the next steps in the licensing process.

Exam preparation and application preparation are separate responsibilities, but both are important. Candidates should keep employment records, affidavits, education documentation when applicable, and Board forms organized throughout the process.

State Requirements

Alabama law requires individuals engaged in the electrical contracting business or performing work as an Electrical Contractor or Electrical Journeyman to be licensed. The Alabama Electrical Contractors Board is the state board responsible for the statewide electrical licensing process.

For the electrical contractor examination pathway, applicants must document qualifying supervisory electrical construction experience. The Board’s application materials state that the applicant must have a minimum of four years or 8,000 hours of supervisory electrical construction experience. The experience must show that the applicant has designed, planned, laid out, and directly supervised electrical construction activities and the installation of electrical components.

Alabama allows qualifying education in an electrical curriculum to be substituted for part of the required experience. The Board’s materials identify a substitution of one year of education for one-half year of electrical experience, up to a maximum of two years of credit toward the four-year experience requirement. Candidates using education toward the requirement should provide proper documentation with the application materials.

The Alabama electrical contractor license is unlimited in voltage and bid requirements. Because of that scope, the state requires applicants to document supervisory experience and demonstrate competency through the examination process. Candidates preparing for the master-level electrical exam should take the requirements seriously and study across both practical field knowledge and code-based material.

Applicants should always use the current Alabama Electrical Contractors Board forms and follow the Board’s current application instructions. Forms, deadlines, fees, payment rules, and administrative procedures may be updated by the Board, so candidates should use the latest application packet when preparing to apply.
