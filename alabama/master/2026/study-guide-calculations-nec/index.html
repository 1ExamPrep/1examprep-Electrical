Exam Details

The Alabama Electrical Contractors Board licenses state electrical contractors and electrical journeymen. Alabama’s master-level electrical licensing route is connected to the Electrical Contractor examination. The Board has contracted with Prov to administer the licensing examination program for state licensed electrical contractors and electrical journeymen. Candidates must complete the appropriate application process with the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board before scheduling the examination.

The Alabama Electrical Contractor exam is a timed examination with 110 scored questions and 5 hours allowed. The examination includes both business and trade-related subject areas. Listed topic areas include business organization, risk management, project management, estimating, safety and environmental, labor laws, payroll and employment taxes, financial management, contracts, lien laws, licensing laws, general electrical knowledge, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special occupancies and equipment, electrical power, motors, low voltage, lighting, illuminated signs, fire detection and alarm systems, safety information, overcurrent protection, and service, feeders, and branch circuits.

The exam is broad because master-level and electrical contractor work requires more than installation knowledge. Candidates must be prepared for technical electrical questions as well as questions tied to planning, supervision, business responsibilities, and compliance. A candidate may be tested on how electrical systems are installed, how code rules apply, how jobsite safety is handled, and how contractor-level responsibilities affect daily operations.

The listed passing score for Alabama Electrical Contractor Board examinations is 75%. Exam results are scored after testing and sent to the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board. Candidates who pass must contact the Board and apply for the license. Candidates who do not pass must contact the Board to reapply for testing.

Master electrician candidates should study with the understanding that the exam is both technical and practical. It may involve code references, calculations, trade knowledge, supervisory judgment, and business-related subjects. The materials in this combo help support the technical and calculation side of preparation while giving students the NEC reference needed to build speed and accuracy with code lookup.

Open Book Test

The Alabama Electrical Contractor Board examinations administered through Prov are listed as open book and timed. Open book testing requires strong reference navigation. Candidates should not rely on the ability to bring a book into the room as a substitute for preparation. The challenge is knowing where to find information quickly, how to interpret the code correctly, and how to apply that information under time pressure.

For an open book electrical exam, students should practice using the National Electrical Code before exam day. The NEC contains definitions, chapters, articles, parts, tables, exceptions, informational notes, annexes, and an index. Familiarity with that structure can help candidates move faster during the exam and avoid wasting time searching through unfamiliar material.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback included in this combo supports reference navigation practice. Candidates can use it to locate common subject areas such as conductors, cables, raceways, boxes, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, motors, lighting, special occupancies, and equipment rules. The more often students use the code book during study, the more natural code navigation becomes.

Open book preparation should include more than highlighting. Students should practice turning broad exam topics into searchable code subjects. For example, a question about conductor sizing may require knowledge of ampacity tables, adjustment or correction factors, terminal temperature limitations, or equipment requirements. A question about grounding may involve grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, service equipment, or fault-current path rules. These topics become easier to manage when students regularly practice finding and applying the correct section.

Candidates should follow all exam-day rules provided by the testing administrator, including rules for approved references, tabs, notes, book condition, identification, scheduling, and testing center procedures.

Licensing Steps

Alabama master-level electrical licensing is tied to the Electrical Contractor classification. A candidate begins by applying through the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board and submitting the required documentation for Board review. The Board determines whether the applicant is qualified to sit for the examination.

After approval, the candidate may schedule the examination through Prov within the eligibility period established through the licensing process. The exam is administered by computer at approved testing locations. Candidates should review the testing rules, bring required identification, and prepare any permitted reference materials according to the testing administrator’s instructions.

After the exam, results are scored and sent to the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board. A candidate who passes must contact the Board and complete the licensing process. A candidate who does not pass must contact the Board to reapply for testing. Candidates should keep copies of submitted documentation and follow Board instructions carefully throughout the process.

A strong licensing plan should begin before the application is submitted. Candidates should gather experience records, review the Board’s application requirements, study the exam content outline, and build a schedule that allows time for trade review, calculations practice, and code navigation. Waiting until an exam date is scheduled can make preparation feel rushed. A steady plan gives students more time to build confidence and correct weak areas.

Because the Electrical Contractor classification involves planning, layout, and supervision, candidates should prepare for exam content with a broader perspective than field installation alone. Study time should include both hands-on electrical subjects and the code rules that support safe, compliant work.

State Requirements

Alabama Electrical Contractor applicants must document qualifying experience to the satisfaction of the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board. The statewide electrical contractor requirement includes 8,000 hours of experience in the overall design, planning, layout, and direct supervision of electrical construction activities and the soliciting and installing of electrical power or control systems.

The scope of licensing for an electrical contractor includes installation, design, planning, layout, and direct supervision of electrical construction activities. The supervised work may be commercial, industrial, or residential. Alabama’s electrical contractor license is unlimited in voltage and bid requirements.

Approved education may be used toward part of the experience requirement. Alabama application materials allow qualified electrical education to substitute for a portion of the required electrical experience, up to the maximum allowed by the Board. Applicants using education should provide the required documentation showing completion of the approved curriculum or program.

Experience documentation must be supported through the Board’s required forms and records. Applicants should carefully follow the application instructions, provide accurate employment details, and ensure required affidavits or supporting documents are completed properly. Incomplete or inaccurate documentation can delay approval to test.

Alabama law requires electrical contractors and journeyman electricians to be licensed. The Electrical Contractor classification is associated with the qualifications, training, and technical knowledge needed to plan, lay out, and supervise electrical wiring, apparatus, or equipment for lighting, heating, power, or controls.
