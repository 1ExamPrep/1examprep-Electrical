Exam Details

The Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors oversees state electrical contractor and journeyman licensing. The board’s testing program is administered through Prov for Alabama electrical contractor and electrical journeyman examinations. Alabama also participates in electrical contractor/master electrician examination pathways recognized through NASCLA for applicable licensing purposes.

Alabama electrical exam preparation commonly requires review of both technical electrical knowledge and code application. Depending on the specific license or exam path, candidates may encounter questions connected to general electrical knowledge, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, services, feeders, branch circuits, motors, overcurrent protection, lighting, low voltage, electrical power, fire detection and alarm systems, safety, project-related knowledge, and electrical code interpretation.

The Alabama Electrical Contractor exam information published by the testing provider lists 110 questions with 5 hours allowed for that exam. The Alabama Electrical Journeyman exam information lists 80 questions with 4 hours allowed. Electrical Contractor test takers are directed to complete both the required theory examination and the Business and Law examination when applicable to the licensing path.

Because exam names, accepted references, and application requirements can vary by license type, candidates should follow the instructions issued by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors for the exact examination they are approved to take. Open Book Test

Alabama electrical examinations administered through the state electrical contractor testing program are listed as open book and timed. Open book does not mean easy. It means candidates may use authorized materials under testing rules, but they still need to know where information is located, how to interpret the question, and how to apply the correct code section.

For open-book electrical exams, speed and organization matter. A candidate who has not practiced with the NEC may spend too much time searching through the book. Licensing Steps

Alabama electrical licensing begins with the licensing authority, not the testing center. Candidates must complete the appropriate application process with the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors for the license type they are seeking. Applications are reviewed by the board, and candidates approved for testing are notified when they are eligible to schedule the examination.

A practical preparation path usually includes these steps:

  1. Identify the correct Alabama electrical license path. Determine whether you are pursuing an electrical contractor, journeyman, master-level, or related examination path based on your work history and licensing goals.
  2. Review the official application requirements. Follow the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors instructions for experience documentation, affidavits, forms, fees, and deadlines.
  3. Submit the required application materials. Candidates should complete all required forms accurately and provide supporting documentation requested by the board.
  4. Wait for testing approval. Once approved, candidates receive instructions for scheduling through the testing provider.
  5. Prepare with code-based study materials. Use the 2026 Alabama Master Electrician Study Guide and the tabbed 2026 NEC to review exam topics and practice code navigation.
  6. Schedule and take the examination. Follow the testing provider’s scheduling rules, identification requirements, check-in procedures, and test-day policies.
  7. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Passing an exam is one part of the process. Candidates must satisfy all licensing requirements set by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors before a license is issued.

Because Alabama licensing requirements are tied to the specific license classification and application being submitted, candidates should use the board’s current forms and instructions for their exact path. This combo supports exam preparation but does not replace official licensing instructions.

State Requirements

The Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors regulates state electrical contractor and journeyman licensing. Alabama law requires individuals engaged in electrical contracting or performing covered electrical work as an electrical contractor or journeyman to be licensed as required by the state.

For electrical contractor examination applicants, Alabama requires documented electrical construction experience in the commercial, industrial, or residential new construction fields. The official electrical contractor examination application states that applicants must have supervisory or managerial electrical construction experience and must provide work affidavits completed by an approved person who can attest to the applicant’s experience.

For journeyman examination applicants, Alabama requires documented electrical construction work experience through the board’s application process. Work affidavits must be completed by someone who can verify the applicant’s qualifying experience. Candidates may not self-certify their own electrical hours.

Alabama candidates should pay close attention to application deadlines, affidavit requirements, license category, exam eligibility windows, retesting rules, and renewal requirements. A strong study plan is important, but it must be paired with a complete and accurate licensing application.

Students using this combo should also be aware that code editions can matter. This product is based on the 2026 NEC. Candidates should match their study materials to the exam edition and reference list required for the specific exam they are taking. When an exam or jurisdiction uses a different NEC edition, candidates should follow the official instructions for that exam.

Test Information and Study Materials

Electrical exams test both knowledge and application. Candidates should be prepared to read questions carefully, recognize the topic being tested, identify the relevant rule or formula, and apply the correct code section or trade principle. The 2026 Alabama Master Electrician Study Guide can be used to organize your review around important electrical topics. The tabbed NEC can be used alongside the guide so you can practice finding related code sections as you study. This approach helps prevent passive reading and encourages active learning.

Important study areas may include grounding and bonding, conductor sizing, raceway fill, box fill, ampacity, overcurrent protection, branch circuits, feeders, services, motor circuits, transformers, wiring methods, special occupancies, electrical safety, definitions, and general electrical theory. Candidates should also review calculation methods carefully, because many electrical questions require more than simply locating a sentence in the code book.

When studying with the NEC, pay attention to how the book is arranged. Article 100 definitions can affect how questions are interpreted. Chapter 2 includes wiring and protection rules. Chapter 3 covers wiring methods and materials. Chapter 4 covers equipment for general use. Chapter 5 addresses special occupancies. Chapter 6 covers special equipment. Chapter 7 covers special conditions. Chapter 8 covers communications systems. Chapter 9 includes tables that are often important for calculations and installations.

Tabs are most helpful when you already understand what you are looking for. During study, practice moving from the question topic to the correct article, then to the correct section, table, exception, or informational note when applicable. The more often you practice that process, the more natural it becomes.

A good weekly study routine may include reading a section of the study guide, looking up related NEC rules, completing practice-style questions, reviewing missed topics, and creating a list of areas that need additional attention. Candidates should avoid cramming only at the end. Electrical exam preparation is stronger when review is repeated over time.

Students should also practice working under timed conditions. Open-book tests still require time management. Spending too long on one difficult question can create pressure later in the exam. Practice sessions can help you learn when to look up a rule, when to calculate carefully, and when to move on and return later.
