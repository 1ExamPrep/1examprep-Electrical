Exam Details

Alabama electrical licensing examinations are administered through Prov, Inc. for the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board. The Alabama Electrical Contractors Board has contracted with Prov to administer the licensing examination program for state licensed electrical contractors and electrical journeymen. Alabama law requires individuals engaged in the electrical contracting business or performing work as an electrical contractor or electrical journeyman to be licensed.

For master-level licensing, the Alabama Electrical Contractor examination is the primary electrical trade examination listed in the current Prov candidate bulletin. The Alabama Electrical Contractor examination contains 110 questions and has a 5-hour time limit. The passing score listed for the Alabama electrical exams is 75%.

The Alabama Electrical Contractor exam outline includes business organization, risk management, project management, estimating, safety and environmental, labor laws, payroll and employment taxes, financial management, contracts, lien laws, licensing laws, general electrical knowledge, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special occupancies and equipment, electrical power, motors, low voltage, lighting, illuminated signs, fire detection and alarm systems, safety information, overcurrent protection, and service, feeders, and branch circuits.

Open Book Test

The Alabama Electrical Contractors Board examination bulletin states that all exams administered by Prov for the Board are open book and timed. The Alabama master-level electrical examination is therefore an open book test. Candidates may use authorized references during the examination, and Prov recommends that candidates become familiar with the allowed reference materials before test day.

Open book testing still requires serious preparation. The candidate must know how to move through the NEC, identify the correct article, interpret code language, and apply the rule to the question. A candidate who has not practiced reference navigation may spend too much time searching and not enough time answering. The more time spent studying outside the testing room, the less time is needed in the testing room trying to locate information.

Prov’s reference material rules allow candidates to prepare references by highlighting text and attaching permanent tabs. The bulletin states that handwritten notes are not allowed in reference books, moveable tabs are never permitted, Post-it notes are not permitted, photocopied documents cannot be added to a reference book, photocopied versions of reference books are not permitted, and test preparation study guides are not approved references. Candidates may not share reference materials during testing and may not highlight, underline, mark, or write information in references during the exam.

The National Electrical Code 2026 paperback with tabs included in this Super Combo is designed to support code study and reference navigation practice. The Alabama Master Electrician Study Guide, Electrician Calculations Study Guide, and Master Electrician Flash Cards are intended for preparation before test day. Exam-room acceptance of any specific book, tabbing method, calculator, code edition, or reference is controlled by the current Alabama Electrical Contractors Board and Prov examination rules for the scheduled exam.

Licensing Steps

The Alabama master electrician licensing path begins with the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board. An applicant must complete the appropriate application with the Board before testing. Applications are reviewed by the Board, and the Board posts application deadlines online. Once the applicant has been approved for testing, the Board notifies the candidate that they are eligible to contact Prov to schedule the examination.

After approval, the candidate is eligible to take the examination within 90 days of the approval date. Candidates who fail the exam or who do not test within the eligibility period must reapply for testing according to the Board’s retesting process. Prov is not authorized to decide which examination a candidate must take, so license classification questions should be resolved directly with the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board before scheduling.

The examination is administered by computer at a Prov testing center. Candidates schedule the approved exam, bring required identification, follow test-day rules, and use only approved items and authorized references. Prov’s scoring system grades the exam immediately upon completion and automatically forwards the results to the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board. The Board mails the candidate’s score.

After passing, the candidate must contact the Board and apply for the license. Candidates who do not pass must contact the Board to reapply for testing. A practical path includes reviewing eligibility, submitting the correct application, receiving Board approval, scheduling through Prov, preparing with the exam outline and allowed reference rules, taking the examination, and completing the Board’s licensing steps after passing.

Study should begin before the exam date is selected. Master-level electrical content is broad, and the exam includes both technical electrical topics and business-related subjects.
