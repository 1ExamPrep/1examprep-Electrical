## Exam Details

Alabama electrical licensing is handled through the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board. The Board has contracted with Prov, Inc. to administer the licensing examination program for state licensed electrical contractors and electrical journeymen. Alabama law requires individuals performing work as an electrical contractor or electrical journeyman to be licensed.

The Alabama Electrical Journeyman examination is listed as an 80-question exam with a 4-hour time limit. The passing score is 75%. Prov’s scoring system grades the exam immediately upon completion and automatically forwards the result to the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board. The Board mails the candidate’s score.

The Alabama Electrical Journeyman exam outline includes conductors and cables, electrical power, fire detection and alarm systems, general electrical knowledge, grounding and bonding, illuminated signs, lighting, low voltage, motors, overcurrent protection, raceways and boxes, safety information, service, feeders and branch circuits, special occupancies and equipment, wiring and protection, and wiring methods and materials.

## Licensing Steps

The Alabama journeyman electrician licensing process begins with the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board. An applicant must complete the appropriate application with the Board before testing. Applications are reviewed by the Board, and deadlines are posted by the Board. Once the applicant is approved for testing, the Board notifies the candidate that they are eligible to contact Prov to schedule the examination.

After approval, the candidate is eligible to take the exam within 90 days of the approval date. Candidates who do not pass or who do not take the exam within the eligibility period must reapply according to the Board’s retesting process. Failing candidates must reapply each time they test.

Once a candidate schedules the exam through Prov, the exam is administered by computer at an approved testing center. The candidate takes the Alabama Electrical Journeyman exam, and the result is automatically sent to the Board. A candidate who passes must contact the Board and apply for the license.

A practical licensing path includes gathering work history documentation, submitting the Board application, waiting for Board approval, scheduling through Prov, studying the exam outline, practicing calculations, reviewing the NEC, taking the examination, and completing the Board’s licensing steps after passing.
