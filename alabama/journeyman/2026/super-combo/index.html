Exam Details

Alabama electrical licensing is handled through the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board. The Board has contracted with Prov, Inc. to administer the licensing examination program for state licensed electrical contractors and electrical journeymen. Alabama law requires individuals performing work as an electrical contractor or electrical journeyman to be licensed.

The Alabama Electrical Journeyman examination is listed as an 80-question exam with a 4-hour time limit. The passing score is 75%. Prov’s scoring system grades the exam immediately upon completion and automatically forwards the result to the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board. The Board mails the candidate’s score.

The Alabama Electrical Journeyman exam outline includes conductors and cables, electrical power, fire detection and alarm systems, general electrical knowledge, grounding and bonding, illuminated signs, lighting, low voltage, motors, overcurrent protection, raceways and boxes, safety information, service, feeders and branch circuits, special occupancies and equipment, wiring and protection, and wiring methods and materials.

The largest listed subject areas include wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, general electrical knowledge, service, feeders and branch circuits, grounding and bonding, and special occupancies and equipment. These areas deserve consistent attention during preparation because they represent a significant portion of the exam outline.

Others may require the candidate to identify the correct conductor, raceway, overcurrent protection method, grounding rule, wiring method, safety requirement, or service and feeder concept. Calculation work may also appear as part of the candidate’s preparation needs, especially when reviewing services, feeders, branch circuits, conductors, motors, and electrical loads.

Open Book Test

The Alabama Electrical Contractors Board examination bulletin states that all exams are open book and timed. The Alabama Electrical Journeyman exam is therefore an open book test. Candidates are allowed to use authorized study materials during the examination, and the bulletin recommends that candidates bring their own code books related to their exams.

Open book testing rewards preparation. A candidate who has practiced with the NEC before exam day can move more quickly through articles, definitions, tables, wiring method rules, grounding and bonding provisions, conductor requirements, and service or feeder rules. Licensing Steps

The Alabama journeyman electrician licensing process begins with the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board. An applicant must complete the appropriate application with the Board before testing. Applications are reviewed by the Board, and deadlines are posted by the Board. Once the applicant is approved for testing, the Board notifies the candidate that they are eligible to contact Prov to schedule the examination.

After approval, the candidate is eligible to take the exam within 90 days of the approval date. Candidates who do not pass or who do not take the exam within the eligibility period must reapply according to the Board’s retesting process. Failing candidates must reapply each time they test.

Once a candidate schedules the exam through Prov, the exam is administered by computer at an approved testing center. The candidate takes the Alabama Electrical Journeyman exam, and the result is automatically sent to the Board. A candidate who passes must contact the Board and apply for the license.

A practical licensing path includes gathering work history documentation, submitting the Board application, waiting for Board approval, scheduling through Prov, studying the exam outline, practicing calculations, reviewing the NEC, taking the examination, and completing the Board’s licensing steps after passing.

Strong preparation should begin before the exam is scheduled. Journeyman electrician content is broad, and a steady study schedule gives students more time to build retention. State Requirements

The Alabama Electrical Contractors Board regulates electrical contractors and electrical journeymen at the state level. Alabama law requires each individual engaged in the electrical contracting business or performing work as an electrical contractor or electrical journeyman to be licensed.

For examination, applicants must complete the required Board application process and receive approval before scheduling through Prov. Prov is not authorized to decide which examination a candidate must take. Candidates must resolve license classification questions directly with the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board before scheduling.

The Alabama Electrical Journeyman exam measures knowledge expected of a qualified electrical journeyman. The exam outline includes a wide range of job-related subjects, including conductors, raceways, boxes, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, motors, lighting, low voltage, fire alarm systems, services, feeders, branch circuits, wiring methods, wiring protection, safety, and general electrical knowledge.

Alabama’s current Board testing information states that all exams currently reference the 2023 NEC and that 2026 NEC testing begins January 1, 2027. Students preparing with this 2026 NEC-based product should use the materials for current-code study and long-term code familiarity while following the Board’s active exam instructions for the specific test date.
