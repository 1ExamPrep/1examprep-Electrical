Exam Details

The Alabama Electrical Contractors Board licenses state electrical contractors and electrical journeymen. Alabama’s electrical journeyman licensing examination is administered through Prov for candidates approved by the Board. Candidates must complete the appropriate application process with the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board before scheduling the examination.

The Alabama Electrical Journeyman exam is a timed examination with 80 questions and 4 hours allowed. The listed subject areas include conductors and cables, electrical power, fire detection and alarm systems, general electrical knowledge, grounding and bonding, illuminated signs, lighting, low voltage, motors, overcurrent protection, raceways and boxes, safety information, service, feeders, branch circuits, and special occupancies and equipment.

Because the exam reaches across many areas of electrical work, preparation should include more than memorizing definitions. Students should practice reading questions carefully, identifying the electrical topic being tested, locating the applicable code section, and applying rules to realistic installation scenarios. Electrical exams often require both code familiarity and judgment. A question may involve a conductor, raceway, grounding method, branch circuit, or equipment rule, but the correct answer often depends on knowing where to look and how to interpret the requirement.

Calculations are also an important part of electrical preparation. Even when a question is multiple choice, the student may need to understand formulas, units, tables, or step-by-step math logic. The included 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide supports this part of preparation by giving students a focused place to work on calculation confidence instead of trying to learn math only from code text.

The passing score listed for the Alabama Electrical Contractor Board examinations is 75%. Results are scored after the exam and sent to the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board. Candidates who pass must contact the Board and apply for the license. Candidates who fail must contact the Board to reapply for testing.

Open Book Test

The Alabama Electrical Contractor Board examinations administered through Prov are listed as open book and timed. Open book testing does not mean the exam is easy. In many cases, open book electrical exams are challenging because they test how quickly and accurately candidates can navigate approved references, interpret code language, and apply rules under time pressure.

For an open book exam, preparation should include repeated work with the code book. Students should become familiar with the structure of the NEC, including chapters, articles, tables, definitions, informational notes, and index usage. Licensing Steps

Alabama statewide journeyman electrician licenses are issued by examination. A candidate must first apply through the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board and provide the required documentation before being approved to test. The Board reviews applications and notifies approved candidates when they are eligible to schedule the examination.

After approval, candidates are eligible to schedule the exam through Prov within the approval period. The exam is administered by computer at approved testing locations. Candidates should arrive prepared with required identification and any permitted materials allowed under the testing rules.

After testing, exam results are sent to the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board. A passing candidate must contact the Board and apply for the license. A candidate who does not pass must reapply for testing through the Board. Candidates who do not test within their eligibility period must also follow the Board’s retesting or reapplication requirements.

A strong licensing plan begins before the test date. Candidates should organize experience documentation, review Board application requirements, prepare study materials early, and build a regular study schedule. For many students, the best preparation approach is to divide study time between code navigation, trade topic review, and calculations practice. This combo supports all three areas.

State Requirements

Alabama journeyman electrician applicants must document the required electrical experience. Statewide journeyman applicants are required to document 8,000 hours of experience showing the qualifications, training, and technical knowledge needed to install electrical wiring, apparatus, or equipment for light, heat, or power.

An applicant for the statewide Journeyman Electrician examination may substitute approved electrical education or apprenticeship program hours for a portion of the total experience requirement. The Board’s application materials allow one year of education in an approved electrical curriculum or apprenticeship program to substitute for 1,000 hours of electrical experience, up to a maximum of 2,000 hours of the 8,000 hours required. Documentation is subject to Board approval.

Experience documentation must be supported by the required affidavits and employment records. The Board’s application materials require affidavits of employment signed and notarized by employers, along with corresponding W-2 forms or 1099 forms, or a work affidavit from a Department of Labor registered apprentice program. Applicants using approved electrical education must provide documentation showing completion of the approved program.

Alabama law requires individuals performing work as an Electrical Contractor or Electrical Journeyman to be licensed. A journeyman electrician is associated with the technical knowledge and qualifications needed to install electrical wiring, apparatus, equipment, lighting, heating, power, or control systems covered by Alabama electrical contractor law. Candidates should make sure they are applying for the correct license classification and should follow the Board’s application instructions carefully.

Common areas for electrical calculation practice include Ohm’s law, power formulas, voltage drop, conductor sizing, box fill, raceway fill, load calculations, motor calculations, and overcurrent protection applications.
