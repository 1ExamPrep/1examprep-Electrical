## Exam Details

The Alabama Electrical Contractors Board administers licensing for statewide electrical contractor and journeyman electrician licensing. Alabama law requires individuals performing work as an electrical contractor or electrical journeyman to be licensed. For journeyman electrician candidates, the Board requires completion of the appropriate examination application before testing approval.

The Alabama Electrical Contractors Board has contracted with Prov, Inc. to administer its licensing examination program for state licensed electrical contractors and electrical journeymen. After the Board approves a candidate for testing, the candidate is notified that they are eligible to contact Prov to schedule the examination. The Board’s Candidate Information Bulletin states that approved candidates are eligible to take the exam within 90 days of the approval date, and failing candidates must reapply each time they test.

The Candidate Information Bulletin also states that Alabama electrical exams are timed and open book. Results are scored through Prov’s system and sent to the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board. The Board mails the score to the candidate. The bulletin states that candidates must achieve a 75% score to pass the exams.

The Alabama Board’s forms and testing information states that current exams reference the 2023 NFPA 70 National Electrical Code and that beginning January 1, 2027, the Board will begin testing on the 2026 NFPA 70 National Electrical Code. This product is based on the 2026 NEC and is designed for candidates preparing with the 2026 code cycle.

Because electrical exams are code-heavy, candidates should spend consistent time learning the structure of the NEC. The National Electrical Code is organized to help users locate definitions, wiring methods, conductor requirements, grounding and bonding rules, equipment provisions, special occupancies, special equipment, and calculation procedures. The more familiar you become with the structure of the code book, the easier it becomes to answer questions that require quick and accurate lookup.

## Licensing Steps

The Alabama journeyman electrician licensing process begins with meeting the experience and application requirements set by the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board. Statewide journeyman licenses are issued by examination. Applicants for the statewide journeyman examination must document a minimum of 8,000 hours of experience showing that they have the qualifications, training, and technical knowledge to install electrical wiring, apparatus, or equipment for light, heat, or power.

The Board’s journeyman examination application states that an applicant may substitute one year of education in an approved electrical curriculum or apprenticeship program for 1,000 hours of electrical experience, up to a maximum of 2,000 hours of the total 8,000 hours required. The applicant must provide appropriate documentation, subject to Board approval. Candidates with a long certificate from a public State of Alabama two-year or community college are awarded 2,000 hours of experience.

To be approved to sit for the examination, the applicant must submit the examination application and required supporting documentation. This may include affidavits of employment signed and notarized by employers and corresponding tax records, or documentation connected to a Department of Labor registered apprenticeship program. The application materials are reviewed by the Board, and once approved, the applicant receives scheduling information.

After passing the appropriate examination, the candidate moves to licensure application steps. Alabama’s forms and testing information states that only the EC-1 application is required for journeyman electricians after successful completion of the examination. The Board also lists a journeyman electrician license fee. Candidates should follow the Board’s current forms and instructions when applying, paying fees, submitting documents, and completing the licensing process.

## State Requirements

Alabama defines a journeyman electrician as a person who possesses the necessary qualifications, training, and technical knowledge to install electrical wiring, apparatus, equipment, lighting, heating, power, or control covered by the state electrical contractor licensing law. A journeyman electrician works under a master or state certified electrical contractor and must be capable of doing work according to plans and specifications and in accordance with standard rules and regulations governing the work.

For statewide journeyman examination eligibility, Alabama requires documented electrical construction experience. The Board’s examination application identifies 8,000 hours as the minimum experience requirement. The application also allows certain approved education or apprenticeship program completion to count toward part of the required hours. Experience affidavits must be completed by employers or appropriate parties, and applicants cannot certify their own electrical hours.

Alabama candidates should keep clean documentation of employment dates, job roles, total hours worked, employer information, and any approved education or apprenticeship completion. Complete documentation helps the Board evaluate whether the candidate has met the qualifications required to sit for the journeyman electrician examination.

Applicants should also pay attention to application deadlines. Alabama’s forms and testing information states that materials must be received by the Board office by the stated deadline before Board meetings. Incomplete applications may delay approval, which can also delay exam scheduling. A strong licensing plan includes gathering employment records early, confirming forms are complete, and studying while the application is being processed.

## Test Information and Study Materials

Journeyman electrician preparation should include both technical review and code book practice. Electrical candidates often focus heavily on memorizing formulas, but the exam also requires practical understanding of code structure and safe installation requirements. A balanced study plan should include reading, calculation practice, code lookup drills, and review of missed questions.

Important study areas for journeyman electrician candidates commonly include electrical theory, conductors, raceways, wiring methods, boxes, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, motors, transformers, services, feeders, branch circuits, load calculations, special occupancies, special equipment, and general NEC organization. Candidates should also be comfortable working with tables and applying rules to real installation scenarios.

The NEC tabs included with this combo are useful because they help candidates divide the code book into more manageable sections. During study, candidates can practice moving between definitions, wiring methods, conductor ampacity tables, grounding and bonding requirements, boxes and conduit fill, motors, services, and other frequently reviewed areas. The more often you practice this movement through the book, the more comfortable you become during timed practice.

A strong study routine may include short daily review sessions instead of one long cram session. Start by reading a topic in the study guide, then locate the related NEC sections. Next, work through practice-style questions or self-check exercises. When you miss a question, do not simply memorize the answer. Go back to the code book, find the rule, and understand why that answer applies. This habit improves long-term retention and helps reduce confusion when similar questions appear in a different form.

For calculation topics, write out each step. Electrical calculations are easier to improve when you can see where mistakes happen. Label the values, identify the formula or NEC requirement involved, and check whether the question is asking for a minimum, maximum, rating, ampacity, adjustment, correction, or required size. Many wrong answers come from rushing through the wording rather than from lack of knowledge.

Because the Alabama exam is timed, candidates should also practice pacing. Code lookup can consume valuable time when the candidate is unfamiliar with the book. The goal is not to memorize every NEC rule. The goal is to know the structure well enough to quickly find the rule, read it accurately, and apply it correctly.
