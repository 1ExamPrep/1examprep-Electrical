Exam Details

The Virginia Journeyman Electrician exam is administered by PSI for the Commonwealth of Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. Candidates apply through the Virginia tradesman licensing process and must meet the Board for Contractors’ qualification requirements before licensure. PSI provides the candidate information bulletin, registration instructions, computer-based testing procedures, and exam-day rules.

The Virginia Journeyman Electrician examination is commonly identified as a 70-question exam with 210 minutes allowed. The passing score is 70%, which means candidates must answer 49 questions correctly. The exam is designed to confirm that the candidate has the minimum knowledge needed for journeyman-level electrical work in Virginia.

Journeyman-level electrical preparation should include National Electrical Code knowledge, electrical theory, services, service equipment, feeders, branch circuits, conductors, wiring methods and materials, raceways, boxes, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, equipment for general use, motors, transformers, control devices, special occupancies, special equipment, safety, and calculations. The exam may include both direct knowledge questions and questions that require code lookup or calculation work.

Because the exam is timed, candidates should practice answering questions efficiently. A 210-minute testing period gives candidates time to work carefully, but NEC navigation and calculation questions can still consume time quickly. Candidates should practice recognizing topics, finding articles and tables, reading code language carefully, solving math problems, and moving through the exam at a steady pace.

This Super Combo is based on the 2026 National Electrical Code for candidates who want current-code study materials, updated code navigation practice, and a complete preparation package. Virginia exam references and adopted code editions are controlled by the state and the exam provider. Candidates should follow the current PSI and DPOR instructions for the exact code edition and materials that apply to their scheduled examination.

The Virginia Journeyman Electrician license is different from a Virginia Master Electrician license and different from a contractor business license. The journeyman license is an individual tradesman license. Electrical contracting businesses follow the Board for Contractors licensing structure, and state law requires the proper tradesman qualification for electrical contractor licensure.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Journeyman Electrician examination is open book under PSI and Virginia DPOR reference rules. Candidates should follow the current PSI candidate information bulletin for the approved reference list, code edition, tab rules, calculator rules, markings, and prohibited materials.

Open-book electrical exams still require serious preparation. Having the NEC available does not mean every answer will be easy to find. Candidates need to recognize the topic being tested, move to the correct article or table, read the rule carefully, apply the code language to the question, and manage time. Code lookup becomes much easier when the candidate has already practiced navigating the NEC during study.

Reference rules may address whether books must be bound, whether tabs are allowed, whether tabs must be permanent, whether highlighting or underlining is permitted, whether written notes are allowed, and whether materials may be marked during the exam. Candidates should prepare reference materials according to the current exam rules before test day and avoid bringing unauthorized loose papers, inserts, removable notes, photocopies, or unapproved study materials.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs included in this Super Combo is intended for study and code navigation practice. Licensing Steps

Virginia candidates should begin by confirming that the Journeyman Electrician tradesman license is the correct credential for their work goals. Virginia also licenses master electricians, and contractor businesses are licensed separately. The correct path depends on whether the candidate plans to work as an individual tradesman, qualify for a higher-level tradesman license, or operate as part of a licensed contractor business.

A practical licensing path begins with building qualifying electrical experience and formal vocational training. Virginia’s journeyman tradesman rules use combinations of practical experience and education to determine eligibility. A common path includes four years of practical experience and 240 hours of formal vocational training in the trade. Other approved combinations may include additional years of practical experience with fewer formal training hours, or approved education pathways that satisfy the Board’s requirements.

Candidates should keep accurate records of employment, supervision, dates worked, job duties, electrical experience, and formal vocational training. Documentation matters because the Board reviews experience and training as part of the licensing process. Applicants must also meet general requirements for licensure, including age, identification, application documentation, address of record, and disclosure requirements established by Virginia regulation.

After the candidate meets the qualification requirements, the candidate applies through the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation process for the tradesman license. Once eligible for examination, the candidate schedules the PSI exam, prepares approved references according to the current bulletin, brings required identification, follows exam center procedures, and completes the examination.

After passing the required examination and completing the state licensing process, the candidate receives the Virginia Journeyman Electrician license through DPOR. Passing the exam is an important milestone, but licensure is issued only after the applicant satisfies the Board’s requirements and completes the full application process.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates individual tradesmen who engage in electrical work. Virginia’s individual tradesman licensing structure is separate from the contractor business licensing structure, but the two systems are connected when a business seeks a contractor license for electrical work.

Virginia’s journeyman tradesman examination approval rules include experience and education standards. Common qualifying combinations include four years of practical experience with 240 hours of formal vocational training, five years of practical experience with 160 hours of formal vocational training, six years of practical experience with 80 hours of formal vocational training, or other approved education and experience pathways under Virginia regulation. Candidates should apply under the pathway that matches their documented background.

Virginia regulations also provide an examination exemption for an individual who successfully completes an apprenticeship program approved by the Commissioner of the Department of Labor and Industry, when supported by the required certificate of completion or other official documentation. Candidates using an apprenticeship completion pathway should submit the required official documentation through the state process.

The Virginia Journeyman Electrician license is different from the Virginia Master Electrician license. Master-level licensure requires a higher level of qualification, such as holding a valid journeyman license in the trade for a required period or meeting an approved practical experience pathway. Candidates planning long-term advancement should understand how journeyman licensure fits into the progression toward master-level authority.

Virginia tradesman licenses have renewal and continuing education requirements. Electrical tradesmen must complete continuing education related to updates to the National Electrical Code during the license renewal cycle. Active license holders should follow DPOR renewal rules, keep license information current, and complete required education before renewal deadlines.

Study materials help candidates prepare, but licensing decisions are made by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation and the Board for Contractors based on the applicant’s qualifications, documentation, examination result, and compliance with Virginia requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

The Virginia Journeyman Electrician Super Combo supports a complete preparation routine by combining structured study, code review, calculation practice, and quick recall. The Virginia Journeyman Electrician Study Guide helps candidates organize preparation around journeyman-level electrical knowledge. The Electrician Calculations Study Guide strengthens math-based preparation. The NEC paperback with tabs supports code navigation. The Journeyman Electrician Flash Cards reinforce important information through repetition.

Electrical calculations should be practiced consistently before the exam. Candidates should review Ohm’s Law, power formulas, amperage, voltage, resistance, watts, conductor sizing, branch circuit calculations, feeder concepts, service load concepts, box fill, conduit fill, motor calculations, transformer concepts, overcurrent protection, and voltage drop. Calculation questions can take more time than direct code questions, so repeated practice helps improve both speed and accuracy.

Code navigation should also be part of every study plan. Candidates should practice using the NEC table of contents, index, article numbers, definitions, tables, notes, and exceptions. The goal is to identify the topic quickly and move to the correct section without wasting time. A candidate who understands how the NEC is organized is better prepared for open-book electrical testing.

Services, feeders, and branch circuits should receive careful attention. Candidates should review service conductors, service disconnects, grounding at services, feeder sizing, branch circuit ratings, required outlets, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, and load calculations. These topics connect code knowledge with practical installation skill.

Grounding and bonding are important areas for journeyman exam preparation. Candidates should review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, raceway bonding, panel bonding, grounded conductors, separately derived systems, and related safety requirements. Grounding and bonding questions often require careful reading because similar terms can lead to different code requirements.

Wiring methods and materials should be studied consistently. Candidates should review conductors, cables, raceways, boxes, fittings, supports, conductor ampacity, insulation types, flexible wiring methods, underground wiring, cabinets, panelboards, switchboards, and installation conditions. These topics appear frequently in electrical work and are central to NEC-based study.

Motors, transformers, controls, and equipment should also be part of the study routine. Candidates should review motor branch circuits, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, disconnecting means, controllers, transformer sizing, transformer protection, luminaires, receptacles, switches, appliances, panelboards, and related installation requirements.

Special occupancies, special equipment, and special conditions should not be ignored. Candidates should review hazardous locations, health care facilities, emergency systems, standby systems, swimming pools, mobile homes, RV parks, data processing equipment, fire alarm systems, low-voltage systems, and similar topics when they appear in the exam outline.

Flash cards are useful for shorter study sessions. Candidates can use them to review formulas, definitions, electrical theory, safety concepts, NEC topics, and trade terms before work, after work, during breaks, or between longer study blocks. Flash cards work best when paired with deeper reading, calculation drills, code lookup practice, and timed review.

A strong study plan should include scheduled reading, NEC navigation, calculation drills, timed practice, and repeated review of weaker topics. Candidates should avoid studying only familiar material. Virginia journeyman electrician preparation rewards steady practice, careful reading, and the ability to apply electrical knowledge under exam conditions.
