Exam Details

The South Dakota Journeyman Electrician examination is administered under the South Dakota Electrical Commission. Candidates must meet the licensing requirements for the license classification they choose before taking the examination. The candidate must submit the appropriate application and required application fee to the Commission office before being approved to test.

The South Dakota Journeyman Electrician exam is commonly listed with 80 questions, a testing time of 4 hours, and a required passing score of 70%. The exam is designed to test the candidate’s ability to understand and apply electrical code requirements, electrical theory, practical installation knowledge, and state electrical requirements used in South Dakota electrical work.

South Dakota examination information states that all tests are based on the 2023 National Electrical Code beginning December 2, 2024. This product is based on the 2026 NEC and includes the National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback for updated study and code navigation practice. This exam requires both electrical understanding and careful test management. Open Book Test

The South Dakota Journeyman Electrician examination is administered as an open book examination with approved reference rules. Open book testing allows candidates to use authorized materials during the exam, but it still requires serious preparation. A code book is only useful when the candidate understands how to locate information, read the rule correctly, and apply it within the time allowed.

Open book electrical exams can be challenging because the NEC is large and detailed. Candidates who have not practiced reference navigation may spend too much time searching for answers. Strong preparation includes learning how to use the NEC index, article headings, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and related sections. A student should know how to turn a question into a searchable code topic and then apply the rule to the condition described.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback included in this combo gives students a physical code reference for building those skills during study. Open book study should include active NEC navigation. This builds the habit of using the NEC as a working reference instead of simply reading it from front to back.

Exam-day reference rules may include requirements for code edition, book condition, tabs, highlighting, notes, calculators, identification, and testing room procedures. Licensing Steps

South Dakota journeyman electrician candidates begin by registering and working as licensed apprentice electricians under proper supervision. Apprentices must gain practical electrical experience while working under the employment and supervision of an electrical contractor. Maintaining accurate apprentice records helps support future journeyman eligibility.

The standard South Dakota journeyman path requires four years of experience, for a total of 8,000 hours, as a licensed apprentice electrician working under the employment and supervision of an electrical contractor. After completing this experience, the candidate is ready to apply to write the journeyman electrician examination.

After meeting the experience requirement, the candidate submits the proper application to the South Dakota Electrical Commission. The application should include the required form, application fee, experience documentation, and any supporting information required by the Commission. Candidates should make sure employment records, supervision details, and dates are accurate before submitting the application.

Once the application is approved, the candidate follows the Commission’s exam scheduling instructions. Candidates should review the current examination information before testing so they understand the exam format, approved references, identification requirements, calculator rules, testing procedures, score reporting, and retake procedures.

After passing the examination, the candidate completes the remaining licensing requirements through the South Dakota Electrical Commission. Passing the exam is an important step, but the licensing process is not complete until all state requirements are satisfied and the journeyman electrician license is issued.

State Requirements

South Dakota requires electricians to be licensed through the South Dakota Electrical Commission. The Commission issues licenses and oversees electrical regulation for electrical work performed in the state. This statewide structure applies to electrical licensing throughout South Dakota and is separate from local-only journeyman licensing systems used in some other states.

The South Dakota journeyman electrician path requires four years of experience, totaling 8,000 hours, as a licensed apprentice electrician working under the employment and supervision of an electrical contractor. This experience requirement supports the candidate’s eligibility to take the journeyman electrician examination.

South Dakota also provides a path toward electrical contractor licensing after journeyman licensure. After becoming a licensed journeyman, a candidate must work an additional two years as a licensed journeyman, totaling 4,000 hours of additional work under the employment and supervision of a contractor, including at least 2,000 hours of commercial work, before being ready to write the electrical contractor examination.

The South Dakota journeyman electrician license is different from an electrical contractor license. A journeyman license supports individual electrical work within the state licensing framework, while contractor licensing involves additional experience and a separate examination path. Candidates should apply for the credential that matches their current experience and career goal.

License holders should also follow South Dakota Electrical Commission requirements for renewal, code updates, and license maintenance. Continuing education and code awareness help electricians stay current with changes to the National Electrical Code, state electrical requirements, and safe installation practices.

Students should also include timed practice. Since the South Dakota Journeyman Electrician exam is timed and open book, pacing matters.
