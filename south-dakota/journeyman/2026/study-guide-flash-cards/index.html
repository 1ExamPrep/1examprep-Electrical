Exam Details

South Dakota electrician licensing is handled through the South Dakota Electrical Commission, which is part of the South Dakota Department of Labor and Regulation. The Commission administers state laws and regulations concerning electrical wiring, inspects wiring installations, investigates electrical wiring complaints, and licenses electricians within the state.

South Dakota has statewide electrical licensing. The licensing structure includes apprentice electricians, journeyman electricians, electrical contractors, Class B electricians, and other electrical classifications. This product is focused on South Dakota journeyman electrician exam preparation and journeyman-level electrical study.

South Dakota electrical exams are based on the 2023 National Electrical Code. The South Dakota Electrical Commission states that starting December 2, 2024, all tests are on the 2023 NEC code. Candidates must meet the requirements for the license selected, submit the appropriate application with the required application fee, and receive examination eligibility before testing.

The South Dakota Journeyman Electrician exam is commonly listed as 80 questions with 4 hours allowed. The passing score is 70%. The exam is open book and should be prepared for as an NEC-based electrical licensing exam that tests both code knowledge and practical electrical understanding.

Students preparing for the South Dakota Journeyman Electrician exam should study general electrical knowledge, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, wiring methods and materials, motors, controls, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, communication systems, calculations, electrical theory, safety, and the structure of the 2023 National Electrical Code.

Open Book Test

The South Dakota Journeyman Electrician exam is an open-book examination. Open book does not mean easy. Candidates still need to understand electrical concepts, recognize how questions are written, locate information quickly, apply code rules correctly, and manage time. A student who depends only on searching through references for every answer can lose valuable time during the exam.

Open-book electrical exams reward reference familiarity. Students should practice using the National Electrical Code table of contents, index, article structure, definitions, tables, exceptions, notes, and common code sections before exam day. Students should also practice calculations repeatedly so conductor sizing, load relationships, grounding and bonding rules, motor requirements, box fill, raceway fill, voltage drop concepts, and overcurrent protection requirements become easier to recognize.

South Dakota exam preparation should be tied to the 2023 National Electrical Code. Candidates should become comfortable moving through Articles 90, 100, 110, 200, 210, 215, 220, 225, 230, 240, 250, 300, 310, 314, 320 through 399, 400, 408, 422, 430, 440, 445, 450, 500 through 590, 600, 645, 680, 690, 700, 705, 725, 760, 800, and other areas that commonly appear in journeyman-level electrical review.

Reference rules may vary by the testing provider and current Commission instructions. Approved references may be allowed with limited preparation such as highlighting or tabs, but candidates should follow the current testing rules for book condition, notes, tabs, loose papers, copied materials, calculators, and exam-room conduct. Practice exams, study guides, removable notes, and unauthorized materials should be used before test day and should not be treated as exam-room references.

A strong open-book strategy includes learning where major NEC topics are located before the exam. Students should practice identifying the correct article, checking definitions first, reading table notes carefully, reviewing exceptions, and confirming whether the question asks for a minimum, maximum, permitted condition, prohibited condition, or calculation result. Flash cards help students recall key concepts faster, while practice exams help build the habit of reading questions carefully and choosing the correct reference path.

Licensing Steps

South Dakota journeyman electrician licensing begins with apprentice registration and supervised electrical work. A candidate must complete qualifying experience as a licensed apprentice electrician while working under the employment and supervision of an electrical contractor. The South Dakota Electrical Commission identifies four years of apprentice work, for a total of 8,000 hours, as the experience needed before a candidate is ready to write the journeyman examination.

A typical South Dakota journeyman path includes registering as an apprentice electrician, gaining supervised on-the-job electrical experience, completing the required apprentice period, documenting work under a licensed electrical contractor, submitting the journeyman examination application, paying the required application fee, receiving exam eligibility, passing the journeyman examination, and completing the remaining licensing steps through the Commission.

Students should keep accurate records during apprenticeship. Helpful records include apprentice registration information, employer names, dates of employment, supervised work hours, work categories, contractor supervision, classroom or apprenticeship training records, application materials, exam notices, and license documents. Accurate records make the application process easier when the candidate is ready to apply for the journeyman examination.

After becoming a licensed journeyman electrician, a person who wants to move toward South Dakota electrical contractor licensing must complete additional qualifying work. South Dakota identifies two years as a licensed journeyman, with 4,000 hours of additional work under the employment and supervision of a contractor, including at least 2,000 hours of commercial work, before writing the electrical contractor examination.

Students can use it while completing apprenticeship work, while documenting experience, before submitting the journeyman application, after receiving examination approval, or during final review before the scheduled exam date.
