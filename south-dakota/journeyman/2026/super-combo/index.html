Exam Details

The South Dakota Journeyman Electrician exam is connected to the South Dakota Electrical Commission licensing process. Candidates must meet the requirements for the license they are seeking and submit the required application to the Commission before taking the examination. Once the application process is completed, candidates follow the exam instructions for scheduling and testing.

The South Dakota Journeyman Electrician exam is commonly identified as the ICC 531 South Dakota Journeyman Electrician examination. The exam is administered as a computer-based test through Pearson VUE. The exam has 80 multiple-choice questions and a 4-hour time limit. The passing score is 70%.

South Dakota exam information states that all electrical tests moved to the 2023 National Electrical Code beginning December 2, 2024. This Super Combo is based on the 2026 National Electrical Code for candidates who want current-code study materials, NEC navigation practice, and forward-looking preparation. Candidates should follow the South Dakota Electrical Commission and exam provider instructions for the exact NEC edition used for their scheduled test.

The South Dakota Journeyman Electrician exam covers major journeyman-level electrical knowledge areas. These include general electrical knowledge, electrical theory, load calculations, code definitions, plan reading, services and service equipment, feeders, branch circuits, conductors, wiring methods and materials, equipment and devices, control devices, motors, generators, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, safety, and calculations.

Because the exam is timed, candidates should practice answering questions efficiently. An 80-question, 4-hour exam gives candidates time to work carefully, but code lookup and calculations can still consume time quickly. Candidates should practice locating NEC articles and tables, reading questions closely, solving math problems, and moving through the exam with a steady pace.

The South Dakota journeyman license is different from a contractor license. A journeyman electrician license is an individual license tied to trade qualifications and examination. Electrical contractor licensing involves additional work experience after becoming a licensed journeyman and additional requirements through the South Dakota Electrical Commission.

Open Book Test

The South Dakota Journeyman Electrician exam is open book under the exam provider’s reference rules. Candidates should follow the current ICC, Pearson VUE, and South Dakota Electrical Commission instructions for approved references, code edition, tab rules, calculator rules, markings, and prohibited materials.

Open-book electrical exams still require serious preparation. Having a code book available does not mean every answer will be easy to find. Candidates need to recognize the topic being tested, move to the correct article or table, understand the wording, apply the rule to the question, and manage time. A candidate who is slow with reference navigation can lose valuable minutes even when the exam allows references.

Reference rules may address whether books must be bound, whether tabs must be permanent, whether highlighting is allowed, whether written notes are permitted, and whether materials can be marked during the exam. Candidates should prepare reference materials according to the current rules before test day and avoid bringing unauthorized papers, inserts, removable notes, loose pages, or unapproved materials.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs included in this Super Combo is intended for study and code navigation practice. Licensing Steps

South Dakota candidates should begin by confirming that the journeyman electrician license is the correct license classification for their work goals. South Dakota issues multiple electrical license types, including apprentice, journeyman, and contractor-level credentials. Each classification has its own requirements and work authority.

A practical licensing path begins with registering and working as an apprentice electrician under the employment and supervision of an electrical contractor. South Dakota requires four years, for a total of 8,000 hours, as a licensed apprentice electrician working under the employment and supervision of an electrical contractor before a candidate is ready to write the journeyman examination.

After meeting the required experience, the candidate submits the proper application to the South Dakota Electrical Commission. The Commission reviews the application and determines whether the candidate meets the requirements for the license examination. Candidates should keep accurate records of employment, supervision, work dates, hours, and the type of electrical work performed.

Once approved for testing, the candidate follows the exam scheduling instructions, prepares the approved reference materials, studies the current content outline, and takes the South Dakota Journeyman Electrician exam. After passing the exam, the candidate completes the remaining licensing steps through the South Dakota Electrical Commission.

Candidates who plan to advance beyond journeyman status should understand the contractor path. South Dakota identifies additional experience after becoming a licensed journeyman before a candidate is ready to write the electrical contractor examination. That contractor path includes additional supervised employment and commercial work experience under the requirements set by the Commission.

State Requirements

The South Dakota Electrical Commission administers the state laws and regulations concerning electrical wiring, inspects wiring installations, investigates complaints related to electrical wiring, and licenses electricians within the state. South Dakota has a state-level licensing structure for electricians, which gives candidates a clear state process to follow.

For the journeyman electrician path, South Dakota requires four years, totaling 8,000 hours, as a licensed apprentice electrician working under the employment and supervision of an electrical contractor. Candidates should make sure their apprentice registration and work experience records remain accurate throughout the training period.

South Dakota recognizes a progression in electrical licensing. An apprentice gains supervised experience. A journeyman electrician demonstrates trade-level competency and passes the required exam. A contractor-level applicant completes additional requirements after becoming a licensed journeyman. Candidates should choose the correct license path based on their current experience and professional goal.

South Dakota candidates should also pay attention to renewal, continuing education, and code update requirements. Active license holders are responsible for maintaining their license according to Commission rules. Electrical codes change over time, and license holders should stay current with code updates, state rules, and safe installation practices.

Study materials help candidates prepare, but licensing decisions are made by the South Dakota Electrical Commission based on the applicant’s qualifications, documentation, examination result, and compliance with state requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

The South Dakota Journeyman Electrician Super Combo supports a complete preparation routine by combining structured study, code review, calculation practice, and quick recall. The South Dakota Journeyman Electrician Study Guide helps candidates organize preparation around journeyman-level electrical knowledge. The Electrician Calculations Study Guide strengthens math-based preparation. The NEC paperback with tabs supports code navigation. The Journeyman Electrician Flash Cards reinforce important information through repetition.

Electrical calculations should be practiced consistently before the exam. Candidates should review Ohm’s Law, power formulas, amperage, voltage, resistance, watts, conductor sizing, branch circuit calculations, feeder concepts, service load concepts, box fill, conduit fill, motor calculations, transformer concepts, overcurrent protection, and voltage drop. Calculation questions can take more time than direct code questions, so repeated practice helps improve both speed and accuracy.

Code navigation should also be part of every study plan. Candidates should practice using the NEC table of contents, index, article numbers, definitions, tables, notes, and exceptions. The goal is to identify the topic quickly and move to the correct section without wasting time. A candidate who understands how the NEC is organized is better prepared for open-book electrical testing.

Services, feeders, and branch circuits should receive careful attention. Candidates should review service conductors, service disconnects, grounding at services, feeder sizing, branch circuit ratings, required outlets, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, and load calculations. These topics connect code knowledge with practical installation skill.

Grounding and bonding are important areas for journeyman exam preparation. Candidates should review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, raceway bonding, panel bonding, grounded conductors, separately derived systems, and related safety requirements. Grounding and bonding questions often require careful reading because similar terms can lead to different code requirements.

Wiring methods and materials should be studied consistently. Candidates should review conductors, cables, raceways, boxes, fittings, supports, conductor ampacity, insulation types, flexible wiring methods, underground wiring, cabinets, panelboards, switchboards, and installation conditions. These topics appear frequently in electrical work and are central to NEC-based study.

Motors, generators, controls, and equipment should also be part of the study routine. Candidates should review motor branch circuits, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, disconnecting means, controllers, generator requirements, transformer sizing, transformer protection, luminaires, receptacles, switches, appliances, and related installation requirements.

Special occupancies, special equipment, and special conditions should not be ignored. Candidates should review hazardous locations, health care facilities, emergency systems, standby systems, swimming pools, mobile homes, RV parks, data processing equipment, fire alarm systems, low-voltage systems, and similar topics when they appear in the content outline.

Flash cards are useful for shorter study sessions. Candidates can use them to review formulas, definitions, electrical theory, safety concepts, NEC topics, and trade terms before work, after work, during breaks, or between longer study blocks. Flash cards work best when paired with deeper reading, calculation drills, and code lookup practice.

A strong study plan should include scheduled reading, NEC navigation, calculation drills, timed practice, and repeated review of weaker topics. Candidates should avoid studying only familiar material. Journeyman electrician preparation rewards steady practice, careful reading, and the ability to apply electrical knowledge under exam conditions.
