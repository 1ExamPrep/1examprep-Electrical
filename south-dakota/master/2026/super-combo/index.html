Exam Details

South Dakota electrical licensing is handled through the South Dakota Electrical Commission, which is part of the South Dakota Department of Labor and Regulation. The Commission administers state laws and regulations concerning electrical wiring, inspects wiring installations, investigates electrical wiring complaints, and licenses electricians within the state.

South Dakota requires candidates to meet the requirements for the license category they have chosen before sitting for an examination. The candidate must submit the required application and fee to the Commission office. After the application is approved, the Commission sends the candidate the details needed to locate the appropriate exam site and sit for the examination. The Commission states that approved candidates have 90 days to make exam arrangements, and candidates may not take the exam without prior written approval.

South Dakota master-level electrical study is closely connected with the Electrical Contractor licensing path. The state licensing page explains that after completing four years, for a total of 8,000 hours, as a licensed apprentice electrician working under the employment and supervision of an electrical contractor, a person may be ready to write the journeyman’s exam. After that, the candidate needs another two years as a licensed journeyman, with a total of 4,000 additional hours under the employment and supervision of a contractor, including at least 2,000 hours of commercial work, to write the electrical contractor’s exam.

The Commission states that experience hours must be licensed in order for working time to count toward the hours required to take the exams. This makes recordkeeping, licensing status, and supervised experience important parts of the South Dakota path. Candidates preparing for contractor-level testing should also be prepared to follow the Commission’s application process, exam authorization process, bond application requirements when applicable, and continuing education rules for renewal.

South Dakota examination preparation should include NEC study, electrical calculations, wiring methods, services, feeders, branch circuits, conductors, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, motors, transformers, special occupancies, special equipment, inspection awareness, and practical code application. Licensing Steps

A practical South Dakota licensing path begins by confirming the correct license category. South Dakota licenses electricians through the South Dakota Electrical Commission, and candidates should make sure they understand whether they are pursuing apprentice registration, journeyman licensing, electrical contractor licensing, or another category recognized by the Commission.

The first major step is gaining licensed apprentice experience under the employment and supervision of an electrical contractor. South Dakota identifies four years, totaling 8,000 hours, as the experience path toward the journeyman examination. Candidates should keep experience documentation organized because only licensed experience hours count toward the hours required to take the exams.

After meeting the journeyman requirements, the candidate submits the required application and fee to the South Dakota Electrical Commission. Once approved, the Commission sends exam scheduling details. The candidate then takes the journeyman exam according to the Commission’s instructions.

The next step toward contractor-level licensing is additional journeyman-level experience. South Dakota identifies another two years as a licensed journeyman, totaling 4,000 additional hours under the employment and supervision of a contractor. At least 2,000 of those hours must be commercial work. After meeting that experience requirement, the candidate may apply to write the electrical contractor’s exam.

After the contractor-level application is approved, the candidate follows the Commission’s exam authorization instructions. Candidates have 90 days to make arrangements after approval, and no candidate may take the examination without written approval from the Commission. If the candidate passes and satisfies the remaining licensing requirements, the candidate completes the licensing process through the Commission.

South Dakota also requires licensees and registrants to meet continuing education requirements for renewal. The Commission identifies 16 hours of continuing education, including a minimum of 8 code hours, for license renewal. This requirement reflects the ongoing responsibility electricians have to stay current with code, safety, and licensing expectations.

State Requirements

South Dakota regulates electrical licensing through the South Dakota Electrical Commission under the Department of Labor and Regulation. The Commission administers electrical wiring laws and regulations, performs inspections, investigates complaints, and licenses electricians throughout the state.

South Dakota’s experience path is structured and progressive. Apprentice-level experience leads toward journeyman examination eligibility. Journeyman-level experience then leads toward electrical contractor examination eligibility. Candidates pursuing contractor-level authority should plan for both the technical exam and the experience documentation needed to support the application.

For journeyman examination eligibility, South Dakota identifies four years of licensed apprentice work, totaling 8,000 hours, under the employment and supervision of an electrical contractor. For electrical contractor examination eligibility, South Dakota identifies two additional years as a licensed journeyman, totaling 4,000 additional hours under the employment and supervision of a contractor, with at least 2,000 hours of commercial work.

South Dakota also identifies continuing education requirements for renewal. Licensees and registrants must have the required continuing education hours in order to renew. The state identifies 16 hours of continuing education, with at least 8 code hours, due by the time the renewal is submitted or by June 30, whichever is sooner.

Contractor and Class B Electrician applicants may also have undertaking and insurance-related requirements connected with the licensing process. South Dakota’s licensing page directs Electrical Contractor and Class B Electrician applicants to the Electrical Contractor and Class B Electricians’ Bond Application. Candidates should follow the current Commission forms and instructions for their specific license category.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong South Dakota Master Electrician study plan should include NEC study, calculation practice, trade knowledge review, state licensing review, reference navigation, recall practice, and timed question work. Electrical exams can cover a wide range of subjects, so candidates should avoid spending all study time on only one topic. A balanced routine helps build confidence across fundamental electricity, services, feeders, branch circuits, wiring methods, equipment, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, calculations, and practical installation concepts.

For NEC study, start by learning the structure of the code book. Review article organization, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and cross-references. Practice moving from a question to the correct article and reading the surrounding language carefully. Many electrical exam questions are missed because a candidate finds a related section but overlooks a condition, exception, table note, or specific wording.

For South Dakota licensing preparation, review the Commission’s licensing path and experience requirements. Contractor-level candidates should understand the role of licensed apprentice hours, journeyman experience, commercial work experience, application approval, written authorization, continuing education, and Commission forms. State licensing knowledge helps candidates see the full path, not just the exam itself.

For calculations, write out each step. Electrical calculation mistakes often happen when a candidate skips a conversion, uses the wrong table, applies the wrong value, or confuses branch circuit, feeder, service, motor, transformer, raceway fill, box fill, or voltage drop requirements. The 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide gives candidates a dedicated way to practice these skills repeatedly.

For open book preparation, use the NEC actively during study. Practice locating sections, reading tables, and confirming answers in the code book. Then practice some questions without immediately opening the code book so you can build recognition. The stronger your recall becomes, the more time you can save during questions that require deeper code lookup.

For flash card study, use short sessions throughout the week. Flash cards can help reinforce formulas, definitions, code concepts, and common electrical terms. They are useful before work, during breaks, after a longer study block, or as final review. Flash cards should not replace full study sessions, but they help strengthen recall and recognition.
