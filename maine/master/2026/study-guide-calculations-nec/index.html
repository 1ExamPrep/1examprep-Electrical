Exam Details

The Maine Master Electrician examination is part of the State of Maine professional licensing examination program. Maine has contracted with Prov, Inc. to provide examinations for approved candidates. Candidates must submit an examination application to the Maine Electricians’ Examining Board and receive Board approval before sitting for the examination.

The Maine Master Electrician exam includes 100 questions and allows 4 hours of testing time. The exam is designed for candidates who have the knowledge and skills to install, repair, alter, add to, or change electrical wires, fixtures, appliances, apparatus, raceways, conduit, or related electrical parts that generate, transmit, transform, or use electrical energy for light, heat, power, or communications in compliance with applicable plans, specifications, codes, laws, and regulations.

The Maine Master Electrician exam content outline includes general electrical knowledge, communication systems, equipment for general use, service feeders and branch circuits, motors and controls, special conditions, special equipment, special occupancies, wiring and protection, and wiring methods and materials. These areas require candidates to understand both practical electrical work and National Electrical Code organization.

Because the exam is timed, preparation should focus on both knowledge and efficiency. Open Book Test

The Maine electrician examinations are delivered in open-book format. For electrician tests, the candidate bulletin identifies the allowed National Electrical Code reference and provides rules for how the book may be used in the examination center. Candidates should follow the current Maine candidate bulletin for the exact NEC edition and exam-room book rules that apply to their scheduled exam.

Open-book testing does not mean the exam is easy. Master electrician exams require candidates to locate code material quickly, read the correct section carefully, understand the question being asked, and apply the requirement accurately. A candidate who has not practiced with the NEC may lose valuable time searching through unrelated sections.

Strong NEC navigation is one of the most important skills for an open-book electrical exam. Licensing Steps

Maine electrical licensing is handled through the Maine Electricians’ Examining Board. A typical master electrician licensing path includes reviewing the license requirements, completing the required application, documenting qualifying experience and education, receiving approval to test, scheduling the examination through the testing provider, preparing with code-based study materials, passing the required exam, and completing final licensing steps with the Board.

Maine’s master electrician requirements include qualifying through examination or reciprocity. The examination path includes documented electrical experience and an approved program of study. Maine also recognizes reciprocity for certain applicants who are licensed in states or territories with a reciprocity agreement, have passed a written examination from the reciprocating state, and meet the required active work experience conditions.

Candidates should begin by reviewing the Maine license classification they are pursuing. A master electrician license is different from a journeyman electrician license or a limited electrician license. Each classification may have its own scope, experience requirements, education requirements, and exam category. Selecting the correct license type is an important part of the process.

After confirming the correct license type, candidates submit the required examination application and wait for Board approval. Once approved, candidates receive instructions for scheduling the examination. Prov is not responsible for determining which examination a candidate must take, so applicants should resolve license-category questions with the Board before registering for an exam.

Once the exam path is clear, candidates can use this combo to build a study schedule. State Requirements

The Maine Electricians’ Examining Board oversees electrician licensing in Maine. The state licensing structure includes master electrician, senior master electrician, journeyman electrician, journeyman-in-training electrician, apprentice electrician, helper electrician, and limited electrician license classifications. Candidates should apply under the license classification that matches their experience, education, and work goals.

For the master electrician examination path, Maine requires qualifying experience and an approved program of study. The state lists a pathway that includes at least 4,000 hours of service as a journeyman electrician or at least 12,000 hours of experience in electrical installations, along with a program of study that includes electrical, electronics, math, controls, motors, blueprint reading or drafting, transformers, current National Electrical Code coursework, and approved degree-related coursework.

Maine also has renewal requirements for master electrician licenses. The renewal term is two years based on the first licensure date, and renewal includes continuing education in the current National Electrical Code course as adopted by the Board. Candidates and license holders should keep records of licensing documents, education, continuing education, examination approval, exam scheduling, and score results.
