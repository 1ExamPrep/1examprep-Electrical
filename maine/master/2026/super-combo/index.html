Exam Details

The Maine Electricians’ Examining Board oversees the licensing process for electricians in Maine, and the State of Maine has contracted with Prov, Inc. to administer its professional licensing examination program. Candidates must receive approval from the Board before scheduling an examination. Prov is not authorized to decide which exam a candidate must take, so candidates should resolve exam eligibility and license classification questions with the licensing authority before registering.

The Maine Master Electrician examination is listed with 100 questions and a 4-hour time limit. Maine’s published information also states that Master Electrician candidates must score at least 70 percent to pass. The exam is computer-based and administered through Prov testing.

The published Maine Master Electrician content outline includes General Electrical Knowledge, Communication Systems, Equipment for General Use, Service Feeders and Branch Circuits, Motors & Controls, Special Conditions, Special Equipment, Special Occupancies, Wiring & Protection, and Wiring Methods & Materials. Wiring Methods & Materials and Wiring & Protection make up a significant share of the listed exam outline, while other categories require focused review as well.

The current Maine candidate bulletin lists references for the Master Electrician exam, including the American Electrician’s Handbook, Maine Laws and Rules, Mike Holt’s Illustrated Guide to Basic Electrical Theory, and NFPA 70 National Electrical Code 2023. Candidates should match final exam preparation to the code edition and references required for their scheduled exam.

Closed Book Test

The State of Maine candidate information bulletin states that electrician examinations are delivered in closed book format unless references are specifically marked as permitted. The listed Maine Master Electrician references are not marked as permitted in the bulletin, so candidates should prepare for the exam as a closed book test unless their official exam authorization or updated bulletin states otherwise.

A closed book electrical exam requires strong recall, careful reasoning, and repeated practice. Candidates cannot rely on looking up every code section during the testing session. Instead, they need to build familiarity with major electrical concepts, common NEC structure, formulas, definitions, wiring rules, equipment requirements, and calculation steps before exam day.

Licensing Steps

Maine master electrician licensing is handled through the Maine Electricians’ Examining Board. A Master Electrician is described by the Board as a qualified individual engaging in, or about to engage in, the business of installing electrical wires, conduits, apparatus, fixtures, and other electrical equipment.

A practical licensing path begins by confirming that the Master Electrician classification is the correct license for the work you plan to perform. Candidates should review the Board’s current requirements, gather the required education and work experience documentation, submit the examination application, and receive Board approval before scheduling the exam. After passing the exam, candidates complete the license application process through the Board.

Maine’s published Master Electrician examination requirements include either at least 4,000 hours of service as a journeyman electrician or at least 12,000 hours of experience in electrical installations, along with a program of study that includes specified electrical courses and related coursework approved by the Electricians’ Examining Board or from an accredited institution.

Candidates should treat the study portion of the licensing process like a structured project. Start by reviewing the exam categories, then divide study time among code knowledge, trade concepts, calculations, theory, wiring methods, motors and controls, communication systems, equipment, and special installations. Use the study guide for organized review, the calculations guide for repeated math practice, the NEC for code familiarity, and the flash cards for quick reinforcement.

State Requirements

Maine licenses electricians at the state level through the Electricians’ Examining Board, which is part of the Office of Professional and Occupational Regulation. The Board provides licensing information for Master Electrician, Senior Master Electrician, Limited Electrician, Journeyman Electrician, Apprentice Electrician, Helper Electrician, and related license categories.

For the Master Electrician license, Maine requires candidates pursuing examination to meet the Board’s experience and education requirements before approval to test. The Board also identifies a two-year license term for Master Electricians and requires completion of a 15-hour current National Electrical Code course as adopted by the Board for renewal.

Maine also publishes reciprocity information for Master Electricians. The Board states that Maine presently has reciprocity agreements for Master Electricians with New Hampshire and Vermont, provided the applicant meets the Board’s reciprocity requirements, including licensure from a reciprocating state, a written examination, and active work as an electrician for the required period.

Because licensing requirements, forms, fees, exam references, and code-cycle rules can change, candidates should follow the Board’s current application and examination process. Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Maine Master Electrician study plan should include content review, calculation practice, NEC study, and memory reinforcement. Because the exam is closed book under the current candidate bulletin format, candidates should focus on building both understanding and recall. Reading the material once is not enough. The better approach is to study by topic, answer practice-style questions, review missed items, and repeat weak areas until the reasoning becomes clearer.

For code study, use the NEC during preparation to understand how requirements are organized. Review article structure, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and cross-references. Even when a test is closed book, working with the code book during study helps candidates understand the language and logic behind electrical rules. This is especially important for questions involving wiring methods, overcurrent protection, services, feeders, branch circuits, equipment, motors, grounding and bonding, and special conditions.

For calculations, write out each step. Calculation mistakes often come from skipping a conversion, selecting the wrong value, applying the wrong formula, or confusing branch circuit, feeder, and service requirements. Flash cards should not replace full study sessions, but they are an effective way to keep important information fresh.
