## Closed Book Test

The Maine electrician examinations are delivered in a **closed book** format unless a reference is specifically marked as permitted for the exam. The current Maine candidate bulletin states that all electrician examinations are closed book and that books are not allowed in the testing room unless the bulletin marks a reference as permitted. For the Master Electrician exam, no exam-room reference books are identified as permitted.

A closed book electrical exam requires a different study strategy than an open book exam. Students cannot depend on looking up every answer during the test. They need to build familiarity with major code concepts, common calculations, subject categories, and electrical rules before exam day. The NEC is still an important study tool, but the goal is to learn the structure, terminology, and logic of the code during preparation rather than relying on the book inside the testing room.

## Licensing Steps

The Maine master electrician licensing path begins with meeting the state’s education and experience requirements. For examination, Maine requires either at least **4,000 hours of service as a journeyman electrician** or at least **12,000 hours of experience in electrical installations**, along with completion of an approved program of study. The listed program includes required coursework in subjects such as Electricity I, Electricity II, Electronics I, Math I, Controls I, Controls II, Motors, Blueprint Reading/Drafting, Transformers, a current National Electrical Code course, and approved degree-related coursework.

After meeting the required education and experience background, the candidate submits an examination application to the Maine Electricians’ Examining Board. The application must include the required documentation so the Board can determine whether the applicant is qualified to sit for the exam. Candidates must receive approval from the Board before scheduling an exam appointment.

Once the Board approves the candidate, the candidate receives an exam approval notice with scheduling instructions. Maine uses Prov for examination administration. Candidates schedule through Prov, select an available testing location or approved testing option, and pay the required examination fee.

On exam day, the candidate completes the computer-administered Master Electrician exam. Results are available at the testing center after the exam is completed. After passing, the candidate completes the online license application process through the state. The Master Electrician license is issued for a two-year term from the date of first licensure.

After licensure, Maine master electricians must meet renewal requirements. Current state information identifies a biennial renewal process and a 15-hour current National Electrical Code course as part of continuing education for renewal. Licensees should keep their license active, complete renewal requirements on time, and maintain compliance with the rules of the Electricians’ Examining Board.

## State Requirements

Maine’s electrical licensing program is regulated through the Electricians’ Examining Board within the Office of Professional and Occupational Regulation. The Board was established to protect the public from hazards related to the use of electricity for light, heat, power, and other purposes through the regulation of electricians in Maine.

A Maine Master Electrician is a qualified individual who engages in, or is preparing to engage in, the business of installing electrical wires, conduits, apparatus, fixtures, and other electrical equipment. The state also issues other electrical license types, including journeyman electrician, limited electrician, apprentice electrician, helper electrician, journeyman-in-training electrician, and senior license categories.

For master electrician examination eligibility, Maine identifies a pathway involving journeyman service or extensive electrical installation experience plus an approved program of study. This makes the Master Electrician credential an advanced electrical license intended for candidates with substantial training and practical experience.

Maine also recognizes reciprocity for master electricians with New Hampshire and Vermont when the applicant meets the state’s reciprocity conditions. Reciprocity applicants must be licensed in a reciprocating state, have passed a written examination from that state, and be actively engaged in electrical work for the required period identified by Maine.

Electrical permitting is also part of Maine’s regulatory structure. State FAQ information states that no electrical installation may be installed or altered unless the master or limited electrician making the installation first obtains a permit from the Board, subject to listed exceptions. This reinforces the importance of proper licensing, permitting, and code-compliant work.
