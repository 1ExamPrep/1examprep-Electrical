Exam Details

Maine journeyman electrician licensing examinations are administered through Prov for the State of Maine Office of Professional and Occupational Regulation. Candidates must submit an examination application to the Electricians’ Examining Board and receive Board approval before scheduling the exam. Once approved, candidates receive exam scheduling instructions and may schedule through Prov.

The Maine Journeyman Electrician examination is listed with 80 questions, a testing time of 3 hours, and a required passing score of 70%. The exam is multiple choice and is designed to test the candidate’s knowledge of electrical work associated with the planning, installation, repair, alteration, and maintenance of electrical conductors, raceways, and equipment for light, heat, and power while employed by a Master Electrician.

The Maine Journeyman Electrician exam content outline includes 10 questions on General Electrical Theory and Principles, 16 questions on Wiring and Protection, 16 questions on Wiring Methods and Materials, 10 questions on Motors and Controls, 10 questions on Equipment for General Use, 5 questions on Special Occupancies, 5 questions on Special Equipment, 5 questions on Special Conditions, and 3 questions on Communication Systems.

This outline shows why students should study across the full range of electrical topics. Wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, motors and controls, equipment for general use, and general electrical theory make up a large portion of the exam, but smaller areas such as communication systems, special conditions, special occupancies, and special equipment can still affect the final score. A strong preparation plan should not ignore any category.

Because the exam is timed, candidates should prepare for both accuracy and pacing. A student may understand the electrical topic but still lose time if they are not comfortable reading a question, identifying the subject, finding the correct reference area, or working through a calculation. The best preparation combines topic review, NEC navigation practice, and repeated work with electrical math.

Closed Book Test

The Maine electrician examination bulletin lists electrician examinations as closed book unless specific references are marked as permitted. For the Maine Journeyman Electrician exam, the listed references are study references, but the examination is delivered in closed book format unless the current bulletin identifies a reference as permitted for the testing room.

Closed book testing requires a different kind of preparation than a fully open book exam. Candidates should still study with the National Electrical Code, but they should not rely on being able to search the code book during every question. Instead, students should focus on understanding concepts, recognizing common code requirements, remembering where key rules are located, and practicing enough questions to build recall and confidence.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback included in this combo gives students a physical code reference for study and review. During preparation, students can practice locating common journeyman exam topics such as wiring methods, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding electrode systems, bonding, equipment grounding conductors, raceways, boxes, motors, equipment for general use, communication systems, and special occupancies.

Closed book study should include active recall. This process helps turn reference reading into working knowledge, which is especially important when the exam does not allow routine code lookup in the testing room.

Licensing Steps

Maine journeyman electrician candidates begin by meeting the state’s qualification requirements for the license. Candidates should review the Electricians’ Examining Board application instructions and complete the proper examination application for the journeyman electrician classification.

The applicant must submit an examination application and receive approval from the Board before scheduling the examination. Maine’s licensing information states that Board approval is required before the candidate schedules the exam. After approval, the candidate receives instructions for scheduling the exam appointment through Prov.

After scheduling the exam, the candidate should review the candidate information bulletin, exam content outline, testing locations, reference rules, identification requirements, rescheduling rules, and score procedures. The exam is delivered by computer at Prov testing locations.

After passing the examination, the candidate completes the license application process. Maine’s licensing information states that a journeyman candidate must complete an application for licensure and submit sufficient proof of the required work experience after passing the exam. Passing the exam is an important step, but the license is not complete until the Board’s licensing requirements are satisfied.

A strong licensing plan begins early. Candidates should organize work records, apprenticeship documentation, education records when applicable, employment verification, and any supporting documents required by the Board. Exam preparation is usually strongest when students study over time through trade review, code study, and calculation practice instead of waiting until the exam date is close.

State Requirements

Maine requires electricians to be licensed through the state electrical licensing program. The Electricians’ Examining Board issues licenses and oversees examination requirements for electricians working within the state. License categories include Master Electrician, Journeyman Electrician, Limited Electrician, Apprentice Electrician, Journeyman-in-Training Electrician, Helper Electrician, and related senior license categories.

A Maine Journeyman Electrician is qualified and tested to perform electrical trade work involving the planning, installation, repair, alteration, and maintenance of electrical conductors, raceways, and equipment for light, heat, and power while employed by a Master Electrician. This makes the journeyman license an individual trade credential connected to practical electrical work under the state licensing structure.

Maine requires journeyman electrician candidates to pass an 80-question multiple-choice examination with a score of 70% or higher. Candidates must receive Board approval before scheduling the examination. After passing, the candidate must complete the licensing application and provide sufficient proof of the required work experience.

Maine also recognizes reciprocity for journeyman electricians from certain states. Maine’s journeyman electrician reciprocity information lists New Hampshire, Oregon, Idaho, Wyoming, North Dakota, and Vermont. Applicants using reciprocity must provide proof of licensure from the state where they are licensed and follow the Board’s reciprocity application process.

Maine journeyman electricians must also complete continuing education before renewal. The Board’s information states that journeyman electricians must complete 15 hours of approved current National Electrical Code update course work prior to renewal.

The 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide supports the math side of preparation. Electrical calculations often require several steps, and students should practice them repeatedly. Useful calculation areas include Ohm’s law, power formulas, conductor sizing, voltage drop concepts, box fill, raceway fill, load calculations, service and feeder calculations, branch circuit calculations, motor calculations, transformer calculations, and overcurrent protection applications.

Calculation study should be active. Students should work problems by hand, write formulas clearly, identify known values, convert units where needed, and check whether the answer makes sense. Reading a solved example may introduce a concept, but repeated practice builds speed and confidence. This is especially important for timed electrical exams because calculation questions can take longer than direct knowledge questions.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback should be used throughout the study process. Students should practice locating definitions, article sections, tables, notes, and exceptions. They should also learn how related code topics connect. A conductor question may involve ampacity, adjustment factors, terminal temperature limitations, overcurrent protection, and equipment rules. A grounding question may involve grounding electrodes, bonding jumpers, equipment grounding conductors, services, and fault-current paths.

A balanced study schedule may rotate between trade review, NEC navigation, and calculation practice. One session might focus on wiring methods and materials. Another might focus on wiring and protection. Another might involve motors and controls. Another might involve conductor sizing or box fill calculations. Another might involve locating special occupancy or communication system rules in the NEC. This rotation helps candidates prepare across the full range of journeyman-level electrical knowledge.

Students should also include timed practice. Since the Maine Journeyman Electrician exam is timed, pacing matters. Practice sessions should help candidates learn when to move forward, when to revisit a question, how to manage calculation time, and how to avoid spending too long on one item.
