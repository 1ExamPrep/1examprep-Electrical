Exam Details

The Maine Electricians’ Examining Board is responsible for ensuring the competency of electricians through examination and licensing. The Board issues licenses to qualified individuals who hold titles such as Master Electrician, Journeyman Electrician, Limited Electrician, Apprentice Electrician, Journeyman-in-Training Electrician, and Helper Electrician. A Maine Journeyman Electrician is a licensed individual who makes electrical installations while employed by a Master Electrician, Limited Electrician, or Electrical Company.

Maine’s examination program is administered through Prov. The Maine Candidate Information Bulletin identifies the Journeyman Electrician examination category and provides exam rules, scheduling information, reference information, and test-day procedures. Candidates should follow the current Maine and Prov instructions for application approval, scheduling, identification, reference materials, calculator rules, and score reporting.

The Maine Journeyman Electrician examination is designed to test a candidate’s knowledge of the planning, installation, repair, alteration, and maintenance of electric conductors, raceways, and equipment for light, heat, and power while employed by a Master Electrician. Candidates should prepare for both code-based questions and practical trade knowledge connected to safe electrical installation work.

Maine journeyman electrician preparation should include general electrical knowledge, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, wiring methods, motors, transformers, special occupancies, special equipment, low-voltage systems, electrical calculations, safety, and code navigation. The Maine journeyman electrician examination is an open book test. Open book electrical exams still require serious preparation. Having access to reference material does not mean every answer will be easy to find. Candidates must understand the structure of the code book, know electrical terminology, and recognize which article, table, exception, or requirement applies to the question.

The tabbed NEC included in this combo is designed to support code navigation practice before the exam. During preparation, candidates should practice locating Article 100 definitions, wiring and protection rules, wiring methods and materials, grounding and bonding rules, service and feeder requirements, box fill and conduit fill tables, motor provisions, special occupancies, special equipment, low-voltage provisions, and calculation-related tables.

Open book preparation should include repeated lookup drills. Licensing Steps

The Maine journeyman electrician licensing path begins with gaining qualifying experience as a licensed apprentice or helper and completing the required education. Maine identifies several pathways to qualify for the journeyman examination, including combinations of documented field experience, approved study, apprenticeship completion, high school electrical program graduation, or community college electrical program graduation.

One common route requires at least 8,000 hours of service as a licensed apprentice or helper and satisfactory completion of a program of study comprising 576 hours approved by the Electricians’ Examining Board or completed through an accredited institution. The 576 hours include required courses such as Electricity I, Electricity II, Math I, Controls I, and a current National Electrical Code course, along with approved trade-related or degree-related electives.

Another pathway applies to licensed apprentices who have worked at least 8,000 hours in the field of electrical installations under direct supervision and completed at least 576 hours prescribed in a board-approved apprenticeship program, along with a 45-hour course in the current National Electrical Code. Maine also allows certain candidates who complete approved apprenticeship study and accumulate at least 4,000 hours as a licensed apprentice electrician to sit for the journeyman examination.

Maine also recognizes certain education-based pathways. A graduate of an accredited applied technology high school two-year electrical program may qualify with 7,000 hours of supervised electrical installation work and completion of a course of not less than 45 hours in the current National Electrical Code. A graduate of a Maine community college electrical program or a vocational-electrical program of the Department of Corrections may qualify with 4,000 hours of supervised electrical installation work and completion of a course of not less than 45 hours in the current National Electrical Code.

After meeting the applicable eligibility pathway, the candidate applies through the Maine Electricians’ Examining Board process and follows the exam scheduling instructions provided for the examination program. After passing the required examination, the applicant completes the remaining license issuance steps required by the Board. Passing the exam is an important milestone, but license issuance still depends on satisfying the Board’s requirements and completing the required licensing process.

State Requirements

Maine regulates electrician licensing through the Electricians’ Examining Board under the Office of Professional and Occupational Regulation. The Board’s primary responsibility is to ensure competency through examination, issue licenses to qualified individuals, investigate complaints, and support compliance with electrical licensing laws and rules.

A Maine Journeyman Electrician is a licensed individual who makes electrical installations while employed by a Master Electrician, Limited Electrician, or Electrical Company. This means the journeyman license supports individual electrical work within the licensed employment structure. It is different from a master electrician credential or an electrical company structure.

Journeyman candidates should keep accurate records of work experience, licensing status as an apprentice or helper, supervision, education, and code coursework. Maine’s qualifying pathways are documentation-driven, so candidates should maintain copies of apprenticeship records, school transcripts, employer records, program completion documents, and proof of required National Electrical Code coursework.

Candidates should also understand the difference between Maine’s electrical classifications. A Helper Electrician license and Apprentice Electrician license support entry-level or supervised work. A Journeyman Electrician license supports qualified electrical installation work while employed under the proper licensing structure. A Master Electrician license is a higher-level credential with additional requirements. Limited licenses apply to specific scopes of work.

Maine electrician licenses must be maintained according to state renewal requirements. License holders should stay current with code updates, renewal instructions, and any continuing education requirements that apply to their license classification. Electrical standards continue to change as new NEC editions are adopted, so ongoing code study remains important after licensure.

For calculation topics, write out each step. Electrical calculations may require identifying what the question gives you, determining what the question asks for, selecting the correct table or rule, applying adjustments or corrections, and checking whether the answer should be a minimum, maximum, ampacity, rating, load, conductor size, or overcurrent device value. A written process helps improve accuracy.

Timed practice is valuable because open book exams still require efficient reference use. Candidates should occasionally answer questions under time limits while using the NEC. The goal is to build a steady rhythm: read the question, identify the topic, locate the rule, verify the language, and answer with confidence.

Review missed questions carefully. When an answer is wrong, return to the study guide and the NEC. Find the rule, read the surrounding language, and understand why the correct answer applies. This helps prepare for questions that test the same concept in a new way.
