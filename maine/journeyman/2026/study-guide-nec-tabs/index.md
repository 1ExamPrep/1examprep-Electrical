## Licensing Steps

The Maine journeyman electrician licensing path begins with gaining qualifying experience as a licensed apprentice or helper and completing the required education. Maine identifies several pathways to qualify for the journeyman examination, including combinations of documented field experience, approved study, apprenticeship completion, high school electrical program graduation, or community college electrical program graduation.

One common route requires at least 8,000 hours of service as a licensed apprentice or helper and satisfactory completion of a program of study comprising 576 hours approved by the Electricians’ Examining Board or completed through an accredited institution. The 576 hours include required courses such as Electricity I, Electricity II, Math I, Controls I, and a current National Electrical Code course, along with approved trade-related or degree-related electives.

Another pathway applies to licensed apprentices who have worked at least 8,000 hours in the field of electrical installations under direct supervision and completed at least 576 hours prescribed in a board-approved apprenticeship program, along with a 45-hour course in the current National Electrical Code. Maine also allows certain candidates who complete approved apprenticeship study and accumulate at least 4,000 hours as a licensed apprentice electrician to sit for the journeyman examination.

Maine also recognizes certain education-based pathways. A graduate of an accredited applied technology high school two-year electrical program may qualify with 7,000 hours of supervised electrical installation work and completion of a course of not less than 45 hours in the current National Electrical Code. A graduate of a Maine community college electrical program or a vocational-electrical program of the Department of Corrections may qualify with 4,000 hours of supervised electrical installation work and completion of a course of not less than 45 hours in the current National Electrical Code.

After meeting the applicable eligibility pathway, the candidate applies through the Maine Electricians’ Examining Board process and follows the exam scheduling instructions provided for the examination program. After passing the required examination, the applicant completes the remaining license issuance steps required by the Board. Passing the exam is an important milestone, but license issuance still depends on satisfying the Board’s requirements and completing the required licensing process.

Journeyman candidates should keep accurate records of work experience, licensing status as an apprentice or helper, supervision, education, and code coursework. Maine’s qualifying pathways are documentation-driven, so candidates should maintain copies of apprenticeship records, school transcripts, employer records, program completion documents, and proof of required National Electrical Code coursework.

Candidates should also understand the difference between Maine’s electrical classifications. A Helper Electrician license and Apprentice Electrician license support entry-level or supervised work. A Journeyman Electrician license supports qualified electrical installation work while employed under the proper licensing structure. A Master Electrician license is a higher-level credential with additional requirements. Limited licenses apply to specific scopes of work.

Maine electrician licenses must be maintained according to state renewal requirements. License holders should stay current with code updates, renewal instructions, and any continuing education requirements that apply to their license classification. Electrical standards continue to change as new NEC editions are adopted, so ongoing code study remains important after licensure.
