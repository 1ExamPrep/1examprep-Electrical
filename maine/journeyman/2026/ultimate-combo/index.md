## Exam Details

The Maine Journeyman Electrician examination is administered by Prov for approved candidates through the State of Maine Office of Professional and Occupational Regulation. The exam is computer-based and is offered at approved Prov testing locations. Candidates schedule their exam after receiving the required approval and testing information.

The Maine Journeyman Electrician exam includes 80 questions and allows 3 hours of testing time. The exam is designed to test a candidate’s knowledge of the planning, installation, repair, alteration, and maintenance of electrical conductors, raceways, and equipment used for light, heat, and power while employed by a Master Electrician.

The Maine Journeyman Electrician exam content outline includes general electrical theory and principles, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, motors and controls, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, and communication systems. Wiring and protection and wiring methods and materials are two of the largest listed exam areas, so candidates should give strong attention to NEC structure, installation rules, conductors, overcurrent protection, raceways, boxes, grounding and bonding concepts, equipment requirements, and practical application.

The subject breakdown includes 10 questions on general electrical theory and principles, 16 questions on wiring and protection, 16 questions on wiring methods and materials, 10 questions on motors and controls, 10 questions on equipment for general use, 5 questions on special occupancies, 5 questions on special equipment, 5 questions on special conditions, and 3 questions on communication systems.

## Licensing Steps

Maine journeyman electrician licensing is handled through the Electricians’ Examining Board within the Office of Professional and Occupational Regulation. Candidates must meet the Board’s licensing requirements, complete the required training and experience pathway, pass the journeyman examination, and submit the required application materials for licensure.

A typical Maine journeyman electrician path includes working as a licensed apprentice or helper, completing the required electrical education or approved program of study, completing required National Electrical Code coursework, gaining qualifying electrical installation experience under proper supervision, applying through the Board process, taking and passing the journeyman electrician examination, and completing the remaining license application steps.

Maine has multiple pathways that may qualify a candidate for the journeyman examination and license. These pathways can include completion of an approved apprenticeship program, completion of required classroom study, graduation from certain accredited electrical programs, or other education and experience combinations recognized by the Board.

Candidates should keep accurate records of apprentice or helper licensure, employment dates, supervising license information, electrical installation experience, classroom training, apprenticeship completion, NEC coursework, and any certificates or transcripts required by the Board. Organized documentation can make the application process smoother and help avoid delays.

## State Requirements

Maine issues statewide electrician licenses through the Electricians’ Examining Board. A Journeyman Electrician is a licensed individual who makes electrical installations in the employment of a Master Electrician, Limited Electrician, or Electrical Company. The license is an individual trade license and is separate from operating as an electrical contractor or business.

One Maine journeyman electrician pathway includes completing at least 8,000 hours of service as a licensed apprentice or helper and satisfactorily completing a program of study consisting of 576 hours approved by the Board or from an accredited institution. Required study includes 45-hour courses in Electricity I, Electricity II, Math I, Controls I, and a current National Electrical Code course, along with approved elective study.

Maine also recognizes pathways involving graduation from an accredited applied technology high school two-year electrical program with 7,000 hours of qualifying electrical installation experience and a current National Electrical Code course, or graduation from a Maine community college electrical program or Department of Corrections vocational-electrical program with 4,000 hours of qualifying experience and a current National Electrical Code course.

A licensed apprentice who has completed an approved apprenticeship program of study and has worked at least 4,000 hours as a licensed apprentice electrician may qualify to sit for the journeyman examination. Candidates should make sure their education, hours, supervision, and documentation match the pathway they are using.

Passing the exam is one part of the Maine journeyman electrician licensing process. Candidates must still satisfy the Board’s licensing requirements, provide the required documentation, and receive license approval from the state. This Ultimate Combo supports exam preparation and application organization, but licensing decisions are made by the Maine Electricians’ Examining Board.
