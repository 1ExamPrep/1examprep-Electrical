## Licensing Steps

The Maine journeyman electrician licensing path begins with choosing the correct license classification and meeting the qualification requirements established by the Electricians’ Examining Board. A Journeyman Electrician is a licensed individual making electrical installations in the employment of a Master Electrician, Limited Electrician, or electrical company.

A common Maine journeyman electrician pathway includes completing at least 8,000 hours of service as a licensed apprentice or helper and satisfactorily completing a program of study comprising 576 hours approved by the Electricians’ Examining Board or from an accredited institution. The required study includes courses in Electricity I, Electricity II, Math I, Controls I, and a current National Electrical Code course, along with additional approved trade-related or degree-related coursework.

Maine also provides other qualification pathways. A graduate of an accredited applied technology high school two-year electrical program may qualify with 7,000 hours of work in electrical installations under appropriate supervision and a current National Electrical Code course. A graduate of a Maine community college electrical program or a Department of Corrections vocational-electrical program may qualify with 4,000 hours of electrical installation work under appropriate supervision and a current National Electrical Code course.

After meeting the applicable qualification path, the candidate submits an examination application to the Maine Electricians’ Examining Board. Once approved, the candidate receives exam approval information and schedules the exam through Prov. The candidate then studies the approved exam references, follows the open-book rules, and sits for the journeyman electrician examination.

After passing the required examination and satisfying the remaining licensing requirements, the candidate completes the licensure process through the Maine Electricians’ Examining Board. Passing the exam is an important step, but the applicant must still complete the Board’s licensing process and submit the required application materials.

## State Requirements

Maine regulates electrician licensing through the Electricians’ Examining Board under the State of Maine Office of Professional and Occupational Regulation. The Board’s purpose is to protect the public from hazards arising from the use of electricity for light, heat, power, and other purposes through the regulation of electricians in Maine.

The Board issues licenses to qualified individuals in several classifications, including Master Electrician, Journeyman Electrician, Limited Electrician, Apprentice Electrician, Journeyman-in-Training Electrician, and Helper Electrician. The Board also investigates complaints, handles licensing matters, and administers electrical permitting responsibilities.

Maine currently requires electrical installations commencing on or after July 1, 2024, to comply with the 2023 National Electrical Code as adopted by the Board, along with applicable Maine statutes, Board rules, and local municipal requirements. Maine also has reciprocity agreements for journeyman electricians with New Hampshire, Vermont, North Dakota, Idaho, Oregon, and Wyoming. The Board’s reciprocity requirements apply to applicants who are licensed in a reciprocating state, have passed a written examination from that state, and meet the applicable active-work requirements.
