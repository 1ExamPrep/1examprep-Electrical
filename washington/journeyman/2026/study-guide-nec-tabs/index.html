Open Book Test

The Washington electrical certification examination is an open book test. Open book testing allows candidates to use approved reference materials under PSI and Washington L&I rules, but it does not make the exam easy. A timed open book electrical exam rewards candidates who know how the NEC is organized, where common topics are located, and how to apply code language quickly and accurately.

Washington candidates are responsible for bringing approved references to the examination. Current Washington exam materials identify the NEC as an exam reference, and Washington also tests state electrical laws and rules. Candidates should follow the current PSI candidate information bulletin for the exact code edition, book format, tabs, highlighting, notes, and printed Washington rule materials allowed in the testing room.

Licensing Steps

  1. Register as an electrical trainee or enter an approved pathway. Washington candidates generally begin as electrical trainees or qualify through approved out-of-state, military, or apprenticeship experience recognized by the state.
  2. Gain supervised electrical experience. General journey level candidates must document qualifying electrical work experience under proper supervision. Washington commonly requires 8,000 hours for the general journey level path.
  3. Meet commercial and industrial experience requirements. Washington’s general journey level path commonly includes at least 4,000 hours in commercial or industrial electrical installation work.
  4. Complete required classroom instruction. Washington requires approved classroom training for electrical trainees, with 96 hours commonly associated with classifications requiring 8,000 hours of work experience.
  5. Track experience carefully. Candidates should keep accurate records of employers, supervisors, dates, work categories, trainee registration, apprenticeship status, and classroom completion.
  6. Submit the application to Washington L&I. Candidates must apply through the Washington Department of Labor & Industries and provide the required documentation before receiving examination authorization.
  7. Schedule the exam with PSI after approval. Once approved, candidates schedule the applicable Washington electrical examination through PSI and follow the candidate bulletin instructions.
  8. Study the NEC and Washington rules. Preparation should include National Electrical Code review, Washington laws and rules, electrical theory, calculations, and timed practice.
  9. Pass the required examination. Candidates must pass the applicable electrical certification exam before the certificate can be issued.
  10. Maintain the certification. Washington electricians must follow renewal and continuing education requirements for their classification.
