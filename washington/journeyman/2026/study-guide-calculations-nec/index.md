## Exam Details

The Washington 01 General Journey Level Electrician exam is administered by PSI for the Washington State Department of Labor & Industries. Electrician candidates must first apply to L&I and receive approval before scheduling the PSI exam. After L&I verifies the candidate’s eligibility, the candidate receives an exam approval letter with scheduling instructions and an Exam Unique ID used for PSI registration.

The Washington 01 General Electrician exam includes separate sections. The electrician exam outline lists an NEC & Theory section with 60 questions and a 3-hour time allowance, plus a Washington Codes section with 17 questions and a 1-hour time allowance. Candidates must pass each section of the examination with a score of 70% or greater. Scores are provided immediately after the exam, and candidates who do not pass receive diagnostic information by examination type.

The NEC & Theory section includes questions across major National Electrical Code and trade knowledge areas. Topics include NEC 90 introduction, NEC 100 through 110 general requirements, wiring and protection, services, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, electrical equipment, motors, HVAC, generators and transformers, equipment over 600 volts, hazardous locations, special occupancies, special equipment, emergency and standby systems, special conditions, communication systems, and general trade knowledge and theory.

A complete Washington journeyman electrician exam preparation plan should include both national code knowledge and Washington-specific rule review.

## Open Book Test

Washington electrical certification examinations are open book. PSI’s Washington electrical candidate information states that candidates may use original copyrighted materials, including material that contains questions and answers, along with a silent, nonprinting, nonprogrammable calculator. Copies of Washington RCW and WAC materials downloaded from the internet are acceptable when placed in a binder.

Reference rules are important for Washington candidates. Copyrighted materials may have highlighting, underlining, and permanent index tabs before entering the examination area. Removable tabs, sticky notes, repositionable notes, and similar removable note materials are not permitted. References may not be written in. Candidates may not add markings during the exam, and testing staff collect provided scratch paper at the end of the examination.

Open book testing does not make the exam easy. It rewards candidates who know how to use references efficiently. Candidates should practice locating definitions, tables, article sections, grounding and bonding rules, overcurrent protection requirements, wiring method provisions, service rules, and Washington law and rule references before the exam date. The more familiar the code book and Washington rules become during preparation, the less time is wasted searching during the test.

The study guides in this package are designed for preparation before the exam. The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback supports code study, navigation practice, and professional reference use. Candidates should distinguish between study materials used at home and materials allowed in the exam room. Washington exam-room materials must meet the testing provider’s rules for reference condition, tabbing, binding, and markings.

## Licensing Steps

Washington electrician certification is handled through the Washington State Department of Labor & Industries. To become a certified general journey level electrician in Washington, candidates generally begin as electrical trainees or qualify through equivalent out-of-state or military experience. All electrician candidates must meet the required training and experience requirements before they can be approved for examination.

For the in-state 01 General Journey Level Electrician pathway, Washington lists 96 hours of basic classroom instruction and 8,000 hours of work experience. No more than 4,000 of those work experience hours may be worked in 4,000-hour specialty categories. Candidates should confirm that their L&I record reflects the required education and work experience before applying for examination.

Washington has also implemented changes affecting those pursuing 01 journey level electrician certification. With some exceptions, electricians wanting to qualify for the 01 certification exam must complete a registered apprenticeship program. Certain trainees and applicants may qualify through transition rules, out-of-state experience, military experience, or reciprocal pathways. Applicants should use the correct L&I application method for their background and certification path.

The general process includes completing required supervised experience and education, documenting experience with the required forms, applying to L&I for examination approval, receiving the approval letter, scheduling the exam with PSI, taking all required exam sections, passing each section with 70% or greater, and completing the remaining certification steps through L&I. Candidates who fail an exam section must wait before retesting and may only retest sections failed during the active one-year exam period.

A strong study plan should begin before submitting the exam application. ## State Requirements

Washington requires electricians working in the electrical construction trade to hold a valid electrician certificate issued by L&I. The electrical construction trade includes installing and maintaining electrical wires and equipment used for light, heat, or power, as well as remote control, signaling, power-limited, and communication circuits or systems.

A Washington 01 General Journey Level Electrician may perform all types of electrical and telecommunications installations. This broad scope makes the 01 certification different from specialty electrician categories, which are limited to specific types of electrical work. Candidates preparing for the 01 exam should be ready for a wide range of code, theory, installation, and Washington rule topics.

Washington’s in-state 01 journey level qualification path includes 8,000 hours of work experience and 96 hours of basic classroom instruction. For 01 journey level exams, no more than 4,000 of the required work experience hours may be worked in 4,000-hour specialties. Specialty electrician categories have separate scopes and separate experience requirements, so candidates should make sure their experience matches the certification they are pursuing.

Washington also has reciprocity with Oregon for certain general journey level electricians. Washington reciprocal certificates are generally available to qualifying applicants who obtained Oregon general journeyman electrician licenses after completing 8,000-hour apprenticeships requiring at least 576 classroom hours of education and passing Oregon exams, as well as certain Oregon General Supervising Electrician license holders. Reciprocity has specific eligibility conditions and does not apply to every applicant.

Washington electricians, master electricians, and administrators must also meet continuing education requirements for renewal. L&I lists 24 hours of continuing education during the three-year certification period, including hours on currently adopted NEC changes and Washington RCW and WAC requirements. Continuing education supports ongoing code awareness after certification is earned.
