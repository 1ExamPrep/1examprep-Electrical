## Exam Details

The Washington 01 General Journey Level Electrician exam is part of the electrical certification process administered through the Washington State Department of Labor & Industries. Electrician candidates must first apply to L&I and receive approval before registering for the PSI exam. After L&I approves an application, the candidate receives an eligibility notice with exam scheduling instructions and an Exam Unique ID used for PSI registration.

The Washington electrical certification exam program is administered by PSI. The exam is computer-based, and candidates receive score reporting after completion. Washington’s PSI bulletin states that candidates must score 70% to pass. If a candidate does not pass, the score report includes diagnostic information by examination type so the candidate can identify weaker areas for additional study.

For the 01 General Electrician classification, the electrician exam is divided into separate sections. The electrician sections listed for 01 General include NEC & Theory and WA Codes. The NEC & Theory section is listed with 60 questions and a 3-hour time allowance. The WA Codes section is listed with 17 questions and a 1-hour time allowance. Candidates should study both national code content and Washington electrical laws and rules because the exam requires knowledge of both.

The NEC & Theory content outline for the 01 General Electrician exam includes NEC introduction, general requirements, wiring and protection, services, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, electrical equipment, motors, HVAC, generators, transformers, equipment over 600 volts, hazardous locations, special occupancies, special equipment, emergency and standby systems, special conditions, communication systems, and general trade knowledge and theory.

The WA Codes section focuses on Washington laws and rules, including the state electrical law and administrative code content used for electrical work and electrical certification. A strong Washington journeyman electrician study plan should include both NEC-based review and Washington code review so the candidate is prepared for the full testing structure.

## Open Book Test

Washington electrical certification exams are open book. The PSI bulletin states that candidates may use original copyrighted material and a silent, nonprinting, nonprogrammable calculator. Copies of Washington state RCW and WAC materials downloaded from the internet are acceptable when placed in a binder. Copyrighted materials may have highlighting, underlining, and permanent index tabs before entering the examination area.

Open-book testing makes preparation with the correct materials especially important. The exam does not simply test whether a candidate owns a code book. It tests whether the candidate can identify the subject of a question, locate the correct article or rule, read the requirement carefully, and apply it under time pressure. Candidates who have not practiced code navigation may lose valuable exam time searching through the book.

Washington’s rules do not allow Post-it notes, sticky notes, repositionable tabs, removable notes, or writing in references. Candidates should build study habits around clean, organized, exam-ready reference use.
