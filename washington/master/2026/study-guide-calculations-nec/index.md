## Exam Details

Washington electrical exams are administered by PSI for the Washington State Department of Labor & Industries. To become a certified electrician, master electrician, electrical administrator, or telecommunications administrator in Washington, candidates must pass the appropriate Washington state examination related to the work they will be performing.

Washington L&I states that electricians and master electricians must complete the required experience and training for their certificate type before submitting an application for examination. After L&I receives the application and verifies the applicant’s experience, the applicant receives information on how to schedule the exam with PSI.

Washington requires candidates to pass each section of the examination with a score of 70% or greater. PSI provides score reporting after the examination, and candidates who do not pass receive diagnostic information showing strengths and weaknesses by examination type.

The Washington PSI bulletin organizes master electrician exams by certification type and examination section. For the 01 General Master Electrician examination, the listed sections include NEC and Theory, Washington Codes, and Major Load Calculations. The bulletin lists 80 questions for the NEC and Theory section, 20 questions for the Washington Codes section, and 10 questions for the Major Load Calculations section. The time allowed for the 01 General Master Electrician sections is listed as 4 hours for NEC and Theory, 1 hour for Washington Codes, and 2 hours for Major Load Calculations.

The 01 General outline includes NEC introduction, general requirements, wiring and protection, services, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, electrical equipment, motors, HVAC, generators and transformers, equipment over 600 volts, hazardous locations, special occupancies, special equipment, emergency and standby systems, special conditions, communication systems, Washington laws and rules, major load calculations, and general trade knowledge and theory.

The Washington PSI bulletin also includes other master electrician categories, including residential and specialty categories. Because Washington has multiple certificate types and specialties, candidates should study for the exact examination listed on their approval letter. ## Open Book Test

The Washington PSI electrical certification bulletin states that all examinations are open book. Candidates may use original copyrighted material and a silent, nonprinting, nonprogrammable calculator. Copies of Washington RCW and WAC material downloaded from the internet are acceptable when printed and placed in a binder.

Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and permanently tabbed before entering the examination area. Removable notes, sticky notes, repositionable tabs, and similar removable materials are not permitted. References may not be written in during the exam.

Because Washington electrical exams are open book, preparation should include more than reading. Students should practice finding answers efficiently, recognizing article organization, using the index, navigating tables, and working calculations under timed conditions. ## Licensing Steps

Washington master electrician certification is handled through the Washington State Department of Labor & Industries. To become a master electrician, a candidate must have the required amount of experience as an electrician and pass the master electrician exam in the appropriate specialty.

Washington recognizes two levels of master electrician certification: journey level master electrician and specialty master electrician. To qualify for the journey level master electrician exam, the candidate must be certified as a general journey level electrician by L&I for at least 4 years. To qualify for a specialty master electrician exam, the candidate must be certified by L&I in a specialty for at least 2 years.

The application process begins by applying for the master electrician examination through L&I. L&I reviews the application to verify that the applicant meets the qualification requirements. After approval, L&I sends a notice with instructions for scheduling the exam through PSI. Candidates are responsible for scheduling the examination and taking the correct exam listed on the approval notice.

After passing the required examination, the candidate follows L&I instructions for certification. Master electricians who meet the eligibility requirements, pass the examination, and are certified with L&I may work with the tools of the trade as installers. When assigned, they may also act as the administrator for an electrical contractor and help ensure the contractor’s work complies with Washington electrical laws and rules.

## State Requirements

Washington master electricians are certified through L&I and are tied closely to electrical contractor compliance. Master electricians and electrical administrators both help ensure that an electrical contractor’s work follows Washington electrical laws and rules. A key distinction is that master electricians may also work with the tools of the trade as installers.

Washington master electrician certificates must be renewed every 3 years. L&I requires 24 hours of approved continuing education within the 3 years between certification renewals. The continuing education requirement includes a minimum of 8 hours of code update and 4 hours of RCW/WAC review.

Washington candidates should understand that eligibility, application approval, examination scheduling, exam performance, and renewal are separate parts of the licensing path.
