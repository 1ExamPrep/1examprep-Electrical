Exam Details

The Michigan Master Electrician examination is administered through PSI for the Michigan Bureau of Construction Codes. The examination is computer-based and is available to candidates after the Department approves their eligibility for testing. Once approved, candidates receive instructions to pay for and schedule the required examination with PSI.

The Michigan Master Electrician exam includes 76 questions, requires a minimum passing score of 75%, and allows 180 minutes of testing time. The examination is designed to test knowledge of the Skilled Trades Regulation Act, rules promulgated under that act, current electrical code rules, the Stille-DeRossett-Hale Single State Construction Code Act, adopted codes, and theory related to those codes.

The master electrician examination includes questions related to electrical installations, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods and installations, boxes and cabinets, services, feeders, motors and motor controls, special occupancies, load calculations, lighting, appliances, box and raceway fill, power-limited circuits, general electrical trade knowledge, and electrical theory. The master exam also covers additional knowledge required to plan and supervise electrical installations.

Because the exam is timed, preparation should focus on both knowledge and efficiency. Candidates need to read each question carefully, recognize the subject area, locate the correct code section, use tables accurately, and work through calculation questions with a repeatable process. A strong study routine helps candidates avoid wasting time, misreading questions, or choosing answers before checking the complete code requirement.

Open Book Test

The Michigan Master Electrician examination is an open book test. Approved reference materials may be brought into the examination site, subject to the rules in the current PSI candidate bulletin. The allowed materials include the National Electrical Code, 2016 PA 407, 1972 PA 230, and Michigan Electrical Code Rules Part 8.

Open-book testing does not mean the exam is easy. Master electrician exams require candidates to locate code material quickly, read the correct section carefully, understand the question being asked, and apply the requirement accurately. A candidate who has not practiced with the NEC may lose valuable time searching through unrelated sections.

Strong NEC navigation is one of the most important skills for an open-book electrical exam. Candidates should practice using the table of contents, index, article headings, definitions, tables, notes, and exceptions. They should also become comfortable moving between related topics, such as branch circuits, conductor ampacity, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, motors, feeders, services, equipment installation, and wiring methods.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback included in this combo gives candidates a code reference for study and navigation practice. The Michigan Master Electrician Study Guide helps connect exam-style review to major code topics, while the Electrician Calculations Study Guide supports problems that may require formulas, tables, load calculations, and multi-step reasoning.

Michigan exam-room rules for reference materials are strict. Reference materials must be bound, and candidates are not permitted to bring additional papers, loose pages, or attached papers with approved references. The NEC Handbook is not allowed in the examination room. Candidates should follow the current PSI candidate bulletin for the exact NEC edition, book condition rules, tabs, markings, and exam-day procedures.

Licensing Steps

Michigan master electrician licensing is handled through the Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs, Bureau of Construction Codes. A typical licensing path includes meeting the state’s master electrician eligibility requirements, completing the required application, receiving Department approval to test, scheduling the examination with PSI, preparing with code-based study materials, passing the required exam, and completing final licensing requirements with the state.

Michigan law requires a master electrician applicant to be at least 22 years old, have at least 12,000 hours of qualifying experience obtained over a period of six years or more, and have electrical construction, building maintenance, electrical wiring, or electrical equipment experience performed under the supervision of a master electrician. Applicants must also hold and maintain an electrical journeyperson license for at least two years.

After the Department approves a candidate for examination, PSI receives the approval information and notifies the authorized candidate to pay for and schedule the examination. Master, journeyman, and sign specialist candidates are limited to two attempts within one year. Once the attempt limit or eligibility period is exceeded, the candidate must reapply with the state to become eligible again.

Candidates should keep copies of all important records, including work experience documentation, journeyperson license information, application materials, examination approval, PSI scheduling confirmation, identification information, exam result, and any correspondence from the Bureau of Construction Codes. Organized paperwork can help reduce delays between application, testing, and final license issuance.

Once the exam path is clear, candidates can use this combo to build a study schedule. For NEC study, candidates should practice locating definitions, finding article sections, reading exceptions, using tables correctly, and recognizing the difference between similar code topics.

State Requirements

Michigan uses a statewide licensing structure for master electricians, journeyman electricians, electrical contractors, and specialty electrical classifications. The Bureau of Construction Codes administers the electrical licensing program, and PSI provides examination services for approved candidates.

A Michigan master electrician license is tied to higher responsibility in the trade, including knowledge required to plan and supervise electrical installations. The master electrician credential is separate from an electrical contractor license. A business performing electrical contracting work may need an electrical contractor license, and a master electrician license cannot be used to meet the eligibility requirement for more than one electrical contractor license at the same time.

The state’s master electrician licensing requirements include age, experience, journeyperson licensure, application approval, and passing the required examination. Candidates should apply under the license classification that matches their experience, credentials, and work goals. Electrical contractor requirements, business licensing, permit responsibilities, and qualifying-person rules are separate from this study package.
