Exam Details

The Michigan Master Electrician examination is administered through PSI for the Michigan Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs, Bureau of Construction Codes. The published PSI candidate information bulletin identifies the Master Electrician exam as a computer-based examination with 76 questions, a 75 percent minimum passing score, and 180 minutes of testing time.

The published examination description states that the Master Electrician exam includes questions designed to test knowledge of the Skilled Trades Regulation Act, rules promulgated under that act, current electrical code rules, the Stille-DeRossett-Hale Single State Construction Code Act, applicable adopted codes, and theory related to those codes. The exam also covers electrical installation knowledge, including grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods and installations, boxes and cabinets, services, feeders, motors and motor controls, special occupancies, load calculations, lighting, appliances, box and raceway fill, power-limited circuits, general electrical trade knowledge, and electrical theory.

The master exam also covers additional knowledge required to plan and supervise electrical installations. That makes a broad study plan important. Candidates should prepare not only for code lookup, but also for planning judgment, trade knowledge, calculation accuracy, and the ability to apply electrical rules under time pressure.

Open Book Test

The Michigan Master Electrician examination is identified as an open book examination. Open book testing does not mean the exam is easy. It means candidates need to know how to use approved references efficiently, understand the structure of the NEC, read code language carefully, and apply rules accurately within a limited amount of time.

The published Michigan PSI bulletin lists the allowed reference materials for the Master Electrician exam as the National Electrical Code, 2016 PA 407, 1972 PA 230, and Michigan Electrical Code Rules Part 8. The bulletin also states that the NEC Handbook is not allowed in the examination room. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own references to the testing center.

Michigan’s exam-room rules are strict. The PSI bulletin states that all reference material must be bound. Materials printed from the Bureau of Construction Codes website must be bound with three staples on the left edge in separate documents. Highlighting, marking, and tabs are not permitted in printed documents. The NEC code book may have factory markings or highlights with factory tabs only. Candidates caught writing, highlighting, underlining, or indexing in references during the examination are reported to the Bureau of Construction Codes, and additional loose or attached papers are not permitted with approved references.

Practice locating articles, reading full sections, checking table notes, and confirming whether exceptions or conditions affect the final answer.

Open book success depends on preparation habits. Candidates should know enough of the material to recognize the topic being tested, then use the reference to confirm the correct rule. Searching from scratch on every question can waste valuable time. Licensing Steps

Michigan electrician licensing is handled through the Michigan Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs, Bureau of Construction Codes. A candidate pursuing the Michigan Master Electrician license should begin by confirming that the master electrician license is the correct credential for the work they plan to perform and by reviewing the Bureau’s current application requirements.

A practical path to the Michigan Master Electrician license includes building the required experience, maintaining the required journeyperson license status, submitting the application to the State of Michigan, receiving approval to test, scheduling the examination through PSI, passing the Master Electrician examination, and completing the licensing process through the state.

Michigan’s published master electrician licensing information states that a master electrician applicant must be at least 22 years old, have at least 12,000 hours of qualifying experience obtained over a period of six years or more, and have experience related to electrical construction, maintenance of buildings, electrical wiring, or electrical equipment under the supervision of a master electrician. Applicants must also be licensed as an electrical journeyperson for at least two years.

After the Department approves a candidate for examination, PSI receives the authorization information and the candidate receives instructions for scheduling. Master, Journeyman, and Sign Specialist candidates are limited to two attempts within one year under the published PSI bulletin. Candidates who exceed the attempt limit must reapply with the state to become eligible again.

The study portion of the licensing path should be treated as a planned project. Candidates should review the exam content, divide study time among code topics and calculations, work through NEC lookup practice, and use flash cards for repeated reinforcement. Consistent preparation helps candidates approach the Michigan Master Electrician exam with a stronger sense of organization and direction.

State Requirements

Michigan regulates electrician licensing through the Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs and the Bureau of Construction Codes. The licensing structure includes apprentice registration, journeyperson electrician licensing, master electrician licensing, electrical contractor licensing, and specialty classifications. The master electrician license represents a higher level of qualification tied to experience, code knowledge, and the ability to plan and supervise electrical installations.

Michigan law identifies the requirements for a master electrician license. The applicant must be at least 22 years old, meet the required licensing qualifications, have at least 12,000 hours of qualifying experience over a period of six years or more, and meet the required journeyperson licensure period. The experience must be related to electrical construction, maintenance of buildings, or electrical wiring or equipment, and it must be performed under the supervision of a master electrician.

The Michigan Master Electrician examination includes state-specific legal and code-related material. Candidates should be prepared to study not only NEC-based electrical topics, but also the Skilled Trades Regulation Act, applicable rules, Michigan Electrical Code Rules Part 8, and related construction code law. This combination of trade knowledge and state-specific reference material makes organized preparation especially important.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Michigan Master Electrician study plan should include state-law review, NEC study, calculation practice, trade knowledge review, and timed question work. The exam covers both electrical installation topics and Michigan-specific rules, so candidates should avoid studying only the NEC or only calculation problems. A balanced routine helps build confidence across the full range of tested subjects.

For NEC study, start by learning the structure of the code book. Review article organization, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and cross-references. Practice moving from a question to the correct article and reading the surrounding language carefully. Many electrical exam questions are missed because a candidate finds a related section but overlooks a condition, exception, table note, or specific wording.

For Michigan-specific study, spend time with the allowed state reference materials listed in the PSI bulletin. The exam includes knowledge tied to Michigan skilled trades regulation, electrical code rules, and related construction code law. Candidates should become familiar with the purpose and structure of these references before testing.

For calculations, write out each step. Electrical calculation mistakes often happen when a candidate skips a conversion, uses the wrong table, applies the wrong value, or confuses branch circuit, feeder, and service requirements. For flash card study, use short sessions throughout the week. Flash cards can help reinforce formulas, definitions, code concepts, and common electrical terms. They are useful before work, during breaks, after a longer study block, or as final review. Flash cards should not replace full study sessions, but they help strengthen recall and recognition.
