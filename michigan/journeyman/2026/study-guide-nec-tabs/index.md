## Exam Details

The Michigan Electrical Journeyman exam is administered through PSI for the Michigan Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs, Bureau of Construction Codes. The State of Michigan determines examination eligibility. After the Department approves a candidate for testing, PSI receives the approval information and notifies the candidate with instructions to pay for and schedule the required examination.

The Michigan Electrical Journeyman exam contains 80 questions, requires a minimum passing score of 75%, and has a time allowance of 150 minutes. The examination is computer-based and is administered through PSI examination centers. Candidates should arrive with proper identification, approved references, and materials allowed under the current candidate bulletin.

The journeyman electrician examination includes questions designed to test knowledge of the Skilled Trades Regulation Act, rules under the act, the current electrical code rules, the Stille-DeRossett-Hale Single State Construction Code Act, applicable adopted codes, and theory related to those codes. This means candidates should prepare for both technical electrical knowledge and Michigan-specific code and law requirements.

The questions on electrical installations include grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods and installations, boxes and cabinets, services, feeders, motor controls, special occupancies, load calculations, lighting, appliances, box and raceway fill, power-limited circuits, general electrical trade knowledge, and electrical theory. These areas reflect the practical knowledge expected of a licensed journeyman electrician working with building electrical systems and equipment.

Grounding and bonding is a key study area because many questions require precise understanding of terminology, grounding electrode systems, bonding methods, equipment grounding conductors, service bonding, and the relationship between safety and fault-current paths. Overcurrent protection, services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor sizing, and load calculations also deserve steady practice because they often combine electrical theory with NEC application.

## Open Book Test

The Michigan Electrical Journeyman examination is open book. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references to the examination center. The listed examination references include the National Electrical Code, 2016 PA 407, 1972 PA 230, and Michigan Electrical Code Rules Part 8.

The National Electrical Code book may have factory markings or highlights with factory tabs only. The NEC Handbook is not allowed in the examination room. Candidates should understand the difference between a code book and a handbook before exam day, because bringing the wrong reference can affect the ability to use that material during the test.

All reference material must be bound. Materials printed from the Bureau of Construction Codes website must be bound by three staples on the left edge in separate documents. Highlighting, marking, and tabs in printed documents are not permitted. Candidates are not allowed to bring additional papers, whether loose or attached, with approved references.

During the examination, candidates may not write, highlight, underline, or index in the references. Any candidate caught writing, highlighting, underlining, or indexing in references during the examination will be reported to the Bureau of Construction Codes. This makes exam-day compliance important. Candidates should prepare their materials according to PSI’s rules before arriving at the testing center.

## Licensing Steps

The Michigan journeyman electrician licensing path begins with apprentice registration and supervised electrical experience. Michigan states that an electrical apprentice must be employed by a master electrician and electrical contractor and must be enrolled in a related technical instruction program. Apprentice registration must remain active and be renewed on time during the apprenticeship period.

To qualify for the Electrical Journeyman license, an applicant must be at least 20 years old. The applicant must have completed 8,000 hours and four years as an electrical apprentice. The work must be related to electrical construction or maintenance of buildings, electrical wiring, or electrical equipment, and it must be performed under the supervision of a Michigan-licensed electrical journeyman or master electrician. Credit may be available for completion of a recognized training or educational program.

After meeting the experience and age requirements, the candidate submits the appropriate application for journeyman examination and licensure to the Bureau of Construction Codes. The Department reviews the application and determines examination eligibility. Once approved, the candidate receives instructions for paying and scheduling the PSI examination.

Candidates must pass the Michigan electrical journeyman exam by scoring at least 75%. PSI handles scheduling and proctoring, while the Bureau of Construction Codes controls eligibility and licensing. Candidates must pass the examination within one year of the eligibility date. For electrical master or journeyman candidates, Michigan’s PSI information states that an applicant who fails two times within a two-year period is ineligible to sit for another examination for at least one year from the date of the second failure.

After passing the examination, the candidate completes the state licensing process through the Bureau of Construction Codes. Passing the exam is an important step, but the license is issued through the state process. Candidates should keep copies of apprentice registration records, experience documentation, education records, application materials, PSI approval information, and score reports.

## State Requirements

The Michigan Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs, Bureau of Construction Codes, administers electrical licensing and registration in Michigan. The licensing system covers electrical apprentices, journeymen, master electricians, electrical contractors, fire alarm specialty technicians, fire alarm contractors, sign specialists, and sign specialty contractors.

Michigan’s Electrical Journeyman license is an individual credential for qualified electrical workers. The state requires the applicant to be at least 20 years old, complete 8,000 hours and four years as an electrical apprentice, work under the supervision of a Michigan-licensed electrical journeyman or master electrician, and pass the electrical journeyman examination with a score of at least 75%.

Michigan does not hold reciprocity with another state or country. A person seeking consideration for licensure based on an out-of-state or out-of-country license must submit a copy of that license and a copy of that jurisdiction’s licensing requirements showing the hours, schooling, and requirements that had to be met to obtain the license.

The electrical contractor license is a separate business credential. Michigan states that an electrical contractor license is required to do business as an electrician. An electrical contractor must either hold a master electrician license or employ a master electrician. The associated master electrician is responsible for Michigan code compliance for electrical wiring and equipment installations and may associate with only one electrical contractor at a time.
