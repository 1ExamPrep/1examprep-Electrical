## Licensing Steps

The Michigan journeyman electrician licensing path begins with electrical apprentice registration. An apprentice must be employed by a Master Electrician and Electrical Contractor and must be enrolled in a related technical instruction program. Michigan requires apprentices to keep registration active and renew it every year on time.

To qualify for the Electrical Journeyman license, an applicant must be at least 20 years old. The applicant must complete 8,000 hours and four years as an electrical apprentice. The qualifying work must be related to electrical construction or maintenance of buildings, electrical wiring, or electrical equipment, and it must be performed under the supervision of someone who holds an Electrical Journeyman or Master Electrician license issued by the State of Michigan.

Michigan may allow some credit for completing a recognized training or educational program. Applicants should maintain accurate records of apprenticeship registration, employers, hours worked, related technical instruction, and any documentation needed to support the experience statement and license application.

After meeting the experience and age requirements, the applicant submits the proper application for the Electrical Journeyman examination and licensure through the Bureau of Construction Codes. Once the Licensing Section approves eligibility, PSI receives the approval information and notifies the candidate to schedule and pay for the examination.

After passing the journey examination with a score of at least 75%, the applicant completes the remaining licensing steps through Michigan’s licensing process. A practical preparation path includes active apprentice registration, supervised electrical work experience, related technical instruction, application approval, exam scheduling through PSI, NEC study, calculation practice, Michigan rule review, reference navigation practice, flash card review, passing the exam, and completing the license process.
