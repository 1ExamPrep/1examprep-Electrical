## Exam Details

The Michigan Electrical Journeyman examination is administered through PSI for the Michigan Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs, Bureau of Construction Codes. Candidates must apply with the state and receive approval before scheduling the examination. After the Bureau approves the candidate’s eligibility, PSI receives the approval information and the candidate may pay for and schedule the required licensing exam.

The Michigan Electrical Journeyman examination is listed with **80 questions**, a testing time of **150 minutes**, and a required passing score of **75%**. The exam is computer-based and is designed to test knowledge needed for journeyman-level electrical work under Michigan’s licensing structure.

## Open Book Test

The Michigan Electrical Journeyman examination is an **open book** examination with approved reference rules. Open book testing allows candidates to use authorized materials during the exam, but it still requires serious preparation. A code book is only useful when the candidate understands how to locate information, read the rule correctly, and apply it within the time allowed.

Michigan’s current PSI bulletin lists the National Electrical Code as an allowed reference for the Electrical Journeyman examination. It also lists 2016 PA 407, 1972 PA 230, and Michigan Electrical Code Rules Part 8 as allowed references. The NEC code book may have factory markings or highlights with factory tabs only. All reference material must be bound. Printed materials from the Bureau of Construction Codes website must be bound by three staples on the left edge in separate documents, and highlighting, marking, and tabs in those printed documents are not permitted.

The NEC Handbook is not allowed in the examination room. Candidates are not permitted to bring additional loose or attached papers with approved references, and writing, highlighting, underlining, or indexing in references during the exam is prohibited. A straight edge rule without calculations may be brought to the exam.

This product is based on the 2026 NEC and includes the National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback for updated study and code navigation practice. Michigan’s current examination bulletin states that exam items are based on the 2023 edition while other editions of the NEC may be used during the examination. Candidates should follow the current Michigan and PSI instructions for the exact code edition, approved reference rules, and testing room procedures used on exam day.

Open book study should include active practice. Students should answer practice-style questions with the code book open, locate the relevant code section, read the rule carefully, and then check whether the answer matches the question. This builds the habit of using the NEC as a working reference instead of simply reading it from front to back.

## Licensing Steps

Michigan journeyman electrician candidates begin by meeting the state’s qualification requirements for the license. The typical path starts with employment under a licensed electrical contractor and registration as an apprentice electrician. Apprentice registration is required shortly after beginning qualifying electrical employment.

The candidate must gain qualifying electrical experience under the direct supervision of a licensed electrician. Michigan’s journeyman path requires at least 8,000 hours of experience obtained over a period of at least four years and related to electrical construction or maintenance of buildings, electrical wiring, or equipment under appropriate licensed supervision.

After meeting the experience requirement, the candidate applies for examination and licensure with the Michigan Bureau of Construction Codes. Experience documentation must be submitted according to the state’s application requirements. Employer verification letters should document dates, type of electrical experience, hours earned, and supervision details, and the documentation must be properly notarized when required.

After the state approves the candidate’s eligibility, PSI provides scheduling instructions. The candidate pays the examination fee directly to PSI and schedules the computer-based exam at an available testing center. Candidates should review the PSI bulletin before testing so they understand identification requirements, reference rules, test center procedures, allowed materials, score reporting, and attempt limits.

After passing the examination, the candidate completes the remaining licensing requirements through the Bureau of Construction Codes. Passing the exam is an important step, but the license process is not complete until the state’s requirements are satisfied and the license is issued.

## State Requirements

Michigan requires an individual to be licensed before performing electrical work covered by the state electrical licensing law. The electrical licensing program is administered through the Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs, Bureau of Construction Codes, with licensing categories that include apprentice, electrical journeyman, master electrician, and related specialty classifications.

To qualify for a Michigan electrical journeyman license, an applicant must be at least **20 years old** and must meet the state’s experience requirements. The standard path requires at least **8,000 hours** of experience obtained over at least **4 years** related to electrical construction or maintenance of buildings, electrical wiring, or equipment under direct supervision of an individual licensed under Michigan electrical licensing law.

Michigan’s published journeyman pathway also emphasizes apprenticeship registration. A person working toward journeyman licensing should register as an apprentice electrician and maintain proper records of employment and training. Work experience documentation is an important part of the application process because the state uses it to determine whether the candidate is eligible for examination and licensure.

Michigan also provides a military experience path for certain applicants. A person honorably discharged within the required time period may use a signed affidavit from a commanding officer attesting to electrical experience when applying under the applicable state rules.

A Michigan electrical journeyman license is different from a master electrician license or electrical contractor license. The journeyman license supports individual trade work within the state’s electrical licensing framework. Master and contractor licensing involve separate qualifications and higher levels of responsibility. Candidates should apply for the credential that matches their current experience and career goal.
