## Licensing Steps

California electrician certification begins with the correct path through the Division of Labor Standards Enforcement Electrician Certification Unit. A person performing electrical work for a C-10 licensed contractor must be properly certified or registered as an electrician trainee under the state’s electrician certification requirements.

A candidate working toward General Electrician certification must accumulate the required qualifying work experience and submit the appropriate application materials to the Electrician Certification Unit. The application process includes documenting qualifying experience and meeting the eligibility requirements for the certification classification requested.

After the application is reviewed and the candidate is found eligible, the candidate receives authorization to schedule the certification exam. The exam appointment is scheduled through the approved testing process. Candidates should prepare before scheduling by reviewing the exam content, studying electrical topics, practicing calculations, and becoming comfortable with code navigation.

After taking the exam, candidates receive results according to the testing program’s procedures. A candidate who passes the exam continues through the certification process with the Electrician Certification Unit. A candidate who does not pass must follow the applicable retesting rules before attempting the exam again.

A strong preparation plan should begin before the final application step. Electricians should keep accurate records of work experience, understand the certification classification they are pursuing, and study consistently. The best study plans include three major parts: trade review, calculation practice, and NEC navigation.

California also has a separate C-10 Electrical Contractor license through the Contractors State License Board. The C-10 license is for electrical contractors, while the General Electrician certification is for individuals performing electrical work under the state electrician certification program. ## State Requirements

California General Electrician certification requires **8,000 hours** of qualifying work experience. The experience must include work in two or more approved work categories. California identifies categories such as troubleshooting and maintenance, finish work and fixtures, fire/life safety and nurse call, industrial wiring, voice data and video installation, underground conduit installation, stock room and material handling, residential wiring, and commercial wiring. The state limits the maximum number of hours that may be credited in each category.

California electrician certification classifications include General Electrician, Residential Electrician, Fire/Life Safety Technician, Voice Data Video Technician, and Non-Residential Lighting Technician. The General Electrician certification is the broad journeyman-level certification path for electrical work covered by that classification.

Electrician trainees must be registered when performing electrical work under the trainee rules. Trainees build supervised experience while working toward certification eligibility. Accurate documentation is important because the Electrician Certification Unit reviews work experience as part of the certification process.

California’s electrician certification program is connected to work performed under a C-10 licensed electrical contractor. A worker’s individual certification and a contractor’s C-10 license are not the same credential. The certification applies to the individual electrician, while the contractor license applies to contracting activity.

That includes electrical installation knowledge, practical safety awareness, code understanding, calculation ability, and the ability to apply rules to real electrical situations.
