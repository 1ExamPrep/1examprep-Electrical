Exam Details

California electrician certification is handled through the Department of Industrial Relations, Division of Labor Standards Enforcement, Electrician Certification Unit. The state certification program applies to electricians performing work under a C-10 licensed contractor, and California provides certification categories including General Electrician and several specialty categories.

The California General Electrician certification is the journeyman-level credential most commonly associated with broad electrical work. California’s General Electrician experience requirement is 8,000 hours of work in the trade. The required experience must include work in two or more listed electrical work areas, with maximum credit limits assigned to certain categories such as troubleshooting and maintenance, finish work and fixtures, fire/life safety and nurse call, industrial wiring, voice data and video installation, underground conduit installation, stock room and material handling, residential wiring, and commercial wiring.

The California General Electrician certification exam is commonly listed as a 100-question examination with a 4 hour and 30 minute time limit. The passing score is listed as 70%. The exam is designed to measure the knowledge needed for certified electrical work, including code application, safety, installation requirements, electrical theory, and practical trade understanding.

State Requirements

California electrician certification is administered by the Department of Industrial Relations, Division of Labor Standards Enforcement, Electrician Certification Unit. California law requires persons performing work as electricians under a C-10 licensed contractor to be certified under the state’s electrician certification standards.

The General Electrician certification requires 8,000 hours of work in the trade. The required hours must include work in two or more listed categories, and California places maximum credit limits on specific types of work. These categories include troubleshooting and maintenance, finish work and fixtures, fire/life safety and nurse call, industrial wiring, voice data and video installation, underground conduit installation, stock room and material handling, residential wiring, and commercial wiring.

California also maintains Electrician Trainee requirements for individuals who are not yet certified and are working toward certification. Electrician trainees must be properly registered and associated with qualifying training while gaining experience. Trainees and applicants should follow the current Electrician Certification Unit requirements that apply to their status.

California electrical contractor licensing is separate from electrician certification. Electrical contractor licensing is handled through the Contractors State License Board for C-10 Electrical Contractor licensing, while electrician certification is handled through DIR and DLSE.
