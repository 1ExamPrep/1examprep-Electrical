## Exam Details

The California General Electrician certification examination is administered through the Department of Industrial Relations, Division of Labor Standards Enforcement, Electrician Certification Unit. The testing program is supported by CPS HR Consulting and Pearson VUE. Candidates must apply to DLSE and receive approval before scheduling the examination.

The California General Electrician exam is listed as 100 questions with a time allowance of 4 hours and 30 minutes. The passing score is 70%. The exam content outline includes Safety, Determination of Electrical System Requirements, Installation, and Maintenance and Repair. The candidate information also notes that the examination may contain questions based on trade knowledge or general industry practices.

Installation represents the largest portion of the California General Electrician exam content outline. Candidates should be prepared to review conductors, wiring methods, boxes, raceways, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, electrical equipment, motors, lighting, special systems, and jobsite installation rules. Determination of electrical system requirements also requires careful review because candidates may need to evaluate loads, apply code rules, and select the correct method for a given electrical condition.

Because this exam is code-based and trade-focused, strong preparation should include repeated practice with the National Electrical Code, calculation review, safety study, and exam-style questions. Candidates should not rely on memory alone. The best study routines combine reading, problem solving, code lookup, and timed practice so candidates can improve both accuracy and pacing.

## Open Book Test

The California General Electrician exam is open book in the sense that official references are provided at the testing center and available during the examination. California’s candidate information states that candidates may not bring or use their own reference materials during the examination. This is an important difference from many other states where candidates bring their own code books.

The testing center provides the references identified in the candidate information bulletin for the specific exam classification. For the General Electrician exam, the current listed testing center references include NFPA 70, the National Electrical Code; NFPA 70E, Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace; and the CAL/OSHA Pocket Guide for the Construction Industry. Candidates should be familiar with these types of references before test day.

Open-book testing does not make the exam easy. Candidates still need to know how the NEC is organized, how to locate articles and tables, how to read exceptions, and how to apply code language to exam questions. Since California provides the reference materials at the testing center, candidates should use their study code book during preparation to build the skill of finding information quickly, even though they will not bring that personal book into the exam room.

## Licensing Steps

California electrician certification begins with meeting the eligibility requirements for the classification being pursued. For the General Electrician classification, California requires 8,000 hours of work for an electrical contractor installing, constructing, or maintaining electrical systems covered by the National Electrical Code.

The 8,000 hours must include work in two or more approved areas. California lists maximum credit hours for categories such as residential wiring, commercial wiring, industrial wiring, voice data and video installation, underground conduit installation, troubleshooting and maintenance, finish work and fixtures, fire/life safety, nurse call, and stock room and material handling. Candidates should keep detailed work records because experience documentation is part of the certification process.

Before taking the exam, the candidate must submit an application to the California Department of Industrial Relations, Division of Labor Standards Enforcement, Electrician Certification Unit. The application must include required supporting documentation and the application fee. DLSE reviews the application and determines whether the candidate is eligible to test.

After approval, the candidate receives instructions for scheduling the exam through the testing process. Current California procedures direct approved candidates to schedule through Pearson VUE after receiving the proper scheduling communication. The examination must be taken within the eligibility period stated in the candidate information. Candidates who do not pass must follow the retest process and waiting period established by the program.

The Application Service included with this Ultimate Combo supports candidates with the administrative side of the process. Exam preparation focuses on knowledge and test readiness, while the certification process also requires proper forms, experience records, documentation, and attention to state instructions.

## State Requirements

California requires certification for persons performing work as electricians under a C-10 licensed electrical contractor, subject to the certification standards established by the Division of Labor Standards Enforcement. The Electrician Certification Unit handles certification and exam applications for classifications such as General Electrician, Residential Electrician, Fire/Life Safety Technician, Non-Residential Lighting Technician, and Voice Data Video Technician.

For the General Electrician classification, California requires 8,000 hours of qualifying work for a C-10 electrical contractor. The work must involve installing, constructing, or maintaining electrical systems covered by the National Electrical Code. The required hours must come from at least two approved categories, and each category has a maximum number of hours that may count toward the total.

California’s certification process is separate from contractor licensing. A C-10 electrical contractor license is a contractor license, while General Electrician certification applies to individuals performing electrical work under a C-10 contractor. Candidates should make sure they are following the correct process for electrician certification, contractor licensing, or both, depending on their goals.

California electrician certificates must be renewed every 3 years. Current certificate renewal information requires 32 hours of continuing education from an approved educational provider before renewal. Candidates who become certified should keep track of renewal dates, continuing education, and contact information updates with DLSE.
