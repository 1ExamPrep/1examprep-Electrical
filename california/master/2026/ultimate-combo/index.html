Exam Details

The California Department of Industrial Relations, Division of Labor Standards Enforcement, Electrician Certification Unit administers the California electrician certification program. The program applies to electricians who perform electrical work for C-10 electrical contractors, unless an exemption applies. The official certification classifications include General Electrician, Residential Electrician, Fire/Life Safety Technician, Voice Data Video Technician, and Non-Residential Lighting Technician.

The California General Electrician certification is for individuals who perform work for an electrical contractor installing, constructing, or maintaining electrical systems covered by the National Electrical Code. The current California General Electrician exam outline lists 100 questions with 4 hours and 30 minutes allowed. The exam content areas include Safety, Determination of Electrical System Requirements, Installation, and Maintenance and Repair. The largest listed content category is Installation, followed by Determination of Electrical System Requirements.

California electrician certification candidates must apply and be approved by the Division of Labor Standards Enforcement before taking the examination. After approval, the candidate receives eligibility information and scheduling instructions. Exams are scheduled through Pearson VUE under the California Department of Industrial Relations Electrician Certification testing program coordinated through CPS HR Consulting.

On test day, candidates are required to report to the examination site with proper identification and follow all testing center rules. The candidate information bulletin states that candidates should arrive at least 30 minutes before the scheduled appointment. Candidates must present two forms of original, valid identification. One form must be government-issued and include the candidate’s first name, last name, photo, and signature. The second form must show the candidate’s first name, last name, and signature or a recent recognizable photo.

The current California General Electrician exam references are provided in the testing center. Candidates may not bring or use their own reference materials during the examination. Because the testing center controls the reference materials, preparation should focus on understanding code structure, recognizing where to locate common rules, and building enough trade knowledge to work efficiently even without personal notes or custom exam-room books.

Open Book Test

The California electrician certification exams use reference materials during the examination. For the California General Electrician exam, the testing bulletin states that certain references are provided in the testing center and available during the exam. Candidates may not bring or use their own reference materials.

This makes preparation different from many open-book electrical exams where candidates rely on their own heavily tabbed and highlighted books. For California, the goal is not simply to customize a code book for exam day. Licensing Steps

California electrician certification begins with the correct application process through the Division of Labor Standards Enforcement Electrician Certification Unit. Candidates must choose the certification classification that matches the work they perform and the examination they need. For broad electrical systems covered by the NEC, many candidates pursue the General Electrician certification classification.

For the General Electrician classification, California lists 8,000 hours of work for an electrical contractor installing, constructing, or maintaining electrical systems covered by the National Electrical Code. The required hours must include work in two or more listed experience categories, subject to the maximum credit hours California assigns to each category. Those categories include areas such as troubleshooting and maintenance, finish work and fixtures, fire/life safety and nurse call, industrial wiring, voice data and video installation, underground conduit installation, stock room and material handling, residential wiring, and commercial wiring.

After gathering the required experience information, the candidate submits the application and required supporting documentation to DLSE. Once DLSE approves the candidate for testing, the candidate receives eligibility information. Candidates then schedule the examination through the approved testing system. The current candidate information bulletin states that examinations must be taken within one year from the date of the eligibility notice.

Candidates who do not pass must follow California’s retest process. The bulletin states that a candidate who does not pass must wait 60 days from the exam date before sending in an application for retest, and another exam fee is required. Candidates should keep their application records, identification, and scheduling information organized throughout the process.

After passing and completing the required certification process, the certification card is issued according to the program’s procedures. Certification must be renewed every 3 years, and the current bulletin states that 32 hours of continuing education from an approved California educational provider must be completed prior to renewal.

State Requirements

California requires certification for electricians who perform work as electricians for contractors licensed as C-10 electrical contractors, unless the worker falls under an exemption. California’s electrician certification rules apply to people who engage in the connection of electrical devices for electrical contractors licensed under the applicable C-10 classification. The state’s FAQ explains that all electricians who make connections greater than 100 volt-amps and work for C-10 contractors are required to be certified.

A licensed C-10 contractor who is the qualifying person under the contractor license may not need a separate electrician certification to perform electrical work for that licensed contractor. However, employees, foremen, non-qualifying partners, and officers who perform covered electrical work under a C-10 contractor may need certification. Out-of-state electricians working in California for C-10 contractors must be certified before performing covered electrical work in the state.

California’s electrician certification structure is different from states that issue a state “Master Electrician” license. California focuses on certification classifications tied to the type of electrical work performed. Candidates should select the classification that matches their work scope and use the official application instructions for that classification.
