Exam Details

California electrician certification examinations are administered through the Department of Industrial Relations, Division of Labor Standards Enforcement, Electrician Certification Unit. The current examination program is supported by CPS HR Consulting, with scheduling through Pearson VUE for exams taken on or after June 1, 2026. Before taking a California electrician certification examination, applicants must apply and be approved by DLSE.

California certification categories include General Electrician, Residential Electrician, Fire/Life Safety Technician, Voice Data Video Technician, and Non-Residential Lighting Technician. The correct examination depends on the certification category being pursued. The General Electrician certification applies to work for an electrical contractor installing, constructing, or maintaining electrical systems covered by the National Electrical Code. The Residential Electrician certification applies to qualifying residential electrical work covered by the NEC within the scope described by California’s program.

The California General Electrician examination contains 100 questions with 4 hours and 30 minutes of allowed testing time. The content outline includes Safety, Determination of Electrical System Requirements, Installation, and Maintenance and Repair. The examination may also include questions based on trade knowledge or general industry practices.

The California Residential Electrician examination contains 80 questions with 3 hours and 30 minutes of allowed testing time. Its content outline also includes Safety, Determination of Electrical System Requirements, Installation, and Maintenance and Repair. Residential candidates should focus on the electrical systems, wiring methods, load requirements, safety rules, and installation standards that apply to residential occupancies within the certification scope.

California also administers examinations for Fire/Life Safety Technician, Voice Data Video Technician, and Non-Residential Lighting Technician certifications. Those exams have their own content outlines and reference materials. Students should prepare for the specific California certification category that matches the work they perform and the credential they are seeking.

The current California electrical certification candidate information identifies a 70 percent minimum passing score. Score results are provided after the exam, and candidates who pass receive certification documentation through the program process. Candidates who do not pass must follow the retest process and waiting period required by the Electrician Certification Unit.

Open Book Test

California electrical certification examinations provide specified references in the testing center for use during the examination. Candidates may not bring or use their own reference materials in the testing room. The testing program provides the approved references that are available during the exam session.

Because references are available during the examination, candidates should still study as if speed and familiarity matter. Having reference access does not remove the need to understand the material. Licensing Steps

California electrician certification begins with identifying the correct certification category. A student should determine whether they are pursuing General Electrician, Residential Electrician, Fire/Life Safety Technician, Voice Data Video Technician, or Non-Residential Lighting Technician certification. Each category has its own scope and examination expectations.

Before taking the examination, the applicant must apply to the Division of Labor Standards Enforcement and receive approval to test. Applications may be completed through the Electrician Certification Unit process. The completed application, supporting documentation, and required fees must be submitted as required by DLSE.

Once DLSE approves the applicant for testing, the candidate receives instructions for scheduling the examination. For examinations taken on or after June 1, 2026, the scheduling process is handled through CPS HR Consulting and Pearson VUE. Candidates receive authorization information and then schedule the exam online or by telephone through the testing system.

The examination must be taken within the eligibility period provided by the program. Candidates should review their authorization instructions carefully and schedule early enough to allow time for preparation, travel planning, and any required identification review. On exam day, candidates must bring proper identification and follow the testing center’s rules for personal items, security procedures, breaks, and conduct.

After the examination, candidates receive their score results. A minimum score of 70 percent is required to pass. Candidates who pass are processed for certification according to the program rules. Candidates who fail must follow the required retest process, including the waiting period and any required retest application and fee.

California electrical contracting is separate from electrician certification. Contractors who want to contract independently for electrical work in California generally pursue the C-10 Electrical Contractor license through the Contractors State License Board. That contractor licensing process is different from the DIR electrician certification exam process and may include separate business, law, trade, experience, bond, insurance, and application requirements.

State Requirements

California’s electrician certification program is administered by the Department of Industrial Relations through the Division of Labor Standards Enforcement, Electrician Certification Unit. Existing law requires persons performing work as electricians under a C-10 licensed contractor to be certified under the state’s electrician certification standards, unless an applicable exception applies.

The state certification program recognizes multiple certification types rather than a single statewide master electrician license. General Electrician certification covers broad NEC electrical system work performed for an electrical contractor. Residential Electrician certification covers qualifying residential electrical work within the program’s defined scope. Fire/Life Safety Technician, Voice Data Video Technician, and Non-Residential Lighting Technician certifications cover more specialized categories.

California certification applicants must meet the requirements for the category they are pursuing and receive approval before scheduling the examination. The Electrician Certification Unit determines eligibility. Students should keep their application requirements separate from their study preparation. Study materials help with exam readiness, while the state application process determines whether the candidate is eligible to test and receive certification.

Certified electricians must also follow renewal requirements. The current California candidate information states that certification renewal is required every three years and that 32 hours of continuing education from an approved California educational provider must be completed before renewal. Electricians should maintain current contact information with DLSE so certification records and renewal information remain accurate.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong California electrical study plan should combine NEC reading, calculation practice, trade review, and timed question practice. The General Electrician and Residential Electrician examination outlines emphasize safety, electrical system requirements, installation, maintenance, and repair. Students should study these categories separately and then practice mixed-topic review so they can switch between concepts quickly.

Safety should not be treated as a small topic. Electrical safety includes workplace hazards, equipment condition, lockout and energized work awareness, protective practices, and safe installation principles. Students should review electrical safety alongside code requirements because many practical questions involve both proper installation and safe work behavior.

Determination of electrical system requirements is another important study area. This may involve understanding loads, services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor requirements, equipment ratings, grounding and bonding, demand factors, and how electrical systems are sized for the work being performed. This is where code navigation and calculations often come together.

Installation is the largest content area for California General Electrician and Residential Electrician exams. Students should spend significant study time on wiring methods, conductor installation, raceways, boxes, equipment, panelboards, grounding, overcurrent protection, devices, luminaires, special equipment, and installation conditions. A strong understanding of installation rules helps candidates answer both direct code questions and practical trade questions.

Maintenance and repair topics require candidates to understand how systems operate and how problems are identified. This may include troubleshooting principles, equipment replacement, circuit evaluation, testing awareness, and recognizing unsafe or noncompliant conditions. Candidates should connect maintenance knowledge to the NEC and general industry practices.

Electrical calculations should be practiced consistently. Students should work on load calculations, conductor ampacity, voltage drop concepts, box fill, raceway fill, overcurrent protection, motor calculations, and service or feeder sizing. A reliable calculation process helps reduce errors: read the question carefully, identify the topic, locate the correct rule or table, set up the calculation, solve it, and check whether the answer must be rounded, adjusted, or compared to a code minimum or maximum.
