## Exam Details

The California C-10 Electrical contractor examination is part of the Contractors State License Board licensing process. CSLB maintains the trade examination content for each contractor classification, and California contractor exams are administered through computer-based testing. The C-10 Electrical exam is the trade exam for applicants pursuing the electrical contractor classification.

The official C-10 Electrical exam content is divided into five major areas: Planning and Estimating; Rough Wiring; Finish Wiring and Trim; Startup, Troubleshooting, and Maintenance; and Safety. These categories reflect the practical knowledge expected of an electrical contractor, qualifying individual, or advanced electrical professional. Candidates should be prepared for questions involving project review, electrical calculations, material selection, code requirements, system layout, installation practices, testing, maintenance, and jobsite safety.

Planning and Estimating includes topics such as existing system evaluation, plan and specification interpretation, electrical calculations, code requirements, material selection, and planning for energy production and storage systems. This part of the exam requires careful reading and practical judgment. Candidates may need to understand how work is planned, how loads are evaluated, how materials are selected, and how project conditions affect electrical decisions.

Rough Wiring includes system layout, clearances, accessibility, raceway installation, panel installation, wire installation, equipment installation, grounding and bonding, and rough wiring for energy production and storage systems. These topics are essential for electricians who work with real installations. Candidates should be comfortable with wiring methods, equipment placement, conductor requirements, grounding rules, and installation sequencing.

Finish Wiring and Trim includes device placement, device installation, motor and equipment installation, labeling, trims, sealants, and finish wiring for energy systems. These topics may represent a smaller part of the exam, but they still require careful attention. Finish work affects system operation, inspection readiness, safety, and professional completion of the job.

Startup, Troubleshooting, and Maintenance includes system energizing, testing, common electrical problems, repair and replacement decisions, use of electrical testing equipment, and energy production and storage system performance. These questions often require practical field judgment. Candidates should understand how to identify faults, use test instruments, evaluate performance issues, and determine appropriate corrective action.

Safety is also a major part of the exam. Candidates should study lockout/tagout procedures, personal protective equipment, tools, equipment use, hazardous materials, and jobsite conditions. Electrical work carries serious hazards, and contractor-level exams include safety because safe work practices are central to professional responsibility.

## Closed Book Test

The California C-10 Electrical contractor examination is a closed-book test. No reference materials may be used during the examination. Candidates should not plan to use code books, handwritten notes, study guides, flash cards, practice exams, or reference manuals during the actual test session.

Closed-book testing changes the way you should study. For open-book exams, candidates often focus heavily on reference navigation. For the California C-10 Electrical exam, candidates must be able to recall and apply the material without looking it up during the test. That does not mean code study is unimportant. It means the code-based concepts must be learned before exam day and practiced until they can be applied from memory.

This combo is designed to support closed-book preparation. The study guide helps you organize your review. The 12 practice exams help you apply what you are studying in question form. The 2 full final exams help you build testing stamina and check readiness. The flash cards help reinforce the facts, formulas, terms, and concepts that need fast recall.

For a closed-book electrical exam, missed-question review is one of the most valuable study habits. When you miss a question, do not simply memorize the answer. Review the concept behind the question. Ask what the question was testing, why the correct answer is best, and why the other choices were not correct. This process builds electrical judgment and helps reduce repeated mistakes.

## Licensing Steps

California electrical contractor licensing is handled by the Contractors State License Board. Applicants pursuing the C-10 Electrical classification must complete the CSLB application process and satisfy the state’s licensing requirements before a license can be issued. The C-10 classification is the main contractor license classification for electrical contracting work in California.

The licensing process begins with selecting the correct classification and submitting the appropriate contractor license application. Applicants identify the qualifying individual, provide required experience information, and complete CSLB’s application review process. The qualifying individual is the person whose experience and knowledge support the license classification.

After CSLB accepts the application and refers the applicant for examination, the candidate receives instructions for scheduling the required exams. Many contractor license applicants must pass both the trade examination and the California Law and Business examination unless an exemption applies. The C-10 Electrical exam covers the electrical trade classification, while the Law and Business exam covers contractor business, legal, and regulatory topics.

After passing the required examination or examinations, the applicant must complete the remaining licensing steps before the license is issued. These may include bonding, fee payment, business entity documentation, workers’ compensation compliance when applicable, fingerprinting or background-related steps, and any additional items required by CSLB.

## State Requirements

California regulates electrical contracting through the Contractors State License Board. A person or business that contracts for electrical work requiring a contractor license must hold the appropriate license classification. The C-10 Electrical classification is the California contractor classification connected to electrical contracting.

California also regulates individual electrician certification through the Department of Industrial Relations, Division of Labor Standards Enforcement, Electrician Certification Unit. This is separate from CSLB contractor licensing. Electrician certification categories include General Electrician, Residential Electrician, Fire/Life Safety Technician, Non-Residential Lighting Technician, and Voice Data Video Technician. Workers performing electrical work for a C-10 contractor may need the proper certification unless an exception applies.

The term “master electrician” is widely understood in the electrical industry, but California’s official structure uses specific state titles and classifications. Students preparing for master-level electrical contracting in California should understand the difference between a C-10 Electrical contractor license and individual electrician certification. The C-10 license is the contractor pathway, while DLSE electrician certification applies to individual electricians performing covered electrical work.

For C-10 contractor applicants, the state process includes application review, qualifying individual requirements, required examinations, and completion of post-exam licensing items. For electrician certification applicants, the process is handled separately through DLSE and depends on the certification category. Candidates should prepare for the exact state path that matches their work goal.

## Test Information and Study Materials

Effective California electrical exam preparation should combine structured review, repeated practice, calculation work, safety review, and closed-book recall. Start with planning and estimating. Review electrical calculations, project conditions, plan reading, material selection, code requirements, and system evaluation. These topics require more than memorization. You need to understand how electrical work is planned, how requirements are interpreted, and how job conditions influence the correct answer.

Next, spend significant time on rough wiring. Study raceways, panels, conductors, grounding and bonding, equipment installation, clearances, accessibility, and system layout. Rough wiring questions often test practical installation decisions. You may need to understand how several concepts work together, such as conductor sizing, equipment grounding, overcurrent protection, and workspace requirements.

Finish wiring and trim should also be included in your review plan. Device placement, equipment labeling, motor and equipment installation, trims, sealants, and final connection work can appear in exam questions. These topics are tied to system completion, safety, inspection readiness, and professional workmanship.

Troubleshooting and maintenance questions require a different kind of thinking. Candidates should practice identifying common electrical problems, selecting testing equipment, evaluating symptoms, and determining safe corrective actions. These questions often feel practical because they reflect problems that electricians may see in the field.

Safety study should be consistent throughout your preparation. Review lockout/tagout, personal protective equipment, proper tool use, hazardous materials, electrical hazards, and jobsite safety conditions. Safety questions may appear straightforward, but they often require careful reading. The best answer is usually the one that protects workers, follows procedure, and avoids unnecessary risk.

Use the flash cards during shorter study sessions. Flash cards are useful before work, after a job, during lunch, or during final review.
