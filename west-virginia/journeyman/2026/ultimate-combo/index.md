## State Requirements

West Virginia requires electrical licensing for regulated electrical work performed for compensation or hire. The State Fire Marshal’s Office administers electrician licensing statewide. West Virginia law also recognizes supervised assistant or helper electrician work performed under a journeyman or master electrician.

For a journeyman electrician license, West Virginia’s electrician licensing rules identify qualification routes for examination. A person may qualify by having at least one year of experience as an assistant or helper electrician performing electrical work under the direction or instruction of a master or journeyman electrician. A candidate may also qualify by completing a United States Department of Labor registered electrical apprenticeship program. Another route includes completion of an approved electrical vocational education program of at least 1,080 hours, including the required training and competency documentation described in the state rule.

Journeyman electrician candidates should keep accurate records of work experience, apprenticeship completion, education, and any documents needed for application review. Because electrician licensing supports public safety, the state expects candidates to understand the electrical code, safe installation practices, and the responsibilities that come with journeyman-level work.

The journeyman electrician license is an important credential for electricians who are advancing from helper, trainee, apprentice, or supervised work toward greater responsibility in the trade.
