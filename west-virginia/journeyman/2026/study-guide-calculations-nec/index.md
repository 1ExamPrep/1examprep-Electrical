## Licensing Steps

West Virginia journeyman electrician licensing is handled by the West Virginia State Fire Marshal’s Office. Applicants must meet the qualifications for the license classification, submit the required application materials, receive authorization for examination, pass the licensing exam, and complete the licensing process through the State Fire Marshal’s Office.

To apply for a West Virginia journeyman electrician license, an applicant must meet at least one of the listed qualification paths. One path is at least 1 year of experience as an assistant/helper electrician performing electrical work under the direction or instruction of a master or journeyman electrician. Another path is completion of a United States Department of Labor/Bureau of Apprenticeship and Training registered electrical apprenticeship program. A third path is completion of a West Virginia Board of Education-approved electrical vocational education program of at least 1,080 hours, including the required testing and instructor affidavit of competency to sit for the journeyman examination.

Applicants must be at least 18 years old to take an electrician licensing examination or to be licensed. West Virginia also requires applicants to provide an application review fee when requesting examination. After passing the examination and paying the required license fee, the applicant may be issued the appropriate electrician license.

A practical preparation plan should begin before the exam application is submitted. Candidates should organize work experience records, apprenticeship documentation, vocational program records, and any required affidavits. They should also review the journeyman exam structure, build a study calendar, practice code navigation, and work through calculation problems before the exam date. ## State Requirements

West Virginia requires electrical work performed for compensation or hire to be performed by properly licensed individuals or businesses unless an exemption applies. Electrician licensing rules are administered through the West Virginia State Fire Marshal’s Office under West Virginia electrical licensing law and State Fire Marshal rules.

A West Virginia journeyman electrician is defined as a person qualified by at least 1 year of electrical work experience to install wires, conduits, apparatus, equipment, fixtures, and other appliances, subject to supervision by a master electrician. The journeyman electrician must have knowledge and understanding of the National Electrical Code as it applies to that work. A journeyman electrician is not authorized to design any part of an electrical system.

West Virginia rules distinguish between master electricians, journeyman electricians, assistant/helper electricians, and specialty electrician classifications. Assistant/helper electricians directly assist licensed electricians and are not permitted to perform electrical work unsupervised. A master electrician has broader supervisory and design responsibilities, including supervising journeyman and assistant/helper electricians and understanding design, layout, and demand load calculations.

The State Fire Marshal’s rules also address jobsite licensing requirements, experience qualifications, examination requirements, licensing fees, renewal, expiration, and reciprocity. West Virginia electrician licenses expire on June 30 each year, and renewal is handled through the State Fire Marshal’s licensing process. Electricians should keep their license current because electrical work may not be performed during a period when the license is expired.

Because the West Virginia journeyman electrician license is tied closely to National Electrical Code knowledge, candidates should study both practical electrical work and NEC application. The exam preparation process should include wiring methods, conductor installation, raceways, boxes, grounding, bonding, services, feeders, branch circuits, overcurrent protection, motors, equipment, special conditions, and calculation-based problem solving.
