## Exam Details

Florida electrical licensing has both state and local components. At the state level, the Florida Electrical Contractors’ Licensing Board is responsible for licensing and regulating electrical contractors. State electrical certification examinations for electrical contractors are administered on behalf of the Electrical Contractors’ Licensing Board and include technical/safety and business components.

Journeyman electrician licensing in Florida is commonly handled by local counties or municipalities rather than through one statewide individual journeyman license. Local jurisdictions may use their own construction trades boards, testing vendors, application forms, experience rules, and exam requirements. Some jurisdictions use testing vendors such as Prov, while state electrical contractor examinations are administered through Pearson VUE.

Because Florida journeyman requirements are local, candidates should confirm the exact license name, application board, experience requirement, exam vendor, code edition, allowed references, number of questions, time limit, and passing score for the jurisdiction where they are applying. A candidate preparing for a Miami-Dade County journeyman electrical exam may have different instructions from a candidate applying through another county or city.

Florida law recognizes local journeyman licenses in the electrical and alarm system trades under certain conditions. A person who holds a valid, active journeyman license issued by a Florida county or municipality may work as a journeyman in the same trade in another county or municipality without taking an additional examination or paying an additional license fee when the statutory conditions are met. Those conditions include passing an approved proctored journeyman examination and meeting qualifying apprenticeship or experience requirements.

## Open Book Test

For example, Miami-Dade County testing information for Prov-administered exams states that the tests are open book and timed, and that candidates receive detailed information about required tests, content, and study materials when applying. An open book electrical exam still requires serious preparation. Having the NEC available does not mean every answer will be easy to find. ## Licensing Steps

The Florida journeyman electrician licensing path usually begins at the local level. Candidates should first identify the county, city, or local board that issues the journeyman electrical credential they need. The local authority will determine the application process, required experience, testing vendor, fee structure, and documents needed for approval.

Many local journeyman pathways require documented electrical work experience. Florida law addressing local journeyman recognition refers to approved proctored examinations and apprenticeship or experience requirements. Local jurisdictions may require completion of an apprenticeship program, a specific number of years in the trade, verified supervised electrical work, or a combination of education and experience.

After identifying the correct local board, the candidate should gather employment verification, apprenticeship records, training documentation, identification, and any forms required by the jurisdiction. Complete records are important because the local board must determine whether the applicant qualifies to sit for the examination or receive the local journeyman credential.

Once approved, the candidate schedules the examination through the testing vendor or local testing office identified by the jurisdiction. The candidate should review the official exam bulletin or local instructions before testing. Those instructions typically identify the exam format, reference books, allowed materials, calculator rules, check-in procedures, time limit, passing score, and retesting rules.

After passing the required examination, the candidate completes any remaining local licensing steps. This may include paying license fees, submitting final score reports, providing updated documents, or completing registration with the local authority. Candidates who plan to work outside the issuing jurisdiction should review Florida’s rules for local journeyman recognition and confirm how the license is treated in the county or municipality where the work will be performed.

## State Requirements

Florida does not issue one statewide individual journeyman electrician license. State-level electrical licensing focuses on electrical contractor licenses issued through the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation and the Electrical Contractors’ Licensing Board. Electrical contractor licenses include certified licenses, which are statewide, and registered licenses, which are connected to local jurisdictions.

Journeyman electrician credentials are commonly issued at the county or municipal level. Local licensing authorities may use different names, forms, experience requirements, and examination procedures. Candidates should treat the local board’s instructions as the controlling requirements for the journeyman exam they plan to take.

Florida law provides a framework for recognition of local journeyman licenses in the electrical or alarm system trade. A person with a valid, active local journeyman license may work as a journeyman in the same trade in another Florida county or municipality without taking another examination or paying another license fee when the law’s conditions are met. Those conditions include having passed an approved proctored journeyman examination and meeting the required apprenticeship or experience standard.

Because Florida’s system includes both state contractor licensing and local journeyman licensing, candidates should understand the difference between a journeyman credential and an electrical contractor license. A journeyman license generally supports work as an individual tradesperson under the appropriate employment or supervision structure. An electrical contractor license relates to contracting, business responsibility, and the legal authority to offer electrical contracting services.

Candidates who want to operate an electrical contracting business in Florida should review the state electrical contractor license categories and application requirements through the Florida DBPR. Candidates who want an individual journeyman credential should work directly with the local county, city, or construction trades board that issues the license they need.

Timed practice is valuable because many Florida local exams are timed. Candidates should occasionally answer questions under time limits while using the NEC.
