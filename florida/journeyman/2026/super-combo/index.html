Exam Details

Florida journeyman electrician exam details vary by local jurisdiction. Many Florida counties and local licensing boards require journeyman candidates to apply locally, document qualifying experience or apprenticeship completion, receive approval, and then take an approved journeyman electrical examination through the testing provider used by that jurisdiction.

A commonly used Florida journeyman electrical exam format is an open-book examination covering the planning, installation, repair, alteration, and maintenance of electric conductors, raceways, and equipment for light, heat, and power. One listed journeyman electrical examination outline contains 80 questions, a 3-hour time limit, and a 75% passing score. Local jurisdictions may use different exam providers, forms, passing standards, eligibility rules, or reference lists, so candidates should follow the exact bulletin provided by their local board or testing agency.

Common journeyman electrical subject areas include general electrical knowledge, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, wiring methods, wiring protection, overcurrent protection, motors, electrical equipment, special occupancies, special equipment, safety, and electrical calculations. These subjects reflect the core trade knowledge expected of a journeyman-level electrician and form the foundation of strong electrical exam preparation.

Florida candidates should also understand the difference between local journeyman certification and state electrical contractor licensing. State certified electrical contractor examinations are administered under Florida DBPR and the Electrical Contractors’ Licensing Board. Journeyman electrician licensing or certificates of competency are typically local and may be handled by county or municipal construction licensing offices. The correct path depends on the credential being pursued.

Licensing Steps

The Florida journeyman electrician path usually begins at the local level. A candidate should first identify the county, city, or local licensing board that controls journeyman electrician credentials in the area where the candidate plans to work. Local offices may use terms such as journeyman electrician, journeyman electrical certificate of competency, or local journeyman registration.

A practical path begins with reviewing the local application instructions. Many local jurisdictions require proof of electrical experience, apprenticeship completion, or verification of construction experience before approving a candidate for examination. Some jurisdictions accept a completed apprenticeship certificate, while others require documented work history from employers or a combination of training and experience.

After the candidate submits the local application, the licensing office or board reviews the file. If the candidate has not already taken an approved exam, the local office may refer the candidate to a third-party testing agency. The testing agency provides scheduling instructions, payment instructions, testing location information, and the reference list for the exam.

Once approved and scheduled, the candidate takes the journeyman electrical examination under the testing provider’s rules. After passing, the candidate submits required score reports or completion documentation to the local jurisdiction, pays any required local registration or certificate fees, and completes the remaining licensing or certificate process.

A strong study path should begin before the exam is scheduled. Journeyman electrical content is broad, and consistent review is more effective than last-minute studying.
