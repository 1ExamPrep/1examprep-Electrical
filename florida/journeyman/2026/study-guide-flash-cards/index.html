Exam Details

Florida does not have one uniform statewide journeyman electrician exam that applies to every individual electrician in the state. State electrical contractor certification examinations are administered on behalf of the Electrical Contractors’ Licensing Board, while journeyman-level electrician examinations and competency cards are commonly handled locally. Depending on the Florida jurisdiction, a candidate may be testing through a county, city, local construction trades board, or third-party testing provider used by that jurisdiction.

Florida’s state electrical contractor certification examination is administered through Pearson VUE and is offered in a computer-based testing format. The state electrical certification examination is divided into a Technical/Safety section and a Business section. That state contractor examination is separate from a local journeyman electrician credential, but it shows how Florida separates statewide contractor licensing from local or jurisdiction-based electrician qualification processes.

Local Florida journeyman electrician exams are commonly designed to measure whether candidates understand electrical theory, code application, wiring methods, calculations, electrical safety, installation practices, and the use of adopted reference materials. Many local exams use the National Electrical Code edition adopted by the jurisdiction. Candidates should use the candidate bulletin, local licensing board instructions, or testing-provider notice for the exact exam name, code edition, time limit, question count, passing score, allowed references, and retesting rules.

Open Book Test

Florida journeyman-level electrical exams are commonly open-book, reference-based exams that allow approved code materials such as the National Electrical Code adopted by the jurisdiction. Local testing rules may vary, so candidates should follow the instructions issued by the specific Florida county, city, board, or testing provider administering the exam.

Open book does not mean easy. Candidates still need to understand electrical theory, code structure, calculations, wiring methods, installation requirements, safety concepts, and trade terminology. A student who relies only on searching through references for every answer can lose valuable time during the exam.

Open-book electrical exams reward preparation and reference familiarity. Students should practice using the table of contents, index, article structure, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and common code sections before exam day. The more comfortable a student is with the reference material, the less time is wasted searching during the test.

Licensing Steps

Florida journeyman electrician licensing steps depend on the jurisdiction. Unlike states with one statewide journeyman electrician license, Florida generally handles individual journeyman-level electrical credentials at the local level. A candidate may need to apply through a county licensing board, municipal construction trades board, local contractor licensing office, or other local authority that recognizes journeyman electrical qualifications.

A typical Florida journeyman-level path may include completing electrical apprenticeship training or supervised electrical work experience, documenting qualifying work hours, submitting an application to the local licensing authority, receiving approval to test, scheduling the examination through the assigned testing provider, passing the required exam, and completing any local licensing or competency-card issuance steps. The exact process depends on the jurisdiction.

Candidates should first identify the correct local licensing authority for the area where they plan to work. After identifying the jurisdiction, students should review the local application packet, experience documentation requirements, exam bulletin, testing provider instructions, adopted code edition, approved references, fees, and renewal rules. Local authorities may use different testing providers or recognize different credential pathways.

The Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation and the Electrical Contractors’ Licensing Board regulate state electrical contractor licensing. State electrical certification examinations are given in two parts: Technical/Safety and Business. These state contractor exams are not the same as a local Florida journeyman electrician exam.

Journeyman-level electrician requirements in Florida are commonly set by local jurisdictions. Some counties or cities may issue journeyman electrician competency cards, require documented electrical work experience, require apprenticeship completion, require a local examination, or recognize certain outside credentials.
