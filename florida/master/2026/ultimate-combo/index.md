## Exam Details

Florida electrical contractor licensing examinations are administered on behalf of the Electrical Contractors’ Licensing Board. The state electrical certification examinations are given in two parts: a Technical/Safety section and a Business section. Candidates should prepare for both parts because Florida certification requires more than technical code knowledge alone.

The Electrical Contractors Certification exam includes a Business section with 50 scored test questions and 2 1/2 hours of testing time. The Technical/Safety section includes 100 scored test questions and 5 hours of testing time. These examination parts may include additional pilot questions that are not scored for or against the candidate.

Florida electrical contractor examinations are administered in computer-based testing format through Pearson VUE. Candidates schedule the examination after completing the required authorization process. Test appointments, identification rules, examination day procedures, and reference-material requirements are controlled by the testing program and the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation.

The Technical/Safety portion is designed to evaluate electrical trade knowledge, code application, and safety-related understanding. Candidates should expect NEC-based topics, electrical theory, installation requirements, wiring methods, services, feeders, branch circuits, conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, motors, equipment, and other electrical contractor knowledge areas. The Business section evaluates business and licensing knowledge needed for contractor practice.

Because Florida’s statewide licensing system is contractor-based, candidates should understand the difference between Certified and Registered electrical contractor licensing. A Certified Electrical Contractor license is a state credential that allows qualifying electrical contracting work throughout Florida. A Registered Electrical Contractor license is connected to local competency and is limited to the jurisdictions where the registration applies.

## Open Book Test

The Florida Electrical Contractors Certification examinations are open-book exams. Open book means candidates must know how to use their references efficiently. The exam clock continues while candidates search, read, compare sections, and work calculations. ## Licensing Steps

Florida electrical contractor candidates should begin by identifying the correct license path. The Electrical Contractors’ Licensing Board regulates electrical contractors in Florida, including certified and registered license categories. Candidates pursuing a statewide credential generally focus on certification, while candidates working under local jurisdictional authority may follow registered contractor requirements.

The exam process begins with completing the state’s examination application and authorization process. After approval, candidates schedule the required examination parts through Pearson VUE. Candidates should follow the current examination instructions, bring required identification, arrive prepared for test center rules, and use only approved reference materials during the exam.

After passing the required examination parts, candidates complete the licensing application process through the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation. Licensing applications may require documentation related to experience, financial responsibility, business structure, background screening, insurance, and other state requirements. Candidates should submit complete and accurate documentation so the Electrical Contractors’ Licensing Board can evaluate the application.

Applicants should keep copies of examination authorizations, score reports, business records, insurance documentation, and licensing correspondence. Candidates planning to qualify a business should also review Florida’s rules for qualifying agents, business organizations, and license responsibilities before submitting final licensing paperwork.

## State Requirements

Florida electrical contractor licensing is regulated by the Department of Business and Professional Regulation through the Electrical Contractors’ Licensing Board. The Board is responsible for licensing and regulating electrical contractors, reviewing applications, considering disciplinary matters, and administering rules connected to electrical contractor licensing.

Florida’s electrical contractor structure includes certified and registered licenses. A certified license is issued by the state and generally allows the license holder to operate statewide within the scope of the license. A registered license is based on local competency and is limited to the local jurisdiction or jurisdictions where the registration is recognized.

The Certified Electrical Contractor classification is the broad statewide electrical contractor license. Florida also recognizes electrical and alarm system contractor classifications and specialty categories, so candidates should select the correct license type before applying. The license classification determines the scope of work, the examination path, and the licensing requirements that apply.

Florida candidates should also understand that passing the examination is not the same as receiving a license. The exam is one part of the overall licensing process. The Board must approve the licensing application and confirm that all required documentation has been submitted before the license is issued.
