Exam Details

Florida state electrical certification examinations are administered on behalf of the Electrical Contractors’ Licensing Board. The examination program is offered in two parts: a Business section and a Technical/Safety section. Both parts are computer-based and scheduled through Pearson VUE after the candidate receives approval from the Department of Business and Professional Regulation.

The Business section contains 50 scored questions and allows 2.5 hours of testing time. The Business section is required for all electrical certification categories. Its content includes maintaining cash flow, estimating and bidding, contracts and agreements, purchasing control, contract scheduling, insurance and bonding, contracting laws and rules, personnel management, payroll and sales tax laws, financial statements and reports, and management accounting.

The Technical/Safety section for the Unlimited Electrical Contractor classification contains 100 scored questions and allows 5 hours of testing time. The exam may also include pilot questions that are not scored. The Technical/Safety section is designed to measure electrical contracting fundamentals and the candidate’s ability to interpret and apply the National Electrical Code and other applicable references to practical problems.

The Unlimited Electrical Contractor Technical/Safety content outline includes general theory and electrical principles, plan and specification reading, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, special occupancies and situations, OSHA and safety procedures, life safety and Americans with Disabilities Act topics, electrical signs and outline lighting, structural considerations, alarms, and limited energy topics.

The minimum percentage score needed to pass both the Business section and the Technical/Safety section is 75 percent. Candidates receive an official exam result report after completing the examination. Candidates who fail the Business section are allowed three attempts before needing to reapply for a new authorization. Candidates who fail the Technical/Safety section must reapply to the department for a new authorization to test.

This product focuses on electrical trade preparation, NEC study, and calculation review. It is not a business exam manual, but the electrical knowledge and reference-navigation practice in this combo can support the Technical/Safety side of Florida contractor-level electrical preparation.

Open Book Test

Florida electrical certification examinations are open book. Candidates are strongly encouraged to bring approved reference materials for their specific certification category to the examination site. No other references are allowed in the examination room.

Approved reference materials are controlled by the candidate information booklet and the exam category. Only one copy of each reference is allowed into the examination. Original edition reference materials are required unless the candidate information booklet specifically identifies a printable reference list item that may be printed and bound. Permanent tabs may be used in reference books. Post-it notes, pull-off labels, removable tabs, class notes, formulas, sample questions, bound or loose-leaf study materials, notes, and similar materials are not allowed in the examination room.

Open-book testing does not mean the exam is easy. The clock continues to run while the candidate searches through references. Students who are unfamiliar with the NEC may waste valuable time moving between articles, tables, definitions, exceptions, and cross-references. The strongest preparation comes from practicing code navigation before test day, learning the layout of the NEC, and building a clean method for locating information quickly.

The NEC 2026 paperback included in this combo supports that habit. Students can use it to practice finding articles, reading definitions, reviewing tables, studying wiring method rules, locating grounding and bonding provisions, and applying calculation requirements. The goal is to build familiarity with the code book before entering the testing center.

Licensing Steps

Florida electrical contractor candidates should begin by identifying the correct license category. Florida offers multiple electrical contractor certification categories, including unlimited electrical contractor and specialty electrical contractor classifications. The statewide Certified Electrical Contractor license is different from a Registered Electrical Contractor credential that is tied to a local jurisdiction.

After choosing the correct category, the candidate applies through the Department of Business and Professional Regulation. Once the department approves the candidate to sit for the examination, the candidate schedules the computer-based exam through Pearson VUE. Candidates may schedule after receiving approval and must provide the required identification and scheduling information through the testing provider.

The Florida electrical contractor certification examination includes both the Business section and the Technical/Safety section. Candidates must pass the required examination parts for the certification category they are pursuing. Passing examination results are part of the licensing process, but the Department and the Electrical Contractors’ Licensing Board determine whether the applicant satisfies all licensure requirements.

After passing the required exam sections, the applicant completes the remaining licensure process with DBPR and the Electrical Contractors’ Licensing Board. The state licensing process may involve application materials, experience documentation, financial responsibility requirements, insurance-related documentation, and other board requirements for the specific license category.

Students should treat study preparation and licensing paperwork as two connected but separate tasks. Study materials help the candidate prepare for the exam content. The state application process determines eligibility, licensing approval, and whether the applicant meets Florida’s legal requirements for the selected electrical contractor license.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Florida electrical contractor study plan should combine NEC reading, trade-topic review, calculation practice, and timed reference navigation. The Unlimited Electrical Contractor Technical/Safety exam covers 100 scored questions across several broad subject areas, so candidates should avoid studying only one topic. A balanced study routine helps build readiness across the full outline.

General theory and electrical principles should include circuit fundamentals, electrical quantities, power, resistance, current, voltage, grounding concepts, transformers, motors, and practical application of electrical theory. Candidates should be able to connect these fundamentals to code requirements and field conditions.

Plan and specification reading is important because electrical contractors must understand how drawings, schedules, details, and written specifications connect to actual installation requirements. Candidates should practice identifying information from plans and applying that information to code and installation questions.

Wiring and protection is a major exam area. Students should review services, feeders, branch circuits, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, conductor protection, disconnecting means, panelboards, and related NEC rules. These topics often require both code lookup and calculation ability.

Wiring methods and materials require repeated code navigation. Students should review raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, conductors, insulation types, support rules, fill requirements, environmental conditions, and installation limitations. Many questions in this area test whether the candidate can recognize the wiring method or installation condition that controls the answer.

Special occupancies and situations should be studied carefully because they often modify normal rules. Candidates should become familiar with how the NEC treats special locations, special equipment, and conditions that require additional protection or different installation methods.

Electrical calculations should be practiced consistently. Candidates should work on load calculations, conductor ampacity, voltage drop concepts, raceway fill, box fill, motor calculations, overcurrent protection, service and feeder sizing, grounding conductor sizing, and equipment rating questions. A dependable calculation process helps reduce mistakes: read the question carefully, identify the topic, locate the controlling rule or table, set up the calculation, solve it, and check whether the question asks for a minimum, maximum, adjusted value, or final selection.

The 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide supports this part of preparation by helping students focus on the math side of electrical exam readiness. Many calculation mistakes happen because the candidate uses the wrong table, skips a condition in the question, rounds too early, or chooses an answer before confirming what the question is asking. Consistent practice helps improve speed and accuracy.

The 2026 Florida Master Electrician Study Guide supports broader trade review. Advanced electrical preparation often requires candidates to connect several concepts in one question, such as wiring method, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding, special occupancy requirements, and safety considerations. Organized study materials help students move through the content with a clearer plan.
