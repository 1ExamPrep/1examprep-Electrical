Exam Details

Indiana electrical licensing is primarily handled at the local municipal or county level. There is not one statewide Indiana master electrician license that applies uniformly across every city and county. Local jurisdictions control their own licensing processes, and requirements may vary depending on where the electrician plans to work, pull permits, supervise electrical work, or qualify an electrical contracting business.

Indianapolis and Marion County are a common example of Indiana’s local licensing structure. The Department of Business and Neighborhood Services licenses individuals and businesses engaged in construction activity within the Consolidated City of Indianapolis. The Indianapolis Board of Electrical Examiners application materials state that licensing is at the discretion of the local municipality in Indiana and that there is not a state electrical license. Indianapolis identifies electrical license types including Master – Unrestricted and Residential.

For Indianapolis electrical licensing, each business must have at least one electrical license holder who has taken and passed the required examination. Electrical licenses expire on December 31 of odd-numbered years and are renewable every two years. Local application materials also address business licensing documentation, craft license holder applications, insurance, bonding, and workers’ compensation documentation as applicable to the business license process.

Some Indiana jurisdictions use nationally recognized electrical exams, including ICC contractor and trades examinations, while others may use local testing procedures or different testing providers. The ICC National Standard Master Electrician exam outline identifies a master electrician exam with 100 multiple-choice questions, an open book format, and a 5-hour time limit. Content areas include general knowledge and plan reading, services and service equipment, feeders, branch circuits and conductors, wiring methods and materials, equipment and devices, control devices, motors and generators, and special occupancies, equipment, and conditions.

Because Indiana licensing is local, students should treat the exam details for their chosen city or county as the controlling requirements. The 2026 Indiana Master Electrician Study Guide & National Electrical Code Combo with Tabs supports the type of NEC-based preparation commonly needed for master-level electrical exams, but the exact exam name, reference edition, passing score, application procedure, and testing rules may depend on the local licensing authority.

Open Book Test

Many master-level electrical exams used by Indiana municipalities are open book, especially when based on recognized NEC contractor and trades testing. The ICC National Standard Master Electrician exam is identified as open book with a 5-hour time limit and 100 multiple-choice questions. Local jurisdictions may adopt a specific ICC exam version, use a local exam, or apply their own reference rules.

An open book electrical exam still requires serious preparation. Candidates cannot rely on searching from the beginning of the NEC for every answer. The code book is large, technical, and organized by subject. Students need to know where definitions are located, how Chapter 2 wiring and protection rules are structured, how Chapter 3 wiring method rules are arranged, where equipment rules appear, how special occupancies are handled, and how Chapter 9 tables are used.

Exam-day reference rules may vary by Indiana jurisdiction. Some exams may specify a particular NEC edition, allow or restrict certain tabs, limit notes, or list additional references. Students should prepare with the 2026 NEC for updated code-cycle study while also matching their exam-day materials to the rules issued by the local city, county, testing provider, or board.

Licensing Steps

The Indiana electrical licensing path begins with identifying the local jurisdiction that controls the license. Because Indiana does not issue one uniform statewide master electrician license, candidates should determine whether they are applying through Indianapolis, Fort Wayne, South Bend, Evansville, Lafayette, another city, a county, or another local authority.

After identifying the jurisdiction, the candidate should review the exact license classification. Local Indiana license titles may include master electrician, master unrestricted electrician, residential electrician, electrical contractor, journeyman electrician, or another local category. The correct classification depends on the work the applicant wants to perform, whether the applicant will supervise work, whether a business will pull permits, and whether the work is residential, commercial, industrial, or mixed in scope.

The next step is to gather the required documentation. Local licensing applications may require work experience records, identity documents, references, proof of insurance, business registration information, bond documentation, workers’ compensation documentation, or proof that a qualifying license holder is attached to the business. Indianapolis materials state that partial application packets will not be accepted and that incomplete or incorrect information may result in the packet being returned for revisions.

Once the application is reviewed, the candidate follows the exam approval and scheduling process used by the local jurisdiction. If the jurisdiction uses an ICC exam, candidates may register through the testing process associated with that exam. If the jurisdiction uses a local board exam, the applicant follows the board’s scheduling and appearance requirements. Some jurisdictions may require board review or an interview before exam approval.

After passing the required examination, the candidate completes the licensing process with the issuing city or county. For business licensing, the electrical contractor or business entity may need to submit company documentation and identify the qualifying electrical license holder. Candidates should also track renewal deadlines, continuing requirements, and local permit rules after the license is issued.

State Requirements

Indiana electrical licensing is local rather than statewide for individual electrician licensing. Cities, counties, and municipalities may regulate electrical contractors and individual electricians under local ordinances. This means an electrician’s licensing requirements can change depending on where the work is performed.

Indianapolis provides a clear example of local regulation. The city’s contractor licensing page states that the Department of Business and Neighborhood Services licenses individuals and businesses engaged in construction activity within the Consolidated City of Indianapolis. That jurisdiction does not include the excluded cities of Lawrence, Beech Grove, Speedway, and Southport. Indianapolis electrical license requirements also identify that each business must have at least one electrical license holder who has passed the required examination.

Indiana candidates should avoid assuming that one city’s license automatically satisfies another city’s rules. Some jurisdictions may have reciprocity or recognition procedures, while others may require a separate application, local registration, or examination. Indianapolis application materials address reciprocity from another area when an applicant can provide proof that the license is current and that the exam meets the Board’s testing requirements for the appropriate license type.

Because the rules are local, students should prepare for the technical exam while also tracking the administrative requirements of the city or county where they intend to work. A strong electrical exam score does not replace required licensing paperwork, insurance, business registration, local permit authorization, or renewal compliance. Test Information and Study Materials

Indiana master electrician exam preparation should focus on strong NEC knowledge, calculation accuracy, and local licensing awareness. Since Indiana licensing is local, the exact exam may vary, but the core electrical knowledge needed for master-level preparation is consistent across many exams. Students should review general electrical knowledge, plan reading, services, service equipment, feeders, branch circuits, conductors, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, raceways, boxes, equipment, motors, generators, control devices, special occupancies, special equipment, and safety requirements.

Calculation practice should be part of every study plan. Students should practice conductor ampacity, adjustment and correction factors, service load calculations, feeder load calculations, branch-circuit calculations, box fill, conduit fill, motor calculations, transformer calculations, overcurrent protection sizing, grounding electrode conductor sizing, and voltage drop concepts. Master-level questions often test whether the candidate can recognize the correct method and apply it carefully.

Code navigation is just as important as content review. Students should learn how the NEC is organized, including Article 90, Article 100 definitions, Chapter 2 wiring and protection, Chapter 3 wiring methods and materials, Chapter 4 equipment for general use, Chapter 5 special occupancies, Chapter 6 special equipment, Chapter 7 special conditions, Chapter 8 communications systems, and Chapter 9 tables. A tabbed NEC helps students move through these areas more quickly during study.

A practical study routine should include reading a topic, answering related practice questions, locating the supporting code section, reviewing incorrect answers, and repeating weak areas. Students should avoid memorizing answers without understanding the rule. The goal is to develop the ability to read a question, identify the subject, locate the applicable code section, and apply the requirement accurately.

Timed practice is also important. Open book exams can create a false sense of security, but a candidate who spends too long searching for each answer can run out of time. Students should practice answering questions in sets, using the NEC efficiently, and marking difficult questions for review.
