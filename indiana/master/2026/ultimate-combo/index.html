Exam Details

Indiana does not use one single statewide master electrician exam that applies identically to every city and county. Electrical licensing and examination requirements are commonly set by local jurisdictions. A candidate may need to apply through a city, county, town, contractor board, building department, or local licensing office depending on where the work will be performed.

Because of this local structure, the exact exam title, application process, reference list, testing provider, passing score, and license classification can vary. Some candidates may be preparing for a master electrician license, while others may be pursuing an electrical contractor license, supervising electrician credential, municipal registration, or another local electrical classification. Open Book Test

Many NEC-based master electrician and electrical contractor exams are administered as open-book exams, but the exact rules depend on the local Indiana jurisdiction and testing provider. Open book testing still requires serious preparation. Candidates must know how to use the code book efficiently, understand where information is located, and apply code language correctly within the time allowed.

An open-book exam does not mean the answer will be easy to find. Electrical exam questions often require candidates to connect multiple code sections, interpret definitions, review table notes, apply exceptions, and perform calculations. A candidate who is unfamiliar with the NEC may lose valuable time searching through the book, even when the correct reference is technically available.

The included National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs supports code-navigation practice during study. Tabs help candidates become familiar with the layout of the NEC, including chapters, articles, tables, and key subject areas. The best use of the code book is active practice: read the question, identify the topic, locate the rule, confirm the answer, and understand why the other answer choices are incorrect.

Before exam day, candidates should review their official candidate bulletin or local testing instructions to confirm which references are allowed. Some exams may permit the NEC and certain tabs, while others may restrict handwritten notes, loose papers, added pages, or non-approved materials. Candidates should prepare with the 2026 NEC while following the exact book and reference rules for the exam they are taking.

Licensing Steps

Because Indiana electrical licensing is commonly managed locally, the licensing process begins by identifying the authority that controls electrical licensing in the city, county, or town where the candidate plans to work. This may be a building department, contractor licensing office, board of electrical examiners, municipal licensing department, or other local authority having jurisdiction.

After locating the correct authority, candidates should confirm the license classification they need. Depending on the jurisdiction, the credential may be called master electrician, electrical contractor, journeyman electrician, supervising electrician, residential electrician, or municipal electrical registration. Each classification may have its own experience requirements, application steps, exam rules, insurance requirements, bond requirements, renewal requirements, and business registration requirements.

The next step is reviewing the application requirements. Candidates may need to provide work experience documentation, employment history, proof of prior licensing, education records, identification, business formation documents, insurance certificates, or other local forms. Some jurisdictions may require board approval before a candidate can schedule the exam.

Once the application path is clear, candidates can prepare for the exam using the applicable code edition and reference list. State Requirements

Indiana electrical licensing requirements can vary significantly by local jurisdiction. A license or registration accepted in one city or county may not automatically authorize electrical work in another location. Candidates should confirm the rules for the exact area where they plan to perform, supervise, or contract electrical work.

Local authorities may set requirements for experience, examination, business registration, insurance, bonding, permits, inspections, continuing education, renewal cycles, and license classification. Some jurisdictions may distinguish between an individual electrician license and an electrical contractor license. An individual credential may demonstrate technical qualification, while a contractor license or registration may authorize a business to offer electrical contracting services.

Candidates should also confirm the NEC edition used by the local exam. This product is based on the 2026 National Electrical Code. If a local exam references a different code edition, candidates should follow the requirements listed by that exam authority.
