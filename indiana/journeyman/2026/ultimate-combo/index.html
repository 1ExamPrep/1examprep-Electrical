Exam Details

Indiana does not use one single statewide journeyman electrician exam for every candidate. Journeyman electrician licensing, registration, and testing requirements are commonly set by local jurisdictions. A candidate may need to follow the rules of a city, county, municipal licensing board, local building department, or other local authority.

A commonly used journeyman-level electrical exam format is the ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician exam. That exam includes 80 multiple-choice questions, allows 4 hours of testing time, and is open book. Its subject areas include general knowledge, services and service equipment, feeders, branch circuits and conductors, wiring methods and materials, equipment and devices, control devices, motors and generators, and special occupancies, equipment, and conditions.

The ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician exam places strong emphasis on wiring methods and materials, branch circuits and conductors, equipment and devices, services and service equipment, and special occupancies, equipment, and conditions. These areas reflect practical journeyman-level work and require candidates to understand both code rules and field application.

Some Indiana jurisdictions may use a different electrical exam, a local application process, a specific code edition, local amendments, separate passing score rules, or a different exam provider. Candidates preparing for a local Indiana journeyman electrician credential should match their study materials to the exam required by the jurisdiction where they are applying.

Open Book Test

The ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician exam is an open book test. Because Indiana journeyman electrician testing is commonly controlled at the local level, candidates must follow the exam-room rules for the specific exam they are scheduled to take.

Open book testing still requires strong preparation. Licensing Steps

Indiana journeyman electrician licensing steps depend on the local authority that issues or recognizes the credential. State Requirements

Because requirements are local, Indiana candidates should prepare for the rules of the jurisdiction where the credential is needed.
