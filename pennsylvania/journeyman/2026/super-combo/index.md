## Exam Details

Pennsylvania electrical exam details depend on the local licensing authority. There is no single statewide Pennsylvania journeyman electrician exam that applies to every city, township, borough, and county. Local jurisdictions may set their own license categories, experience requirements, application steps, exam providers, passing scores, fees, approved references, renewal rules, and work authorization requirements.

Philadelphia and Pittsburgh are examples of Pennsylvania cities with local electrical licensing structures. Philadelphia requires an electrical license for individuals performing electrical work in the city, including low-voltage wiring. Pittsburgh requires an electrical trade license to perform electrical work within the city. Other municipalities may use their own local requirements, and some areas may have different rules for journeyman electricians, master electricians, electrical contractors, registrations, or permit-qualified individuals.

For candidates preparing at the journeyman level, the ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician exam is a common exam model used by various authorities. The ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician examination has 80 multiple-choice questions and a 4-hour time limit. It is an open-book examination and tests general knowledge, services and service equipment, feeders, branch circuits and conductors, wiring methods and materials, equipment and devices, control devices, motors and generators, and special occupancies, equipment, and conditions.

The ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician content outline includes 6% general knowledge, 11% services and service equipment, 4% feeders, 19% branch circuits and conductors, 26% wiring methods and materials, 13% equipment and devices, 4% control devices, 6% motors and generators, and 11% special occupancies, equipment, and conditions.

Journeyman-level electrical study should include NEC definitions, electrical theory, services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor ampacity, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, raceways, boxes, cables, panelboards, motors, transformers, equipment for general use, low-voltage systems, safety, special occupancies, special equipment, and electrical calculations. These areas support local Pennsylvania journeyman-level exams and provide a strong foundation for electricians who may later pursue master electrician, electrical contractor, or local trade license credentials.

## Open Book Test

Many Pennsylvania journeyman-level electrical exams are prepared for as open-book, NEC-based exams, especially when the local authority or testing provider uses an ICC-style journeyman electrician examination or allows approved code references. The ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician examination is open book and uses the National Electrical Code as the primary reference.

Open-book exam rules depend on the local jurisdiction and testing provider. Candidates should follow the exact rules for the exam they are taking, including the approved NEC edition, allowed reference books, tab rules, calculator rules, highlighting rules, note restrictions, and prohibited materials. Some exam providers allow permanent tabs while prohibiting temporary tabs, loose notes, handwritten inserts, photocopied references, or unapproved study materials.

Open book does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates still need to know how to read the question, identify the subject, locate the correct reference, interpret the code language, and answer within the time limit. A candidate who is familiar with the NEC can spend less time searching and more time solving the question.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs included in this Super Combo is intended for study and code navigation practice. Tabs help candidates become familiar with major NEC articles, definitions, tables, and commonly used sections. A strong study routine should include repeated practice moving through the code book instead of relying only on memory or last-minute review.

## Licensing Steps

Pennsylvania candidates should begin by identifying the jurisdiction where they plan to work. Because electrician licensing is local, the correct license path depends on the city, county, township, borough, or local board that regulates electrical work in that area. A candidate working in Philadelphia may follow a different process than a candidate working in Pittsburgh, Allentown, Harrisburg, Erie, Reading, Scranton, Lancaster, or another municipality.

A practical path begins with confirming the local license classification. Candidates should determine whether the jurisdiction requires a journeyman electrician license, master electrician license, electrical contractor license, electrical trade license, registration, permit qualification, or another local credential. The license title matters because each classification may have different experience requirements, exam rules, work scope, insurance requirements, and renewal requirements.

After identifying the correct local license, candidates should gather required documentation. This may include proof of work experience, apprenticeship records, employer verification, education or classroom training records, examination results, identification, insurance information, business documentation, tax records, or local application forms. Requirements vary by jurisdiction.

Once the application is complete, the local authority may approve the candidate to test, require additional documentation, or direct the candidate to a testing provider. Candidates should schedule the correct exam, prepare with the approved reference list and content outline, complete the examination, and submit any passing score documentation required by the local licensing office.

Passing an exam is an important step, but it does not automatically authorize electrical work everywhere in Pennsylvania. A license issued by one local jurisdiction may not authorize work in another. Candidates who plan to work across multiple areas should confirm the requirements for each jurisdiction where they will perform electrical work.
