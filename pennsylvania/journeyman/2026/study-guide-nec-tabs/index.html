Exam Details

Pennsylvania does not have one statewide journeyman electrician exam that applies to every candidate across the Commonwealth. Electrical licensing and examination requirements are commonly handled by local jurisdictions. A candidate preparing for a Pennsylvania journeyman electrician credential should first identify the authority responsible for electrical licensing in the area where the work will be performed.

Local exam requirements may vary. Some jurisdictions use standardized contractor or trade examinations, while others maintain their own application and licensing procedures. Philadelphia requires proof of successful completion of the Philadelphia Electrical Contractor Examination for its electrical contractor license, and that examination is administered by the International Code Council. Pittsburgh requires an Electrical Trade License to perform electrical work within the city and identifies proof of passing a certified standardized test as part of the new license requirements.

Because Pennsylvania requirements are local, exam format, eligibility, application forms, testing provider, fees, approved references, passing score, time limit, and renewal rules may differ by municipality. Open Book Test

Many contractor and electrical trade examinations that rely on the National Electrical Code are open book, and local Pennsylvania exam processes may use approved-reference testing through national exam providers. Philadelphia’s ICC contractor/trades examination bulletin directs candidates to exam-specific information for approved references, open or closed book status, exam length, item count, time limit, and passing score. Pittsburgh directs electrical trade license candidates to schedule certified standardized ICC electrical examinations using exam codes listed by the city.

Open book testing still requires serious preparation. Licensing Steps

The Pennsylvania licensing path begins with identifying the local authority that regulates electrical work in the city, municipality, or county where the candidate intends to work. Since Pennsylvania does not issue one statewide journeyman electrician license, the correct application process depends on the local jurisdiction.

A common path may include reviewing the local license classification, confirming experience requirements, completing the required application, submitting proof of experience or education, registering for the required examination, passing the exam, submitting insurance or employment documentation when required, paying applicable fees, and completing any final steps before the license is issued. Some jurisdictions may require continuing education for renewal.

Philadelphia’s electrical contractor license process includes experience documentation, proof of successful completion of the Philadelphia Electrical Contractor Examination, business tax and commercial activity license requirements, insurance requirements, tax compliance, photo identification, and continuing education related to NFPA 70. Individuals who perform electrical work can apply for that license when they meet the listed requirements.

Pittsburgh requires an Electrical Trade License to perform electrical work within the city. The city states that trade licenses are issued to individuals, not companies, and that new electrical trade license applicants must provide proof of passing the certified standardized test, a resume or documentation showing a minimum of six years of combined experience and education, and a non-expired government-issued photo ID. Pittsburgh trade license holders must complete continuing education for renewal.

State Requirements

Pennsylvania electrical licensing requirements are not handled through one statewide journeyman electrician board. Local jurisdictions may regulate electrical work through their own building departments, licensing offices, boards, or permit authorities. This means candidates should not assume that requirements in one Pennsylvania city automatically apply in another.

Local requirements may include documented electrical experience, apprenticeship or education records, proof of employment, proof of passing an exam, insurance documentation, tax compliance, continuing education, renewal fees, and compliance with local codes. Larger cities may publish detailed instructions, while smaller municipalities may have their own local ordinances or permit requirements.

Philadelphia and Pittsburgh provide examples of how local licensing can differ. Philadelphia’s electrical contractor license is tied to the City of Philadelphia Department of Licenses and Inspections and requires the Philadelphia Electrical Contractor Examination administered by ICC. Pittsburgh’s Electrical Trade License is issued through the Department of Permits, Licenses, and Inspections and requires proof of passing a certified standardized test along with experience and identification documentation.

Because local authorities control the requirements, candidates should prepare for both technical electrical knowledge and local licensing compliance. The 2026 NEC is an important study resource for code-based exams, but candidates may also need to review local amendments, municipal rules, application instructions, and any approved reference list named by the testing provider.
