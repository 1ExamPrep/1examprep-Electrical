Open Book Test

Many Pennsylvania local journeyman-level electrical exams are prepared for as open-book, NEC-based exams, especially when the local authority or testing provider allows candidates to use approved code references. Because Pennsylvania does not have one statewide journeyman exam, candidates must follow the exact open-book or closed-book instructions issued by the local jurisdiction or testing provider administering their exam.

Open book does not mean easy. Electrical candidates still need to understand the material, know how questions are written, find code information quickly, apply rules correctly, and manage time during the exam. A student who depends only on searching through the code book for every answer may struggle to complete the test within the time allowed.

Open-book electrical preparation rewards reference familiarity. Students should practice using the table of contents, index, article structure, definitions, tables, exceptions, notes, and common code sections before test day. Students should also practice calculations repeatedly so formulas, load relationships, conductor sizing concepts, grounding rules, bonding rules, motor requirements, transformer concepts, box fill, raceway fill, voltage drop concepts, and overcurrent protection rules become easier to recognize.

Reference rules for Pennsylvania electrical testing may vary by city, county, municipality, or testing provider. Approved books may need to be permanently bound, free of loose papers, and prepared according to the exam rules. Some exams may allow highlighting or permanent tabs, while others may restrict markings, notes, tabs, handwritten content, copied materials, or removable flags. Candidates should not bring practice exams, study guides, removable notes, unauthorized tabs, loose papers, or unapproved materials into the testing room.

Licensing Steps
