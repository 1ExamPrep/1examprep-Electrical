Open Book Test

The Philadelphia Standard Master Electrician – 211 exam is treated as an open book test under the ICC master electrician exam structure. Open book testing allows candidates to use approved references, but the exam still requires speed, accuracy, and familiarity with how the NEC is organized.

Licensing Steps

The Pennsylvania licensing path begins with identifying the local licensing authority. Electricians should determine whether the license is needed in Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, Allentown, Erie, Scranton, Reading, Harrisburg, or another city, borough, township, or county. Each jurisdiction may have its own application process, license title, exam provider, experience requirement, insurance requirement, renewal cycle, and permit process.

For Philadelphia, the applicant pursues the Electrical Contractor License through the city’s Department of Licenses and Inspections process. This license is required to perform electrical work in Philadelphia, including low-voltage wiring. The applicant must provide proof of successful completion of the Philadelphia Electrical Contractor Examination administered by ICC. The correct test is the Pennsylvania, Philadelphia Standard Master Electrician – 211 exam.

Philadelphia also requires experience documentation. The city’s licensing process identifies a minimum employment experience requirement performing electrical work for a company licensed through a local or state jurisdiction. Applicants also follow the city’s business, tax, insurance, and license application requirements for the Electrical Contractor License.

Outside Philadelphia, candidates follow the licensing steps required by the local jurisdiction. A municipality may require apprenticeship or journeyman experience, proof of employment, a local master electrician exam, ICC testing, insurance, business registration, background information, local code knowledge, permit registration, continuing education, or renewal documentation.

After passing the required examination and completing the local application process, the candidate receives the license, registration, or contractor credential issued by that local authority. Because Pennsylvania licensing is local, a credential issued by one municipality may not automatically authorize electrical contracting or permit work in another municipality.
