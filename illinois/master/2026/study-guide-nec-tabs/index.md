## Exam Details

Illinois electrical licensing is primarily handled by local cities and municipalities. This means there is not one single statewide Illinois master electrician exam that applies uniformly across every city. Instead, candidates typically follow the licensing requirements of the municipality where they plan to work, supervise electrical work, pull permits, or operate as an electrical contractor. Local exams may use different license names, including master electrician, supervising electrician, electrical contractor, journeyman electrician, or specialty electrical classifications.

Chicago is one of the most important examples of local electrical licensing in Illinois. The City of Chicago Department of Buildings administers trade licensing for supervising electricians and electrical contractors. Continental Testing Services processes supervising electrician applications and administers written examinations on behalf of the City of Chicago. A licensed supervising electrician in Chicago has demonstrated knowledge of the installation, alteration, repair, and maintenance of electrical equipment and apparatus under the Chicago Electrical Code.

For Chicago, applicants for a supervising electrician license must be at least 21 years old and provide an original signed letter from a current or former employer’s City-registered Supervising Electrician. The letter must verify dates of employment and establish at least two years of cumulative experience in the installation, maintenance, or alteration of building wiring systems related to the applicant’s examination category. The letter must also describe the applicant’s duties and responsibilities and include the electrical contractor’s license number.

Chicago electrical contractor licensing is also tied to the supervising electrician credential. A business seeking a Chicago electrical contractor license must employ at least one licensed supervising electrician. Chicago identifies general electrical contractor and low voltage electrical contractor license classifications, and the contractor license type is connected to the type of examination passed by the supervising electrician.

Outside Chicago, Illinois municipalities may use their own electrical licensing exams and procedures. Some jurisdictions accept licenses issued by other municipalities, while others require their own testing or registration. Students preparing with this combo should use the **2026 Illinois Master Electrician Study Guide** and tabbed **National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback** to build strong code skills while following the requirements of the specific Illinois city or local authority where they are applying.

## Licensing Steps

The Illinois electrical licensing path begins with identifying the local authority that controls the license being pursued. Because Illinois does not operate under one uniform statewide master electrician license system, candidates should determine whether their target license is issued by Chicago, another city, a county, or another local jurisdiction.

After identifying the correct jurisdiction, the candidate should review the local license title and eligibility requirements. Some jurisdictions may require documented electrical experience, proof of employment under a licensed electrician or contractor, local residency or business requirements, insurance documents, contractor registration, or successful completion of an examination. Chicago’s supervising electrician process requires qualifying experience documentation and an application through Continental Testing Services.

Once eligibility materials are gathered, candidates submit the required application to the appropriate local licensing office or testing administrator. For Chicago supervising electrician applicants, Continental Testing Services processes the application and examination. After approval, the candidate follows the exam scheduling and testing process. Candidates who fail the Chicago supervising electrician exam must wait at least six months before reapplying.

After passing the applicable local exam, the candidate completes the license issuance process required by that jurisdiction. In Chicago, the supervising electrician applicant pays the initial license fee after passing and completes the licensing process through the Department of Buildings. Electrical contractor businesses must meet the applicable contractor license requirements, including having a licensed supervising electrician associated with the business.

For students, the study process should begin before the application is complete. NEC review, calculation practice, and reference navigation take time. ## State Requirements

Illinois electrical licensing is local rather than statewide for most electrician credentials. This local structure makes it important for candidates to understand the rules in the city where they plan to work. A license or registration accepted in one municipality may not automatically meet the requirements of another municipality.

Chicago’s Department of Buildings administers trade licenses for supervising electricians and electrical contractors. A supervising electrician license demonstrates knowledge of electrical installation, alteration, repair, and maintenance under the Chicago Electrical Code. A Chicago electrical contractor license is issued to a business, including a sole proprietorship, that employs at least one licensed supervising electrician.

Chicago electrical contractor licenses are issued in general and low voltage classifications. A general electrical contractor license allows the licensed business and its employees to perform electrical work, subject to the limitations in the Chicago rules. A low voltage license is limited to work involving Class 1, 2, and 3 remote-control, signaling and power-limited circuits, and communications circuits.

Chicago supervising electrician and electrical contractor licenses are valid for one year from issuance. Existing licenses are renewed through the Department of Buildings web portal. A supervising electrician license cannot be renewed if it has been expired for three or more years, and expired electrical contractor licenses may only be renewed within three years of expiration.

For Illinois municipalities outside Chicago, candidates should follow the licensing rules of the local building department, electrical commission, or municipal licensing office.
