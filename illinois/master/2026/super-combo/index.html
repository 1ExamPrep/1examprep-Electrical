Exam Details

Illinois electrical licensing is local rather than statewide. Candidates should identify the city, village, county, or local authority where they need to be licensed before selecting an exam path. Local jurisdictions may use different license titles, including master electrician, supervising electrician, journeyman electrician, electrical contractor, or other local classifications. The correct exam depends on the jurisdiction and the license classification being pursued.

For candidates working in Chicago, the City of Chicago Department of Buildings issues the Supervising Electrician License. A licensed supervising electrician has demonstrated knowledge of the installation, alteration, repair, and maintenance of electrical equipment and apparatus under the Chicago Electrical Code. A licensed electrical contractor in Chicago must be owned by or employ at least one licensed supervising electrician, and electrical work performed by the contractor must be performed by or under the supervision and direction of a licensed supervising electrician.

The Chicago Supervising Electrician licensure examination is administered through Continental Testing Services. It is an open-book examination based on the Chicago Electrical Code reference specified for the exam. The Chicago exam is an important example of how Illinois electrical licensing works at the local level: the license is not issued by the State of Illinois as a statewide master electrician license, but by the local authority that regulates electrical work in that jurisdiction.

Outside Chicago, Illinois municipalities may set their own licensing requirements and examination rules. Some jurisdictions may recognize licenses from other Illinois municipalities, while others may require a local exam, local registration, local contractor licensing, insurance documentation, bonding, permit registration, or other municipal requirements. Candidates should prepare for NEC-based technical questions while also confirming the exact local requirements for the place where they intend to work.

Common master electrician-level exam topics include general electrical knowledge, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, wiring methods and materials, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, equipment for general use, overcurrent protection, motors, transformers, special occupancies, special equipment, lighting, low voltage, safety, electrical theory, and calculations. Candidates should expect a broad review process rather than a narrow test of one code topic.

Open Book Test

Illinois exam rules depend on the local licensing jurisdiction. For the City of Chicago Supervising Electrician examination, the test is open book. The only book allowed for that exam is the Chicago Electrical Code edition specified by Continental Testing Services. Tabs are allowed to mark sections of the code, but notes, highlighting, or other changes to the published text are prohibited.

Because reference rules are local, candidates should always follow the instructions issued by the licensing authority or testing provider for their specific Illinois jurisdiction. Licensing Steps

Illinois candidates should begin by identifying the local jurisdiction where they need to be licensed. Because Illinois does not issue one statewide electrician license, the first step is determining whether the city, village, county, or other authority having jurisdiction requires a master electrician license, supervising electrician license, journeyman license, electrical contractor license, registration, or another local credential.

A practical licensing path begins with contacting the local licensing office or reviewing the local electrical licensing instructions. Candidates should confirm the exact license classification, experience requirements, application form, required documentation, exam provider, reference list, passing score, fees, insurance requirements, bonding requirements, renewal rules, and whether the jurisdiction recognizes licenses from other Illinois municipalities.

Once the correct license path is identified, candidates submit the required application and documentation, receive approval to test when required, schedule the examination through the local testing provider, prepare using the exam content and reference list, take the examination, and complete any final licensing steps required by the local authority.

For Chicago candidates, the Supervising Electrician License process is handled through the City of Chicago Department of Buildings, and the examination is administered through Continental Testing Services. Candidates must follow the City of Chicago license requirements, application instructions, and testing rules for that classification.

Passing an exam is an important licensing step, but it does not automatically issue every license or contractor credential. Local authorities may require application approval, documentation, fees, insurance, bonding, business registration, continuing education, or renewal compliance before a candidate may legally perform or supervise electrical work.
