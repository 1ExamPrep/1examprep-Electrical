Licensing Steps

Illinois journeyman-level electrician candidates should begin by identifying the local authority that regulates electrical licensing in the area where they plan to work. This may be a city, county, municipality, local building department, or local licensing board. Because Illinois does not issue one statewide individual journeyman electrician license, the correct starting point depends on the local jurisdiction.

After identifying the local authority, the candidate should review the application requirements for the electrical credential. Local requirements may include documented electrical work experience, apprenticeship completion, classroom training, employment verification, affidavits, application forms, fees, proof of identification, and approval before testing.

Once the application is approved, the candidate schedules the required exam through the local testing process or testing provider. The candidate should review the exam bulletin, approved reference list, testing procedures, calculator rules, identification requirements, score reporting, and retesting rules before exam day.

After passing the exam, the candidate completes the local licensing or registration process. A local journeyman electrician credential may allow the holder to perform electrical work within the issuing jurisdiction under the conditions established by that local authority. A local credential may not automatically transfer to every city or county in Illinois, so electricians should confirm where the credential is accepted.

Students who want to work toward broader electrical career goals should also understand the difference between an individual electrical worker credential and an electrical contractor license or business registration. Independent electrical contracting may require additional licensing, business registration, insurance, bonding, or local approval depending on the location and type of work performed.

Instead of studying random code sections without direction, students can work through one topic at a time and connect that subject to real electrical work. This approach helps students understand how electrical exam questions may present jobsite conditions, code rules, and practical installation choices.

The 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide supports the math side of preparation. Electrical calculations often require several steps, and students should practice them repeatedly. Useful calculation areas include Ohm’s law, power formulas, conductor sizing, voltage drop concepts, box fill, raceway fill, load calculations, service and feeder calculations, branch circuit calculations, motor calculations, transformer calculations, and overcurrent protection applications.

Calculation study should be active. Students should work problems by hand, write formulas clearly, identify known values, convert units where needed, and check whether the answer makes sense. Reading a solved example may introduce a concept, but repeated practice builds speed and confidence. This is especially important for timed electrical exams because calculation questions can take longer than direct code lookup questions.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback should be used throughout the study process. Students should practice locating definitions, article sections, tables, notes, and exceptions. They should also learn how related code topics connect. A conductor question may involve ampacity, adjustment factors, terminal temperature limitations, overcurrent protection, and equipment rules. A grounding question may involve grounding electrodes, bonding jumpers, equipment grounding conductors, services, and fault-current paths.

A balanced study schedule may rotate between trade review, NEC navigation, and calculation practice. One session might focus on grounding and bonding. Another might focus on services, feeders, and branch circuits. Another might involve conductor sizing or box fill calculations. Another might involve locating raceway, lighting, or equipment rules in the NEC. This rotation helps candidates prepare across the full range of journeyman-level electrical knowledge.

Students should also include timed practice. Since many local electrical exams are timed and reference-based, pacing matters. Practice sessions should help candidates learn when to look up a rule, when to calculate, when to move forward, and how to manage the exam without spending too long on one question.
