## State Requirements

Kentucky regulates electrician licensing under state law through the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction. The state licenses electrical workers and electrical contractors to help ensure that electrical work is performed by qualified individuals and that installations support fire and life safety.

Kentucky law provides experience, training, and examination requirements for electrical licensure. For an electrician license, applicants must document qualifying electrical trade experience and achieve a passing score on the required examination. Kentucky law also recognizes certain education-based substitutions for experience, including approved electrical technology education from qualifying institutions.

Electrical work must be performed according to Kentucky licensing requirements and applicable code standards. Candidates should understand the difference between an electrician license, a master electrician license, and an electrical contractor license. An electrician license supports individual electrical work within the scope allowed by law. A master electrician license is a higher-level individual credential. An electrical contractor license relates to contracting authority and business responsibility.

Kentucky also recognizes authorized local licensing programs under state law. Local requirements may affect permits, inspections, registration, or business responsibilities depending on where work is performed. Candidates should follow state licensing requirements and also confirm whether a local authority has additional administrative requirements connected to electrical work.

Licensed Kentucky electricians must maintain their credentials according to renewal and continuing education requirements. Continuing education helps electricians stay current with code changes, installation practices, and safety requirements. Candidates should plan for ongoing code education after becoming licensed because electrical standards continue to change as new NEC editions are adopted.
