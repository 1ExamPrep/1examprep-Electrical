Exam Details

Kentucky electrician licensing is handled through the Commonwealth of Kentucky Electrical Licensing program under the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction. Kentucky licenses electricians and electrical contractors at the state level, and the licensing examination is part of the process for becoming a Kentucky journeyman electrician.

The Kentucky electrical licensing examinations are computer-based, timed, and open book. Kentucky uses testing providers approved for its electrical licensing process, including Prov, NASCLA, and Pearson VUE for applicable examination routes. Candidates should follow the current instructions provided by the Kentucky Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction for the exam path they are using.

The Kentucky Journeyman Electrician exam is listed with 80 questions and 3 hours allowed for testing. The passing score is 70%. The published subject areas include General Electrical Theory and Principles, Plan Reading, Wiring and Protection, Wiring Methods and Materials, Motors and Controls, Equipment for General Use, Special Occupancies, Special Equipment, Special Conditions, and Communication Systems.

Kentucky announced that electrical examinations for electricians and master electricians would align with the 2023 National Electrical Code, with applicants able to choose either 2017 or 2023 NEC-based examinations during the transition period ending January 1, 2026. Beginning January 1, 2026, Kentucky electrical examinations are based on the 2023 NEC. Students should follow current Kentucky testing instructions for the exact references and exam route assigned to them.

Open Book Test

The Kentucky Journeyman Electrician exam is an open-book test. Open book does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates still need to understand electrical concepts, recognize how exam questions are written, move through approved references efficiently, and answer questions within the time allowed.

Open-book electrical exams reward preparation and reference familiarity. Students should practice using the table of contents, index, article structure, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and common code sections before exam day. The more comfortable a student is with the reference material, the less time is wasted searching during the test.

Kentucky testing rules for open-book exams allow authorized references and require those references to be checked by the test administrator. Reference materials may be prepared with highlighting, handwritten notes in pen, and permanent tabs. Moveable tabs, Post-it notes, photocopied documents added to a book, photocopied versions of reference books, test preparation study guides, and practice exams are not approved references for the testing center.

Students should use this study guide, practice exams, final exams, and flash cards before test day. During the actual exam, candidates should bring only the approved references allowed for the Kentucky Journeyman Electrician exam and follow the testing-provider instructions for calculator use, reference preparation, identification, and test-center procedures.

A strong open-book strategy includes learning how the NEC is organized, practicing with article structure, using definitions carefully, studying tables, reviewing exceptions, and practicing calculations before the exam. Flash cards help students recall key ideas faster, while practice exams help them build the habit of reading questions carefully and choosing the correct code path.

Licensing Steps

Kentucky journeyman electrician licensing is handled through the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction. Candidates must pass the Journeyman Electrical Exam and submit the required licensing application materials to the state.

For the Kentucky electrician license, current state checklist materials require proof of experience totaling 8,000 hours in the electrical trade since the applicant’s sixteenth birthday. Experience may be documented by a notarized letter from an employer or former employer who can verify the work performed and hours worked, signed by the electrical contractor or master electrician. Kentucky also identifies other documentation routes, including tax documentation listing the job title as electrician.

Kentucky allows certain education and training to substitute for part of the verifiable experience requirement. Completion of two years of a minimum four-year approved apprenticeship-training program may be used, with the remainder of the course required to be completed within the state’s specified period. Completion of an approved apprenticeship-training program with a minimum of 288 classroom hours may substitute for 4,000 hours of verifiable experience. An associate’s degree or diploma in electrical technology from the Kentucky Community and Technical College System or another accredited college or university may also substitute for 4,000 hours of verifiable experience. Two years of teaching electrical technology or a related field at an accredited college or university may substitute for 4,000 hours of verifiable experience.

After passing the required exam, applicants submit a copy of passing test results, proof of qualifying experience, a completed application, the required application fee, and a passport-sized color photo. Candidates should follow current Kentucky Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction application instructions for accepted documents, fees, license issuance, renewal, and status.
