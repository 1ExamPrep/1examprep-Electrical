Exam Details

Kentucky Master Electrician examinations are part of the licensing process overseen by the Commonwealth of Kentucky Electrical Licensing Division. The Kentucky candidate information bulletin identifies the Master Electrician exam as a computer-based, open book, timed examination. The Master Electrician exam contains 100 questions, allows 4 hours of testing time, and is scored against a 70% cut score.

The Kentucky Master Electrician exam outline includes General Electrical Theory & Principles, Plan Reading, Wiring & Protection, Wiring Methods & Materials, Motors & Controls, Equipment for General Use, Special Occupancies, Special Equipment, Special Conditions, and Communication Systems. The outline places significant weight on wiring methods, wiring and protection, motors and controls, and equipment for general use, so students should give those areas consistent attention during preparation.

Open Book Test

The Kentucky Master Electrician exam is an open book test. Candidates are allowed to use authorized references during the examination, and all reference materials are checked by the test administrator before the candidate enters the testing room. Kentucky’s reference material rules allow highlighting, handwritten notes written in pen, and permanent tabs. Moveable tabs are not permitted, Post-it notes are not permitted, photocopied documents cannot be added to a reference book, photocopied versions of a reference book are not permitted, and test preparation study guides are not approved references in the testing center.

The Kentucky Master Electrician exam reference list includes Ugly’s Electrical References and approved editions of NFPA 70, National Electrical Code. The candidate bulletin identifies NEC editions used for the exam and states that candidates may use the allowed NEC edition for the exam version being taken. Kentucky’s Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction has also announced that electrical examinations for electricians and master electricians align with the 2023 NEC after the state’s code update schedule.

An open book exam is not easy simply because references are allowed. The NEC is large, technical, and full of details that require careful reading. Candidates who have not practiced code navigation may lose valuable time searching for basic sections. The best preparation method is to use the code book throughout study, not only at the end. Students should practice identifying the topic, moving to the correct article, reading the section carefully, checking exceptions, and applying the requirement to the question.

Licensing Steps

The Kentucky master electrician licensing process begins with meeting the state’s licensing requirements and preparing for the required examination. A person seeking a Kentucky master electrician license submits the required application materials to the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction and provides proof of the experience required for master electrician licensure.

To obtain a Kentucky master electrician license, applicants must pass the Master Electrician exam through an accepted testing route. Kentucky’s new application checklist identifies passing the Master Electrician exam with PROV, NASCLA, or Pearson VUE as part of the application process. Applicants must send a copy of the passing test results as part of the licensing packet.

Applicants also submit the completed electrical license application, the required application fee, a passport-sized color photo, and required proof of qualifying electrical experience. Kentucky’s application checklist also identifies an option for proof of two years of teaching electrical technology or a related field at an accredited college or university to substitute for 4,000 hours of verifiable electrical trade experience.

After the candidate passes the exam and submits the required licensing materials, the state reviews the application for licensure. Electrical contractor applicants follow a separate contractor licensing process and must be associated with a Kentucky licensed master electrician. Electrical contractors also submit business-related documentation, including insurance documentation, as required by the state.

Students should begin exam preparation before reaching the final application stage. Master electrician study takes time because the exam covers broad subject areas and requires skill with the NEC. State Requirements

Kentucky licenses electricians, master electricians, and electrical contractors through the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction. State regulations establish application procedures for electrical contractors, master electricians, and electricians. For a master electrician license, the applicant submits the required electrical license application, application fee, and proof of qualifying experience.

The electrical contractor license is different from the individual master electrician license. Kentucky’s application checklist states that an applicant seeking an electrical contractor license must have a Kentucky master electrician license or employ a Kentucky licensed master electrician. This makes the master electrician credential an important qualification for individuals and businesses involved in electrical contracting.

Kentucky also recognizes specific reciprocity pathways. The application checklist identifies Ohio electrical contractor reciprocity and Virginia or West Virginia reciprocity for master electrician or journeyman electrician licenses. Reciprocity applicants submit the required license documentation, application materials, and supporting documents identified by the state.

Licenses are issued for a biennial period, and application fees are tied to the license classification. The current Kentucky electrical licensing regulation identifies the master electrician application fee as a biennial license fee and requires all electrical license applicants to submit a passport-sized color photograph taken within the required time period. Licensees must also follow renewal and continuing education requirements that apply to their credential.

Test Information and Study Materials

Kentucky Master Electrician exam preparation should be built around the official exam outline. Students should study general electrical theory and principles, plan reading, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, motors and controls, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, and communication systems. Each topic should be reviewed with both the study guide and the NEC open so the student learns where the supporting rules are located.

Wiring and protection is an important study area because it includes topics such as branch circuits, feeders, services, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, and conductor requirements. Students should practice questions that require code lookup, calculations, and careful reading of exceptions. Grounding and bonding should receive special attention because small wording differences can change the correct answer.

Wiring methods and materials also deserve focused review. Students should study raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, cabinets, panelboards, conductor fill, conductor protection, support requirements, wet-location rules, underground installations, flexible cords, and wiring method limitations. These subjects often require candidates to move between multiple NEC articles and tables.

Motors and controls should be studied with both concept review and calculation practice. Students should become comfortable with motor branch-circuit conductor sizing, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, disconnecting means, controllers, and related NEC tables. Equipment for general use should include appliances, luminaires, switches, receptacles, panelboards, transformers, and related installation rules.

Special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, and communication systems can be challenging because they are more specialized. Students should review hazardous locations, health care facilities, assembly occupancies, mobile homes, recreational vehicle parks, swimming pools, emergency systems, fire alarm systems, data processing equipment, and low-voltage or communication circuit rules when applicable to the exam outline.

Calculation practice should be repeated throughout the study period. Students should practice service load calculations, feeder calculations, branch-circuit calculations, conductor ampacity, adjustment and correction factors, box fill, conduit fill, voltage drop concepts, motor calculations, transformer calculations, grounding electrode conductor sizing, and overcurrent protection sizing. Each calculation should be worked step by step so the student understands the method instead of memorizing only a final answer.

A strong routine is to answer a question, identify the topic, use the tabs to reach the likely article, locate the exact section, read the surrounding language, check related tables or exceptions, and then confirm the answer. This turns each question into both a knowledge review and a code-navigation exercise.
