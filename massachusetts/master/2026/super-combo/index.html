Exam Details

The Massachusetts master electrician examination is administered through PSI for the Massachusetts Board of State Examiners of Electricians. The licensing examination is a two-part examination, and candidates must pass both parts to obtain licensure. Each part is scored independently, and both parts must be completed within the required scoring window.

Massachusetts Master Electrician Part 1 is listed with 90 questions, a 70 percent passing score, and 240 minutes of testing time. The Part 1 content outline includes State Specific Electrical Requirements, Advanced Electrical Knowledge and Electrical Theory, Services, Grounding and Bonding, Wiring Methods and Devices, Motors, Transformers, Low-Voltage Distribution, Special Occupancies and Equipment, Overcurrent Protection, Lighting, Alarm Systems, Photovoltaic, and Energy Storage Systems.

The Business and Law portion for Master Electrician and Systems Contractor licensing is listed with 50 questions, a 70 percent passing score, and 120 minutes of testing time. The Business and Law outline includes Massachusetts Licensing, Estimating and Bidding, Lien Law, Financial Management, Tax Laws, Labor Laws, Project Management, Contracts, Business Organization, Risk Management, and Environmental and Safety.

This exam structure shows why Massachusetts master electrician preparation should not be limited to one subject area. Candidates need technical electrical knowledge, code navigation skill, calculation ability, and familiarity with business and law topics tied to professional licensing. Open Book Test

The Massachusetts master electrician examination is identified as an open book, computer-generated, two-part examination. Open book testing does not mean the exam is easy. It means candidates must know how to use approved references efficiently, understand the structure of the NEC, read code language carefully, and apply rules under a strict time limit.

Massachusetts exam rules allow certain materials in the examination area, including the current adopted edition of the National Electrical Code, Massachusetts State Amendments, applicable Massachusetts General Laws, Board Rules and Regulations, NFPA 72, and listed business and safety-related references for the applicable exam part. The rules allow tabbed or un-tabbed references, highlighted and underlined original text, formulas written before the examination on blank pages of the codebook, and handwritten notes in the margins of permitted reference materials. During the examination, writing is limited to scrap paper provided by PSI.

The National Electrical Code 2026 paperback with tabs included in this Super Combo supports open book preparation by helping candidates practice locating articles, tables, definitions, exceptions, and installation requirements. Open book success depends on knowledge, organization, and pacing. Licensing Steps

Massachusetts electrician licensing is handled by the Massachusetts Board of State Examiners of Electricians. The Board licenses electricians, sets standards for electrical work, and oversees the licensing process for electrical professionals in the Commonwealth.

A practical path to the Massachusetts Master Electrician license begins with holding the proper Massachusetts journeyman electrician license. A licensed journeyman electrician applying for the master electrician examination must provide documentation showing at least one year of experience as the holder of a Massachusetts journeyman electrician license while actively engaged in electrical work.

Candidates must also complete the required education and licensing application process. Massachusetts directs candidates to complete the applicable education program, complete code-specific continuing education, gain the required work experience, and submit the online application through PSI before scheduling the examination. After approval, candidates schedule and complete the required examination parts.

Once the examination process is completed successfully, the candidate proceeds through the licensing steps required by the Board. The study portion should be treated as a structured project. Candidates should review the exam content outline, study electrical theory and code topics, practice calculations, work through NEC navigation, and spend time on business and law subjects. Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Massachusetts Master Electrician study plan should include technical review, business and law review, code navigation, calculation practice, and timed question work. For NEC study, begin by learning how the code book is organized. Review article structure, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and cross-references. Practice moving from a question to the correct article instead of relying only on memory. In an open book exam, speed matters, but careful reading matters just as much. Many missed questions come from finding a related section but overlooking a condition, table note, or exception.

For calculations, write out each step. Electrical calculation mistakes often happen when a candidate skips a conversion, applies the wrong value, selects the wrong table, or confuses branch circuit, feeder, and service requirements.
