## Exam Details

The Massachusetts Board of State Examiners of Electricians oversees individual electrician licensing. The Board has contracted with PSI to process applications, notify candidates, and conduct examinations. Candidates must have the required work experience and education before being approved for examination. After the application and supporting documentation are approved, the candidate becomes eligible for the two-part examination process.

The Massachusetts journeyman electrician examination is a two-part examination. Candidates should prepare for both electrical trade and code knowledge as well as Massachusetts-specific laws, regulations, amendments, and professional requirements. The exam process is designed to measure whether a candidate has the knowledge needed to perform electrical work safely and in compliance with Massachusetts electrical licensing standards.

Massachusetts examination reference rules allow the electrical code book, Massachusetts State Amendments, Massachusetts General Laws applicable to examination questions, and Board Rules and Regulations in the examination room when prepared according to the current rules. The Candidate Information Bulletin also identifies additional references for certain examinations and explains what types of highlighting, underlining, tabs, notes, and reference preparation may be permitted.

The Board adopts the electrical code on January 1 of the code year, and the examination content is changed to comply with the code in July of the code year. ## Open Book Test

The Massachusetts journeyman electrician examination is an open book test. Candidates may use approved references during the examination according to the current PSI Candidate Information Bulletin and Board rules. Reference materials may include the electrical code book, Massachusetts amendments, Massachusetts General Laws applicable to examination questions, and Board rules and regulations. Candidates must follow the current rules for tabs, highlighting, underlining, handwritten notes, formulas, and other reference preparation.

An open book electrical exam still requires serious preparation. Having access to reference material does not mean every answer will be easy to find. ## Licensing Steps

The Massachusetts journeyman electrician licensing path begins with education and supervised electrical work experience. Candidates for the journeyman examination must complete a Board-approved education program and gain the required work experience before applying. The state’s education requirements for journeyman examination eligibility include 600 hours of required electrical education.

The 600-hour education requirement includes important learning objectives such as DC theory, AC theory, National Electrical Code application, branch circuit calculations and requirements, feeder and service calculations and requirements, electrical bonding and grounding, conductor selection, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, motors and controls, transformers, low-voltage systems, fire warning and security systems, fiber, data and communications wiring, use of code book tables, Massachusetts Electrical Code and amendments, laws and regulations, inspection requirements, and jobsite safety.

In addition to education, candidates must document the required electrical work experience. Massachusetts journeyman applicants commonly prepare through apprenticeship and supervised field work. Candidates should keep careful records of employment, hours, supervision, classroom training, continuing education, and any documentation required by the Board or PSI application process.

Before applying, candidates should review the requirements for becoming a licensed electrician, including the regulations and the Candidate Information Bulletin. The application process is completed through PSI, the Board’s vendor. Candidates submit the online application and required supporting documents before scheduling the exam. Required documentation may include proof of education, code-specific continuing education, work experience records, a notarized CORI form, a passport-style photo, and any applicable accommodation, veteran, military spouse, or out-of-state verification materials.

After the application is approved, the candidate schedules and takes the required examination. A candidate is considered licensed to install electrical work for hire only after receiving notice of a passing score on each required part of the examination and having a printed photo license issued by the Board or a temporary license in possession. Passing the exam is an important milestone, but license status depends on completing the Board’s licensing process.

## State Requirements

Massachusetts regulates electrician licensing through the Board of State Examiners of Electricians. The Class B Journeyman Electrician license is the state journeyman-level license. This credential supports individual electrical work within the scope allowed by Massachusetts law and Board rules. A Class A Master Electrician license is a higher-level credential with additional requirements and responsibilities.

Massachusetts requires candidates to complete approved education, gain qualifying experience, and pass the required examination before licensure. The journeyman education requirement includes 600 hours of approved electrical education. Candidates must also complete code-specific continuing education and provide supporting documentation as part of the examination application process.

Massachusetts electrical licensing also requires attention to state-specific rules. Candidates should study the Massachusetts Electrical Code and amendments, applicable Massachusetts General Laws, and Board regulations. These materials are important because the Massachusetts exam is not limited to general NEC knowledge. It also reflects state licensing rules, inspection requirements, conduct expectations, and legal responsibilities connected to electrical work.

Massachusetts candidates should understand the difference between individual licensure and business authority. A journeyman electrician license is an individual credential. A master electrician license and business-related requirements may be needed for broader responsibilities, supervision, or contracting activities. Candidates planning to operate an electrical business should review the state’s master electrician and business licensing requirements separately.

Licensed electricians must also maintain their credentials according to Massachusetts renewal and continuing education requirements. Code cycles change, state amendments are updated, and electrical safety expectations continue to evolve. Continued code study is an important part of maintaining professional competence after the journeyman license is issued.
