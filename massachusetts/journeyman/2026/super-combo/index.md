## Licensing Steps

The Massachusetts journeyman electrician licensing path begins with meeting the Board’s education and work experience requirements. A common journeyman pathway includes completing 600 hours of Board-required electrical education as a prerequisite to sit for the journeyman examination and completing the required supervised electrical work experience. Candidates should keep accurate records of approved education, employment, work experience, and any documents needed for the application.

After completing the required education and work experience, the candidate submits the examination application and supporting documentation through the process established by the Board and PSI. PSI processes applications and provides candidate instructions after the application is approved. Candidates should review the current bulletin carefully so they understand examination parts, approved references, allowed materials, testing center rules, and procedures for receiving examination results.

Once approved, the candidate schedules and takes the two-part journeyman electrician examination. Preparation should include code navigation, calculation practice, Massachusetts law and rule review, electrical theory, wiring methods, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, motors, controls, equipment, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, and communication systems.

After passing the required examination parts, the candidate completes the remaining licensing steps through the Massachusetts Board of State Examiners of Electricians. Passing the exam is an important step, but the applicant must still satisfy Board requirements and complete the licensing process. A journeyman license is also an important requirement for electricians who later plan to pursue a Massachusetts master electrician license, which requires holding a Massachusetts journeyman license, additional work experience, and Board-approved education.

A practical preparation path includes completing the required apprentice or supervised work experience, completing the required electrical education, submitting the exam application, studying the exam bulletin, reviewing the NEC, practicing calculations, building reference navigation speed, using flash cards for recall, passing the required exam parts, and completing the Board’s licensure process.

## State Requirements

Massachusetts regulates electrician licensing through the Board of State Examiners of Electricians under the Division of Occupational Licensure. The Board’s mission is to protect Massachusetts citizens by setting standards for electrical work. The Board licenses electricians, enforces regulations, settles disputes between electricians and inspectors, assists in fire investigations related to electricity, and handles appeals on inspection decisions.

Massachusetts electrical licensing includes several license types, including journeyman electrician, master electrician, systems technician, systems contractor, and business-related electrical licenses. The journeyman electrician license is an individual license for qualified electrical workers who meet the Board’s education, experience, examination, and application requirements.

Massachusetts regulations identify 600 hours of electrical education as a prerequisite to sit for the journeyman examination. The state also requires applicants to document qualifying work experience and education before approval for the examination. Candidates should use the Board’s current application forms and instructions when preparing documentation.

Massachusetts has reciprocity with New Hampshire for qualifying journeyman and master electricians, subject to Board requirements. The Board does not provide reciprocal licensure for companies. Business licenses must be applied for by Massachusetts-licensed master electricians or by companies that retain a Massachusetts-licensed master electrician as the licensee of record.

For exam preparation, students should understand that state qualification requirements and study preparation are related but separate. State requirements determine whether an applicant may test and become licensed. Study materials help the candidate prepare for the knowledge and code portion of the process.
