Exam Details

The Colorado State Electrical Board contracts with PSI Services LLC to conduct the electrical licensing examination program. The PSI candidate information bulletin covers the application and examination process for Residential Wireman, Journeyman Wireman, Master Electrician, and the Colorado journeyman wireman examination for apprentices with six or more years of apprentice registration.

The Colorado Master Electrician examination is computer-based and administered through PSI. The current PSI outline lists 90 scored items, 240 minutes of testing time, and a 70 percent passing standard, which equals 63 correct scored items. The examination may also include up to 10 non-scored items, with additional time included for those questions. Non-scored questions are used for future exam development and do not count toward the candidate’s final score.

The Colorado Master Electrician examination content outline includes general electrical knowledge, services, feeders and branch circuits, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special conditions, special occupancies, special equipment, electrical devices, motors, transformers, and electrical calculations. These categories require both code familiarity and practical electrical understanding.

PSI provides score results immediately after the examination is completed. Passing the examination does not automatically guarantee licensure. Candidates must still demonstrate that they meet Colorado’s minimum qualifications for licensure under state law and board requirements. After passing, the candidate files the license application with the Colorado Division of Professions and Occupations.

Colorado’s electrical licensing examinations reference the National Electrical Code edition used by the testing program. The PSI candidate bulletin states that examinations are based on the 2023 NEC until the transition date and that, effective August 1, 2026, all examination questions are consistent with the 2026 National Electrical Code. This product is based on the 2026 NEC and includes the National Electrical Code 2026 paperback for candidates studying under the 2026 code cycle.

Open Book Test

The Colorado electrical licensing examination is an open-book examination in the sense that the testing center provides the approved code reference and formula page. Candidates may not bring their own reference materials into the testing room. The testing center provides the National Electrical Code book and formula page used for the examination.

For exams based on the 2026 code cycle, the 2026 National Electrical Code book and formula page are provided at the test center. Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, or index on the references provided during the examination. Candidates may not use any other materials. Because the reference is provided and controlled by the testing center, students should practice code navigation before test day rather than depending on personally marked exam-room materials.

Open-book testing still requires strong preparation. The time limit does not allow students to slowly search the NEC for every answer. Candidates need to understand the layout of the code book, how the index works, where common topics are located, how tables are organized, and how to connect a question to the correct NEC article. Students who practice regularly with a printed NEC are usually more comfortable locating answers under timed conditions.

The National Electrical Code 2026 paperback included in this combo supports that preparation. Students can use it before the exam to practice finding definitions, grounding rules, conductor tables, service and feeder requirements, raceway provisions, motor rules, transformer requirements, and calculation references. The goal is to build familiarity with the code book before entering the testing center.

Licensing Steps

Colorado master electrician candidates should begin by reviewing the state’s experience and education pathways. Colorado lists several ways to qualify for the master electrician license. A candidate may qualify with 10,000 hours of electrical experience earned in no less than five years. A candidate may also qualify with a bachelor’s degree in electrical engineering from an accredited college or university and 2,000 hours of construction experience earned in no less than one year.

Colorado also allows a pathway for graduates of an electrical trade school or community college who have 8,000 hours of experience earned in no less than four years. Another pathway allows 2,000 hours of experience earned in no less than one year in addition to the journeyman wireman requirements, with some hours in planning and layout and some hours in supervision while holding a journeyman electrician license.

All practical experience must be documented on the Affidavit of Experience form provided by the Board and completed by the electrical contractor supervising the work performed. Candidates using education must document that education with the required transcript or similar statement when applicable. The Board reviews submitted evidence to determine whether the applicant meets the qualifications for licensure.

The examination process is handled through PSI. Candidates register and schedule the required examination through PSI’s testing system. PSI offers computer-based testing through a network of examination centers. Colorado testing locations listed in PSI’s candidate information include Broomfield, Colorado Springs, Denver area locations, Durango, Englewood, Fort Collins, Grand Junction, Lakewood, and Pueblo, with additional PSI examination centers available in other regions.

After passing the examination, the candidate files the license application with the Colorado Division of Professions and Occupations. Passing the exam is one important requirement, but the state still determines whether the candidate meets the legal qualifications for licensure. Candidates should keep exam preparation, experience documentation, and application requirements organized throughout the process.

State Requirements

The Colorado State Electrical Board regulates electrical licensing in Colorado. Colorado license types include Residential Wireman, Journeyman Electrician, and Master Electrician. The master electrician license represents an advanced level of electrical licensure and is tied to experience, examination, and state application requirements.

Colorado’s master electrician experience requirements include several approved qualification paths. The 10,000-hour pathway requires experience earned in no less than five years. The electrical engineering degree pathway requires a bachelor’s degree from an accredited college or university and 2,000 hours of construction experience earned in no less than one year. The trade school or community college pathway requires graduation and 8,000 hours of experience earned in no less than four years. The additional journeyman pathway requires 2,000 hours beyond the journeyman wireman requirements, including planning and layout and supervision while holding the journeyman license.

Electrical contractor registration is separate from the individual master electrician license. To perform electrical contracting in Colorado, a contractor must be registered with the State of Colorado and must hold or employ a licensed master electrician to serve as the responsible individual for the company. The master electrician and company owner, when they are separate people, must sign the required Acknowledgment of Responsibility form. The selected master electrician may be the responsible party for one company only.

Colorado-licensed Residential Wireman, Journeyman, and Master Electricians must complete 24 hours of continuing education during each three-year license period before renewal. The continuing education requirement does not apply to the first renewal of a license when the applicant successfully completed a licensing examination as a condition of issuance. Continuing education must be selected from board-listed core competencies, and at least four hours must be in National Electrical Code changes.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Colorado Master Electrician study plan should include code reading, trade-topic review, calculation practice, and timed reference navigation. The Colorado Master Electrician exam outline includes 13 content areas, so students should avoid studying only one topic. A balanced routine helps candidates build stronger readiness across the full examination outline.

Grounding and bonding is one of the largest content areas on the Colorado Master Electrician examination. Students should review grounding electrode systems, bonding jumpers, equipment grounding conductors, grounding electrode conductors, service grounding, separately derived systems, fault-current paths, and the relationship between grounding rules and safe installation practices.

Services, feeders, and branch circuits are also important study areas. Candidates should be able to distinguish between service, feeder, and branch-circuit requirements, identify conductor and overcurrent protection rules, apply load calculation concepts, and recognize how NEC requirements change depending on the equipment, occupancy, or installation condition described in the question.

Electrical calculations require repeated practice. Students should work on load calculations, conductor ampacity, voltage drop concepts, raceway fill, box fill, motor calculations, transformer calculations, service and feeder sizing, overcurrent protection, and grounding conductor sizing. A dependable calculation routine helps reduce errors: read the question carefully, identify the topic, locate the controlling rule or table, set up the calculation, solve it, and check whether the question asks for a minimum, maximum, adjusted value, or final selection.

The 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide supports this part of preparation by helping students focus on the math side of electrical exam readiness. Many calculation mistakes happen because the candidate uses the wrong table, skips a condition in the question, rounds too early, or chooses an answer before confirming what the question is asking. Consistent practice helps build speed and accuracy.
