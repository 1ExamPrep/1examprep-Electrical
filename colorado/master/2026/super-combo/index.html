Exam Details

The Colorado State Electrical Board contracts with PSI Services LLC to administer the Colorado electrical licensing examinations. The Colorado Master Electrician examination is a computer-based exam administered through PSI testing centers.

The Colorado Master Electrician examination includes 90 scored items and has a time allowance of 240 minutes for the scored portion. The passing standard is 70%, which equals 63 correct scored items. The exam may also include up to 10 non-scored items with an additional 30 minutes. Non-scored items are used for examination development and do not count toward the final score.

The Master Electrician examination content outline includes the following areas: general electrical knowledge, services, feeders, and branch circuits, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special conditions, special occupancies, special equipment, electrical devices, motors, transformers, and electrical calculations. Each area requires more than memorization. Candidates need to understand the topic, apply code rules, work through calculations, and select the best answer under timed testing conditions.

Effective August 1, 2026, Colorado electrical licensing examination questions are consistent with the 2026 National Electrical Code. The test center provides the 2026 National Electrical Code book and Formula Page for the exam. Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, or index on the references provided at the test center, and other materials may not be used during the exam.

Scores are provided immediately after completion of the examination. Passing the examination is an important licensing step, but applicants must still meet the minimum qualifications for licensure under Colorado law and file the required license application with the Colorado Division of Professions and Occupations.

Licensing Steps

Colorado Master Electrician candidates should follow the licensing process established by the Colorado State Electrical Board and the Colorado Division of Professions and Occupations. The examination is administered by PSI, and after passing the exam, candidates file the license application with the Division of Professions and Occupations.

A practical preparation path begins with reviewing Colorado eligibility requirements, registering for the proper examination through PSI, studying the official exam content areas, practicing NEC navigation, strengthening electrical calculations, and completing timed practice before test day. Candidates should arrive at the testing center early, bring required identification, follow PSI security procedures, and be prepared for a computer-based testing format.

After passing the examination, candidates must still demonstrate that they meet the minimum qualifications for licensure. Passing the examination alone does not automatically issue a license. The licensing application, documentation, and state review process remain part of the pathway to becoming licensed as a Colorado Master Electrician.

State Requirements

The Colorado State Electrical Board regulates electrical licensing in Colorado through the Division of Professions and Occupations. Colorado electrical licensing includes classifications such as Residential Wireman, Journeyman Wireman, and Master Electrician. The Master Electrician level is intended for candidates who are prepared to demonstrate advanced electrical knowledge and meet the state’s licensing qualifications.

Colorado’s electrical licensing examinations are designed to measure competence across job task areas that licensed electricians are legally allowed to perform. The Master Electrician examination is not limited to one narrow specialty. Candidates should be ready for a broad test that covers practical trade knowledge, NEC application, electrical calculations, installation rules, equipment requirements, and safety-related code concepts.

Because Colorado licensing requirements are controlled by the state, applicants should use the official licensing application process and follow the requirements in effect at the time they apply. Exam preparation materials can help candidates study, but licensing approval is determined by the state based on the applicant’s qualifications, documentation, exam results, and compliance with Colorado law.
