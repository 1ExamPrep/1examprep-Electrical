Exam Details

The Colorado State Electrical Board has contracted with PSI Services LLC to conduct its electrical examination program. PSI provides examinations through computer examination centers in Colorado and other approved locations. The Colorado electrical licensing examinations are designed to test entry-level competence in the areas in which licensees are legally allowed to practice.

The Colorado Journeyman Wireman examination is a computer-based exam. The current PSI Candidate Information Bulletin lists the Journeyman Wireman exam as 90 scored items with 240 minutes allowed. The bulletin also lists up to 10 non-scored items with 30 additional minutes. The passing standard is 70%, which equals 63 scored items correct.

The Colorado Journeyman Wireman content outline includes general electrical knowledge, services, feeders and branch circuits, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special occupancies, special equipment, electrical devices, motors, transformers, photovoltaics, and electrical calculations. These subject areas reflect the broad knowledge expected of a journeyman-level electrician.

Colorado examination information states that the licensing examinations for Residential Wireman, Journeyman, Master Electrician, and 6-Year apprentices reference the National Electrical Code edition identified in the current bulletin. Effective August 1, 2026, examination questions are consistent with the 2026 National Electrical Code, and the 2026 NEC book and Formula Page are provided at the test center. Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, or index on the provided references, and no other materials may be used.

Because the exam is timed, candidates should prepare to work efficiently. The test displays one question at a time on the computer screen, and the time remaining is shown during the exam. Colorado’s bulletin states that the score is given immediately following completion of the examination. Passing the examination is an important step, but the candidate must still meet the state’s minimum qualifications for licensure.

Open Book Test

The Colorado Journeyman Wireman examination is a reference-based open book test. The PSI bulletin states that the National Electrical Code book and Formula Page are provided at the test center for the applicable code cycle. Candidates may not bring their own reference materials into the exam room, and they may not write, highlight, underline, or index on the references provided during testing.

An open book electrical exam still requires serious preparation. Having a code book available does not replace the need to understand electrical theory, calculations, installation principles, or the structure of the NEC. Candidates must know how to locate rules quickly, read technical code language accurately, and apply the correct section to the situation described in the question.

The tabbed NEC included in this combo is a study tool for preparation before the exam. Tabs help organize the book and make it easier to return to major code areas during review. During preparation, practice reading a question carefully before opening the code book. Identify the subject, determine whether the question involves a definition, installation rule, table, calculation, exception, or equipment requirement, and then locate the correct NEC area. This habit helps build the code navigation skill needed for a timed test.

Colorado’s exam format makes reference practice especially important. Candidates should build speed with common code areas, but accuracy matters more than rushing. Read the complete code section, pay attention to exceptions and notes, and make sure the answer matches exactly what the question is asking.

Licensing Steps

The Colorado journeyman electrician licensing path begins with meeting the state’s experience and education requirements. The Colorado State Electrical Board lists the Journeyman Electrician experience requirement as 8,000 hours of experience earned in no less than 4 years. Of those hours, 2,000 hours must be in commercial or industrial work.

Colorado also requires 288 hours of classroom education for all journeyman applicants. The education must be documented with a transcript or similar statement. Candidates should keep clear records of classroom instruction, apprenticeship training, employment, supervision, and work categories throughout the licensing process.

After meeting the required experience and education standards, the candidate follows the Colorado electrical license application process through the Division of Professions and Occupations. Candidates must submit the required application materials and documentation. The state reviews the application to determine whether the candidate meets the qualifications for the license classification.

The Colorado PSI bulletin states that upon passing the examination, the candidate files a license application with the Division of Professions and Occupations. Passing the exam alone is not a guarantee of licensure. The applicant must also demonstrate that the minimum qualifications required by Colorado law have been met.

Once the candidate has completed the exam and application requirements, the licensing process continues through the state’s online licensing system. Candidates should monitor their email carefully because the Division of Professions and Occupations identifies email as its primary communication method for application status and process updates.

State Requirements

Colorado regulates electrical licensing through the Colorado State Electrical Board. A journeyman electrician license is intended for individuals who have demonstrated the experience, education, and examination performance required to perform electrical work within the scope allowed by Colorado law.

For the Journeyman Electrician classification, Colorado requires 8,000 hours of experience earned in no less than 4 years, with 2,000 hours of that experience in commercial or industrial work. The state also requires 288 hours of classroom education for all applicants. Documentation is an important part of the process, and applicants should be prepared to submit records that support both experience and education.

Colorado also provides license by endorsement and reciprocity pathways under specific conditions. The Colorado State Electrical Board participates in the National Electrical Reciprocal Alliance for journeyman electrician reciprocal licensing agreements with listed states. Reciprocity applicants must meet the state’s requirements, including holding a current qualifying license that was obtained through a state-administered examination and that meets Colorado’s current NEC testing requirements.

Electrical contractor registration is separate from the journeyman license. To perform electrical contracting in Colorado, a business must be registered with the state as an electrical contractor and must hold or employ a licensed master electrician to serve as the responsible individual for the company. A journeyman electrician license supports individual electrical practice, while contractor registration relates to the business side of offering electrical contracting services.

Candidates should also be aware that local requirements may apply depending on the city or county where work is performed. Colorado state licensing is central to the process, but local jurisdictions may have permitting, inspection, or administrative requirements connected to electrical work.

Test Information and Study Materials

Colorado journeyman electrician preparation should include a combination of trade review, code study, calculation practice, and repeated self-testing. Candidates should review general electrical knowledge, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special occupancies, special equipment, electrical devices, motors, transformers, photovoltaics, and electrical calculations.

The National Electrical Code is one of the most important study tools for a Colorado journeyman candidate. The NEC contains the rules and structure used to support safe electrical installations. Candidates should become familiar with Article 100 definitions, wiring and protection requirements, wiring methods and materials, equipment rules, special occupancies, special equipment, and tables used for sizing, fill, adjustment, correction, and installation decisions.

When studying with this combo, begin with one topic in the Colorado Journeyman Electrician Study Guide, then locate related NEC sections in the 2026 code book. This approach helps connect study guide review with actual code language. It also helps candidates avoid relying only on memorized answers.

For calculation topics, write out the full process. Electrical calculations often require identifying the information given, determining what the question is asking for, choosing the correct formula or table, and applying the proper rule. Candidates should pay close attention to whether the question asks for a minimum, maximum, ampacity, rating, load, conductor size, or overcurrent device value.

Because the Colorado exam is timed, code lookup practice should also be timed occasionally. Set aside study sessions where you answer questions while using the NEC, but give yourself a reasonable time limit. This helps develop speed while still requiring careful reading.

Review missed questions closely. When an answer is wrong, return to the study guide and NEC. Identify the rule, read the surrounding language, and understand why the correct answer applies. This habit builds stronger understanding and prepares you for questions that test the same topic in a different way.
