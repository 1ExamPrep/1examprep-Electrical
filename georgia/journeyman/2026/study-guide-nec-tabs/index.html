Exam Details

Georgia state electrical licensing is administered through the Georgia State Board of Electrical Contractors. The Board is part of the Georgia Construction Industry Licensing Board structure and provides information for electrical contractor applicants and licensees. Georgia’s statewide electrical contractor license categories include Class I Restricted and Class II Non-Restricted.

A Class I Restricted electrical contractor license is limited in scope. Georgia rule describes Class I electrical contracting as restricted to single-phase electrical installations that do not exceed 200 amperes at the service drop or service lateral. A Class II Non-Restricted electrical contractor license is the broader statewide electrical contractor classification.

Georgia uses PSI as the testing vendor for electrical contractor examinations. Applicants must first apply through the Board process and receive approval before registering for the examination. The Board’s application materials state that approved applicants receive testing information from PSI, including the Candidate Information Bulletin with exam process and test day instructions.

Georgia does not have a single statewide individual journeyman electrician exam administered in the same way some states do. Candidates searching for a “Georgia journeyman electrician” path should understand that the statewide credential most closely associated with independent electrical licensing is the electrical contractor license. Local authorities, apprenticeship programs, and employers may use journeyman-level credentials or expectations, but statewide licensure is contractor-based.

Because Georgia’s statewide electrical exams and local journeyman-level expectations are code-heavy, candidates should prepare broadly. Important study areas include electrical theory, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, wiring methods, motors, transformers, special occupancies, special equipment, safety, print reading, and electrical calculations.

Open Book Test

Georgia electrical contractor exam preparation should be treated as open book, reference-based preparation. The state’s electrical contractor process uses PSI examination materials, and electrical contractor candidates are expected to follow the official reference rules issued for their exam. Candidates preparing for local journeyman-level examinations should also confirm whether their local exam is open book or closed book before test day.

An open book electrical exam still requires serious preparation. Having the NEC available does not mean every answer will be easy to find. Candidates must understand the structure of the code book, know the meaning of electrical terms, and recognize which article, table, exception, or requirement applies to the question. Time pressure can make code lookup difficult for candidates who have not practiced before exam day.

The tabbed NEC included in this combo is designed to support code navigation practice. Tabs can help candidates move through major sections of the code book more efficiently while studying. During preparation, candidates should practice locating definitions, wiring and protection rules, wiring methods and materials, grounding and bonding rules, service and feeder requirements, box fill and conduit fill tables, motor provisions, special occupancies, special equipment, and calculation-related tables.

Open book preparation should include repeated lookup drills. Read a question, identify the subject, locate the applicable NEC section, read the code language carefully, and apply it to the facts provided. This process helps build the reference skills needed for a timed examination. It also helps candidates avoid relying only on memorized answers, which may not be enough when the same concept is tested in a different way.

Candidates should also confirm whether their specific exam allows personally tabbed books, highlighted references, handwritten notes, loose papers, photocopies, or other markings. Testing rules vary by vendor and exam type. A clean, properly prepared NEC is a valuable study tool, and candidates should make sure any exam-day reference materials comply with the rules issued for their specific Georgia exam.

Licensing Steps

The Georgia electrical licensing path depends on the credential being pursued. For statewide licensing, candidates apply through the Georgia State Board of Electrical Contractors for the appropriate electrical contractor classification. Candidates should determine whether the Class I Restricted or Class II Non-Restricted license matches the scope of work they plan to perform.

The state application process requires candidates to submit the proper application through the current licensing portal and provide the required documentation. Georgia’s Board materials identify the electrical contractor examination application process and explain that applicants must be approved by the Board before they can sit for the exam. Deficient applications may delay approval, so candidates should review the application checklist carefully before submission.

Once the Board approves the applicant to sit for the examination, PSI provides the Candidate Information Bulletin and exam registration instructions. The applicant is responsible for registering with PSI and scheduling the examination by the required deadline. After registration, PSI issues examination admission information before the test date.

After the examination, results are provided to the candidate and to the Board. Candidates who fail must follow the retesting process provided by PSI and the Board. Georgia rules also state that certain applicants who fail an examination twice must complete an approved review course before being scheduled for a third examination.

For candidates following a local journeyman-level path, the process may be different. A local county, city, employer, apprenticeship sponsor, or training organization may have its own experience, classroom, examination, and documentation requirements. Candidates should gather employment records, apprenticeship completion documents, classroom records, employer verification, and any local forms required for the credential or exam they are pursuing.

State Requirements

Georgia regulates statewide electrical contracting through the Georgia State Board of Electrical Contractors. The state’s electrical contractor license is intended for individuals who will perform, offer to perform, bid for, or contract electrical work within the scope of the license. This is different from a local or program-based journeyman credential used to show trade progression or employment qualification.

Georgia’s Class I Restricted electrical contractor license is limited to single-phase electrical installations not exceeding 200 amperes at the service drop or service lateral. The Class II Non-Restricted electrical contractor license is broader and is used for electrical contracting work outside the restricted Class I scope. Candidates should choose the license classification that matches the work they intend to contract.

The Georgia Secretary of State’s electrical contractor board page provides official licensing services, forms, renewal information, rules, and resources for electrical contractor applicants and licensees. The Board also requires licensed electrical contractors to meet continuing education requirements for renewal. Electrical contractor licenses renew on the Board’s established cycle.

Georgia candidates should understand the difference between employment as an electrical worker and independent electrical contracting. The statewide contractor license relates to contracting authority and business responsibility. An employee performing electrical work for a licensed electrical contractor may not need the same credential as the person qualifying or operating the electrical contracting business, depending on the work arrangement and local requirements.

Because local rules may apply, candidates should also check with the authority having jurisdiction for permits, inspections, local registration, business licensing, or journeyman-level recognition. State licensing, local permitting, and employer requirements can all affect what a candidate needs before working on a specific electrical project.

Test Information and Study Materials

Georgia journeyman-level electrical preparation should include a balanced mix of trade review, code study, calculation practice, and repeated self-testing. Candidates should review general electrical knowledge, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, overcurrent protection, motors, transformers, special occupancies, special equipment, low-voltage concepts, print reading, and safety.

The National Electrical Code is central to electrical exam preparation because it contains the rules and structure used to support safe electrical installations. Candidates should become comfortable using Article 100 definitions, wiring and protection requirements, wiring methods and materials, equipment rules, special occupancies, special equipment, and tables used for sizing, fill, adjustment, correction, and installation decisions.

When studying with this combo, begin with one topic in the Georgia Journeyman Electrician Study Guide, then locate related NEC sections in the 2026 code book. This approach helps connect study guide review with actual code language. It also helps candidates practice the same thinking process used during code-based electrical exams.

For calculation topics, write out each step. Electrical calculations may require identifying what the question gives you, determining what the question asks for, selecting the correct table or rule, applying adjustments or corrections, and checking whether the answer should be a minimum, maximum, ampacity, rating, load, conductor size, or overcurrent device value. A written process helps improve accuracy.

Timed practice is valuable because electrical examinations are designed to measure both knowledge and the ability to apply that knowledge efficiently. Candidates should occasionally answer questions under time limits while using the NEC. The goal is to build a steady rhythm: read the question, identify the topic, locate the rule, verify the language, and answer with confidence.

Review missed questions carefully. When an answer is wrong, return to the study guide and the NEC. Find the rule, read the surrounding language, and understand why the correct answer applies. This helps prepare for questions that test the same concept in a new way.
