## Exam Details

Georgia electrical contractor licensing is handled by the Georgia Construction Industry Licensing Board, Division of Electrical Contractors, under the Georgia Secretary of State. The Board reviews applications and determines whether an applicant is approved to sit for the examination. The examination is administered through PSI as a computer-based test at PSI test centers.

Georgia offers electrical contractor examinations for Class I Restricted and Class II Non-Restricted licenses. Both examinations are multiple-choice exams and are administered in two parts. The candidate handbook states that eight hours are allotted to complete both parts of the exam, with four hours for Part I followed by a break, and four hours for Part II. The time limit includes beta questions.

The Georgia electrical contractor examination content is divided into Regulations, Laws, and Administrative Functions and Technical Functions. Regulations, Laws, and Administrative Functions include compliance with laws, regulations, administrative requirements, and work planning. Technical Functions include electrical circuits, electrical controls and devices, rotating equipment, lighting systems, raceway systems, wiring systems, grounding systems, special systems, service equipment, and additional trade functions listed in the exam outline.

The Class I and Class II exams both include 30 questions in Regulations, Laws, and Administrative Functions and 110 questions in Technical Functions. The candidate handbook also identifies beta questions that are included in the total number of examination questions. The Class I exam includes 15 beta questions, and the Class II exam includes 22 beta questions. Beta questions are used for exam development and do not count toward the final score.

The minimum passing final scaled score for each Georgia electrical contractor examination is 70. After the examination, PSI provides the candidate with a score report. PSI also provides the official score report to the Board, and Board staff communicates with the applicant regarding results and next steps.

## Open Book Test

The Georgia electrical contractor examination is an open-book exam. The candidate handbook states that only the reference materials listed in the examination reference list may be used during the examination, and no other reference materials are allowed. Some questions may also be based on field experience and trade practices, so candidates should not rely on reference materials alone.

Open book does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates still need to know how to use references efficiently, find information quickly, understand the question being asked, and apply the correct rule or method. The exam is long, and candidates must manage both technical questions and administrative or regulatory questions within the allotted time.

## Licensing Steps

Georgia electrical contractor candidates begin by choosing the correct license classification. The Class I Restricted license and the Class II Non-Restricted license have different scopes and experience expectations. Candidates pursuing broad electrical contractor authority usually focus on the Class II Non-Restricted path.

The applicant must submit the required application to the Georgia Construction Industry Licensing Board, Division of Electrical Contractors. The Board must approve the applicant before the applicant can sit for the exam. The application requires documentation and information related to the license type selected. Experience must be gained under a licensed electrical contractor and documented through the required forms.

For the Class I Restricted license, the applicant must document experience in at least six of the primary experience areas. For the Class II Non-Restricted license, the applicant must document experience in all primary experience areas and experience with electrical installations in excess of single-phase, 400-ampere systems while working under a Class II Non-Restricted licensed contractor. The experience record must show at least four years of qualifying experience gained under a licensed contractor.

After the Board approves the application, the applicant receives approval to sit for the exam. PSI provides the candidate information needed to register and schedule the examination. Candidates are responsible for scheduling the exam by the posted deadline, following PSI’s test-day instructions, bringing required identification, and using only approved materials during the exam.

After the exam is completed, PSI provides results to the candidate and to the Board. Passing the examination is one part of the licensure process. The Board completes the licensing process after receiving official results and confirming that the applicant has satisfied the requirements for the license classification.

## State Requirements

Georgia regulates electrical contractors through the Georgia Construction Industry Licensing Board, Division of Electrical Contractors. Electrical contractors are licensed at the state level, and applicants must meet the Board’s requirements before receiving a license. The Board offers Class I Restricted and Class II Non-Restricted electrical contractor licenses.

The Class I Restricted license is limited compared with the Class II license. The Class II Non-Restricted license is the broader classification and is associated with unrestricted electrical contractor work within the state licensing structure. Candidates should select the license classification that matches the work they intend to perform and the experience they can document.

Georgia’s application process requires candidates to file through the Secretary of State licensing system and provide the required documentation. Applications are reviewed by the Board. Completed applications may be reviewed by Board staff and may be presented to the Board at a scheduled meeting. Incomplete applications may delay approval.

Georgia electrical contractor licenses renew every two years, with renewal due by June 30 of even-numbered years. Licensed electrical contractors must meet continuing education requirements for renewal, and the Board uses CE Broker to track continuing education completion.
