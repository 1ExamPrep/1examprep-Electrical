## Exam Details

The Georgia electrical contractor licensing examinations are administered through PSI for the Georgia Construction Industry Licensing Board, Division of Electrical Contractors. The exams are computer-based and administered at PSI test centers. Georgia offers two electrical contractor licensing examinations: Class I Restricted and Class II Unrestricted.

Both Georgia electrical contractor examinations are multiple-choice exams. The exam is administered in two parts, with a total of 8 hours allotted to complete both parts. Candidates have 4 hours to complete Part I, followed by a break, and 4 hours to complete Part II. The time limit includes beta or pretest questions.

The total number of scored test questions is 140 for both Class I Restricted and Class II Unrestricted. The Class I exam includes 15 additional pretest questions, bringing the total number of test and pretest questions to 155. The Class II exam includes 22 additional pretest questions, bringing the total number of test and pretest questions to 162. Pretest questions do not count toward the final score but are included in the exam session.

The minimum passing final scaled score for each Georgia electrical contractor examination is 70. Scores are scaled, which means the raw number of correct answers is converted into a scaled score that accounts for exam form difficulty. Candidates receive a score report after the examination showing whether they passed or did not pass and their final scaled score.

The Georgia electrical contractor exam content includes two major areas: regulations, laws, and administrative functions, and technical functions. The regulations, laws, and administrative portion includes complying with laws, complying with regulations, administrative requirements, and planning and organizing work. The technical portion includes basic electrical circuits, electrical controls and devices, rotating equipment, transformers, interior electrical systems, special equipment, special conditions, and special locations.

Class I and Class II candidates should be prepared for a broad range of trade and business topics. The content outline includes workers’ compensation, unemployment insurance, tax guide information, Georgia Construction Industry Licensing Board requirements, state sales and use tax, OSHA, NEC, NFPA, UL or other National Testing Laboratory certification, electrical permits and inspections, insurance, financing, bookkeeping, job cost estimating, bidding procedures, contracts, project management, and plan interpretation.

The technical content includes grounding systems, overloaded or shorted circuits, batteries, series and parallel circuits, wire terminations, splices, meters, wiring devices, control relays, drum switches, motor controls, overload relays, pressure switches, temperature switches, programmable controllers, panels, circuit breakers, disconnects, motors, motor loads, overload protection, transformers, interior wiring systems, grounding, panelboards, conduit, cables, circuit protection, temporary services, junction boxes, special occupancies, hazardous locations, data processing equipment, standby systems, signs, mobile homes, low-voltage systems, and over-600-volt systems.

## Open Book Test

The Georgia electrical contractor examinations are open book. Only approved reference materials listed for the examination may be used during the test. Candidates may bring as many or as few of the listed references as they choose, but no other reference materials are allowed in the testing room.

Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, or tabbed with permanent tabs. Photocopied materials, handwritten notes, and extra materials are not allowed, even if they are pasted into a reference book. Additional materials may be removed from the reference and confiscated, and a candidate may be removed from the examination for violating reference rules.

The open-book format makes reference navigation a major part of preparation. Open book does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates still need to understand what the question is asking, determine which reference applies, locate the correct section, interpret the requirement, and answer within the time allowed. The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs included in this Super Combo helps candidates practice code movement and become more comfortable with NEC structure during study.

Candidates are allowed to use silent, non-programmable calculators without alpha keys or printing capabilities in the testing room. The examination is computer-based, and one question is presented at a time. Candidates may skip questions and return to them later during the examination session, and questions may be bookmarked for review before the exam is completed.

## Licensing Steps

Georgia electrical contractor applicants apply through the Georgia Secretary of State licensing process. Candidates must select the correct electrical contractor license type, complete the application, provide required documentation and information, and submit the required fee. The Board must grant approval before an applicant may sit for the electrical contractor examination.

A practical licensing path begins with choosing the correct license classification. Class I Restricted and Class II Unrestricted licenses have different scopes and exam classifications. Candidates should review the classification that matches the electrical contracting work they intend to perform and apply under the proper license type.

After the application is submitted, Board staff reviews the application for completeness. Applications may be approved to sit for the exam, returned for more information, or denied. Once the Board approves a candidate for testing, the candidate receives an exam approval letter with instructions for registering through PSI. Exam approval letters are valid for one year.

After passing the required examination, PSI provides the Board with an official score report. The Board uses the score report and licensing file to continue the licensing process. Passing the examination is an important step, but the license is issued through the state licensing process after all applicable requirements have been satisfied.

Candidates who fail the examination must retake and pass the exam to be considered for licensure. A candidate who fails the examination twice must complete a Board-approved review course before being allowed to test a third time.

## State Requirements

The Georgia State Board of Electrical Contractors licenses electrical contractors, not individual electricians. The state electrical contractor license classifications include restricted and non-restricted electrical contractor licenses. In common exam-prep language, candidates may refer to these advanced contractor-level exams as master electrician exams, but the official state license structure is electrical contractor licensing.

To apply as an electrical contractor in Georgia, applicants use the Georgia Secretary of State licensing system. The application process requires the applicant to submit the correct application, required fee, and supporting documents. Required documents include a secure and verifiable document, a background check from the applicant’s local law enforcement agency, and three completed reference forms, with at least one reference from a licensed electrical contractor.

Electrical contractor license renewal is completed online. Renewal must be completed by June 30 of even-numbered years, and the renewal period occurs during April through June of even years. Continuing education is required for renewal. Electrical contractors must complete 4 continuing education hours each year, for a total of 8 hours per renewal cycle.

Applicants should make sure their application materials, references, background check, identification documentation, and exam approval steps are completed correctly. Exam preparation materials help candidates study for the examination, but licensing approval is handled by the Georgia Board based on the applicant’s qualifications, application file, exam results, and compliance with state requirements.
