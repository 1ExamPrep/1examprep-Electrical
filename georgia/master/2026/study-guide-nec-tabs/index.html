Exam Details

Georgia electrical contractor licensing is handled through the Georgia State Board of Electrical Contractors. Candidates pursuing Georgia electrical contractor licensure by examination must apply for approval, meet the applicable experience requirements, and follow the examination scheduling process after approval. Georgia recognizes electrical contractor license categories, including Class I restricted and Class II non-restricted licensing. The correct category depends on the type of electrical work and scope of experience involved.

For the Georgia electrical contractor exam process, applicants submit the required application through the state licensing process and receive approval before scheduling the examination. Once approved, candidates register with the testing vendor and schedule the exam according to the state’s posted deadlines and procedures. Examination results are provided to the candidate and to the Board.

The Georgia electrical contractor application materials identify qualifying experience gained under a licensed electrical contractor as an important part of the application process. For Class I restricted licensing, applicants document experience in required primary experience areas. For Class II non-restricted licensing, applicants document experience in all primary experience areas and experience with electrical installations in excess of single-phase, 400-ampere systems while working under a Class II non-restricted licensed contractor.

Students preparing with this 2026 Georgia Master Electrician Study Guide & National Electrical Code Combo with Tabs should focus on mastering both technical knowledge and code navigation. Important study areas include raceway systems, conductors, service equipment, grounding and bonding, branch circuits, feeders, overcurrent protection, motors, transformers, special occupancies, electrical calculations, safety rules, and NEC definitions. A strong preparation plan should include repeated practice finding NEC rules, interpreting code language, and applying electrical formulas accurately.

Open Book Test

The Georgia electrical examination process allows certain listed references to be used during the exam. Candidates may bring only the reference materials allowed for their specific exam category. Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, or tabbed with permanent tabs. Photocopied materials, handwritten notes, and additional unapproved materials are not allowed in the examination room.

An open book electrical exam still requires serious preparation. Many students make the mistake of assuming that having a code book available will make the test easy. In reality, success depends on knowing how the NEC is organized, understanding the meaning of electrical terms, and being able to locate requirements quickly. Timed exams leave little room for slow searching, so the best study strategy is to practice code lookup repeatedly before exam day.

Licensing Steps

The path to Georgia electrical contractor licensing generally begins with identifying the correct license classification. Candidates should determine whether they are applying for a restricted or non-restricted electrical contractor license and prepare documentation that supports the selected classification.

Applicants then complete the state licensing application and provide the required information, fee, and experience documentation. The application process includes documentation of qualifying electrical experience. Georgia application materials note that experience must be gained under a licensed electrical contractor, and the experience record must be completed by a licensed qualifying electrical contractor who can certify the applicant’s work history and experience.

After the application is reviewed, approved applicants receive authorization to sit for the examination. The candidate then registers with the testing vendor, schedules the exam, and follows the instructions in the candidate information materials. After the exam is completed, results are provided to the applicant and to the Board. Candidates who do not pass follow the retesting process provided by the state and testing vendor.

For study planning, candidates should begin preparing before waiting until the final scheduling stage. Electrical code study takes time, especially for candidates who need to strengthen calculation speed, NEC navigation, and interpretation of technical language. A consistent schedule using the 2026 Georgia Master Electrician Study Guide and the tabbed National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback can help students build a stronger foundation before exam day.

State Requirements

Georgia electrical contractor applicants are regulated through the Georgia State Board of Electrical Contractors. The state requires applicants to follow the official licensing process, submit the required application information, and document qualifying experience. Application materials distinguish between Class I restricted and Class II non-restricted applicants.

For Class I restricted applicants, Georgia application materials identify required experience in primary experience areas. For Class II non-restricted applicants, the materials identify experience in all primary experience areas and experience with electrical installations in excess of single-phase, 400-ampere systems while working under a Class II non-restricted licensed contractor. The experience documentation is a significant part of the application, and it must be completed carefully.

Georgia electrical contractors must also follow renewal and continuing education requirements issued by the Board. The Georgia Secretary of State’s electrical contractor page identifies license renewal information and continuing education tracking through CE Broker. Candidates and licensees should keep their licensing profile current and follow the renewal instructions provided by the state.
