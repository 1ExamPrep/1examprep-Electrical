## Exam Details

The Iowa Journeyman Electrician examination is administered through PSI for the Iowa Electrical Examining Board. Candidates must submit a license application and testing sponsorship request to the Board. After the Board approves the request, the candidate receives authorization to test and then schedules the examination through PSI.

The Iowa Journeyman Electrician exam is listed with **80 scored items**, **180 minutes** of testing time for scored items, and a required passing score of **70%**. The exam may also include **8 non-scored items** with an additional **30 minutes**. Non-scored items are used for examination development and do not count toward the final score.

The Iowa Journeyman Electrician content outline includes **13%** General Electrical Knowledge, **25%** Wiring and Protection, **19%** Wiring Methods and Materials, **19%** Equipment for General Use, **13%** Special Occupancies, **6%** Special Equipment, and **6%** Special Conditions. These categories cover a wide range of practical and code-based electrical knowledge.

This exam outline shows why students should study across the full range of electrical topics. Wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, and equipment for general use make up a large part of the examination, but smaller categories can still affect the final score. A candidate may need to interpret a wiring method requirement, size conductors, apply overcurrent protection rules, understand equipment requirements, identify special occupancy rules, or solve a calculation.

Because the test is timed, candidates should prepare for both accuracy and speed. Iowa’s journeyman exam gives candidates a limited amount of time to answer code-based, trade-based, and calculation-based questions. Strong preparation should include topic review, NEC navigation, and repeated problem practice instead of relying on field experience alone.

The Iowa Journeyman Electrician exam is an **open book** examination because approved reference material is allowed in the examination center. The current PSI candidate bulletin lists the National Electrical Code, 2023 edition, or the National Electrical Code Handbook, 2023, as allowed references for the journeyman examination.

## Licensing Steps

Iowa journeyman electrician candidates begin by meeting the state’s qualification path for the license. The Class A Journeyman path is tied to completion of a qualifying electrical training program, such as a registered apprenticeship training program, or an approved equivalent post-secondary electrical program recognized by the Electrical Examining Board.

After meeting the required training path, the candidate submits the license application and testing sponsorship request to the Iowa Electrical Examining Board. The Board reviews the application and determines whether the applicant is approved to test. A license fee is not required until after the test is passed.

Once approved, the candidate receives an authorization to test letter. The candidate then contacts PSI, pays the examination fee, and schedules the journeyman electrician exam. The authorization letter is valid for two examination attempts or six months. Candidates who do not pass after two attempts must contact the Board for another authorization and follow the Board’s rules.

After passing the exam, the candidate contacts the Iowa Electrical Examining Board for instructions regarding the license. Passing the exam is a major step, but the licensing process is not complete until all Board requirements are satisfied and the license is issued.

A strong licensing plan begins early. Candidates should keep training records organized, complete required program requirements, review the exam outline, and build a steady study routine. Journeyman exam preparation is usually strongest when students study over time through trade review, code navigation, and calculation practice instead of waiting until the exam date is close.

## State Requirements

Iowa issues state electrical licenses through the Iowa Electrical Examining Board. The Class A Journeyman Electrician license path requires successful completion of an apprenticeship training program registered by the United States Department of Labor’s Bureau of Apprenticeship and Training, or completion of a Board-recognized equivalent training pathway.

The Iowa Electrical Examining Board also recognizes some post-secondary electrical programs as equivalent pathways for journeyman licensing. For an approved post-secondary electrical program path, students may complete an approved program and documented on-the-job training before becoming eligible to be sponsored for the journeyman electrician exam.

A commonly used Iowa training model includes **8,000 hours** of qualifying electrical preparation. Under an approved post-secondary electrical program path, the 8,000-hour total may include **2,000 hours** from the approved program and **6,000 hours** of documented on-the-job training. Candidates should follow the exact pathway accepted by the Iowa Electrical Examining Board for their license type.

Iowa also has different license classifications, and students should make sure they are preparing for the correct credential. A Class A Journeyman license is different from a Class B license, a residential electrician license, a master electrician license, a residential master license, and an electrical contractor license. Each classification has its own scope and requirements.

That includes electrical installation knowledge, practical safety awareness, code understanding, calculation ability, and the confidence to apply electrical rules to real-world installation questions.
