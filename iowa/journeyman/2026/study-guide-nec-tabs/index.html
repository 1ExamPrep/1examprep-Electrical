Exam Details

The Iowa Electrical Examining Board oversees the statewide electrical licensing program. Iowa’s electrical examinations are administered through PSI Services LLC. Candidates must meet the state’s testing sponsorship requirements before scheduling the examination. The testing process is connected to the license classification being pursued, including Class A Journeyman Electrician, Class A Master Electrician, Residential Electrician, Residential Master Electrician, and certain special electrician license categories.

The Iowa journeyman electrician examination is a computer-based exam administered through PSI. Current Iowa testing information identifies the journeyman exam as requiring a score of 70% or higher to pass. The exam is designed to evaluate whether the candidate has the electrical knowledge needed to qualify for Iowa licensing at the journeyman level.

The Iowa journeyman electrician exam is commonly structured around National Electrical Code knowledge and practical electrical trade topics. Candidates should prepare for general electrical knowledge, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, conductors, raceways, boxes, motors, transformers, and electrical calculations.

Having access to code references does not mean every answer will be easy to find. Candidates must understand the structure of the code book, know the meaning of electrical terms, and recognize which article, table, exception, or requirement applies to the question.

Licensing Steps

The Iowa journeyman electrician licensing path begins with selecting the correct license classification and building the experience required for that path. Iowa offers Class A and Class B journeyman electrician licenses, and the requirements differ. The Class A Journeyman Electrician license is the examination-based journeyman credential commonly pursued by candidates who complete apprenticeship training and pass the approved journeyman examination.

A common Class A journeyman pathway includes successfully completing a registered apprenticeship program, completing four years of experience as an apprentice electrician, and passing a supervised written journeyman electrician examination approved by the Board with a score of 70% or higher within the required application period. Candidates should maintain complete records of apprenticeship registration, program completion, work experience, and examination results.

Iowa also recognizes certain experience-based and prior-license pathways under Board rules. A Class B journeyman electrician license may be available to individuals who meet the required experience standards under the applicable rules. Candidates should review the Iowa Electrical Examining Board requirements for the specific license class they are pursuing because Class A, Class B, residential, master, and contractor licenses are not the same credential.

Before testing, candidates must satisfy Iowa’s testing sponsorship requirements. After eligibility is established, the candidate schedules the exam through PSI and follows the current testing instructions. The candidate should review the PSI bulletin for identification requirements, check-in rules, reference material rules, calculator rules, exam format, and score reporting procedures.

After passing the required examination, the applicant completes the license application process through Iowa’s electrical licensing system. The applicant should submit the required application, fees, and documentation according to the current Iowa Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing instructions. Passing the exam is an important step, but license issuance still depends on meeting the Board’s requirements and completing the required licensing process.

State Requirements

Iowa regulates electrical licensing through the Electrical Examining Board within the Iowa Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing. Iowa has a statewide electrical licensing and inspection program, and electrical workers must hold the proper credential for the work being performed. The state licensing structure includes apprentice, unclassified person, residential, journeyman, master, and contractor categories.

For the Class A Journeyman Electrician license, Iowa rules identify a path involving successful completion of a registered apprenticeship program, four years of experience as an apprentice electrician, and a passing score on a Board-approved journeyman electrician examination. This pathway reflects the importance of both practical field experience and examination performance.

Iowa’s testing sponsorship information states that passing the State of Iowa PSI exam with a score of 70% or higher may qualify candidates for the applicable Iowa electrician license classification, including Class A Master or Journeyman, Residential Master, Residential Electrician, and certain special electrician licenses. Candidates should make sure they are applying for the correct exam and license type before scheduling.

Electrical contractor licensing is separate from an individual journeyman license. A journeyman electrician license supports individual trade qualification, while an electrical contractor license relates to contracting authority and business responsibility. Candidates who plan to operate an electrical contracting business in Iowa should review the contractor licensing requirements in addition to journeyman requirements.

Iowa license holders must also maintain their credentials according to renewal and continuing education requirements. Continuing education helps electricians stay current with code changes, installation practices, and safety requirements. Candidates should plan for ongoing code education after becoming licensed because electrical standards continue to change as new NEC editions are adopted.

The National Electrical Code is central to electrical exam preparation because it contains the rules and structure used to support safe electrical installations. Candidates should become comfortable using Article 100 definitions, wiring and protection requirements, wiring methods and materials, equipment rules, special occupancies, special equipment, communication system provisions, and tables used for sizing, fill, adjustment, correction, and installation decisions.

When studying with this combo, begin with one topic in the Iowa Journeyman Electrician Study Guide, then locate related NEC sections in the 2026 code book. This approach helps connect study guide review with actual code language. It also helps candidates practice the same thinking process they will need during an open book exam.

For calculation topics, write out each step. Electrical calculations may require identifying what the question gives you, determining what the question asks for, selecting the correct table or rule, applying adjustments or corrections, and checking whether the answer should be a minimum, maximum, ampacity, rating, load, conductor size, or overcurrent device value. A written process helps improve accuracy.

Timed practice is valuable because open book exams still require efficient reference use. Candidates should occasionally answer questions under time limits while using the NEC. The goal is to build a steady rhythm: read the question, identify the topic, locate the rule, verify the language, and answer with confidence.

Review missed questions carefully. When an answer is wrong, return to the study guide and the NEC. Find the rule, read the surrounding language, and understand why the correct answer applies. This helps prepare for questions that test the same concept in a new way.
