Exam Details

Nevada journeyman electrician qualification testing is commonly tied to the ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician examination used for electrical qualification card purposes. ICC electrician exams are scheduled through the ICC exam system and administered through Pearson VUE testing options, including testing center appointments and approved online testing where available.

The ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician exam has 80 multiple-choice questions and a 4-hour time limit. The exam is open book. The content outline includes general knowledge, services and service equipment, feeders, branch circuits and conductors, wiring methods and materials, equipment and devices, control devices, motors and generators, and special occupancies, equipment, and conditions.

The content distribution for the National Standard Journeyman Electrician exam includes 6% general knowledge, 11% services and service equipment, 4% feeders, 19% branch circuits and conductors, 26% wiring methods and materials, 13% equipment and devices, 4% control devices, 6% motors and generators, and 11% special occupancies, equipment, and conditions. These topics make the exam broad enough that candidates should prepare with both code study and practical trade review.

General knowledge may include electrical theory, general electrical load calculations, code definitions, terms, and plan reading. Services and service equipment may include services, available fault current, service load calculations, service equipment, service conductors, transformers, grounding and bonding, and temporary service wiring. Feeder topics may include load calculations and equipment grounding.

Branch circuits and conductors may include branch circuit calculations, overcurrent protection, conductor rules, required outlets, GFCI protection, AFCI protection, and equipment grounding. Wiring methods and materials may include raceway installations, underground wiring methods, boxes, enclosures, cabinets, panelboards, switchboards, clearance and guarding, flexible cords, and cables.

Equipment and devices may include luminaires, receptacles, switches, appliances, utilization equipment, heating and cooling equipment, and related installation requirements. Control devices may include switches, disconnects, and controllers. Motors and generators may include motor circuits, generator concepts, equipment protection, and related NEC requirements. Special occupancies, equipment, and conditions may include pools, mobile home and RV parks, data processing equipment, emergency systems, fire alarm systems, signaling systems, and other specialized installations.

Because Nevada electrical testing may depend on the examination code selected in the ICC catalog and the requirements of the electrical qualification program or jurisdiction involved, candidates should follow the current exam listing and application instructions for their specific testing path. Open Book Test

The ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician examination is open book. The published exam outline identifies the National Electrical Code as the primary reference and lists Ugly’s Electrical References as an allowed but not required reference. Candidates should follow the current ICC bulletin and Pearson VUE rules for the exact exam code, allowed references, code edition, calculator rules, tab rules, and test-day requirements.

Open book does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates still need to understand the question, identify the subject, locate the correct reference, read the code language carefully, and apply the requirement to the situation described. Many candidates lose time not because they lack trade experience, but because they are slow at moving through the NEC or unsure which article applies.

Electrical calculations should be practiced consistently before the exam. Candidates should review Ohm’s Law, power formulas, amperage, voltage, resistance, watts, conductor sizing, branch circuit calculations, feeder calculations, service load concepts, box fill, conduit fill, motor calculations, transformer concepts, overcurrent protection, and voltage drop. Calculation questions can take more time than direct code lookup questions, so repeated practice helps improve both speed and accuracy.

Code navigation is one of the most important parts of journeyman electrician preparation. Candidates should practice using the NEC table of contents, index, article numbers, definitions, tables, notes, and exceptions. A candidate who can quickly identify the topic being tested is better prepared to locate the correct rule and apply it correctly.

Services and service equipment should receive careful attention. Candidates should review service conductors, service disconnects, grounding at services, bonding, service load calculations, available fault current, transformers, temporary services, and service equipment installation requirements. These topics combine code knowledge, calculations, and practical installation understanding.

Branch circuits and conductors are major journeyman-level topics. Candidates should study branch circuit ratings, conductor ampacity, adjustment factors, correction factors, overcurrent protection, required outlets, GFCI protection, AFCI protection, equipment grounding, and conductor installation requirements. These topics are common in both field work and exam preparation.

Wiring methods and materials should be studied thoroughly. Candidates should review raceways, underground wiring methods, boxes, enclosures, cabinets, panelboards, switchboards, flexible cords, cables, supports, fittings, clearance, guarding, and wiring protection. This is one of the largest topic areas on the National Standard Journeyman Electrician exam.

Motors, generators, and control devices also require focused preparation. Candidates should review motor circuits, disconnecting means, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, switches, disconnects, generators, and related installation requirements. These questions may combine code rules with electrical theory and calculations.

Special occupancies, equipment, and conditions should not be ignored. Candidates should review swimming pools, mobile home parks, RV parks, data processing equipment, emergency systems, signaling systems, fire alarm systems, and other special installations. These topics may represent a smaller portion of the exam than wiring methods, but they can still affect the final result.

Flash cards are useful for shorter study sessions. Candidates can use them to review formulas, definitions, electrical theory, safety concepts, NEC topics, and trade terms before work, after work, during breaks, or between longer study blocks. Flash cards work best when paired with deeper reading, calculation drills, and code lookup practice.

A strong study plan should include scheduled reading, NEC navigation, calculation drills, timed practice, and repeated review of weaker topics. Candidates should avoid studying only familiar material. Journeyman electrician preparation rewards steady practice, careful reading, and the ability to apply electrical knowledge under exam conditions.
