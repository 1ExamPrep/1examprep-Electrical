## Exam Details

Nevada journeyman electrician qualification testing is commonly tied to the ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician examination used for electrical qualification card purposes. ICC electrician exams are scheduled through the ICC exam system and administered through Pearson VUE testing options, including testing center appointments and approved online testing where available.

The ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician exam has 80 multiple-choice questions and a 4-hour time limit. The exam is open book. The content outline includes general knowledge, services and service equipment, feeders, branch circuits and conductors, wiring methods and materials, equipment and devices, control devices, motors and generators, and special occupancies, equipment, and conditions.

The content distribution for the National Standard Journeyman Electrician exam includes 6% general knowledge, 11% services and service equipment, 4% feeders, 19% branch circuits and conductors, 26% wiring methods and materials, 13% equipment and devices, 4% control devices, 6% motors and generators, and 11% special occupancies, equipment, and conditions. These topics make the exam broad enough that candidates should prepare with both code study and practical trade review.

General knowledge may include electrical theory, general electrical load calculations, code definitions, terms, and plan reading. Services and service equipment may include services, available fault current, service load calculations, service equipment, service conductors, transformers, grounding and bonding, and temporary service wiring. Feeder topics may include load calculations and equipment grounding.

Branch circuits and conductors may include branch circuit calculations, overcurrent protection, conductor rules, required outlets, GFCI protection, AFCI protection, and equipment grounding. Wiring methods and materials may include raceway installations, underground wiring methods, boxes, enclosures, cabinets, panelboards, switchboards, clearance and guarding, flexible cords, and cables.

Equipment and devices may include luminaires, receptacles, switches, appliances, utilization equipment, heating and cooling equipment, and related installation requirements. Control devices may include switches, disconnects, and controllers. Motors and generators may include motor circuits, generator concepts, equipment protection, and related NEC requirements. Special occupancies, equipment, and conditions may include pools, mobile home and RV parks, data processing equipment, emergency systems, fire alarm systems, signaling systems, and other specialized installations.
