## Exam Details

Nevada state law requires contractors to be licensed and regulated by the Nevada State Contractors Board, and eligibility for examination is determined by the Board. The Nevada C-2 Electrical Contractor classification covers the installation of electrical wires, fixtures, appliances, apparatuses, raceways, and conduits. ## Open Book Test

The Nevada C-2 Electrical Contractor examination is identified as an open book examination under the published C-2 Electrical Contractor outline. Open book testing does not make the exam easy. It means candidates must know how to use approved references efficiently, read code language carefully, understand table notes and exceptions, and apply requirements under time pressure.

The published C-2 outline states that the National Electrical Code or National Electrical Code Handbook is allowed in the examination center. It also states that reference material may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed, but must otherwise be unmarked, may not be written in, and may not contain additional loose or attached papers. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references because the references are not provided at the examination center.

The Nevada State Contractors Board also explains that trade exams are generally closed book except for certain code references, and candidates must refer to the specific exam content outline to determine which reference materials may be used. For the C-2 Electrical Contractor outline, the NEC reference is listed as allowed. Candidates should follow the current PSI and Board instructions for the exact reference edition, tabs, markings, highlighting, indexing, and exam-room rules tied to their scheduled exam.

## Licensing Steps

Nevada electrical contractor licensing is handled through the Nevada State Contractors Board. A practical licensing path begins by identifying the correct classification for the work being performed. For broad electrical work, the C-2 Electrical Contractor classification covers electrical wires, fixtures, appliances, apparatuses, raceways, and conduits. Candidates should confirm that the classification matches the work they plan to perform before beginning the application process.

The licensing process begins with completing the Nevada State Contractors Board application process and submitting the required documentation. The Board determines whether the applicant qualifies to register for examination. After the Board accepts the applicant’s qualifications, the candidate receives a Candidate Eligibility letter and examination instructions. PSI then administers the examination through its computer-based testing program.

Candidates should complete the required examination portion or portions, follow PSI scheduling rules, bring proper identification to the examination center, and comply with the examination security procedures. After the candidate passes the examination, PSI forwards the results to the Board for processing. The Board then continues the licensing process according to its requirements.

Nevada contractor licensing may also involve business documents, financial information, qualifying individual details, fingerprints, insurance, bonding, application fees, license fees, and other Board requirements. The exact requirements depend on the license application and classification. Candidates should follow the Board’s current application instructions for their license type.
