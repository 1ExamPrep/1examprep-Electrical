Open Book Test

The Vermont Journeyman Electrician exam is an open-book examination using approved references. Open book does not mean easy. Candidates still need to understand electrical concepts, recognize how questions are written, locate information quickly, apply code rules correctly, and manage time across 90 questions in 4 hours.

Open-book electrical exams reward reference familiarity. Students should practice using the National Electrical Code table of contents, index, article structure, definitions, tables, exceptions, notes, and common code sections before exam day. Students should also review Vermont Electrical Safety Rules, local amendments, and Vermont statutes because the exam includes Vermont-specific content.

Reference preparation matters. Candidates should use the current Vermont candidate bulletin for the exact rules on approved books, notes, tabs, highlighting, handwritten materials, calculators, loose papers, and exam-room materials. Study guides, practice exams, flash cards, and unauthorized notes should be used before test day and should not be treated as exam-room references unless the testing instructions specifically allow them.

A strong open-book strategy includes learning where major NEC topics are located before the exam. Students should practice identifying the correct article, checking definitions first, reading table notes carefully, reviewing exceptions, and confirming whether the question asks for a minimum, maximum, permitted condition, prohibited condition, calculation result, or Vermont-specific rule. Flash cards help students recall key concepts faster, while practice exams help build the habit of reading questions carefully and choosing the correct reference path.

Students should prepare for both code navigation and core knowledge. General theory, formulas, calculations, wiring methods, grounding, bonding, overcurrent protection, motors, equipment, and special conditions are easier to answer when the student already understands the concept before looking up supporting code language.

Licensing Steps

Vermont journeyman electrician licensing begins with education, supervised field experience, and exam preparation. Candidates commonly complete a structured electrical apprenticeship that combines classroom instruction with on-the-job training under a licensed master electrician or other qualifying supervision recognized by the state.

A typical Vermont journeyman pathway includes registering or participating as an apprentice, completing required classroom instruction, gaining supervised electrical experience, documenting work hours, preparing for the journeyman examination, applying through the Vermont Division of Fire Safety process, scheduling the Prov examination, passing the Vermont Journeyman Electrician exam, and completing the remaining license issuance steps.

Vermont apprenticeship preparation commonly includes 8,000 hours of on-the-job training and 576 hours of classroom instruction. Apprenticeship programs often divide classroom training across four years, with 144 hours of classroom instruction per year. Candidates should maintain accurate records of employers, dates of work, supervised hours, classroom completion, apprenticeship documents, exam application materials, and licensing notices.

After becoming licensed, a Vermont journeyman electrician may perform electrical installations under the direction of a master electrician and may supervise an apprentice electrician or electrician’s helper employed by a master electrician under that master electrician’s direction. A Vermont master electrician license is a separate credential with separate experience and examination requirements.

This study guide and flash card combo supports the exam-preparation stage of the licensing process. Students can use it while completing apprenticeship training, while documenting work experience, before applying for the exam, after scheduling the exam, or during final review before the testing date.
