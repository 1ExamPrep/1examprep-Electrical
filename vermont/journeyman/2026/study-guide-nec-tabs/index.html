Exam Details

The Vermont journeyman electrician exam is used to evaluate whether a candidate has the trade knowledge, electrical code understanding, and practical application skills needed for journeyman-level electrical work. Published Vermont exam information commonly lists the journeyman electrician exam as an 80-question, multiple-choice examination with a 4-hour time limit and a 70% passing score.

Important exam subject areas include electrical theory, general electrical knowledge, services, feeders, branch circuits, wiring methods, conductors and cables, raceways, boxes, cabinets, panelboards, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, motors, transformers, special occupancies, special equipment, electrical safety, and calculations. Students should be prepared to work through both knowledge-based questions and questions that require locating and applying NEC requirements.

Open Book Test

The Vermont journeyman electrician exam is listed as an open book examination. Open book testing allows candidates to use approved reference materials under testing rules, but it still requires strong preparation. A timed electrical exam rewards candidates who know where to look, how to read code language, and how to apply the rule without wasting time.

Open book does not mean the answer will be obvious. Many NEC questions require the candidate to identify the correct article, read the full section, apply table notes, understand exceptions, and connect multiple code rules. For example, a conductor-sizing question may require ampacity tables, adjustment factors, correction factors, terminal temperature limitations, and overcurrent protection rules. A grounding question may require the candidate to distinguish between a grounding electrode conductor, equipment grounding conductor, grounded conductor, and bonding jumper.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs helps students practice code navigation throughout the study process. Tabs are useful for organizing major articles, tables, definitions, and frequently used sections. Students should use the tabs while practicing, so locating common NEC areas becomes more natural before exam day.

Students should also practice timed lookup. During study sessions, answer questions with the NEC open, locate the supporting rule, read the surrounding language, and review the explanation. Over time, this process helps students develop the speed, accuracy, and confidence needed for open book electrical testing.

Licensing Steps

  1. Register and gain supervised electrical experience. Vermont’s standard journeyman path includes documented on-the-job electrical experience through registered apprenticeship or equivalent training accepted by the state.
  2. Complete the required classroom instruction. Vermont’s common journeyman path includes 576 hours of related classroom instruction in electrical theory, code, safety, and trade subjects.
  3. Accumulate the required experience hours. Vermont commonly requires 8,000 hours of documented electrical work experience for the journeyman path.
  4. Apply for examination or licensure through the proper state process. Candidates must follow the Vermont Division of Fire Safety and Electricians’ Licensing Board application procedures.
  5. Prepare for the journeyman electrician exam. Study electrical theory, calculations, NEC navigation, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, overcurrent protection, and safety.
  6. Take and pass the journeyman electrician exam. Candidates must meet the required passing standard and follow all testing rules for identification, reference materials, calculators, and conduct.
  7. Complete final licensing requirements. After passing, candidates must satisfy any remaining state requirements before the license is issued.
  8. Maintain the license. Vermont journeyman and master electricians must complete approved continuing education for renewal, including National Electrical Code instruction.

State Requirements

Vermont electrician licensing is handled through the Vermont Department of Public Safety, Division of Fire Safety, and the Vermont Electricians’ Licensing Board. Vermont law and electrical safety rules regulate electrical installations, licensing, inspections, renewals, continuing education, and enforcement.

A Vermont journeyman electrician is authorized to perform electrical installations within the scope allowed by the license. A journeyman electrician works under the direction of a licensed master electrician and may supervise apprentices or helpers as allowed by Vermont law and rule. Operating as an electrical contractor or taking responsibility for broader electrical contracting work may require a master electrician license or additional business compliance.

The common Vermont journeyman electrician path includes 8,000 hours of documented on-the-job electrical experience and 576 hours of related classroom instruction. Vermont also recognizes equivalent training and experience when accepted under the rules and application process. Candidates should keep accurate records of work history, apprenticeship status, classroom completion, employers, supervisors, and dates of experience.

Vermont electrical rules also require continuing education for license renewal. Journeyman and master electricians must complete approved National Electrical Code instruction during the renewal cycle. Continuing education helps licensees stay current with code changes, safety updates, and state requirements.

Because electrical licensing affects the legal ability to perform work, students should treat study preparation and licensing compliance as separate responsibilities. This combo helps students prepare for the knowledge and code-navigation portion of the journey, while the candidate remains responsible for following Vermont’s application, documentation, testing, and renewal requirements.
