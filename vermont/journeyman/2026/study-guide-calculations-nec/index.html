Exam Details

The Vermont Journeyman Electrician exam is administered by Prov for the Vermont Division of Fire Safety. The exam is computer-based and is part of Vermont’s trade licensing and certification testing program. Candidates approved for testing may schedule through Prov and take the exam at available Prov testing locations.

The Vermont Journeyman Electrician exam contains 90 questions and has a 4-hour time allowance. Exams are scored against a 70% cut score, and candidates who achieve 70% or higher receive a passing grade. Results are provided at the testing center after the exam is completed, and the State is notified automatically of the candidate’s results.

The Vermont Journeyman Electrician exam subject breakdown includes general electrical theory and principles, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, motors and controls, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, and local amendments. The largest topic areas are wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, and equipment for general use, so candidates should devote enough study time to those sections while still reviewing the smaller categories.

The exam content is designed to measure the knowledge a qualified electrical professional should use during normal work operations. Candidates should prepare by reviewing the listed subject areas, practicing with their reference materials, and building speed with code navigation. The more time spent studying before test day, the less time is needed during the exam to search for unfamiliar information.

Open Book Test

Vermont Division of Fire Safety examinations are designed to allow candidates to use authorized references during testing. The Vermont Journeyman Electrician exam reference list includes the State of Vermont Electrical Safety Rules, NEC Notes, NFPA 70 National Electrical Code, Ugly’s Electrical References, and Vermont Statutes Title 26 Chapter 15.

Open book testing still requires strong preparation. Having approved references available does not replace knowing where information is located, how code articles are organized, or how tables and definitions apply to exam questions. Candidates should practice using the National Electrical Code regularly before exam day so they can move through definitions, chapters, tables, indexes, and article sections with confidence.

Prov reference rules allow candidates to prepare approved reference materials using highlighting and permanent tabs. Moveable tabs are not permitted. Reference books cannot contain Post-it notes, photocopied documents added to a reference book, or photocopied versions of a reference book. Test preparation study guides and practice exams are not approved references and are not permitted in the testing center. Candidates may not highlight, underline, mark, or write any information in reference materials during testing.

Because this product includes study guides and the NEC, candidates should understand the difference between study materials and exam-room references. The 2026 Vermont Journeyman Electrician Study Guide and 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide are designed for preparation before the exam. The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback is included for code study and reference practice. Candidates should follow the testing provider’s approved reference list and exam-day rules for the specific materials permitted in the testing room.

Licensing Steps

Vermont electrician licensing is handled through the Vermont Division of Fire Safety. Candidates seeking a Vermont journeyman electrician license should complete the required training and experience, submit the required application materials to the licensing authority, receive approval to test, schedule the required examination, pass the Vermont Journeyman Electrician exam, and complete any remaining licensing steps required by the Division.

A practical preparation timeline should begin before the candidate is ready to schedule the exam. First, organize your work experience and training records. Next, review the Vermont journeyman electrician exam outline so your study plan matches the tested topics. Then, use the Vermont Journeyman Electrician Study Guide to review core subject areas and the Electrician Calculations Study Guide to strengthen math skills. Finally, use the National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback to practice locating rules, tables, definitions, and article references under timed conditions.

After approval to test, candidates can schedule through Prov. Candidates should plan ahead for the exam date, arrive early, bring required identification, and follow all testing center rules. A government-issued photo-bearing identification is required at check-in, and candidates must follow the testing center’s rules regarding approved items, calculators, references, and prohibited materials.

Passing the exam is one part of the licensing process. Candidates should continue to follow Vermont Division of Fire Safety instructions for application status, licensing documents, and any additional requirements that apply to their individual situation. Keeping paperwork organized can help reduce delays and make the licensing process easier to manage.

State Requirements

Vermont regulates electrical licensing through the Division of Fire Safety. The Vermont Journeyman Electrician license is for individuals who perform electrical installations under the direction of a master electrician and may supervise an apprentice electrician or electrician’s helper employed by a master electrician under the master electrician’s direction.

Vermont’s exam program includes multiple electrical examinations, including Vermont Journeyman Electrician, Vermont Master Electrician, and several Type-S specialty electrical exams. Candidates should make sure they are preparing for the correct exam classification before scheduling. Taking the wrong exam can delay progress and may result in forfeited exam fees.

The Vermont Journeyman Electrician exam measures knowledge across general electrical theory and principles, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, motors and controls, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, and local amendments. These areas reflect the broad responsibilities of a journeyman electrician, including understanding electrical systems, interpreting code requirements, applying safe installation practices, and recognizing Vermont-specific rules.

For study purposes, candidates should pay close attention to wiring and protection because it is the largest subject area on the exam. This may include topics connected to branch circuits, feeders, services, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, conductor ampacity, and safe installation requirements. Wiring methods and materials also deserve regular review, since candidates must understand raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, conductor installation, and the conditions that affect how wiring systems are selected and installed.

Test Information and Study Materials

The Vermont Journeyman Electrician exam includes 90 questions with a 4-hour time limit. The subject area breakdown includes 7 questions on general electrical theory and principles, 30 questions on wiring and protection, 20 questions on wiring methods and materials, 3 questions on motors and controls, 15 questions on equipment for general use, 4 questions on special occupancies, 3 questions on special equipment, 3 questions on special conditions, and 5 questions on local amendments.

Because the exam is timed, candidates should practice both accuracy and pacing. Four hours gives candidates time to read carefully, but it does not leave room for constant searching through unfamiliar materials.
