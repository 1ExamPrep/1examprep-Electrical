Exam Details

The Vermont Master Electrician examination is used for candidates pursuing master-level electrical licensure through the Vermont Department of Public Safety, Division of Fire Safety. Candidates must meet Vermont’s eligibility requirements, apply through the state licensing process, and receive approval to test before completing the examination process.

Vermont master electrician exam preparation should focus on NEC knowledge, electrical theory, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, motors and controls, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, and Vermont local amendments. Candidates should prepare for both technical code questions and practical trade questions that test the ability to interpret and apply electrical rules.

The Vermont Master Electrician exam is commonly structured as a timed, open book electrical examination. The current Vermont master electrician exam outline used in exam-preparation materials lists 105 questions and a 5-hour testing window. That makes the master license an advanced credential built on trade experience, state approval, and examination success.

The Vermont Master Electrician exam is an open book test. Open book electrical exams allow candidates to use approved references, but they still require strong preparation. Licensing Steps

The Vermont master electrician licensing path begins with meeting the state’s experience and licensing requirements. A common pathway is to hold a Vermont journeyman electrician license for at least two years before applying for master electrician licensure. Vermont also allows comparable experience and training, within or outside the state, when accepted by the Board.

A Vermont journeyman electrician generally reaches that license level through apprenticeship, documented electrical work experience, related classroom instruction, and successful completion of the journeyman examination. After becoming a journeyman, the electrician gains additional experience and prepares for the master electrician exam.

When ready to pursue the master license, the applicant submits the required application materials to the Vermont Department of Public Safety, Division of Fire Safety. The application process may include licensing history, documentation of experience, required forms, fees, and any supporting material requested by the state.

After the application is reviewed and the candidate is approved for examination, the candidate schedules and takes the Vermont Master Electrician exam. Preparation should begin before scheduling because the exam requires knowledge of the NEC, electrical theory, wiring and protection, wiring methods, motors and controls, special occupancies, equipment rules, and Vermont-specific amendments.

After passing the required examination and completing the licensing process, the applicant may be issued the Vermont Master Electrician license. Licensees must continue to follow Vermont electrical laws, Division of Fire Safety requirements, renewal rules, continuing education requirements, permit procedures, inspection requirements, supervision responsibilities, and the scope of work authorized by the license held.

State Requirements

Vermont electrician licensing is administered statewide through the Vermont Department of Public Safety, Division of Fire Safety, and the Electricians’ Licensing Board. Vermont requires electricians performing regulated electrical work to hold the proper license classification unless an exemption applies.

Vermont license classifications include journeyman electrician, master electrician, and Type-S journeyman specialist categories. A journeyman electrician may perform electrical work within the scope allowed by law, while a master electrician may operate at a higher level of responsibility, including supervising electrical work and qualifying for broader business and permitting responsibilities.

To qualify for master electrician licensure, Vermont law requires the applicant to have been licensed as a journeyman electrician for at least two years or to have comparable experience and training acceptable to the Board. The applicant must also pass an examination to the satisfaction of the Board. This makes the Vermont Master Electrician license an advancement credential for electricians who have already developed substantial trade experience.

Vermont licensees must also comply with renewal and continuing education requirements. Electrician licenses are renewed on a cycle set by the state, and continuing education includes NEC-based instruction. Vermont also requires electricians to complete the Vermont Energy Goals Education Module as part of the licensing and renewal structure.
