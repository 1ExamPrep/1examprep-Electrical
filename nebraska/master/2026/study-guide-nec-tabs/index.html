Exam Details

Nebraska electrical licensing exams are part of the statewide licensing process administered through the Nebraska State Electrical Division. The State Electrical Board approved PSI as the examination provider beginning October 1, 2023. After an applicant is approved to test, the State Electrical Division submits eligibility to PSI, and PSI sends scheduling instructions by email. The PSI candidate bulletin provides exam-specific details, including time allowed, number of questions, topic categories, and what may be brought to the examination.

The Nebraska State Electrical Division states that examinations for license types other than apprentice registration cover subjects including the National Electrical Code, Basic Electricity Theory, the Nebraska State Electrical Act, Blueprint Reading, and Emergency Circuits, including Fire Alarm Circuits. The Division also emphasizes that thorough knowledge of the National Electrical Code is the greatest asset for passing the examination.

For Nebraska contractor and master-level electrical preparation, students should expect broad NEC-based study. Licensing Steps

The Nebraska master-level licensing path begins with determining the correct license type. Nebraska electrical license categories include apprentice registration, journeyman electrician, electrical contractor, fire alarm installer, residential journeyman wireman, and reciprocal licensing options. Candidates preparing for master-level authority commonly focus on the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination pathway.

For an electrical contractor license, Nebraska identifies two qualification pathways. An applicant may be a graduate of a four-year electrical engineering course from an accredited college or university and have at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a licensed journeyman electrician. An applicant may also qualify with at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a licensed journeyman electrician.

The candidate submits an exam application through the Nebraska State Electrical Division’s licensing system or by mail, depending on the application type. The Division reviews the application and determines eligibility. Once the applicant is approved to test, the Division submits eligibility to PSI, and PSI emails the candidate with scheduling instructions.

After scheduling, the candidate takes the examination at a PSI testing site. Nebraska testing through PSI is not offered as at-home testing. PSI testing locations in Nebraska include Grand Island, Lincoln, Norfolk, North Platte, Omaha, Scottsbluff, and Sidney, with additional PSI locations outside Nebraska available through the testing system.

After passing the exam, the candidate completes the licensing process required by the Nebraska State Electrical Division. Nebraska states that candidates who pass an exam are awarded six hours of continuing education credit toward the total needed for license renewal. Licensees must keep credentials current and follow renewal requirements issued by the State Electrical Board.

State Requirements

Nebraska electrical licensing is regulated statewide through the Nebraska State Electrical Division and the Nebraska State Electrical Board. The Division’s mission is public protection through electrical licensing and inspection, with electrical installations reviewed for safety and compliance with the National Electrical Code.

The electrical contractor license is the master-level business and supervisory path most closely associated with master electrician preparation in Nebraska. Applicants must meet the Board’s qualification requirements, receive approval to test, pass the required examination, and complete the license process. A candidate who does not yet meet contractor-level qualifications may need to build experience as a licensed journeyman electrician before applying.

Nebraska’s current exam information lists the National Electrical Code, basic electricity theory, the Nebraska State Electrical Act, blueprint reading, and emergency circuits including fire alarm circuits as exam coverage areas. This makes Nebraska preparation broader than simple NEC lookup. Candidates should study both trade knowledge and state-specific electrical law and rule content.

Nebraska licenses and registrations expire on December 31 of even-numbered years. To renew an electrical license, the license holder must provide proof of at least 12 hours of State Electrical Board-approved continuing education, with at least 6 hours on the National Electrical Code. Continuing education hours are not required to renew an apprentice registration.

These topics are central to master-level electrical work and frequently require both NEC lookup and calculation skill.

Wiring methods and materials should include raceways, cables, boxes, cabinets, conduit bodies, pull boxes, junction boxes, conductor fill, support rules, wet-location requirements, underground installations, fittings, and Chapter 9 table use. Students should practice reading table notes and article-specific requirements because small details can change the correct answer.

Equipment for general use should include switches, receptacles, luminaires, panelboards, appliances, motors, transformers, capacitors, fixed electric space-heating equipment, and related installation rules. Motors and controls should be reviewed with attention to conductor sizing, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, disconnecting means, controllers, and control circuit concepts.

Emergency circuits and fire alarm circuits should also be included in the study plan. Students should review emergency systems, legally required standby systems, optional standby systems, fire alarm circuits, power-limited circuit rules, signaling systems, and related NEC organization. Blueprint reading should include symbol recognition, plan interpretation, circuit layout, equipment schedules, panel schedules, riser diagrams, and understanding how drawings connect to code requirements.

Calculation practice is essential. Students should practice Ohm’s Law, Watt’s Law, series and parallel circuits, service and feeder load calculations, conductor ampacity, adjustment and correction factors, conduit fill, box fill, motor calculations, transformer calculations, grounding electrode conductor sizing, and overcurrent protection sizing. Each calculation should be worked step by step so the student understands the process and not just the answer.
