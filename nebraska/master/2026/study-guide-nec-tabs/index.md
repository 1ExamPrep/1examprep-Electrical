## Licensing Steps

The Nebraska master-level licensing path begins with determining the correct license type. Nebraska electrical license categories include apprentice registration, journeyman electrician, electrical contractor, fire alarm installer, residential journeyman wireman, and reciprocal licensing options. Candidates preparing for master-level authority commonly focus on the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination pathway.

For an electrical contractor license, Nebraska identifies two qualification pathways. An applicant may be a graduate of a four-year electrical engineering course from an accredited college or university and have at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a licensed journeyman electrician. An applicant may also qualify with at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a licensed journeyman electrician.

The candidate submits an exam application through the Nebraska State Electrical Division’s licensing system or by mail, depending on the application type. The Division reviews the application and determines eligibility. Once the applicant is approved to test, the Division submits eligibility to PSI, and PSI emails the candidate with scheduling instructions.

After scheduling, the candidate takes the examination at a PSI testing site. Nebraska testing through PSI is not offered as at-home testing. PSI testing locations in Nebraska include Grand Island, Lincoln, Norfolk, North Platte, Omaha, Scottsbluff, and Sidney, with additional PSI locations outside Nebraska available through the testing system.

After passing the exam, the candidate completes the licensing process required by the Nebraska State Electrical Division. Nebraska states that candidates who pass an exam are awarded six hours of continuing education credit toward the total needed for license renewal. Licensees must keep credentials current and follow renewal requirements issued by the State Electrical Board.

Nebraska electrical licensing is regulated statewide through the Nebraska State Electrical Division and the Nebraska State Electrical Board. Nebraska licenses and registrations expire on December 31 of even-numbered years. To renew an electrical license, the license holder must provide proof of at least **12 hours** of State Electrical Board-approved continuing education, with at least **6 hours** on the National Electrical Code. Continuing education hours are not required to renew an apprentice registration.
