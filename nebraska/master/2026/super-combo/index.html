Exam Details

Nebraska electrical examinations are connected with the Nebraska State Electrical Board and the Nebraska State Electrical Division. The State Electrical Board approved a switch in exam providers from ICC to PSI, and testing for Electrical Contractor, Journeyman, and Residential Wireman exams moved to PSI beginning October 1, 2023. Candidates apply through the Nebraska State Electrical Division and must be approved before scheduling through PSI.

The Nebraska State Electrical Division states that the examination for license types other than Apprentice Registration covers, but is not limited to, the National Electrical Code, Basic Electricity and Theory, the Nebraska State Electrical Act, Blueprint Reading, and Emergency Circuits, including Fire Alarm Circuits. The Division also emphasizes that thorough knowledge of the National Electrical Code is one of the most important assets for passing the examination.

Nebraska is listed as a participating state agency for the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination program through the Nebraska State Electrical Division. The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is identified as a Master Electrician/Unlimited Electrician examination and is designed for individuals who conduct business in the installation, maintenance, and repair of electrical work conforming to applicable standards and codes.

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination is listed with 100 questions, a minimum passing score of 75, and 270 minutes of testing time. The content outline includes Project Design & Management, Safety, Electrical Theory & Principles, General Code Requirements, Wiring & Protection, Wiring Methods & Materials, Equipment for General Use, Special Occupancies, Special Equipment & Special Conditions, and Communication Systems. The bulletin also states that 10 non-scored experimental questions are administered in addition to the scored examination items.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination used through the Nebraska State Electrical Division pathway is identified as an open book examination. Open book testing does not make the exam easy. It means candidates must know how to use approved references efficiently, read code language carefully, understand tables and exceptions, and apply requirements under time pressure.

The published NASCLA electrical contractor reference rules allow reference materials in the examination center, including a physical diagram or blueprint packet provided onsite, the National Electrical Code or National Electrical Code Handbook in the listed editions, OSHA construction and occupational safety references, project management and business references, NFPA 70E, Ugly’s Electrical References, and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications. Reference material may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed, but it must otherwise be unmarked, may not contain written notes, and may not contain additional loose or attached papers. References are not provided at the examination center. Permanent tabs are allowed, while temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not allowed.

Licensing Steps

Nebraska electrician licensing is handled through the Nebraska State Electrical Division and Nebraska State Electrical Board. A practical licensing path begins by identifying the correct license type for the work being performed. Nebraska licensing categories include electrical contractor, journeyman electrician, residential journeyman wireman, fire alarm installer, apprentice electrician registration, and reciprocal licensing pathways.

For examination, candidates use the Nebraska electrician registration and license application process. The State Electrical Division lists the online application for initial exam applications, retake exam applications, and new apprentice registration. Mail-in applications are also listed for exam applications, reciprocal licensing, and apprentice registrations. Candidates must apply and be approved before scheduling the examination.

After approval, eligibility is submitted to PSI, and candidates receive scheduling information from PSI. Nebraska’s PSI information states that candidates schedule through PSI after approval and should use the candidate bulletin for the number of questions, time allowed, topic categories, and exam-room materials. Candidates approved for the NASCLA electrical program have 90 days from the application approval date to take the exam, and the published bulletin identifies one attempt per eligibility. If a candidate fails, the candidate must reapply with the Nebraska State Electrical Board.

The study portion of the licensing path should be treated as a structured project. Candidates should review the Nebraska State Electrical Division exam subjects, study the 2026 NEC, practice calculations, work through code navigation, review state electrical act concepts, and use flash cards for repeated reinforcement. Consistent preparation helps candidates approach Nebraska master electrician and electrical contractor exam study with more organization and confidence.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nebraska Master Electrician study plan should include NEC study, calculation practice, trade knowledge review, state electrical act review, blueprint reading, emergency circuit review, reference navigation, and timed question work. A balanced routine helps build confidence across project design, safety, electrical theory, general code requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods, equipment, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, communication systems, and calculations.

For NEC study, start by learning the structure of the code book. Review article organization, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and cross-references. Practice moving from a question to the correct article and reading the surrounding language carefully. Many electrical exam questions are missed because a candidate finds a related section but overlooks a condition, exception, table note, or specific wording.

For Nebraska-specific study, review the Nebraska State Electrical Act and the state-related topics connected with the exam. For calculations, write out each step.
