Exam Details

Kansas does not operate under one uniform statewide electrician license for every city and county. Electrical licensing is commonly handled by local jurisdictions, and candidates should follow the requirements of the city or county where they plan to work, supervise electrical work, pull permits, or qualify an electrical contracting business.

Many Kansas jurisdictions recognize standard contractor and trades examinations as part of the licensing process. The State of Kansas Contractor/Trades Examination Information Bulletin identifies the KGD Kansas Standard Master Electrician examination. This exam is used to help licensing agencies evaluate trade knowledge, but passing an exam does not automatically guarantee that a local jurisdiction will issue a license. The licensing authority still controls application approval, experience review, license issuance, and local work authorization.

The Kansas Standard Master Electrician exam is listed as an open book examination with 100 multiple-choice questions and a 5-hour time limit. The listed passing score is 75%. The exam is administered through Pearson VUE as part of the ICC contractor and trades testing program.

The Kansas Standard Master Electrician exam outline includes several major content areas. These include general knowledge and theory, services and service equipment, feeders, branch circuits and conductors, wiring methods and materials, equipment and devices, control devices, motors and generators, and special occupancies, equipment, and conditions. These subject areas require students to understand both practical electrical work and the structure of the National Electrical Code.

For study purposes, candidates should focus heavily on NEC organization and code application. Important subjects include Article 100 definitions, conductor ampacity, service sizing, feeder calculations, branch-circuit requirements, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceway installations, box fill, conduit fill, switches, receptacles, luminaires, appliances, motors, generators, swimming pools, mobile homes, recreational vehicle parks, emergency systems, fire alarms, and data processing equipment.

Open Book Test

The Kansas Standard Master Electrician exam is listed as an open book examination. Open book testing allows candidates to use approved references during the exam, but it does not remove the need for serious preparation. The exam is timed, and candidates must be able to locate information quickly, read code language carefully, and apply the correct rule to the question being asked.

The Kansas Standard Master Electrician exam reference list includes the National Electrical Code and Ugly’s Electrical References. The Kansas bulletin identifies the NEC edition used for that listed exam. Students should always bring the exact references and editions required for the exam they are scheduled to take through their local jurisdiction or testing provider.

Licensing Steps

The Kansas licensing path begins with identifying the local jurisdiction that controls the license. Because Kansas electrical licensing is commonly handled at the city or county level, candidates should determine whether they are applying through Johnson County, Sedgwick County, Wichita, Topeka, Lawrence, Kansas City, another city, or another local authority.

After identifying the jurisdiction, the candidate should review the local license classification. A Kansas jurisdiction may use titles such as master electrician, journeyman electrician, electrical contractor, residential electrician, or another local classification. The correct license depends on the scope of work, whether the applicant will supervise work, whether the applicant will qualify a business, and whether the work is residential, commercial, industrial, or mixed in scope.

The next step is to gather the required documentation. Local applications may require proof of trade experience, prior licenses, examination results, business registration, insurance, bonding, identification, employment documentation, or a designated qualifying individual. Requirements can vary, so candidates should follow the local application instructions carefully.

Once the application requirements are understood, the candidate follows the exam approval or registration process used by that jurisdiction. Some local authorities recognize ICC contractor and trades exams, including the Kansas Standard Master Electrician exam. Others may have their own procedures or additional requirements. Candidates should confirm the exam code, exam title, approved references, passing score, testing provider, and required NEC edition before scheduling.

After passing the required examination, the candidate completes the local licensing process. This may include submitting the passing score report, paying license fees, providing insurance documentation, naming the qualifying person for a business license, or completing local registration. Once licensed, electricians and contractors must also follow renewal rules, continuing education requirements, permit rules, and local inspection requirements.

State Requirements

Kansas electrical licensing is local in structure. Cities and counties may regulate individual electricians and electrical contractors through local licensing offices, contractor licensing departments, building departments, or trade boards. Because of this, Kansas candidates should not assume that one jurisdiction’s approval automatically authorizes work in another jurisdiction.

Johnson County provides one example of Kansas local contractor licensing. Its Contractor Licensing program is responsible for issuing and tracking multiple types of construction contractor licenses and recognizes standard examinations as a method of demonstrating trade qualification and code knowledge. Sedgwick County and the City of Wichita also require master and journeyman trade certificates for electrical, mechanical, and plumbing trades in that area.

Local Kansas jurisdictions may adopt different NEC editions, use different exam providers, and set different documentation requirements. Some may require continuing education before renewal. Some may require business licensing in addition to an individual trade credential. Some may recognize a standard exam score, while others may require additional local steps.

For Kansas master electrician candidates, the safest study approach is to build strong NEC knowledge while also following the requirements of the exact local authority where the license will be issued.
