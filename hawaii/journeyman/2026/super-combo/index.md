## Licensing Steps

The Hawaii journey worker electrician licensing path begins with meeting the state’s minimum qualifications. For the Journey Worker Electrician license, Hawaii lists the requirement as at least five years, but not less than 10,000 hours, in residential or commercial wiring, plus satisfactory completion accepted by a University of Hawaii Community College of 240 hours of electrical academic coursework.

A practical path begins with gaining qualifying supervised electrical experience and completing the required electrical academic coursework. Applicants should keep detailed records of work experience, supervision, dates, employers, and coursework completion. Hawaii requires experience verification documentation as part of the application process.

After the applicant gathers the required documentation, the applicant submits the license application to the Hawaii Board of Electricians and Plumbers through the Professional and Vocational Licensing Division. The Board reviews applications and determines whether the applicant meets the experience and coursework requirements. A complete application and required fees must be received according to the Board’s filing deadlines.

After the Board approves the application, the candidate receives authorization to test. PSI then provides examination scheduling instructions. The candidate schedules the Hawaii Journey Worker Electrician examination, follows PSI identification and test-day rules, and takes the examination through the PSI testing process.

After passing the examination and meeting the Board’s licensing requirements, the candidate completes the remaining licensing process through Hawaii DCCA. The electrician license authorizes the licensee to perform work within the scope of that classification. It does not authorize the holder to contract for electrical work; contracting requires the appropriate contractor license under Hawaii contractor licensing law.

## State Requirements

Hawaii electrician licensing is regulated by the Department of Commerce and Consumer Affairs, Professional and Vocational Licensing Division, Board of Electricians and Plumbers. The Board issues electrician and plumber licenses under Hawaii law and administrative rules.

Hawaii’s Journey Worker Electrician license classification is identified by the license type code EJ. To qualify for the examination, an applicant must meet the age, experience, and coursework requirements in effect at the time of application. The current listed Journey Worker Electrician requirement includes five years, but not less than 10,000 hours, of residential or commercial wiring experience, along with 240 hours of electrical academic coursework accepted by a University of Hawaii Community College offering an appropriate program of study.

Hawaii also has other electrical license classifications, including Journey Worker Specialty Electrician, Supervising Electrician, Supervising Specialty Electrician, Maintenance Electrician, Journey Worker Industrial Electrician, and Supervising Industrial Electrician. Each classification has its own experience and coursework requirements. Students should make sure they are preparing for the correct license classification before applying or scheduling an examination.

The Hawaii DCCA application materials state that Hawaii does not reciprocate with any jurisdiction. Applicants must meet the requirements in effect at the time of application, be qualified by the Board of Electricians and Plumbers, and attain a passing score on Hawaii’s licensure examination.
