Exam Details

Hawaii electrician licensing is regulated by the Hawaii Board of Electricians and Plumbers through the Professional and Vocational Licensing Division. Electrical licensing examinations are administered through PSI. Candidates must first receive eligibility approval before scheduling the required examination.

The Hawaii Supervising Electrician examination is a common advanced-level exam for electricians seeking to direct and supervise electrical work and perform electrical work. The Supervising Electrician exam has 70 questions, a minimum passing score of 70%, and a time allowance of 180 minutes.

The Hawaii Supervising Electrician exam content outline includes general electrical knowledge, services, feeders, and branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special occupancies, conditions, and equipment, electrical power, motors, low voltage and communication circuits, lighting, illuminated signs, safety information, and overcurrent protection.

The Hawaii Journey Worker Electrician examination also has 70 questions, a minimum passing score of 70%, and a time allowance of 180 minutes. The journey worker exam content includes general electrical knowledge, services, feeders, and branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special occupancies, conditions, and equipment, electrical power, motors, equipment for general use, low voltage and communication circuits, and safety information.

Hawaii also has specialty, maintenance, and industrial electrician exam classifications. The correct exam depends on the license classification the applicant is pursuing. Candidates studying with this Super Combo should match their study plan to the Hawaii license type they intend to obtain, with special attention to supervising electrician-level content when preparing for advanced electrical licensing.

Because the supervising electrician exam includes both broad code topics and applied trade knowledge, candidates should prepare for more than one type of question. Some questions may involve direct code lookup. Others may require electrical theory, calculations, installation knowledge, safety judgment, or familiarity with common electrical systems. A balanced study plan should include reading, calculations, code navigation, and timed practice.

Open Book Test

The Hawaii electrical licensing examinations are open book. For the Supervising Electrician exam, the examination center provides the allowed reference material. The official exam reference listed for the Supervising Electrician examination is the National Electrical Code edition identified in the current PSI candidate information bulletin.

The exam-room reference is provided by the examination center. Materials not allowed in the examination center include references identified by the bulletin as not permitted for use during the exam. Candidates should follow PSI testing rules and use only the materials allowed for their specific examination classification.

Open book does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates still need to know how to read the question, identify the correct subject area, locate the proper code rule, interpret the wording, and answer within the time allowed. The most successful open-book preparation includes repeated code lookup practice and careful reading of NEC language.

The tabs help candidates become more familiar with the structure of the NEC during preparation. Licensing Steps

Hawaii electrician candidates begin through the Hawaii Board of Electricians and Plumbers. The applicant must select the proper license classification, complete the required application, submit supporting documentation, and receive approval before testing. Once approved, the candidate receives eligibility information and schedules the exam through PSI.

A practical licensing path begins with identifying the correct Hawaii electrician classification. Candidates should determine whether they are pursuing Journey Worker Electrician, Supervising Electrician, Journey Worker Specialty Electrician, Supervising Specialty Electrician, Maintenance Electrician, Journey Worker Industrial Electrician, or Supervising Industrial Electrician licensure. Each classification has its own scope and eligibility requirements.

After selecting the correct classification, applicants submit the application and required documentation to the Board. Once the Board determines that the applicant is eligible, PSI provides examination scheduling instructions. Candidates then schedule the exam, prepare using the exam content outline, arrive with required identification, and complete the exam according to PSI rules.

Passing the examination is an important step, but licensing is completed through the state licensing process. The Board determines whether the license is issued based on the applicant’s qualifications, application file, examination result, and compliance with Hawaii requirements.

State Requirements

Hawaii requires electricians to be licensed for the electrical work covered by the state’s electrician licensing law. The Board of Electricians and Plumbers regulates electrician licensing classifications, including journey worker and supervising-level electrical licenses.

For the Supervising Electrician examination, Hawaii law provides that an applicant must have been registered with the Board as a journey worker electrician for at least four years or have equivalent experience in the trade. For the Supervising Specialty Electrician examination, an applicant must have been registered as a journey worker specialty electrician for at least four years or have equivalent experience in the trade. For the Supervising Industrial Electrician examination, an applicant must have been registered as a journey worker industrial electrician for at least four years or have equivalent experience in the trade.

The Supervising Electrician classification is important for electricians who are moving beyond journey worker-level performance and into a role that includes directing and supervising electrical work. Candidates preparing for this level should be ready for broad NEC content, electrical theory, installation requirements, system knowledge, and safety rules.

State requirements are controlled by Hawaii law, Board procedures, application rules, and the current exam bulletin. Exam preparation materials help candidates study, but the state determines eligibility, exam approval, and license issuance.
