## Exam Details

Hawaii electrician licensing is handled by the Hawaii Board of Electricians and Plumbers through the Department of Commerce and Consumer Affairs, Professional and Vocational Licensing Division. PSI administers the Hawaii electrician examination program. Candidates are not allowed to register for an exam until the Board approves the application and sends the candidate a letter of approval.

The Hawaii Supervising Electrician examination includes 70 questions, requires a minimum passing score of 70%, and allows 180 minutes of testing time. The examination may also include a small number of experimental questions that do not count toward the candidate’s score. The candidate’s score is displayed on screen at the end of the examination, and a score report is provided at the test site.

The Supervising Electrician content outline includes General Electrical Knowledge, Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits, Grounding and Bonding, Conductors and Cables, Raceways and Boxes, Special Occupancies, Conditions, and Equipment, Electrical Power, Motors, Low Voltage and Communication Circuits, Lighting, Illuminated Signs, Safety Information, and Overcurrent Protection.

Hawaii also licenses other electrical classifications, including Journey Worker Electrician, Journey Worker Specialty Electrician, Maintenance Electrician, Journey Worker Industrial Electrician, Supervising Industrial Electrician, and Supervising Specialty Electrician. Candidates should select the examination that matches the license classification they are applying for.

For candidates pursuing broader business authority, Hawaii electrical contracting is handled separately through the contractor licensing structure. A supervising electrician license is an individual license. Operating an electrical contracting business may require a separate contractor license classification and additional business-related licensing steps.

## Open Book Test

The Hawaii Supervising Electrician exam is an open-book exam. The examination center provides the reference material used during the exam. The PSI candidate bulletin lists the National Electrical Code, 2020 edition, as the reference material provided by the examination center for the Supervising Electrician exam.

Candidates may not bring unauthorized reference materials into the examination room. The bulletin identifies several materials as not allowed in the examination center, including other listed electrical references and safety publications. Candidates should follow all PSI test-center rules for identification, calculators, personal items, scratch paper, and examination conduct.

Open book does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates still need to know how to read questions carefully, recognize the subject being tested, locate information efficiently, and apply the correct rule. Because the test center provides the reference book, candidates should focus their preparation on understanding NEC organization rather than relying on personal notes or custom exam-room materials.

## Licensing Steps

Hawaii Supervising Electrician candidates begin by reviewing the licensing requirements established by the Hawaii Board of Electricians and Plumbers. Candidates must apply through the Board and receive approval before scheduling the examination with PSI. The Board’s application process requires the candidate to submit the proper application form, fee, and required supporting documentation.

To be eligible for the Supervising Electrician examination, Hawaii law requires the applicant to have been registered with the Board as a journey worker electrician for at least four years or to have equivalent experience in the trade. Candidates should document experience carefully and follow the Board’s instructions for application materials and verification.

After the Board approves the candidate, the candidate receives eligibility information and may schedule the examination with PSI. The PSI examination fee is listed in the candidate bulletin, and the candidate’s eligibility period is valid for two years. Candidates should schedule the exam under the same legal name that appears on their government-issued identification.

On exam day, candidates should arrive at least 30 minutes before the appointment. Candidates must bring valid identification that matches the registered name. PSI provides an online calculator during the exam, and candidates who bring their own calculator must use one that meets PSI’s calculator rules. Scratch paper and a pencil are provided by the test center and must be returned to the proctor at the end of the examination.

After passing the examination, candidates must complete any remaining licensing steps required by the Board. Candidates should keep copies of application forms, approval notices, score reports, and licensing correspondence for their records.

## State Requirements

Hawaii requires individuals who perform or supervise electrical work to hold the appropriate license. A person who directs or supervises electrical work in the state is required to have a supervising electrician’s license. A person who performs electrical work is required to have a journey worker electrician’s license unless the person is working under a permitted exception and proper supervision.

Hawaii’s electrical licensing structure includes multiple electrician categories. The Supervising Electrician license is the supervising-level license for directing and supervising electrical work while also allowing the license holder to perform electrical work. Specialty and industrial classifications have separate license paths and separate examination outlines.

For the journey worker electrician path, Hawaii requires qualifying work experience and electrical academic coursework. The supervising electrician path builds on journey worker-level registration or equivalent experience. Candidates should follow the license classification that matches their work history and intended scope of work.

Electrical contracting in Hawaii may require a separate contractor license. Individual electrician licensing through the Board of Electricians and Plumbers is not the same as business licensing for electrical contracting. Candidates who plan to operate a contracting business should review the contractor licensing requirements that apply to electrical contractors in Hawaii.
