Exam Details

The Montana Journeyman Electrician examination is administered through PSI for the Montana State Electrical Board. Only applicants approved by the Montana State Electrical Board are allowed to register for the examination. Approved applicants receive admission information and may schedule the exam through PSI.

The Montana Journeyman Electrician examination has 60 questions. The required passing score is 75% correct, and the time allowed is 180 minutes. The examination is administered by computer and is designed to test the candidate’s knowledge and technical application skills as they relate to journeyman-level electrical work.

The Montana Journeyman Electrician examination is based on National Electrical Code knowledge and general trade knowledge and theory. The exam also includes electrical concepts such as Ohm’s Law and the layout and practical installation of electrical circuits. Candidates should be prepared for questions that require both code lookup and applied electrical understanding.

Current Montana exam information states that exams are based on the 2020 NEC. This Super Combo is based on the 2026 National Electrical Code for candidates who want to study with 2026 NEC materials, strengthen code navigation, and prepare with current code-based learning resources. Candidates should follow the exam admission letter and Montana State Electrical Board instructions for the code edition and references required at the time they test.

The exam may include questions connected to practical electrical installation, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors, cables, raceways, boxes, overcurrent protection, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, safety, and electrical theory. A strong candidate should study the code book and also practice applying the code to real exam-style questions.

Open Book Test

The Montana Journeyman Electrician examination is open book. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references to the examination center. The official exam information for the Montana Journeyman Electrician exam lists the National Electrical Code edition required for the exam and the Montana Building Codes Amendments as allowed reference material.

Montana’s exam-room reference rules are strict. Reference materials must be unmarked. No highlighting, underlining, writing, or indexing may exist in the reference materials. Candidates are not allowed to write, highlight, underline, or index references during the examination. Additional papers, whether loose or attached, are not permitted with approved references.

The code book may have factory markings or highlights with factory tabs only when permitted under the listed exam reference rules. Candidates should prepare their materials carefully before exam day and make sure their references match the Montana State Electrical Board and PSI requirements for the exam session.

Open book does not mean the exam is simple. Candidates still need to know how to read the question, identify the topic, find the correct code section, understand the rule, and answer within the time limit. The included National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs is useful during study because it helps candidates practice moving through the code book and becoming familiar with the NEC structure before the examination.

Licensing Steps

Montana journeyman electrician licensing is handled by the Montana State Electrical Board through the Department of Labor and Industry. Candidates must meet the licensing qualifications, apply through the state process, receive approval to test, pass the required examination, and complete the remaining licensing steps required by the Board.

A practical path begins with meeting the experience or training requirement for journeyman electrician licensure. Montana law allows an applicant for a journeyman electrician license to qualify by completing an approved apprenticeship program or a substantially equivalent program in the electrical trade. An applicant may also qualify by documenting 8,000 hours of legally obtained practical experience in wiring for, installing, and repairing electrical apparatus and equipment for light, heat, and power.

Montana law also provides a pathway based on electrical maintenance experience. Under that path, the applicant must document at least 20,000 hours in the electrical maintenance field, accompanied by written certification from the employer that the applicant is qualified to take the examination and has attained the required maintenance experience. A minimum of 8,000 of those hours must be practical experience.

After meeting the qualification path, the applicant applies to the Montana State Electrical Board. Applications for examination must be approved before the candidate may register for the exam. Once approved, the candidate receives an admission letter and schedules the exam through PSI.

After passing the examination, the candidate must complete the state licensing process. Passing the exam is an important step, but the license is issued through the Board only after the applicant satisfies the applicable state requirements and completes the required process.

State Requirements

The Montana State Electrical Board regulates electrician licensing in Montana. The Board issues electrical licenses and provides examination approval for applicants who meet the requirements for their license classification. Montana license categories include journeyman electrician, residential electrician, master electrician, and electrical contractor licensing.

For journeyman electrician candidates, Montana requires evidence of an approved apprenticeship or equivalent trade program, 8,000 hours of legally obtained practical experience, or the qualifying maintenance experience pathway described under Montana law. The journeyman examination must include at least 60 questions and is designed to test knowledge and technical application skills in the National Electrical Code, board rules, and applicable laws.

Montana journeyman electricians must also complete continuing education for renewal. The current Montana journeyman electrician information lists 16 hours of Board-approved courses per renewal cycle, with a minimum of 8 hours on code update. The Board accepts continuing education from reciprocal states.

Montana also issues electrical contractor licenses. An electrical contractor applicant must provide proof of compliance with workers’ compensation and unemployment laws and must identify a full-time Montana licensed journeyman or master electrician responsible for all work performed in Montana.

Because licensing is controlled by the Montana State Electrical Board, candidates should follow the state’s application instructions and exam admission rules for their license classification. Study materials help candidates prepare, but the Board determines eligibility, examination approval, license issuance, renewal compliance, and enforcement of licensing requirements.

The goal is to recognize the topic quickly and move to the correct section without wasting time. A candidate who understands how the NEC is organized is better prepared for an open-book exam.

Services, feeders, and branch circuits should receive careful attention. Candidates should review service conductors, service disconnects, grounding at services, feeder sizing, branch circuit ratings, required outlets, protection requirements, and load calculations. These topics connect code knowledge with practical installation skill.

Grounding and bonding are also important areas for journeyman exam preparation. Candidates should review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, raceway bonding, panel bonding, grounded conductors, and related safety requirements. Grounding and bonding questions often require careful reading because similar terms can lead to different code requirements.

Wiring methods and materials should be studied consistently. Candidates should review conductors, cables, raceways, boxes, fittings, supports, conductor ampacity, insulation types, flexible wiring methods, underground wiring, and installation conditions. These topics appear frequently in electrical work and are common areas of exam preparation.

Equipment for general use should also be part of the study plan. Candidates should review panelboards, switchboards, devices, receptacles, luminaires, appliances, disconnects, motors, transformers, cabinets, and related installation requirements. Many questions in this area combine practical trade knowledge with NEC rules.

Flash cards are useful for shorter study sessions. Candidates can use them to review formulas, definitions, electrical theory, safety concepts, NEC topics, and trade terms before work, after work, during breaks, or between longer study blocks. Flash cards work best when paired with deeper reading, calculation drills, and code lookup practice.

A strong study plan should include scheduled reading, NEC navigation, calculation drills, timed practice, and repeated review of weaker topics. Candidates should avoid studying only familiar material. Journeyman electrician preparation rewards steady practice, careful reading, and the ability to apply electrical knowledge under exam conditions.
