The Montana Journeyman Electrician examination is administered through PSI for the Montana State Electrical Board. Candidates must apply with the Board and receive approval before scheduling the examination. Once approved, the candidate receives an admission letter, and the approval information is sent to PSI so the candidate can schedule the exam.

The Montana journeyman electrician examination must include at least 60 questions designed to test the applicant’s knowledge and technical application skills in the National Electrical Code, board rules, and applicable Montana laws. Licensing Steps

Montana journeyman electrician candidates begin by meeting the state’s qualification requirements for the license. The applicant may qualify through completion of an approved inside wireman apprenticeship program in the electrical trade or through completion of an appropriate training program conducted by a bona fide union or trade association.

Montana also recognizes experience-based qualification paths. A journeyman applicant may qualify with at least 8,000 hours of practical experience in the electrical trade, or through electrical maintenance experience when the applicant has at least 20,000 hours in the electrical maintenance field and at least 8,000 of those hours are practical experience.

After meeting the qualification requirements, the candidate applies to the Montana State Electrical Board for approval to test. The application process requires the candidate to submit the proper forms and supporting documentation. Only applicants approved by the Board may register for the examination.

Once approved, the candidate receives an admission letter. The approval is also submitted to PSI, and the candidate may schedule the examination through PSI. Candidates should review the current exam information sheet, admission instructions, testing procedures, identification requirements, reference rules, score procedures, and rescheduling rules before exam day.

After passing the examination, the candidate completes the remaining licensing requirements through the Montana State Electrical Board. Passing the exam is an important step, but the licensing process is not complete until the Board’s requirements are satisfied and the license is issued.

State Requirements

Montana requires electricians to be licensed through the Montana State Electrical Board. The Board issues statewide electrical licenses, including journeyman electrician, residential electrician, master electrician, and related license categories. This statewide licensing structure makes Montana different from states where journeyman licensing is handled only at the local level.

To qualify for a Montana journeyman electrician license by examination, an applicant must meet the Board’s education or experience requirements. Qualification may include completion of an approved inside wireman apprenticeship program, completion of an appropriate training program conducted by a bona fide union or trade association, or documented practical experience that satisfies Montana’s requirements.

The Montana journeyman path includes at least 8,000 hours of practical electrical experience. Montana also provides a maintenance experience pathway requiring at least 20,000 hours in the electrical maintenance field with at least 8,000 hours of practical experience. Documentation must support the applicant’s eligibility before the Board approves the candidate for examination.

The journeyman examination is designed to test knowledge and technical application skills in the National Electrical Code, Board rules, and applicable laws. Candidates should prepare for both code-based electrical knowledge and state-specific rule content. A strong study plan should include NEC review, practical trade review, calculation practice, and review of Montana licensing rules connected to electrical work.

A Montana journeyman electrician license is different from a master electrician license or electrical contractor license. The journeyman license supports individual electrical work within the state’s licensing framework. Master and contractor licensing involve separate qualifications and higher levels of responsibility. Candidates should apply for the credential that matches their current experience and career goal.
