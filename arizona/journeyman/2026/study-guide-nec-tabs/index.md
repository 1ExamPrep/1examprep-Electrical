## Exam Details

The Arizona Registrar of Contractors licenses and regulates residential and commercial contractors in Arizona. For electrical contracting, Arizona uses license classifications rather than a single statewide individual journeyman electrician license. Electrical contractor classifications include residential electrical, commercial electrical, and dual electrical classifications. The appropriate classification depends on the type of electrical work being performed and the scope of the contracting business.

The Arizona Registrar of Contractors has contracted with PSI to conduct its trade examination program. PSI provides trade examinations through computer examination centers in Arizona and in other areas of the United States, and Arizona applicants may also have remote proctored options for certain exams. The PSI Candidate Information Bulletin explains that applicants use the Arizona Registrar of Contractors license classification requirements to determine which examination or examinations apply to the classification they are seeking.

Arizona contractor applicants may be required to complete the Arizona Statutes and Rules Exam and, depending on the classification, a trade-specific examination. For electrical classifications, applicants should confirm whether they are pursuing a residential electrical, commercial electrical, or dual electrical classification. Arizona applicants for CR-11 Electrical, C-11 Electrical, and R-11 Electrical classifications may have an option to use a NASCLA electrical examination in lieu of the AZ ROC trade examination, depending on the classification and current ROC requirements.

The PSI Candidate Information Bulletin states that trade-specific content outlines identify the time limit, subject areas, number of items in each subject area, and references for the specific examination. Electrical candidates should always match their study materials to the classification and exam path they are taking. A candidate preparing for residential electrical work may have a different exam path from a candidate preparing for commercial or dual electrical contracting.

This combo is based on the 2026 National Electrical Code and is intended to help candidates build electrical code familiarity using the 2026 NEC. The NEC is central to electrical exam study because many electrical questions require understanding of safe installation practices, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, conductor sizing, service requirements, and code-based calculations.

## Closed Book Test

Arizona PSI examination security rules prohibit outside references and resources during the exam. The PSI Candidate Information Bulletin also lists reading material, notebooks, paper, electronic devices, and other personal items as prohibited items. Candidates are provided scratch paper and a pencil at the test center, and the bulletin states that candidates may use a basic, silent, nonprinting, nonprogrammable, nonscientific calculator in the examination center.

A closed book exam places greater importance on preparation before test day. Candidates should not rely on being able to search through a code book during the actual exam. Instead, study time should be used to understand the layout of the NEC, reinforce code concepts, and practice applying rules until the material becomes more familiar. Even when a code book is not used in the exam room, the NEC remains one of the most important preparation tools because it teaches the rules and language that electrical exams are built around.

The tabbed 2026 NEC in this combo helps during preparation by making study sessions more organized. Candidates can use the tabs to move between important sections, return to key tables, and practice identifying where code rules are located. The goal is not to memorize every sentence of the NEC. The goal is to understand the structure, know the common subject areas, and become comfortable applying code-based thinking to electrical questions.

Closed book preparation should include repeated review, self-testing, calculation practice, and careful study of missed questions. When you miss a question, return to the related NEC section during study and identify why the correct answer applies. That habit builds deeper understanding and helps reduce confusion when similar topics appear in a different format.

## Licensing Steps

The Arizona contractor licensing process begins by choosing the correct license classification. Electrical applicants should determine whether the work falls under residential electrical, commercial electrical, or dual electrical contracting. The classification matters because it affects the scope of work and the examination requirements for the qualifying party.

After choosing the classification, applicants review the Arizona Registrar of Contractors license classification requirements to determine the required exams. New license applicants who were not recently listed as a qualifying party on another Arizona contractor license may be required to complete the Arizona Statutes and Rules Exam. Depending on the electrical classification, the qualifying party may also need to pass a trade-specific exam or submit an accepted exam waiver or NASCLA result where allowed.

The PSI Candidate Information Bulletin states that exam requirements must be completed by the person who will act as the qualifying party. Once the required exams are completed, the applicant submits the completed license application and original score report to the Arizona Registrar of Contractors for processing within the required period stated by the testing bulletin.

The Arizona Registrar of Contractors license checklist also identifies additional application items, including forming the legal entity when applicable, choosing the classification, passing exams or obtaining an approved waiver, submitting background checks for required persons, providing government-issued identification, completing the required bond, paying fees, and completing the license application through the ROC process.

Electrical candidates should keep their documentation organized throughout the process. Exam scores, qualifying party information, business entity records, background check materials, bond documents, identification, and application forms should be prepared carefully. Missing or incorrect information can delay application review.
