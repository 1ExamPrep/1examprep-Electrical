Exam Details

The Arizona Registrar of Contractors uses PSI for trade-specific contractor examinations. Arizona applicants may be required to complete the Arizona Statutes and Rules Exam and a trade-specific examination depending on the license classification. Electrical candidates commonly prepare for classifications such as R-11 Electrical Residential, C-11 Electrical Commercial, or CR-11 Electrical Dual. Each classification has its own scope, requirements, and content outline.

The PSI candidate information for Arizona contractor licensing explains that trade-specific content outlines identify the examination time limit, subject areas, number of items in each area, and reference materials. Candidates should study the content outline connected to the exact Arizona electrical classification they are pursuing. This is important because residential, commercial, and dual classifications may have different scopes and exam requirements.

The Arizona R-11 Electrical Residential content outline lists 110 questions, a 70% minimum passing score, and 240 minutes of testing time. The listed subject areas include general electrical knowledge, service feeders and branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special occupancies and equipment, electrical devices, motors, low voltage, lighting, fire detection and alarm systems, safety, overcurrent protection, and photovoltaics.

Open Book Test

Arizona ROC electrical trade exams administered through PSI use approved reference materials. The Arizona R-11 Electrical Residential content outline identifies reference materials allowed in the examination center and states that candidates are responsible for bringing their own references. This makes reference familiarity a key part of preparation.

Approved references may be highlighted, underlined, annotated, and indexed before the examination session, but candidates may not write, highlight, underline, or index in the references during the examination. The PSI reference rules also state that references may not include additional loose or attached papers. Permanent tabs are allowed, while temporary removable tabs are not allowed.

Open-book testing does not remove the need for strong preparation. A candidate who is unfamiliar with the code book may lose valuable time searching for basic information. A candidate who studies with the code book regularly can move faster between articles, tables, definitions, and exceptions. The included National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs is designed to support this kind of code-navigation practice.

Use the NEC throughout your study sessions. When answering a practice question, locate the code rule that supports the answer. Read the entire section, check related definitions, review table notes, and look for exceptions. This habit helps candidates develop the practical code skills needed for open-book electrical exams.

Licensing Steps

Arizona electrical licensing is handled through the Arizona Registrar of Contractors rather than through a separate statewide journeyman electrician licensing board. The state licenses contracting businesses and qualifying parties for the appropriate contractor classification. Electrical candidates who want to operate as contractors or serve as qualifying parties should follow the ROC licensing path for the applicable classification.

The process begins by identifying the correct license classification. Residential electrical work is associated with the R-11 Electrical classification. Commercial electrical work is associated with the C-11 Electrical classification. Dual electrical work is associated with the CR-11 Electrical classification. The selected classification determines the scope of work, required examinations, and application path.

Arizona ROC applicants must complete the examination requirements for the license classification unless an approved waiver applies. The PSI candidate information explains that exam requirements must be completed by the person who will act as the qualifying party. The qualifying party is the individual whose experience and examination record support the license application.

After completing the required examination or examinations, applicants submit the completed license application and original score report to the Arizona Registrar of Contractors for processing within the required time period. Licensing applications are not accepted until the examination requirements have been completed. Candidates should keep copies of their score reports, experience records, and application materials.

State Requirements

Arizona regulates electrical contracting through the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. The state’s contractor licensing structure focuses on business licensing and qualifying parties rather than issuing one universal statewide journeyman electrician license. Candidates working toward electrical contractor licensing should identify the correct ROC classification and follow the current requirements for that classification.

Electrical contractor classifications are based on the type of work being performed. Residential electrical contractors, commercial electrical contractors, and dual electrical contractors have different license classifications. The license classification determines the authorized scope of work and the trade exam content outline that applies to the qualifying party.

For Arizona ROC licensing, the qualifying party is central to the application process. This individual must meet the experience and examination requirements for the license classification. The qualifying party is responsible for demonstrating the trade knowledge and experience needed to support the license application.

The Arizona ROC candidate information explains that applicants should use the License Classification Requirements form to determine which examination or examinations are required. Trade exams are scheduled through PSI, and certain applicants may also need to complete the Arizona Statutes and Rules Exam. Exam waiver conditions may apply in specific circumstances, but those are handled through the ROC licensing process.

Because Arizona’s system is contractor-classification based, candidates should avoid assuming that a “journeyman electrician license” means the same thing in Arizona that it means in another state. In Arizona, journeyman-level preparation is valuable for trade competency, field advancement, and electrical contractor exam readiness, but the state licensing path is organized around ROC contractor classifications.
