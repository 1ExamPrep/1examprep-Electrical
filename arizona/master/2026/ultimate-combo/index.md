## Exam Details

Arizona contractor license applicants use the Arizona Registrar of Contractors licensing process. The trade examination program is administered through PSI for Arizona residential and commercial contractor license examinations. Depending on the license classification, the qualifying party may be required to complete the Arizona Statutes and Rules Exam and a trade-specific electrical exam.

For Arizona electrical contractor classifications, applicants should determine the exact exam requirement based on the license classification they are applying for. The Arizona Registrar of Contractors issues separate classifications for residential work, commercial work, and dual residential/commercial work. Electrical candidates may encounter R-11 Electrical Residential, C-11 Electrical Commercial, or CR-11 Electrical Dual classification requirements.

The Arizona R-11 Electrical Residential content outline lists 110 questions, a 70% minimum passing score, and 240 minutes of testing time. The content outline includes general electrical knowledge, service feeders and branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special occupancies and equipment, electrical devices, motors, low voltage, lighting, fire detection and alarm systems, safety, overcurrent protection, and photovoltaics.

The listed subject areas include general electrical knowledge, service feeders and branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special occupancies and equipment, devices, motors, low voltage, lighting, illuminated signs, fire detection and alarm systems, safety, overcurrent protection, and photovoltaics.

Applicants applying for certain Arizona electrical classifications may have a choice between an Arizona ROC electrical trade exam and an applicable NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination. Candidates should match their study plan to the exam required for their chosen classification. ## Open Book Test

The Arizona ROC electrical trade exams are open book examinations with specific reference rules. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references to the examination center. Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, annotated, and indexed before the examination session. Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, or index in their references during the exam.

Arizona PSI electrical exam reference rules also state that references may be tabbed or indexed with permanent tabs only. Temporary tabs, including removable notes or tabs that can be removed without tearing the page, are not allowed. Candidates should prepare their references before exam day and practice using them under timed conditions.

A silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator is allowed in the examination center. For electrical exams, this makes calculation practice an important part of preparation. Candidates should be able to work through formulas, conductor sizing concepts, service and feeder questions, motor-related problems, load calculations, and other math-based questions without losing too much exam time.

Open book does not mean easy. Many candidates struggle because they know the material generally but cannot find the correct section quickly enough during the exam. ## Licensing Steps

The Arizona electrical contractor licensing process begins by identifying the correct license classification. Candidates should determine whether the intended work is residential, commercial, or both. Arizona electrical contractor classifications include residential electrical work, commercial electrical work, and dual classifications that may cover both areas depending on the license selected.

After identifying the correct classification, the applicant must determine the qualifying party. Arizona requires exam requirements to be completed by the person who will act as the qualifying party for the contractor license. The qualifying party is the individual connected to the license who demonstrates the required trade knowledge and qualifications for the classification.

Applicants may be required to complete the Arizona Statutes and Rules Exam. The Arizona Statutes and Rules Exam is a computer-based course and exam provided through the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Trade-specific exams are scheduled through PSI when required for the selected classification.

Once the required exams have been completed, the applicant submits the contractor license application and required documentation to the Arizona Registrar of Contractors for processing. The Arizona PSI bulletin states that completed license applications and original score reports must be submitted within two years from the date of passing the examination.

## State Requirements

Arizona electrical contractor licensing is regulated by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. The state licenses contracting businesses by classification rather than issuing a separate statewide individual master electrician credential. This means the business license classification, qualifying party requirements, and exam requirements are central to the licensing path.

The R-11 Electrical Residential classification applies to residential electrical work. The C-11 Electrical Commercial classification applies to commercial electrical work. The CR-11 Electrical Dual classification is associated with both residential and commercial electrical work. Candidates should select the classification that matches the scope of work they plan to contract for in Arizona.

The C-11 Electrical Commercial scope includes installation, alteration, and repair of wiring, related electrical material, and equipment used in generating, transmitting, or utilizing electrical energy less than 600 volts. It also includes specific outside, overhead, underground, and building wiring work less than 600 volts within the scope described for the classification.

Arizona candidates should be ready to study both electrical trade knowledge and state licensing requirements.
