## Exam Details

The Arizona Registrar of Contractors uses PSI for trade-specific contractor examinations. Arizona electrical contractor applicants may be required to complete the Arizona Statutes and Rules Exam and a trade-specific electrical examination, depending on the license classification and the applicant’s qualifying party status. ## Open Book Test

Arizona electrical trade examinations are open book for approved reference materials. PSI’s Arizona electrical exam information lists reference materials allowed in the examination center and states that candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references. The examination may also contain questions based on trade knowledge or general industry practices.

PSI’s Arizona electrical exam rules allow approved references to be highlighted, underlined, annotated, and indexed before the exam session. Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, or index in their references during the examination. References may be tabbed or indexed with permanent tabs only. Permanent tabs are defined as tabs that would tear the page if removed. Temporary tabs, including Post-it style tabs or removable tabs, are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins.

These rules make code-book organization an important part of preparation. Students who practice with a tabbed code book can become more comfortable moving through articles, tables, definitions, and key code sections. During study, candidates should practice locating information without depending only on memory. A strong routine includes reading the question carefully, identifying the subject area, finding the applicable code section, and confirming that the selected answer matches the exact conditions described in the question.

PSI also allows a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator in the examination center. Exam references must match PSI’s approved reference rules for the scheduled exam. For code questions, PSI states that the examination is based only on the edition of the code book listed for that exam. Candidates should prepare with the reference materials required for their specific Arizona exam classification and use this 2026 NEC combo as a structured study resource for current code review.

## Licensing Steps

The Arizona electrical licensing process begins with choosing the correct license classification. Arizona electrical work is licensed through contractor classifications such as residential electrical, commercial electrical, or dual electrical, depending on the scope of work. Applicants use the Arizona Registrar of Contractors license classification requirements to determine which exam or exams are required for the license they want to obtain.

The person who satisfies the examination requirements is the qualifying party for the license. PSI’s Arizona contractor bulletin states that examination requirements must be completed by the person who will act as the qualifying party. The qualifying party is the individual whose experience and exam completion support the license application.

Many applicants must complete the Arizona Statutes and Rules Exam. This is a computer-based course and exam provided and maintained by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. It covers Arizona statutes and rules that apply to contractor licensing. In addition to the Arizona Statutes and Rules Exam, applicants may also be required to pass a trade-specific electrical examination administered by PSI.

After completing the required examinations, the applicant submits the completed license application and original score report to the Arizona Registrar of Contractors for processing. PSI’s Arizona contractor bulletin states that licensing applications cannot be accepted until all examination requirements have been completed. The bulletin also states that the completed license application and original score report must be submitted within two years from the date of passing the examination.

Arizona candidates should build their study plan around the classification they are pursuing. A candidate preparing for commercial electrical work may focus heavily on commercial service, feeder, branch circuit, grounding, raceway, special equipment, and photovoltaic topics. A candidate preparing for residential electrical work may focus more on residential service calculations, dwelling unit wiring, grounding and bonding, branch circuits, devices, lighting, safety, and residential photovoltaic content.

## State Requirements

The Arizona Registrar of Contractors licenses and regulates contractors in Arizona, including electrical contractors. Electrical contracting work is tied to license classifications that define the scope of work the license holder may perform. The Arizona electrical classifications include residential electrical work, commercial electrical work, and dual electrical work, with specific scope descriptions attached to each classification.

The C-11 and CR-11 Electrical Commercial scope includes installation, alteration, and repair of wiring, electrical material, and equipment used in generating, transmitting, or utilizing electrical energy less than 600 volts. The scope also includes certain outside, overhead, underground, and building wiring work under the classification description.

The R-11 Electrical Residential scope includes installation and repair of electrical systems for residential work. Applicants pursuing residential electrical contracting should study the residential electrical content outline and prepare for the examination that applies to their selected path.

Applicants for some Arizona electrical classifications may have a choice between an Arizona ROC trade examination and a NASCLA electrical trade examination option. The available path depends on the classification and the current Arizona Registrar of Contractors requirements. Candidates should select the exam path that fits the license classification and work scope they intend to pursue.

Experience, qualifying party requirements, exam completion, application documents, bonds, and other licensing requirements are handled through the Arizona Registrar of Contractors.
