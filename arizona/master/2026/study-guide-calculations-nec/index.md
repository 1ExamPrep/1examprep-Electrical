## Exam Details

The Arizona Registrar of Contractors licenses and regulates contractors in Arizona. Electrical contractor applicants use AZ ROC license classification requirements to determine which examination or examinations apply to the license classification they are pursuing. The person serving as the qualifying party must complete the required examination requirements for the license application.

Arizona contractor applicants may be required to complete the Arizona Statutes and Rules Exam, also called the SRE. The SRE is a computer-based course and exam provided and maintained by AZ ROC. It teaches applicable Arizona statutes and rules and tests the applicant on that material during the training. In addition to the SRE, electrical contractor applicants may also be required to pass a trade-specific examination through PSI.

PSI conducts Arizona trade-specific contractor examinations for AZ ROC. The trade-specific content outlines identify the examination time limit, subject areas, number of items in each area, and reference materials for the specific exam. Arizona electrical classifications include residential and commercial electrical categories, and applicants should match their study plan to the classification they are pursuing.

For the AZ ROC R-11 Electrical Residential examination, PSI lists 110 questions, a 70 percent minimum passing score, and 240 minutes of allowed testing time. The content outline includes general electrical knowledge, service feeders and branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special occupancies and equipment, electrical devices, motors, low voltage, lighting, fire detection and alarm systems, safety, overcurrent protection, and photovoltaics.

For the AZ ROC C-11 Electrical Commercial examination, PSI also lists 110 questions, a 70 percent minimum passing score, and 240 minutes of allowed testing time. The commercial content outline includes general electrical knowledge, service feeders and branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special occupancies and equipment, devices, motors, low voltage, lighting, illuminated signs, fire detection and alarm systems, safety, overcurrent protection, and photovoltaics.

Arizona applicants pursuing C-11 or CR-11 Electrical Commercial work may have a choice between the NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors and the AZ ROC Electrical Commercial exam. Applicants pursuing R-11 Electrical Residential may have a choice between the NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Residential Electrical Contractors and the AZ ROC Electrical Residential exam. The exams are not identical, so candidates should prepare for the exam that matches their licensing path.

## Open Book Test

Arizona electrical trade examinations administered by PSI allow specified reference materials into the examination center. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references. Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, annotated, and indexed before the exam session. Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, or index references during the examination.

PSI allows permanent tabs in approved references. Temporary tabs, such as removable notes or tabs that can be removed without tearing the page, are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins. Candidates may use a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable, non-scientific calculator in the examination center.

Because the exam is open book with strict reference rules, students should practice using the code book before test day. Open book does not mean easy. Candidates still need to find answers quickly, understand the question, identify the correct code section, and avoid wasting time searching through unfamiliar material. A strong preparation plan includes repeated practice using the NEC index, table structure, definitions, article layout, and calculation rules.

## Licensing Steps

Arizona electrical contractor licensing begins with selecting the correct AZ ROC license classification. Electrical work may fall under residential, commercial, or dual classifications depending on the type of work the business intends to perform. The applicant must identify the proper classification and determine which examination requirements apply.

The qualifying party must complete the examination requirements for the license classification. Depending on the classification and the applicant’s circumstances, this may include the Arizona Statutes and Rules Exam, a trade-specific electrical examination, or an approved examination option such as a NASCLA electrical exam where accepted for that classification.

After the required examinations are completed, the applicant submits a completed license application to AZ ROC with the required documentation and original score report. Licensing applications are processed after examination requirements have been completed. AZ ROC’s candidate information states that the completed license application and original score report must be submitted within two years from the date of passing the examination.

Applicants should organize their records before applying. Contractor licensing may involve business information, qualifying party information, examination records, financial responsibility requirements, and any documents required by AZ ROC for the selected classification. Study materials help with exam preparation, while the applicant remains responsible for completing the state licensing process for the classification being pursued.

## State Requirements

Arizona electrical contracting is regulated by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. AZ ROC licenses and regulates residential and commercial contractors in the state. Electrical contractor applicants must follow the requirements for the license classification they are seeking, and the qualifying party must satisfy the examination requirements tied to that classification.

Arizona’s electrical contractor classifications include residential electrical work, commercial electrical work, and combined electrical classifications. The R-11 Electrical Residential scope covers installation and repair of electrical systems for residential electrical contracting. The C-11 Electrical Commercial scope covers installation, alteration, and repair of wiring, related electrical material, and equipment used in generating, transmitting, or utilizing electrical energy under 600 volts, including related overhead and underground electrical work described in the classification scope.

Applicants should understand that Arizona’s system is contractor-license focused. Individual electricians working in the field are not issued a separate statewide master electrician credential by AZ ROC. Instead, the contractor license authorizes contracting activity under the selected classification, and the qualifying party demonstrates the required knowledge and experience for the license.

Arizona contractor applicants may be required to pass the SRE and a trade-specific examination. Exam requirements depend on the license classification and the applicant’s history as a qualifying party. Some exam waiver conditions may exist under AZ ROC rules, but applicants should follow the requirements tied to their own classification and application status.
