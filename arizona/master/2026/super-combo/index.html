Exam Details

Arizona contractor licensing is administered through the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Electrical contractor applicants use the Registrar’s license classification requirements to determine which examinations are required for the license classification being pursued. Arizona electrical classifications include commercial electrical, residential electrical, and dual electrical contracting classifications.

For commercial electrical contractor preparation, the C-11 or CR-11 Electrical Commercial classification is especially relevant to advanced electrical study. The listed scope includes installation, alteration, and repair of wiring, related electrical material, and equipment used in generating, transmitting, or utilizing electrical energy under 600 volts, including specified overhead and underground electrical construction and wiring in or on buildings under the listed scope.

Arizona applicants applying for C-11 or CR-11 Electrical Commercial may have a choice of trade examinations. Arizona ROC exam information identifies the option to take either the NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors or the AZ ROC Electrical Commercial exam. Applicants should compare the current PSI candidate information bulletin and classification instructions before selecting the trade examination that fits the license application.

The AZ ROC Electrical Commercial exam is listed as 110 questions with a 240-minute time limit and a 70% minimum passing score. Its content outline includes General Electrical Knowledge, Service Feeders and Branch Circuits, Grounding and Bonding, Conductors and Cables, Raceways and Boxes, Special Occupancies and Equipment, Devices, Motors, Low Voltage, Lighting, Illuminated Signs, Fire Detection and Alarm Systems, Safety, Overcurrent Protection, and Photovoltaics.

The examination may contain questions based on the listed references, trade knowledge, or general industry practices. For code questions, the exam is based only on the code edition listed in the exam bulletin. Some Arizona PSI materials list NFPA 70, National Electrical Code 2017, for the AZ ROC Electrical Commercial exam. Open Book Test

Arizona electrical contractor trade examinations administered through PSI use open-book reference rules when approved references are listed for the specific examination. The AZ ROC Electrical Commercial exam reference instructions list approved materials allowed in the examination center and state that candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references to the examination center.

The AZ ROC Electrical Commercial reference instructions allow a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator in the examination center. Approved reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, annotated, and indexed before the examination session. Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, or index references during the examination session. Additional loose or attached papers are not permitted with approved references.

Arizona PSI reference rules allow permanent tabs only. Permanent tabs are defined as tabs that would tear the page if removed. Temporary tabs, including Post-it notes or other removable tabs, are not allowed and must be removed from the reference before the exam begins. If a reference is downloaded from the internet, it may be brought into the testing center only when properly bound, such as spiral bound or placed in a binder after hole punching.

Open-book electrical testing still requires serious preparation. A candidate who is unfamiliar with the National Electrical Code can lose valuable time searching through definitions, article sections, tables, exceptions, and related rules. A prepared candidate can move more efficiently through wiring methods, raceway requirements, box rules, grounding and bonding provisions, conductor requirements, service rules, feeder rules, branch-circuit requirements, motor provisions, special occupancy provisions, safety topics, photovoltaic provisions, fire alarm topics, and equipment-specific requirements.

Tabs can make study sessions more efficient by helping students learn the structure of the NEC and move between major articles. Licensing Steps

The Arizona advanced electrical path begins with identifying the correct contractor license classification. Electrical contractor applicants should determine whether the work aligns with a commercial electrical classification, residential electrical classification, or dual electrical classification. The Arizona Registrar of Contractors classification requirements identify which examination or examinations apply to each license classification.

Arizona contractor licensing generally requires a qualifying party. The qualifying party is the individual who meets the experience and examination requirements for the classification and qualifies the license. For an electrical contractor license, the qualifying party should have the trade knowledge and experience required for the electrical classification being pursued.

After selecting the classification, the applicant reviews the current Arizona ROC license application requirements. The process may include establishing the business entity, identifying the qualifying party, documenting experience, passing required examinations, completing the application, providing required financial and bonding information, and meeting any additional licensing requirements listed by the Registrar.

The candidate then schedules the required examination or examinations through the approved testing process. Depending on the classification, Arizona applicants may take the AZ ROC electrical trade exam or an accepted NASCLA electrical trade examination when allowed. Contractor applicants may also need to complete a statutes and rules or business-related examination, depending on the current ROC requirements for the classification.

After passing the required examination, the applicant completes the remaining license application steps through the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Passing the exam is an important step, but the applicant must still meet the Registrar’s licensing requirements and submit all required materials. A practical preparation path includes identifying the correct classification, reviewing the current ROC instructions, studying the exam bulletin, practicing NEC navigation, reviewing electrical calculations, using flash cards for repeated recall, passing the required exam, and completing the contractor license process.
