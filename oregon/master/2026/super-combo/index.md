## Exam Details

Oregon electrical individual licensing is handled through the Oregon Building Codes Division. Candidates applying for an electrical individual license complete the required application, work history, affidavit, and experience verification materials before receiving exam authorization. After the application is reviewed and approved, the division mails a letter of authorization with instructions for scheduling the licensing examination.

The Oregon General Supervising Electrician license is the master-level license commonly associated with the authority to design, plan, lay out work, sign permits, and supervise electrical service installation or alteration. Oregon’s license directory identifies the General Supervising Electrician examination as a 4-hour, two-section, open book exam with 52 code questions and 12 calculation questions. The license directory also states that approved exam material is provided to the candidate upon approval.

The General Supervising Electrician license carries a high level of responsibility. Oregon rules state that a general supervising electrician may direct, supervise, make, or control electrical installations; may design, plan, and lay out work for customers of the contractor with whom the supervising electrician is continuously employed; and is the only individual authorized to direct, supervise, or control the installation or alteration of an electrical service. A signing supervising electrician must also sign permits, ensure installations meet minimum safety standards, ensure proper electrical safety procedures are used, and ensure electricians have proper licenses for the work performed.

## Licensing Steps

A practical Oregon licensing path begins by confirming the correct license category. Oregon has multiple electrical individual license types, and candidates preparing for master-level work commonly focus on the General Supervising Electrician license. This license is tied to the ability to supervise, design, plan, lay out work, sign permits, and control electrical service installation or alteration under Oregon rules.

Oregon’s application process begins with the Electrical License Application. Candidates complete the work history, sign the applicant affidavit, identify a preferred exam location, and use the application checklist. Oregon also requires experience verification, and candidates submit one Electrical Experience Verification Form for each employer. Experience must be listed in the required work categories under Oregon Administrative Rule requirements.

For the General Supervising Electrician license, Oregon’s license directory lists two qualification paths. One path is to be currently licensed as an Oregon journeyman electrician and provide verification of an additional 8,000 hours of on-the-job journeyman experience. Another path is to meet the qualifications to be licensed as an Oregon journeyman electrician and provide verification of an additional 8,000 hours of on-the-job journeyman experience, with Oregon’s experience rules applying to that pathway.

After Oregon receives and approves the application, the division mails the candidate a letter of authorization that explains how to schedule the licensing exam. After the exam is completed, Oregon states that results are mailed no later than two weeks after the exam. If the candidate passes, the license is mailed.

## State Requirements

Oregon regulates electrical individual licensing through the Building Codes Division. The General Supervising Electrician license is a statewide electrical individual license that authorizes high-level supervising responsibilities. Oregon identifies the license description as the authority to design, plan, and lay out work; sign all permits; and act as the only individual authorized to direct, supervise, or control the installation or alteration of an electrical service.

Oregon rules place significant responsibility on the supervising electrician. A general signing supervising electrician must sign permits, ensure electrical installations meet minimum safety standards, ensure proper electrical safety procedures are used, ensure required labels and permits are used and signed, ensure electricians have proper licenses for the work performed, comply with corrective notices from the inspecting authority, and notify the division in writing within five days if the supervising electrician relationship with the electrical contractor ends.

The General Supervising Electrician license renews every three years on October 1. Oregon’s license directory identifies 24 total continuing education hours as required for the license. Candidates and license holders should follow the current Oregon Building Codes Division process for applications, renewals, continuing education, address updates, reciprocal licenses, and approved testing materials.

Oregon also has reciprocal license agreements for the General Supervising Electrician and General Journeyman Electrician licenses. Candidates pursuing reciprocity should use Oregon’s current reciprocal license process and provide the required documentation for the license involved.

For NEC study, start by learning the structure of the code book. Review article organization, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and cross-references. Practice moving from a question to the correct article and reading the surrounding language carefully. Many electrical exam questions are missed because a candidate finds a related section but overlooks a condition, exception, table note, or specific wording.

Candidates should understand the role of the supervising electrician, the importance of permit responsibility, the use of proper electrical licenses, safety compliance, corrective notices, and the relationship between the supervising electrician and the electrical contractor.

For calculations, write out each step. Electrical calculation mistakes often happen when a candidate skips a conversion, uses the wrong table, applies the wrong value, or confuses branch circuit, feeder, service, motor, transformer, raceway fill, box fill, or voltage drop requirements. For open book preparation, use the NEC actively during study. Practice locating sections, reading tables, and confirming answers in the code book. Then practice some questions without immediately opening the code book so you can build recognition. For flash card study, use short sessions throughout the week. Flash cards can help reinforce formulas, definitions, code concepts, and common electrical terms.
