## State Requirements

Oregon requires electricians to be licensed through the Building Codes Division. The General Journeyman Electrician license is a statewide individual electrical license that authorizes journeyman-level electrical work within the scope of the classification. Oregon’s statewide licensing structure makes it different from states where journeyman licensing is handled only by local jurisdictions.

The Oregon General Journeyman Electrician license is separate from a General Supervising Electrician license. A General Journeyman Electrician may perform electrical work within the scope of the license, while a General Supervising Electrician license is a higher-level credential connected to supervising work and qualifying certain electrical contracting activity. Candidates should apply for the credential that matches their experience, work role, and career goal.

Oregon’s standard journeyman path commonly includes **8,000 hours** of qualifying electrical experience and **576 hours** of related classroom instruction through an approved apprenticeship or equivalent accepted pathway. Candidates using experience or training from outside Oregon should follow the Building Codes Division’s current process for documentation review.

Oregon also uses the Oregon Electrical Specialty Code, which is based on the National Electrical Code with Oregon amendments. This means candidates should not study the NEC alone. Strong preparation should include NEC navigation, Oregon-specific electrical code requirements, Oregon laws, Oregon administrative rules, practical applications, and calculations.

License holders should also follow Oregon Building Codes Division requirements for renewal, continuing education, and license maintenance. Continuing education and code updates help electricians stay current with electrical code changes, Oregon amendments, state rules, and safe installation practices.
