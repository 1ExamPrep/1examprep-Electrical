Exam Details

The Oregon General Journeyman Electrician examination is administered through the Oregon Building Codes Division examination process. Oregon electrical examinations are prepared under the authority of the Electrical and Elevator Board and are tied to Oregon’s electrical licensing requirements. Candidates must apply for the proper electrical license classification and receive approval before taking the required examination.

The Oregon journeyman examination is commonly identified as a 52-question examination with a 3-hour time allowance. The passing score is 75%, which means candidates must answer 39 questions correctly on a 52-question exam. The exam includes code lookup and application questions drawn from the National Electrical Code, Oregon Electrical Specialty Code, Oregon Revised Statutes, and Oregon Administrative Rules.

Oregon’s journeyman-level electrical exam preparation should include both general code knowledge and Oregon-specific rules. Candidates should be prepared for questions related to National Electrical Code provisions, Oregon electrical licensing law, Oregon administrative rules, practical electrical applications, electrical theory, formulas, and calculations. A candidate who studies only the NEC without reviewing Oregon-specific rules may miss important exam content.

The General Journeyman Electrician license is different from Oregon’s Supervising Electrician license. Oregon uses supervising electrician classifications for higher-level supervisory authority. The journeyman license supports work under the supervision structure established by Oregon law and rule, while supervising electrician licenses involve additional requirements and responsibilities.

This Super Combo is based on the 2026 National Electrical Code for candidates who want current-code study materials, calculation support, and NEC navigation practice. Oregon exam references and adopted code editions are controlled by the Building Codes Division and the applicable exam guidelines. Candidates should follow the current Oregon exam authorization and Building Codes Division instructions for the exact code edition and materials required for the examination they are scheduled to take.

Because the Oregon exam is timed, candidates should practice working efficiently. A 3-hour exam can move quickly when questions require code lookup, Oregon rule review, or calculations. Candidates should practice recognizing topics, locating articles and tables, applying rules carefully, and moving on when a question takes too long. Timed practice helps candidates build confidence and pacing before exam day.

Open Book Test

The Oregon Journeyman Electrician examination is open book under the Oregon Building Codes Division’s electrical license exam material guidelines. Candidates may use approved materials during the examination when those materials meet the state’s rules for exam use.

Oregon’s approved exam material rules are strict. Candidates should follow the Building Codes Division’s current guidelines for acceptable materials, markings, tabs, handwritten notes, and prohibited materials. The exam guidelines control what may be used during the examination and how reference materials may be prepared.

Open book does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates still need to understand what the question is asking, identify whether it is based on the NEC, Oregon Electrical Specialty Code, Oregon Revised Statutes, Oregon Administrative Rules, theory, formulas, or practical application, and then locate the correct answer efficiently. Licensing Steps

Oregon candidates should begin by confirming that the General Journeyman Electrician license is the correct license classification for their work goals. Oregon offers multiple electrical license classifications, including general journeyman, limited residential, limited energy, plant journeyman, limited renewable energy, general supervising, and other specialty classifications. The correct license depends on the type of electrical work the candidate plans to perform.

A practical licensing path begins with completing the required electrical training and work experience, documenting qualifying experience, applying to the Oregon Building Codes Division for the correct license classification, receiving examination approval, preparing with the approved exam materials and content areas, taking the examination, and completing the remaining licensing steps after passing.

For the General Journeyman Electrician license, Oregon requires qualifying education, training, and on-the-job experience. A common path includes completion of an approved electrical apprenticeship program that provides the required supervised work experience and classroom training. Oregon also has rules for applicants seeking approval through equivalent experience, and those applicants must document qualifying work categories and minimum experience hours.

After the application is reviewed and accepted, the candidate is authorized to take the examination. Candidates should follow the exam scheduling instructions provided through the Building Codes Division process, prepare their approved materials according to the state’s exam guidelines, bring required identification, and follow all examination rules.

Passing the examination is an important milestone, but the license is issued through the Building Codes Division after the applicant satisfies the applicable requirements. Candidates should complete the full state process before performing work that requires an Oregon electrical license.

State Requirements

The Oregon Building Codes Division issues individual electrical licenses and electrical contractor licenses. Oregon requires individuals performing electrical work to hold the proper license or work under the supervision required by state law and rule. The General Journeyman Electrician license is one of the primary individual electrical licenses for electricians who have completed the required training and experience.

Oregon’s General Journeyman Electrician qualification rules include detailed experience categories and minimum on-the-job training requirements. Candidates applying through an equivalent experience path must document qualifying experience in the electrical trade according to Oregon Administrative Rules. An approved apprenticeship program is a common pathway because it combines supervised field experience with related classroom instruction.

Oregon also uses supervising electrician licenses for electricians who plan, lay out, direct, and supervise electrical installations at a higher level. A General Supervising Electrician license is not the same as a General Journeyman Electrician license. Candidates should select the license classification that matches their experience, duties, and career goal.

License renewal and continuing education requirements apply to Oregon electrical license holders. Licensees should follow the Building Codes Division’s renewal process and continuing education rules for their license classification. Maintaining an active license requires compliance with Oregon requirements, including renewal deadlines and required education when applicable.

Study materials help candidates prepare, but licensing decisions are made by the Oregon Building Codes Division based on the applicant’s qualifications, documentation, examination result, and compliance with Oregon law and rule.

Code navigation should also be part of every study plan. Candidates should practice using the NEC table of contents, index, article numbers, definitions, tables, notes, and exceptions. The goal is to identify the topic quickly and move to the correct section without wasting time. A candidate who understands how the NEC is organized is better prepared for open-book electrical testing.

Services, feeders, and branch circuits should receive careful attention. Candidates should review service conductors, service disconnects, grounding at services, feeder sizing, branch circuit ratings, required outlets, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, and load calculations. These topics connect code knowledge with practical installation skill.

Grounding and bonding are important areas for Oregon journeyman exam preparation. Candidates should review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, raceway bonding, panel bonding, grounded conductors, separately derived systems, and related safety requirements. Grounding and bonding questions often require careful reading because similar terms can lead to different code requirements.

Wiring methods and materials should be studied consistently. Candidates should review conductors, cables, raceways, boxes, fittings, supports, conductor ampacity, insulation types, flexible wiring methods, underground wiring, and installation conditions. These topics appear frequently in electrical work and are central to NEC-based study.

Motors, transformers, controls, and equipment should also be part of the study routine. Candidates should review motor branch circuits, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, disconnecting means, controllers, transformer sizing, transformer protection, luminaires, receptacles, switches, appliances, panelboards, and related installation requirements.

Oregon-specific content should not be ignored. Candidates should include Oregon Electrical Specialty Code material, Oregon Revised Statutes, and Oregon Administrative Rules in their study plan. State-specific rules may affect licensing, installation requirements, inspection expectations, and exam questions.

Flash cards are useful for shorter study sessions. Candidates can use them to review formulas, definitions, electrical theory, safety concepts, NEC topics, Oregon rule reminders, and trade terms before work, after work, during breaks, or between longer study blocks. Flash cards work best when paired with deeper reading, calculation drills, and code lookup practice.

A strong study plan should include scheduled reading, NEC navigation, Oregon rule review, calculation drills, timed practice, and repeated review of weaker topics. Candidates should avoid studying only familiar material. Journeyman electrician preparation rewards steady practice, careful reading, and the ability to apply electrical knowledge under exam conditions.
