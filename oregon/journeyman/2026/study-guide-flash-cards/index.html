Exam Details

Oregon electrician licensing is handled through the Oregon Building Codes Division. Oregon has statewide electrical licensing, and the Building Codes Division administers the application process for electrical individual licenses. Candidates applying for an electrical individual license must complete the required application materials, document qualifying work history, submit experience verification, receive approval, and follow the licensing examination instructions issued by the division.

For an Oregon General Journeyman Electrician license, candidates apply to the Building Codes Division and provide work history using the required experience verification process. The division requires applicants to list experience in the work categories identified in Oregon Administrative Rule 918-282. After the application is received and approved, the division mails a Letter of Authorization that explains how to schedule the licensing examination.

Oregon electrical licensing examinations are prepared under the Electrical and Elevator Board process and administered through the Building Codes Division testing system. The passing score for Oregon licensing exams is 75 percent correct or better. After the exam is completed, results are mailed by the division. Applicants who pass receive the license in the mail with the results, and applicants who do not pass receive results along with strength and weakness information and reapplication instructions.

The Oregon General Journeyman Electrician exam should be prepared for as an Oregon-specific open-book electrical licensing exam. Candidates should study the National Electrical Code, Oregon amendments, Oregon Revised Statutes chapter 479, Oregon Administrative Rules division 918, general electrical theory, services, feeders, branch circuits, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, conductor sizing, raceways, boxes, motors, equipment, special occupancies, communication systems, and calculation methods.

Open Book Test

The Oregon Journeyman Electrician exam is an open-book examination. Oregon’s approved electrical license examination materials include the National Electrical Code/NFPA 70 and errata with Oregon amendments, the National Electrical Code Handbook, Oregon Revised Statutes chapter 479, Oregon Administrative Rules division 918, American Electrician’s Handbook, Electrical Black Book, Ugly’s Electrical References, Ferm’s Fast Finder Index, Tom Henry’s Key Word Index, and a silent, non-printing, non-programmable calculator.

Open book does not mean easy. Candidates still need to understand electrical concepts, recognize how questions are written, locate information quickly, apply code rules correctly, and manage time. A student who depends only on searching through references for every answer can lose valuable time during the exam.

Oregon allows approved published materials to be altered for candidate use, including tabs and personal handwritten or printed notes within the margins of the approved materials. Practice tests and class preparation materials are not allowed during the examination. Materials not on the approved list may be removed by the proctor, and other published materials or electronics are prohibited.

Open-book electrical exams reward reference familiarity. Students should practice using the National Electrical Code table of contents, index, article structure, definitions, tables, exceptions, notes, and common code sections before exam day. Students should also practice calculations repeatedly so conductor sizing, load relationships, grounding and bonding rules, motor requirements, transformer concepts, box fill, raceway fill, voltage drop concepts, and overcurrent protection requirements become easier to recognize.

A strong Oregon exam strategy includes learning where major NEC topics are located before the exam, reviewing Oregon amendments, studying Oregon electrical laws and rules, practicing calculations, and using practice exams to build confidence with code-based questions. Flash cards help students recall key concepts faster, while practice exams help build the habit of reading questions carefully and choosing the correct reference path.

Licensing Steps

Oregon journeyman electrician licensing begins with meeting the qualification requirements for the General Journeyman Electrician license. Candidates must complete qualifying electrical work experience, document that experience properly, submit the required application materials, receive approval from the Building Codes Division, pass the licensing examination, and complete the remaining license issuance steps.

A typical Oregon General Journeyman Electrician path includes completing a registered electrical apprenticeship or otherwise documenting qualifying electrical experience that satisfies Oregon’s work category requirements. Oregon commonly requires 8,000 hours of qualifying on-the-job electrical training for the general journeyman pathway, along with approved related classroom instruction. Candidates should maintain accurate documentation throughout apprenticeship and employment so the application can be supported when they are ready to apply.

Applicants complete the Oregon Electrical License Application, complete the work history, sign the applicant affidavit, indicate the preferred exam location, and use the application checklist. The Building Codes Division also requires experience verification. Applicants submit an Electrical Experience Verification Form for each employer and must list experience in the required work categories under Oregon Administrative Rule 918-282.

After the application is received and approved, the division mails the Letter of Authorization. The letter explains how to schedule the licensing exam and identifies approved testing materials. The candidate must provide the Letter of Authorization and photo identification at the testing location. Examinations are not given without those required documents.

After passing the required examination, the division mails the license to the applicant. A candidate who does not pass must follow the reapplication and waiting-period instructions before retesting. Oregon also has reciprocal license agreements for General Journeyman Electrician and General Supervising Electrician licenses, and candidates applying through reciprocity must follow the state’s reciprocal licensing process.

Students can use it while completing apprenticeship work, while documenting experience, before submitting the license application, after receiving testing approval, or during final review before the scheduled exam date.
