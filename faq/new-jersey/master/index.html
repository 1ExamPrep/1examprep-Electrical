The exam

Is the New Jersey Electrical Contractor exam open book?

Yes. The New Jersey Electrical Contractor examination is identified as open book. The current PSI bulletin states that the NEC reference is provided at the test center and that candidates may not use their own copy of the NEC for that examination.

How many questions are on the New Jersey Electrical Contractor exam?

The New Jersey Electrical Contractor examination is listed with 100 questions.

How much time is allowed for the New Jersey Electrical Contractor exam?

The New Jersey Electrical Contractor examination is listed with 260 minutes of testing time in the current PSI bulletin.

What is the passing score for the New Jersey Electrical Contractor exam?

The passing score for the New Jersey Electrical Contractor examination is 70%, which equals 70 correct answers out of 100 questions.

What score is required to pass the New Jersey Electrical Contractor exam?

The New Jersey Electrical Contractor examination is listed with a 70 percent passing score, equal to 70 correct answers.

Who administers New Jersey electrical contractor licensing?

New Jersey electrical contractor licensing is administered through the New Jersey State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors.

Is the New Jersey Electrical Contractors Licensing Examination open book?

Yes. The New Jersey Electrical Contractors Licensing Examination is open book. Candidates should follow the current Board and PSI exam instructions for the exact references, tabs, notes, calculators, and code edition allowed during testing.

Licensing and experience

What exams are required to qualify for New Jersey Electrical Contractor licensure?

The current PSI bulletin states that candidates must pass the Electrical Contractor, Alarm Systems Electrical Contractor, and Business and Law examinations to qualify for New Jersey Electrical Contractor licensure.

What experience is required to apply for the New Jersey Electrical Contractor licensing examination?

New Jersey applicant instructions require work experience certification forms showing five or more years of electrical experience. The Board also identifies pathways involving an approved four-year apprenticeship program with at least one year of hands-on experience, or a bachelor’s degree in electrical engineering with two years of practical electrical construction experience.

Does New Jersey issue a statewide Master Electrician license?

New Jersey’s primary statewide electrical contracting credential is the Electrical Contractor license issued through the New Jersey State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. This product uses master electrician study language for advanced electrical preparation and supports New Jersey Electrical Contractor exam study.

What NEC edition is currently listed for the New Jersey Electrical Contractor exam?

The current PSI bulletin lists the 2023 National Electrical Code for the New Jersey Electrical Contractor exam. This product is based on the 2026 NEC for updated code-cycle study and reference-navigation practice.

Can I bring my own tabbed NEC to the New Jersey Electrical Contractor exam?

No. For the Electrical Contractor exam, the NEC reference is provided at the test center, and candidates may not use their own copy of the NEC. The tabbed 2026 NEC in this combo is intended for study and code-navigation practice.

Who provides the New Jersey electrical contractor licensing exam?

PSI administers the New Jersey Electrical Contractor Licensure Examination for candidates authorized by the Board.

What topics are covered on the New Jersey Electrical Contractor exam?

The published content outline includes General Electrical Knowledge, Raceways and Enclosures, Services, Feeders, and Branch Circuits, Overcurrent Protection, Conductors and Cables, Grounding and Bonding, Equipment for General Use, Special Occupancies, Special Equipment and Conditions, Motors and Controls, Low Voltage and Communications Circuits, and Safety.

Does New Jersey have state electrical licensing?

Yes. New Jersey electrical contractor licensing is handled at the state level through the New Jersey Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors within the Division of Consumer Affairs.

Should I confirm New Jersey licensing requirements before testing?

Yes. Candidates should confirm the required credential, eligibility requirements, application process, code edition, reference rules, and post-exam licensing steps before scheduling or taking an exam.
