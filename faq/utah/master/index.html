The exam

Who administers Utah electrical licensing exams?

Utah electrical licensing exams are administered through Prov under the Utah Division of Professional Licensing examination process.

Is the Utah Master Electrician exam open book?

Utah Master Electrician exam preparation should include open-book code navigation and reference-use practice. Candidates should follow the current Utah Division of Professional Licensing and testing-provider exam instructions for the exact references, tabs, notes, calculators, and code edition allowed during testing.

Did Utah change its electrical exam structure?

Yes. Beginning August 1, 2025, Utah updated the electrician licensing exam structure. Journeyman and residential electrician candidates take a combined theory and code exam, while master electrician candidates take a Law and Rule exam.

Is the Utah Master Electrician Law and Rule exam open book?

Yes. The Utah Master Electrician Law and Rule examination is an open book examination. Candidates must follow Prov’s reference rules for approved books, permanent tabs, highlighting, underlining, handwritten notes, added documents, and marking in books during the exam.

How many questions are on the Utah Master Electrician Law and Rule exam?

The Utah Master Electrician Law and Rule examination is listed with 80 questions.

How much time is allowed for the Utah Master Electrician Law and Rule exam?

The Utah Master Electrician Law and Rule examination is listed with 240 minutes of testing time.

What score is required to pass the Utah Master Electrician Law and Rule exam?

The Utah Master Electrician Law and Rule examination requires a passing score of 75 percent.

Licensing and experience

Who regulates Utah Master Electrician licensing?

Utah Master Electrician licensing is regulated by the Utah Division of Professional Licensing under the Utah Department of Commerce.

What experience is required for a Utah Master Electrician license?

A common pathway requires 8,000 hours of experience as a licensed journeyman electrician. Utah also provides education-based pathways involving an accredited electrical engineering degree with qualifying apprentice experience or an associate degree in applied science from an electrical trade school with qualifying journeyman experience.

Can an electrical engineering degree help qualify for the Utah Master Electrician license?

Yes. Applicants with a bachelor’s or master’s degree in an electrical engineering program accredited by EAC/ABET may qualify with 2,000 hours of work experience as a licensed apprentice electrician and the required examinations.

Can an associate degree help qualify for the Utah Master Electrician license?

Yes. Applicants with an associate degree in applied science from an electrical trade school course of study may qualify with 4,000 hours of work experience as a licensed journeyman electrician and the required examinations.

Does Utah issue a Master Electrician license?

Yes. Utah issues a statewide Master Electrician license for applicants who meet the required experience or education pathway and pass the required examination components.

Does a Utah Master Electrician license automatically allow contracting?

No. Individual master electrician licensure and electrical contracting authority are separate responsibilities. Electrical contracting may require the proper contractor license, qualifying individual relationship, business registration, bond, insurance, and other state requirements.

Who handles Utah master electrician licensing?

Utah master electrician licensing is handled through the Utah Division of Professional Licensing under the Utah Department of Commerce.

Does Utah have a state Master Electrician license?

Yes. Utah has state electrical licensing and recognizes master electrician and residential master electrician classifications through the Utah Division of Professional Licensing.

Does a Utah Master Electrician license automatically equal a contractor license?

No. A Utah Master Electrician license is an individual electrical license. Electrical contracting activity may also require the proper contractor license or contractor classification, depending on the work being offered or performed.

Should I confirm Utah licensing requirements before testing?

Yes. Candidates should confirm the required license classification, eligibility requirements, application process, code edition, reference rules, and post-exam licensing steps before scheduling or taking an exam.

Code book and materials

Can I use the 2026 NEC to study for Utah electrical exams?

Yes. The 2026 NEC is useful for updated code-cycle study, reference-navigation practice, calculations, and long-term electrical knowledge. Candidates should use the official NEC edition required for their scheduled exam.

Why are NEC tabs helpful?

NEC tabs help candidates locate major articles, chapters, tables, and subject areas faster during study. This supports stronger code-navigation practice and helps candidates become more comfortable using the NEC as a reference.

Can I bring these books into the exam room?

Exam-room reference rules are controlled by the current Utah licensing and testing instructions. Candidates should review the official rules before test day to confirm which books and materials are permitted.

Should I study Utah laws and rules in addition to the NEC?

Yes. Candidates should review Utah electrical laws, licensing rules, supervision responsibilities, contractor requirements, and any state-specific subjects listed in the current examination instructions.

More questions

Who provides Utah electrical exams?

Utah electrical examinations are administered through the state electrical exam program used for DOPL electrician licensing candidates.

Is the Utah Electrician Practical Exam required for Master Electrician candidates?

Master Electrician candidates must pass the Utah Electrician Practical Exam unless they previously passed the practical exam when earning a Utah Journeyman Electrician license.

What is the difference between a Master Electrician and a Residential Master Electrician in Utah?

The Master Electrician and Residential Master Electrician classifications are different license paths with different scopes. Candidates should confirm the classification that matches the type of electrical work they plan to perform or supervise.
