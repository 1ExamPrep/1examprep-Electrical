## The exam

### Are Georgia electrical exams open book?

Georgia electrical contractor exam preparation should be treated as open book, reference-based preparation. Candidates should follow the current PSI Candidate Information Bulletin and Board instructions for the exact reference rules that apply to their examination.

### What score is required for Georgia electrical contractor exams?

Georgia rules require a minimum score of 70 on the appropriate electrical contractor examination.

### Who administers Georgia electrical contractor exams?

Georgia uses PSI as the testing vendor for electrical contractor examinations after applicants are approved by the Board to sit for the exam.

### Are Georgia electrical contractor exams open book?

Georgia electrical contractor examinations use approved reference materials listed for the applicable examination. Candidates should follow the current Georgia Board and PSI instructions for approved books, code edition, and exam-room rules.

### Is the journeyman electrician exam open book?

The ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician exam is open book. Exam-room rules, approved references, and code edition requirements depend on the exam provider and the credentialing authority using the exam.

### How many questions are on the ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician exam?

The ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician exam includes 80 multiple-choice questions.

### How much time is allowed for the ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician exam?

The ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician exam allows 4 hours of testing time.

## Licensing and experience

### Does Georgia have a statewide journeyman electrician license?

Georgia’s statewide electrical licensing structure is contractor-based through the Georgia State Board of Electrical Contractors. Journeyman-level recognition may be handled by local jurisdictions, employers, apprenticeship programs, or project requirements.

### Does Georgia issue a statewide journeyman electrician license?

No. Georgia does not issue one single statewide individual journeyman electrician license. Georgia’s statewide electrical licensing is contractor-focused through the Georgia State Board of Electrical Contractors. Local jurisdictions, employers, apprenticeship programs, and training organizations may still use journeyman-level credentials or expectations.

### Who oversees statewide electrical contractor licensing in Georgia?

Statewide electrical contractor licensing is handled through the Georgia State Board of Electrical Contractors under the Georgia Secretary of State’s Professional Licensing Boards Division.

### What are the Georgia electrical contractor license classes?

Georgia offers Class I Restricted and Class II Non-Restricted electrical contractor licenses. Class I is limited to single-phase electrical installations that do not exceed 200 amperes at the service drop or service lateral. Class II is the broader non-restricted classification.

### What Georgia electrical licenses should candidates know about?

Georgia electrical contractor licensing includes Class I Restricted and Class II Unrestricted electrical contractor classifications, along with low-voltage classifications. Candidates should review the Georgia State Board of Electrical Contractors requirements for the classification they plan to pursue.

### Is a journeyman-level electrician the same as a Georgia electrical contractor?

No. Journeyman-level trade knowledge is not the same as holding a Georgia electrical contractor license. Independent electrical contracting in Georgia requires the proper state electrical contractor license.

### Who licenses electrical contractors in Georgia?

Georgia electrical contractor licensing is handled through the Georgia Secretary of State and the Georgia State Construction Industry Licensing Board, Division of Electrical Contractors.
