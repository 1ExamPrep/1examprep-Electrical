## The exam

### Is the Georgia electrical exam open book?

Georgia electrical examinations allow certain listed references during the exam. Candidates may bring only the approved reference materials for their specific examination. References may be highlighted, underlined, or tabbed with permanent tabs, but handwritten notes, photocopied materials, and unapproved materials are not allowed.

### How is the Georgia Electrical Contractor exam structured?

The exam is administered in two parts. Candidates have 4 hours for Part I, followed by a break, and 4 hours for Part II, for a total of 8 hours.

### What score is required to pass the Georgia Electrical Contractor exam?

The minimum passing final scaled score for each Georgia electrical contractor examination is 70.

### Does Georgia require Board approval before scheduling the exam?

Yes. Candidates must be approved by the Georgia Construction Industry Licensing Board before scheduling the examination through PSI.

### Who administers the Georgia electrical contractor exam?

PSI administers Georgia electrical contractor licensing examinations after the candidate is approved by the Board to sit for the exam.

### What score is required to pass?

The minimum passing final scaled score for Georgia electrical contractor examinations is 70.

### How many questions are on the Georgia electrical contractor exam?

Both Class I and Class II exams include 140 scored test questions. The Class I exam includes 15 additional pretest questions, and the Class II exam includes 22 additional pretest questions.

### How much time is allowed for the Georgia electrical contractor exam?

The exam is administered in two parts. Candidates have 4 hours for Part I and 4 hours for Part II, for a total of 8 hours.

## Licensing and experience

### Does Georgia have a license officially called Master Electrician?

Georgia does not issue a statewide license officially titled “Master Electrician.” Georgia licenses electrical contractors as Class I Restricted and Class II Unrestricted through the Georgia Construction Industry Licensing Board, Division of Electrical Contractors.

### What topics are covered on the Georgia Electrical Contractor exam?

The exam covers regulations, laws, administrative functions, project organization, basic electrical circuits, controls and devices, motors, rotating equipment, transformers, interior electrical systems, safety, and related trade knowledge.

### What is the difference between Georgia Class I and Class II electrical contractor licenses?

Georgia offers Class I Restricted and Class II Unrestricted electrical contractor licenses. The Class II Unrestricted license is the broader electrical contractor classification and is the path most closely aligned with master-level electrical contractor preparation.

### What Georgia license is closest to master electrician-level preparation?

The Georgia Class II Non-Restricted Electrical Contractor license is the unrestricted electrical contractor license and is the closest match for advanced contractor-level electrical preparation.

### Who regulates Georgia electrical contractors?

Georgia electrical contractors are regulated by the Georgia State Board of Electrical Contractors under the Georgia Construction Industry Licensing Board.

### How long is the Georgia electrical contractor exam?

The examination is administered in two parts, with eight total hours allotted. Candidates have four hours for Part I and four hours for Part II.

### Does Georgia have a Master Electrician license?

Georgia licenses electrical contractors through the Georgia Construction Industry Licensing Board, Division of Electrical Contractors. Many candidates use “Master Electrician” as a common exam-prep term for advanced contractor-level electrical study.

### What Georgia electrical contractor licenses are tested?

Georgia offers electrical contractor examinations for Class I Restricted and Class II Unrestricted electrical contractor licensing.

### Can I use a calculator during the Georgia electrical contractor exam?

Silent, non-programmable calculators without alpha keys or printing capabilities are allowed in the testing room.

## Code book and materials

### Are permanent tabs allowed in Georgia exam references?

Yes. References may be highlighted, underlined, or tabbed with permanent tabs. Handwritten notes and other photocopied materials are not allowed.

### Why are tabs helpful for NEC study?

Tabs help students move through the NEC more efficiently during study. They can make it easier to locate major articles, chapters, tables, definitions, and frequently used sections while practicing code lookup.

### Should I practice with the NEC before exam day?

Yes. Students should practice locating code sections, tables, definitions, exceptions, and calculation rules before the exam. Open book testing rewards candidates who can use references quickly and accurately.

### Can I use tabs in my reference books during the Georgia exam?

Yes. References may be tabbed with permanent tabs. Temporary tabs, extra notes, photocopied materials, and additional inserted materials are not allowed.
