## The exam

### Is the North Carolina electrical contractor exam open book?

Yes. The North Carolina electrical contracting examinations are identified as open book examinations. Candidates must bring their own approved references and follow the Board’s rules for reference condition, annotations, permanent tabs, and exam-room materials.

### How many questions are on the North Carolina Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited electrical contracting exams?

The North Carolina Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited electrical contracting examinations each consist of 100 multiple-choice questions.

### How much time is allowed for the North Carolina electrical contractor exam?

The Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited electrical contracting examinations allow a maximum of 6 hours for completion.

### What happens after I pass the North Carolina electrical contractor exam?

After passing, the candidate must complete the new license application for the desired classification, submit the required license fee, and complete the licensing process through the Board before the license is activated.

### Who administers North Carolina electrical contractor licensing?

North Carolina electrical contractor licensing is administered through the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors.

### Who administers North Carolina electrical contracting exams?

PSI administers and scores the examinations for the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. Candidates must apply through the Board and receive approval before scheduling through PSI.

## Licensing and experience

### Does North Carolina issue a statewide Master Electrician license?

North Carolina uses electrical contractor license classifications rather than the same statewide Master Electrician license structure used in some states. The Unlimited electrical contractor classification is the broadest North Carolina electrical contractor classification.

### Who regulates electrical contractors in North Carolina?

Electrical contractors in North Carolina are regulated by the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors.

### Does North Carolina have one statewide master electrician license?

North Carolina’s state licensing structure is focused on electrical contractor license classifications such as Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and Special Restricted classifications. Candidates should confirm the classification they need before applying.

### Is North Carolina electrical licensing statewide?

Yes. Electrical contracting licensure is regulated statewide through the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors.

### What subjects are covered on the North Carolina electrical contractor exam?

The exam covers NEC knowledge, electrical trade topics, permit and inspection requirements, business practices, and North Carolina statutes and rules applicable to electrical contracting.

### What are the main North Carolina electrical contractor classifications?

North Carolina electrical contractor classifications include Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and Special Restricted classifications. The correct classification depends on the scope of work the candidate plans to perform.

### Does North Carolina have state electrical contractor licensing?

Yes. North Carolina electrical contractor licensing is handled at the state level through the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors.

### Should I confirm North Carolina licensing requirements before testing?

Yes. Candidates should confirm the required classification, eligibility requirements, application process, code edition, reference rules, and post-exam licensing steps before scheduling or taking an exam.

## Code book and materials

### What references are allowed in the North Carolina exam room?

The approved exam-room references include the National Electrical Code and, for applicable classifications, the National Fire Alarm Code. Candidates must bring their own approved references to the test center.

### Can I use the 2026 NEC to study for the North Carolina exam?

Yes. The 2026 NEC is useful for updated code-cycle study, code-navigation practice, calculations, and long-term electrical knowledge. Candidates should use the official NEC edition required by the Board for exam day.

### Why are NEC tabs helpful?

NEC tabs help candidates locate major articles, chapters, tables, and subject areas faster during study. This supports stronger code-navigation practice and helps candidates become more comfortable using the NEC as a reference.

### What references are allowed for the North Carolina electrical contracting exam?

The North Carolina examination handbook lists the National Electrical Code as a required reference. Candidates taking the Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, SP-SFD, or SP-FA/LV classifications are also required to bring the National Fire Alarm Code. Candidates should use the editions required by the current Board handbook for their scheduled exam.

### Why is calculation practice important for this exam?

Calculation practice is important because electrical exams can include load calculations, conductor sizing, branch circuit work, service and feeder calculations, box fill, raceway fill, motor calculations, transformer calculations, electrical theory, and other math-based topics. A dedicated calculations study guide helps candidates practice these skills consistently.

### Can I bring these books into the exam room?

Exam-room reference rules are controlled by the current candidate bulletin and testing instructions. Candidates should review the official rules before test day to confirm which books and materials are permitted.

### What topics are covered on the North Carolina electrical contracting exam?

The exam may include National Electrical Code content, analysis of electrical plans and specifications, estimating of electrical installations, fundamentals of electrical work and equipment, electrical contractors licensing law, Board rules, and contractor business, law, and project management concepts.
