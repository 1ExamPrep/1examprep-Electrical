## The exam

### Are North Carolina electrical exams open book?

North Carolina electrical contractor examinations are open book with approved references. Many local journeyman-level exams are also prepared for as open-book, NEC-based exams, but candidates should follow the exact rules from the local authority or testing provider administering their exam.

### Is the North Carolina Journeyman Electrician exam open book?

North Carolina journeyman electrician exam preparation should include open-book code navigation and reference-use practice. Candidates should follow the current testing instructions for the exact references, tabs, notes, calculators, and code edition allowed during testing.

### Who administers North Carolina electrical contractor examinations?

PSI administers and scores the North Carolina electrical contractor examinations under contract with the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors.

### How many questions are on the North Carolina Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited electrical contractor exams?

The Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited electrical contracting examinations each contain 100 multiple-choice questions.

### How much time is allowed for the North Carolina electrical contractor exam?

Candidates have a maximum of 6 hours to complete the Limited, Intermediate, or Unlimited electrical contracting examination.

## Licensing and experience

### Does North Carolina have a statewide journeyman electrician license?

North Carolina does not use one uniform statewide journeyman electrician license in the same way some states do. Statewide licensing is focused on electrical contractor licensing through the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors, while journeyman-level recognition may depend on local authorities, apprenticeship programs, employers, or jobsite requirements.

### Is a North Carolina electrical contractor license the same as a journeyman credential?

No. A North Carolina electrical contractor license is connected to contracting authority and is issued through the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. A journeyman-level credential or recognition usually relates to individual trade qualification, apprenticeship advancement, local requirements, or employer standards.

### Does North Carolina use electrical license classifications?

Yes. North Carolina electrical contractor licensing is administered through classifications, including Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and Special Restricted classifications.

### Does North Carolina issue a statewide journeyman electrician license?

No. North Carolina does not issue one single statewide individual journeyman electrician license. Individual journeyman credentials are generally handled by local cities, counties, or municipalities.

### Who regulates North Carolina electrical contractor licensing?

Electrical contractor licensing is regulated through the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. Contractor licensing is separate from local journeyman credentialing.

### Is journeyman-level recognition the same as an electrical contractor license in North Carolina?

No. Journeyman-level recognition and electrical contractor licensing are different credential concepts. Contractor licensing is connected to offering, bidding, contracting, advertising, or performing electrical work as a business.

### Should I confirm North Carolina licensing requirements before testing?

Yes. Candidates should confirm the required credential, registration path, eligibility requirements, application process, code edition, reference rules, and post-exam steps before scheduling or taking an exam.

## Code book and materials

### What reference book should I study?

The National Electrical Code is the primary reference for NEC-based electrical exam preparation. Students pursuing contractor-level licensing should also review North Carolina Electrical Contractors Licensing Law, NFPA 72 when applicable, and the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management: North Carolina Electrical Edition.

### Why is the NEC important for electrical exam preparation?

The NEC contains the electrical installation rules used to answer many code-based exam questions. Learning how to find and apply NEC rules is a key part of preparing for open book electrical exams.

### Can I use the 2026 NEC for my North Carolina exam?

This combo is built around the 2026 NEC. Candidates should confirm the official reference edition required for their specific North Carolina exam before test day.

### Can I use tabs in my reference book during the North Carolina exam?

Permanent tabs are allowed. Temporary tabs, Post-it notes, stapled tabs, and divider tabs are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins.

### Can I write in my reference book during the exam?

No. Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and annotated before the examination session, but candidates may not write in the references during the exam.

### Can I bring these books into the exam room?

Exam-room reference rules are controlled by the current testing instructions for the exam being taken. Candidates should review the official rules before test day to confirm which books and materials are permitted.

### Why are NEC tabs helpful?

NEC tabs help candidates locate major articles, chapters, tables, and subject areas faster during study. This supports stronger code-navigation practice and helps candidates become more comfortable using the NEC as a reference.

### Should I study local rules in addition to the NEC?

Yes. Candidates should review local electrical laws, inspection expectations, permit-related requirements, contractor licensing distinctions, and any jurisdiction-specific subjects listed in the current examination instructions.

### What topics should I study?

Students should review electrical theory, NEC navigation, calculations, wiring methods, conductors, raceways, boxes, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, motors, controls, transformers, lighting, safety, fire alarm systems, communication systems, special occupancies, special equipment, and local amendments when required.

### How should I use the practice exams?

Use the 12 practice exams to test your knowledge, improve pacing, and identify weak areas. Review every missed question and return to the related study guide section or NEC topic before moving forward.

### How should I use the 2 full final exams?

Use the full final exams after completing topic review and several practice exams. Take them in a focused setting, track time, review missed answers, and return to weak topics before test day or before your next major study session.

### How should I use the flash cards?

Use the flash cards for daily active recall. Separate cards into topics you know, topics you partly know, and topics that need more review. Study the weakest group most often.
