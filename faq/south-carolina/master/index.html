The exam

Who administers the South Carolina electrical contractor exam?

PSI administers the South Carolina commercial contractor examination program for the South Carolina Contractor’s Licensing Board.

Is the South Carolina commercial Electrician exam open book?

Yes. South Carolina commercial contractor examinations are identified as open book examinations. Candidates must follow PSI rules for approved references, highlighting, underlining, indexing, permanent tabs, written notes, and additional papers.

How many questions are on the South Carolina Electrician - Commercial Contractor exam?

The South Carolina Electrician - Commercial Contractor examination contains 80 questions.

How much time is allowed for the South Carolina Electrician - Commercial Contractor exam?

The exam allows 5 hours for completion.

What score is required to pass the South Carolina electrical contractor exam?

Candidates must answer 56 questions correctly out of 80, which equals a 70% passing score.

Is the South Carolina electrical exam open book?

South Carolina electrical contractor and master-level trade examinations are commonly open book for approved references, subject to the current exam bulletin or licensing authority instructions. Candidates should follow the reference rules for their exact exam.

How many questions are on the South Carolina Electrician exam?

The South Carolina Electrician commercial contractor examination is listed with 80 questions.

How much time is allowed for the South Carolina Electrician exam?

The South Carolina Electrician commercial contractor examination is listed with 5 hours of testing time.

Licensing and experience

Who regulates commercial electrical contractor licensing in South Carolina?

Commercial electrical contractor licensing is regulated by the South Carolina Contractor’s Licensing Board, under the South Carolina Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation.

Can South Carolina electrical contractor candidates take the NASCLA electrical exam?

Yes. South Carolina Electrician - Commercial Contractor candidates may choose between the state-specific electrician exam and the NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractor when that path fits their licensing needs.

What references are approved for the South Carolina electrical contractor exam?

Approved references include the National Electrical Code with South Carolina modifications or the NEC Handbook, NFPA 72, American Electricians’ Handbook, 29 CFR Part 1926, Key Word Index by Tom Henry, and the South Carolina 811 Excavator Manual.

Does South Carolina have a statewide Master Electrician license?

South Carolina does not use one simple statewide Master Electrician license title for every electrician. Electrical licensing is handled through contractor classifications and residential specialty licensing structures.

Who regulates commercial electrical contracting in South Carolina?

Commercial electrical contractor licensing is regulated through the South Carolina Contractor’s Licensing Board under mechanical contractor electrical classifications.

Who regulates residential electrical specialty work in South Carolina?

Residential electrical specialty licensing is handled through the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission.

Who handles South Carolina electrical licensing?

South Carolina electrical licensing may involve the South Carolina Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation, the Contractor’s Licensing Board, the Residential Builders Commission, municipal licensing programs, or approved testing programs depending on the type of work and credential involved.

Does South Carolina have more than one electrical license path?

Yes. South Carolina electrical licensing can vary by commercial contracting, residential electrical work, municipal master electrician recognition, and specialty requirements. Candidates should confirm the exact credential needed for their work.

Who handles South Carolina commercial electrical contractor licensing?

South Carolina commercial electrical contractor licensing is handled through the South Carolina Contractor’s Licensing Board under the South Carolina Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation.

Does South Carolina have state electrical contractor licensing?

Yes. South Carolina has state contractor licensing requirements that may apply to electrical contracting work, depending on the project type, scope, and business activity.

Should I confirm South Carolina licensing requirements before testing?

Yes. Candidates should confirm the required license or credential, application process, code edition, reference rules, exam format, and post-exam licensing steps before scheduling or taking an exam.

More questions

Does South Carolina require a Business Management and Law exam?

Yes. South Carolina Electrician - Commercial Contractor candidates are required to pass the Business Management and Law for Contractors examination in addition to the electrical trade examination.

What should I study for a South Carolina residential electrical exam?

Residential electrical candidates should study dwelling-unit wiring, services, feeders, branch circuits, load calculations, grounding and bonding, GFCI and AFCI protection, residential equipment, safety, and approved residential code references.

Is a Business Management and Law exam required?

Yes. South Carolina commercial electrical contractor candidates are required to pass the Business Management and Law for Commercial Contractors examination in addition to the required technical exam or accepted trade exam route.

What topics are covered on the South Carolina Electrician exam?

The content outline includes general electrical knowledge, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special occupancies and equipment, electrical power, motors, low voltage, lighting, illuminated signs, fire detection and alarm systems, and safety information.

Does South Carolina have local electrician requirements?

Some South Carolina jurisdictions may have local electrician credentials, registrations, permit requirements, or inspection rules. Candidates should confirm the requirements for the city, county, municipality, or authority having jurisdiction where they plan to work.

Should I study business and law topics?

Candidates pursuing state contractor licensing should review business and law topics when required by the licensing authority or testing provider. Electrical contractor preparation often includes both trade knowledge and contractor business responsibilities.

Does one South Carolina credential automatically cover every type of electrical work?

No. Requirements can depend on the license type, project scope, business activity, and jurisdiction. Candidates should confirm the authority and credential that apply to the work they plan to perform or contract for.
