The exam

Is the South Carolina Journeyman Electrician exam open book?

South Carolina journeyman electrician exam preparation should include open-book code navigation and reference-use practice. Candidates should follow the current testing instructions for the exact references, tabs, notes, calculators, and code edition allowed during testing.

How many questions are on the South Carolina Journeyman Electrician exam?

The South Carolina Journeyman Electrician exam is listed as 70 questions.

How much time is allowed for the exam?

The South Carolina Journeyman Electrician exam is listed as a 3-hour exam.

What score is required to pass?

The passing score for the South Carolina Journeyman Electrician certification exam is 70%.

Is the South Carolina electrical exam open book?

Reference rules depend on the exam, credentialing body, and testing provider. Candidates should follow the current PROV, PSI, CAGC, Contractors Licensing Board, or Residential Builders Commission instructions for approved books, tabs, calculators, and allowed materials.

Who administers the CAGC SC Trades Certification examination?

PROV is the contracted examination provider for the CAGC SC Trades Certification Program. Approved applicants receive notification from PROV and schedule their own exam date.

How many questions are on the South Carolina residential electrical technical examination?

The South Carolina Residential Builders Commission lists the residential specialty contractor electrical technical examination as a 60-question examination.

Licensing and experience

Is journeyman certification the same as contractor licensing in South Carolina?

No. Journeyman certification supports individual trade qualification, while contractor licensing is connected to contracting authority, business activity, and regulated work scope.

Does South Carolina issue a statewide journeyman electrician license?

No. South Carolina does not issue one single statewide individual journeyman electrician license. Individual journeyman credentials are generally handled by local cities, counties, or municipalities.

Who regulates South Carolina electrical contractor licensing?

Commercial electrical contractor licensing is regulated through the South Carolina Contractor’s Licensing Board, while residential electrical specialty contractor licensing is regulated through the Residential Builders Commission.

Is a journeyman credential the same as a South Carolina electrical contractor license?

No. A journeyman credential generally supports hands-on electrical work under local rules. South Carolina electrical contractor licensing is handled separately through state contractor licensing boards.

Is a South Carolina trade certification card the same as a contractor license?

No. The CAGC SC Trade Certification card is not the same as a South Carolina Mechanical Contractors License or a South Carolina Residential Builders Commission Specialty Contractors License.

Does South Carolina have a residential electrical license path?

Yes. Residential electrical licensing is handled through the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission. Residential candidates must meet the Commission’s application, experience, and examination requirements.

Does South Carolina have one statewide Journeyman Electrician license?

South Carolina electrical requirements may include contractor licensing and local journeyman-level requirements. Candidates should confirm the requirements connected to the authority, employer, jurisdiction, or exam path they are pursuing.

Is journeyman-level recognition the same as an electrical contractor license in South Carolina?

No. Journeyman-level recognition and electrical contractor licensing are different credential concepts. Contractor licensing is connected to offering, bidding, contracting, advertising, or performing electrical work as a business.

Should I confirm South Carolina licensing requirements before testing?

Yes. Candidates should confirm the required credential, registration path, eligibility requirements, application process, code edition, reference rules, and post-exam steps before scheduling or taking an exam.

Code book and materials

What references should I study?

Students should study the National Electrical Code edition required by the current exam instructions and the American Electricians’ Handbook when listed for the exam. Candidates should follow the current candidate instructions for the approved reference list.

Can I bring removable notes or temporary tabs into the exam room?

No. PSI rules for open book testing allow highlighted, underlined, and indexed references, but the books must otherwise be unmarked and may not contain loose or attached papers. Tabs must be permanent tabs. Temporary removable tabs are not allowed.

Why are NEC tabs useful for electrical exam prep?

NEC tabs help students move through the code book faster by organizing important articles and sections. Tabs are especially useful when practicing questions involving grounding and bonding, services, feeders, branch circuits, conductors, raceways, boxes, and overcurrent protection.

Can I bring these books into the exam room?

Exam-room reference rules are controlled by the current testing instructions for the exam being taken. Candidates should review the official rules before test day to confirm which books and materials are permitted.

Why are NEC tabs helpful?

NEC tabs help candidates locate major articles, chapters, tables, and subject areas faster during study. This supports stronger code-navigation practice and helps candidates become more comfortable using the NEC as a reference.

Should I study local rules in addition to the NEC?

Yes. Candidates should review local electrical laws, inspection expectations, permit-related requirements, contractor licensing distinctions, and any jurisdiction-specific subjects listed in the current examination instructions.

Candidates should study NEC navigation, electrical theory, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, conductors, raceways, boxes, overcurrent protection, motors, transformers, equipment, safety, special occupancies, and calculations.

How should I use the practice exams?

Use the 12 practice exams to test your knowledge, improve pacing, and identify weak areas. Review every missed question and return to the related study guide section, NEC topic, calculation method, or trade concept before moving forward.

How should I use the 2 full final exams?

Use the full final exams after completing topic review and several practice exams. Take them in a focused setting, track time, review missed answers, and return to weak topics before test day.

What topics should I study?

Study areas include electrical theory, wiring methods, conductors, raceways, boxes, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, motors, controls, lighting, equipment, special occupancies, safety, low-voltage systems, communication systems, and calculations.

Who handles South Carolina journeyman electrician trade certification?

The CAGC SC Trades Certification Program handles trade certification cards that were formerly associated with the Municipal Association of South Carolina Trade Certification Program. The CAGC Foundation oversees the process as of January 1, 2026.

Can the South Carolina journeyman electrician certification exam be taken online?

The CAGC SC Trades Certification Program states that exams may be taken online or in person at an approved testing facility. Candidates should follow the current scheduling and exam security instructions from PROV.

Yes. Flash cards are useful for quick review of formulas, definitions, electrical theory, safety concepts, NEC topics, and trade knowledge. They work well alongside longer study sessions and timed practice.
