The exam

Is the Nebraska Master Electrician exam open book?

Nebraska master electrician and electrical contractor preparation should include open-book code navigation and reference-use practice. Candidates should follow the current Nebraska State Electrical Division and PSI exam instructions for the exact references, tabs, notes, calculators, and code edition allowed during testing.

Is the Nebraska electrical contractor or master electrician exam open book?

Yes. The NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination used through the Nebraska State Electrical Division pathway is identified as an open book examination. Candidates must follow the current exam bulletin rules for approved references, permanent tabs, highlighting, underlining, indexing, and written notes.

How do I schedule a Nebraska electrical exam?

After the Nebraska State Electrical Division approves the applicant to test, eligibility is submitted to PSI. PSI then emails the candidate with scheduling instructions and access to the candidate bulletin.

Who administers Nebraska electrical exams?

Nebraska electrical examination eligibility is handled through the Nebraska State Electrical Division and Nebraska State Electrical Board. PSI administers Electrical Contractor, Journeyman, and Residential Wireman testing after a candidate is approved.

How many questions are on the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician exam?

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination is listed with 100 scored questions, plus 10 non-scored experimental questions.

How much time is allowed for the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician exam?

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination is listed with 270 minutes of testing time.

What score is required to pass?

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination is listed with a minimum passing score of 75.

Is the Nebraska master electrician exam administered through PSI?

Yes. Nebraska electrical exams are scheduled through PSI after the candidate submits an exam application and receives approval to test through the Nebraska State Electrical Division.

Licensing and experience

Who regulates electrician licensing in Nebraska?

Electrical licensing in Nebraska is administered by the Nebraska State Electrical Division under the authority of the Nebraska State Electrical Board.

What are Nebraska Electrical Contractor qualification pathways?

Nebraska identifies pathways that include a four-year electrical engineering course with at least one year of acceptable journeyman experience, at least one year of acceptable experience as a licensed journeyman electrician, or at least five years of acceptable experience in planning, laying out, supervising, and installing electrical wiring, apparatus, or equipment.

Does Nebraska require state electrical licensing?

Yes. Nebraska uses a state electrical licensing system through the Nebraska State Electrical Division. The state licenses multiple classifications, including contractor, master, journeyman, residential, and fire alarm classifications.

Is Nebraska electrical licensing statewide?

Yes. Nebraska electrical licensing is administered statewide through the Nebraska State Electrical Division and the Nebraska State Electrical Board.

What experience is required for a Nebraska Electrical Contractor license?

Nebraska identifies a pathway requiring at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a licensed journeyman electrician. A graduate of a four-year electrical engineering course from an accredited college or university must also have at least one year of acceptable licensed journeyman electrician experience.

What topics are covered on the Nebraska electrical licensing exam?

The Nebraska State Electrical Division identifies exam coverage that includes the National Electrical Code, Basic Electricity and Theory, the Nebraska State Electrical Act, Blueprint Reading, and Emergency Circuits, including Fire Alarm Circuits. The NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician content outline also includes project design and management, safety, electrical theory, general code requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, and communication systems.

Does Nebraska have state electrical licensing?

Yes. Nebraska electrical licensing is handled at the state level through the Nebraska State Electrical Division and the State Electrical Board.

Does a Nebraska master-level license automatically replace an electrical contractor license?

No. Nebraska has separate electrical license classifications, including master and electrical contractor classifications. Candidates who plan to operate an electrical contracting business should review the Nebraska electrical contractor requirements for the classification they need.

Should I confirm Nebraska licensing requirements before testing?

Yes. Candidates should confirm the required license classification, eligibility requirements, application process, code edition, reference rules, and post-exam licensing steps before scheduling or taking an exam.

Why is calculation practice important for this exam?

Calculation practice is important because electrical exams can include load calculations, conductor sizing, service and feeder work, box fill, raceway fill, motor calculations, transformer calculations, electrical theory, and other math-based topics. A dedicated calculations study guide helps candidates practice these skills consistently.

Can I bring these books into the exam room?

More questions

What is a Class A Master Electrician in Nebraska?

A Class A Master Electrician is a person with the qualifications, training, experience, and technical knowledge to properly plan, lay out, and supervise the installation of wiring, apparatus, and equipment for electric light, heat, power, and other purposes.

What subjects are covered on Nebraska electrical exams?

Nebraska identifies exam coverage that includes the National Electrical Code, basic electricity theory, the Nebraska State Electrical Act, blueprint reading, and emergency circuits including fire alarm circuits.

Who provides Nebraska electrical testing?

PSI provides testing services for Nebraska electrical examination categories assigned by the Nebraska State Electrical Division, including Electrical Contractor, Journeyman, and Residential Wireman exams.
