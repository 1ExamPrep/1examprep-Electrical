Exam Details

The Maryland Journeyperson Electrician exam is administered by PSI Services LLC for the Maryland State Board of Electricians. PSI provides examination registration, approval processing, scheduling, test administration, and score reporting. Candidates apply through PSI for permission to sit for the examination, upload required documentation, and receive scheduling instructions after approval.

The Maryland Journeyperson Electrician exam contains 70 questions, requires 49 correct answers for a 70% passing score, and has a time allowance of 210 minutes. The examination tests knowledge of applicable codes, laws, and principles of electrical installation and is designed to determine fitness for a Maryland state journeyperson electrician license.

The Maryland Journeyperson Electrician content outline includes electrical theory, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, and special communications. Electrical theory includes definitions, theory including Ohm’s Law, techniques, troubleshooting, equipment and devices, instruments, materials, math, conversions, diagrams, and symbols.

Wiring and protection is one of the largest exam areas and includes NEC Article 100, Article 110, Articles 200 through 210, Articles 215 through 225, Article 230, Article 240, and Articles 250 through 285. Candidates should study definitions, general installation requirements, branch circuits, feeders, services, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, and surge-related protection topics.

Wiring methods and materials include NEC Article 300, Articles 310 through 314, Articles 320 through 330, Articles 332 through 356, Articles 358 through 374, and Articles 376 through 398. Candidates should study conductor requirements, raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, support rules, physical protection, conductor fill, box fill, and permitted wiring methods.

Equipment for general use includes flexible cords, switches, receptacles, appliances, fixed electric space-heating equipment, motors, transformers, capacitors, phase converters, and other general-use equipment areas. Special occupancies include hazardous locations, health care facilities, manufactured buildings, mobile homes, temporary installations, and similar installation-specific topics. Special equipment and special communications cover selected NEC articles related to signs, electric vehicle power transfer, pools, emergency systems, communications systems, and related specialty installations.

The current Maryland PSI bulletin lists the 2020 National Electrical Code as the only reference allowed in the testing room for the current exam cycle. This product is based on the 2026 NEC and is designed for current code study, updated code familiarity, and long-term electrical reference practice. Candidates taking a scheduled exam should use the exact NEC edition and reference rules listed by PSI for that exam appointment.

Open Book Test

The Maryland Journeyperson Electrician exam is an open-book examination. Candidates may bring the approved reference book into the testing room, but study guides are not allowed during the exam. The approved reference is not provided at the test center, so candidates are responsible for bringing the allowed reference listed in the PSI bulletin.

Reference books may be indexed and may contain highlighted or underlined text. All materials must be unmarked, meaning the reference book may not contain handwritten notes. The reference book also may not contain additional papers, whether loose or attached. Candidates should prepare their exam reference carefully before test day and follow PSI’s book-preparation rules.

Permanent tabs are allowed when they meet PSI’s requirements. Acceptable tab examples listed by PSI include Avery Swift Tabs Self-Adhesive Permanent Plastic Tabs and Redi-Tag Self-Stick Permanent Adhesive Index Tabs. Post-It Index Flags and Post-It Flags are not acceptable. If a PSI proctor can remove the tabs without ripping the page, the candidate must remove the tabs before taking the exam. Tabs with paper inserts are not allowed.

The open-book format does not remove the need to study. Candidates still need to understand electrical concepts, know where to look in the NEC, manage the exam clock, and apply the correct rule to the question. A candidate who is unfamiliar with the code book may lose valuable time searching. A candidate who has practiced consistently with the NEC can move more efficiently through the exam.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs included in this combo is useful for building open-book study habits. Students can use it to learn NEC structure, mark key areas, practice locating rules, review tables, and connect exam topics to code language. For the actual examination, candidates should bring the exact NEC edition and tabs allowed by the current Maryland PSI instructions.

Licensing Steps

The Maryland journeyperson electrician licensing path begins with meeting the state’s qualification requirements. To take the journeyperson electrician license examination, an applicant must have been engaged or employed regularly and principally in providing electrical services for all types of electrical equipment and apparatuses in training to become a master electrician for at least four years under the direction and supervision of a Maryland licensed master electrician or a similarly qualified employee of a governmental unit.

The Board waives the experience requirement for a journeyperson electrician license applicant who provides written proof of successful completion of an electrician apprenticeship program approved by the Maryland Apprenticeship and Training Council or the Federal Office of Apprenticeship. That apprenticeship program must include at least 576 classroom hours and 8,000 hours of work experience, and the applicant must have received the certificate within two years of applying to the Board.

After meeting the eligibility requirements, the applicant applies through PSI to sit for the journeyperson electrician examination. PSI reviews the submitted documentation. Once approved, the candidate receives instructions for paying the examination fee and scheduling the test appointment. Maryland PSI testing centers are listed in locations including Baltimore, College Park, Hagerstown, Lanham, and Salisbury.

After passing the examination, the candidate applies for the appropriate license through the Maryland State Board of Electricians. Passing the examination is an important step, but the license is issued through the state application process. Applicants should follow the Board’s licensing instructions and submit required supporting documents within the required application window.

Candidates should keep documentation organized throughout the process. Useful records include work-experience certification, apprenticeship completion documentation, formal electrical training records, PSI approval information, examination results, and Maryland licensing application materials. A complete document file can help reduce delays during examination approval and final license application review.

State Requirements

The Maryland State Board of Electricians is responsible for qualifications, examination, licensing, enforcement, and discipline for Master, Journeyperson, and Apprentice Electricians. Maryland law and Board rules are designed to support public safety by requiring electrical work to be performed by properly licensed electrical professionals.

Maryland states that all electrical work must be performed by a licensed Master, Journeyperson, or Apprentice Electrician. The journeyperson license is an individual license tied to a worker’s experience, training, examination, and state approval. It is an important credential for electrical workers advancing beyond apprentice status and toward higher levels of professional responsibility.

For the journeyperson examination path, Maryland requires at least four years of qualifying electrical work experience under the direction and supervision of a Maryland licensed master electrician or a similarly qualified employee of a governmental unit. The Board may waive the experience requirement for applicants who successfully complete an approved apprenticeship program with at least 576 classroom hours and 8,000 hours of work experience and who apply within the required time after receiving the certificate.

Maryland also provides licensing application options for reciprocity and waiver situations. Reciprocal journeyperson electrician applications currently identify Virginia, Delaware, and the District of Columbia as reciprocity states, with specific conditions tied to how the license was obtained. Candidates using reciprocity or waiver pathways should follow the state’s exact application instructions for that path.
