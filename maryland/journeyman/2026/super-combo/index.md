## Open Book Test

The Maryland Journeyperson Electrician examination is an open book test. The current Maryland PSI bulletin states that candidates may bring reference books into the examination and that no study guides are allowed. The bulletin also states that the National Electrical Code is the only reference allowed in the testing room.

The Maryland PSI bulletin allows reference books to be indexed and to contain highlighting or underlining. All materials must be unmarked, meaning not written in, and may not contain additional loose or attached papers. The bulletin identifies acceptable tabs and does not allow Post-it index flags or Post-it flags. The reference book is not provided at the test center, so candidates must bring the allowed reference listed in the active bulletin.

## Licensing Steps

The Maryland journeyperson electrician licensing path begins with meeting the qualifications to sit for the examination. To take the journeyperson electrician license examination, the applicant must have been engaged or employed regularly and principally in providing electrical services for all types of electrical equipment and apparatuses for at least four years while under the direction and supervision of a master electrician or a similarly qualified employee of a governmental unit.

After meeting the required experience standard, the applicant applies to PSI to sit for the Maryland journeyperson electrician exam. PSI reviews the required documentation and, after approval, provides instructions for paying and scheduling the examination. PSI testing centers are available in Maryland locations listed by PSI and the Maryland Department of Labor.

The candidate then studies the exam content outline, approved reference material, open-book rules, and test-day procedures. Preparation should include electrical theory review, NEC article navigation, wiring and protection study, wiring methods and materials review, equipment rules, special occupancies, special equipment, special communication systems, and calculation practice.

After passing the examination, the candidate applies to the Maryland State Board of Electricians for the appropriate journeyperson electrician license. Passing the exam is an important step, but the applicant must still complete the Board’s licensing process and provide the required application materials.

Maryland also provides a journeyperson license pathway by apprenticeship program. The State Board may waive the experience and examination requirements for an applicant who provides written proof of successful completion of an electrician apprenticeship program approved by the Maryland Apprenticeship and Training Council or the Federal Office of Apprenticeship. The approved program must include at least 576 classroom hours and 8,000 hours of work experience, and the certificate must be received within two years of applying to the Board.
