Exam Details

The Maryland Journeyperson Electrician examination is administered through PSI for the Maryland State Board of Electricians. The exam tests knowledge of applicable codes, laws, and principles of electrical installation and is used to help determine fitness for a Maryland state electrician license. Applicants must complete the examination registration process through PSI and submit the required documentation before approval to schedule.

The Maryland Journeyperson Electrician exam is listed with 70 questions, a testing time of 210 minutes, and a required passing score of 70%. A small number of experimental questions may also be administered during the examination. Experimental questions are not scored and do not count against the candidate’s examination time.

The Maryland Journeyperson Electrician content areas include electrical theory, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, communication systems, and calculations. These subjects reflect the knowledge a journeyperson electrician needs when working with electrical systems, equipment, conductors, raceways, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding, bonding, and other code-based installation conditions.

This exam requires both electrical understanding and careful test management. A candidate may need to answer direct theory questions, apply NEC concepts, interpret code-based installation rules, and work math-based electrical problems. Licensing Steps

Maryland journeyperson electrician candidates begin by meeting the experience or approved training requirements for the license. A candidate preparing for the examination must have been engaged or employed regularly and principally in providing electrical services for all types of electrical equipment and apparatus for at least four years under the direction and supervision of a Maryland licensed master electrician or a similarly qualified employee of a governmental unit.

Applicants may also qualify through an approved electrician apprenticeship program. Maryland recognizes programs approved by the Maryland Apprenticeship and Training Council or the Federal Office of Apprenticeship that consist of at least 576 classroom hours and 8,000 hours of work experience. When the approved apprenticeship certificate is received within the required time period, the Board may waive the experience and examination requirements for the journeyperson license application.

Candidates who are taking the examination apply through PSI for the Maryland Journeyperson Electrician exam. Required documentation is submitted through the PSI registration process. After PSI receives and processes the documentation, the candidate receives approval instructions to pay for and schedule the examination.

After passing the examination, the candidate applies to the Maryland State Board of Electricians for the journeyperson electrician license. Passing the exam is an important step, but the licensing process is not complete until the Board’s application requirements are satisfied and the license is issued.

A strong licensing plan begins early. Candidates should organize experience records, apprenticeship documentation, classroom training records when applicable, and any required certification of experience. Exam preparation is usually strongest when students study over time through trade review, NEC navigation, and calculation practice instead of waiting until the exam date is close.

State Requirements

Maryland journeyperson electrician applicants must satisfy the state’s licensing requirements through the Maryland State Board of Electricians. To take the journeyperson electrician license examination, an applicant must have at least 4 years of qualifying electrical experience providing electrical services for all types of electrical equipment and apparatus under the direction and supervision of a Maryland licensed master electrician or a similarly qualified employee of a governmental unit.

The State Board may allow credit toward the experience requirement for applicants who have completed formal electrical installation study or professional training comparable to the required experience. Applicants using education or training credit should provide the documentation required during the examination registration process.

Maryland also recognizes an approved apprenticeship pathway. The Board may waive the experience and examination requirements for a journeyperson electrician license applicant who provides written proof of successful completion of an electrician apprenticeship program approved by the Maryland Apprenticeship and Training Council or the Federal Office of Apprenticeship. The approved program must include at least 576 classroom hours and 8,000 hours of work experience, and the certificate must be received within the required application period.

A journeyperson electrician license is different from a master electrician license. The journeyperson license supports individual electrical work within Maryland’s licensing framework, while the master electrician license carries a higher level of responsibility and requires a separate experience and examination path. Apprentice licensing is also separate and applies to workers gaining supervised experience.

That includes electrical installation knowledge, practical safety awareness, code understanding, calculation ability, and the confidence to apply electrical rules to real-world installation questions.
