## Exam Details

Maryland electrician licensing is handled through the Maryland State Board of Electricians under the Maryland Department of Labor. PSI Services LLC administers the Maryland Master and Journeyperson Electrician examinations for the Board. Before making a final application to the Board for a journeyperson license, a candidate is required to pass the written examination unless the candidate qualifies through an approved waiver pathway.

The Maryland Journeyperson Electrician exam contains 70 questions. The passing score is 49 correct answers, which is 70%. The time allowed is 210 minutes. The examination tests knowledge of applicable codes, laws, and principles of electrical installation and is designed to determine readiness for a Maryland state journeyperson electrician license.

The published Maryland Journeyperson Electrician exam content outline includes Electrical Theory, Wiring and Protection, Wiring Methods and Materials, Equipment for General Use, Special Occupancies, Special Equipment, and Special Communications. Electrical Theory includes definitions, theory including Ohm’s Law, techniques, troubleshooting, equipment and devices, instruments, materials, math, conversions, diagrams, and symbols. Wiring and Protection includes NEC Article 100, Article 110, Articles 200 through 210, Articles 215 through 225, Article 230, Article 240, and Articles 250 through 285.

The exam outline assigns 20 questions to Electrical Theory, 20 questions to Wiring and Protection, 12 questions to Wiring Methods and Materials, 6 questions to Equipment for General Use, 4 questions to Special Occupancies, 2 questions to Special Equipment, and 6 questions to Special Communications. Candidates should also review Ohm’s Law, electrical theory and mathematics, practical electrical field work practices, and local code rulings as applicable.

## Open Book Test

The Maryland Journeyperson Electrician exam is an open-book examination. Candidates may bring in reference books, but study guides are not allowed in the testing room. The approved reference is not provided at the test center, so candidates are responsible for bringing the permitted reference material on exam day.

Open book does not mean easy. Candidates still need to understand electrical concepts, recognize how exam questions are written, move through the approved reference efficiently, and answer questions within the time allowed. A student who depends only on searching through the code book for every answer can lose valuable time during the exam.

Maryland testing rules allow reference books to be indexed and to contain highlighted or underlined text. All materials must be unmarked, not written in, and may not contain additional papers, whether loose or attached. Permanent tabs may be allowed when they meet the testing-provider rules. Temporary tabs, removable flags, and tabs that can be removed without ripping the page may be rejected by the test center.

Students should use this study guide, practice exams, final exams, and flash cards before test day. During the actual exam, candidates should bring only the approved reference allowed for the Maryland Journeyperson Electrician exam and follow the testing-provider instructions for identification, reference preparation, calculator use, and test-center procedures.

A strong open-book strategy includes learning how the NEC is organized, practicing with article structure, using definitions carefully, studying tables, reviewing exceptions, and practicing calculations before the exam. Flash cards help students recall key ideas faster, while practice exams help them build the habit of reading questions carefully and choosing the correct code path.

## Licensing Steps

Maryland journeyperson electrician licensing begins with meeting the qualification requirements established by the State Board of Electricians. To take the journeyperson electrician license examination, an applicant must have been engaged or employed regularly and principally in providing electrical services for all types of electrical equipment and apparatuses for at least four years while under the direction and supervision of a Maryland licensed master electrician or a similarly qualified employee of a governmental unit.

Maryland also provides a pathway for applicants who completed an approved apprenticeship program. The State Board waives the experience requirements for an application for a journeyperson electrician license when the applicant provides written proof of successful completion of an electrician apprenticeship program approved by the Maryland Apprenticeship and Training Council or the Federal Office of Apprenticeship. The program must include at least 576 classroom hours and 8,000 hours of work experience, and the certificate must have been received within two years of applying to the Board.

Candidates applying by examination submit the required documentation through PSI’s online process. Once PSI receives and reviews the proper documentation, approved candidates receive confirmation with instructions on paying for and scheduling the examination. After passing the examination, the candidate applies to the Board for the journeyperson electrician license.

Maryland also recognizes certain reciprocal and waiver pathways. A journeyperson license obtained by examination in a local jurisdiction may be eligible for reciprocity when the applicant meets the state’s criteria, including having a valid local license or registration, obtaining it by examination, holding it for at least one year, and showing that it is a journeyperson-level license. Maryland also identifies a journeyperson reciprocity pathway for Virginia when the required criteria are met.

Students can use it while completing apprenticeship training, while documenting work experience, before applying for exam approval, after receiving scheduling approval, or during final review before the scheduled exam date.
