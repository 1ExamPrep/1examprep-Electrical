## Exam Details

The Maryland Master Electrician examination is administered through PSI for the Maryland State Board of Electricians. The examination tests the applicant’s knowledge of applicable codes, laws, and principles of electrical installation and is designed to help determine fitness for state licensure.

The Maryland Master Electrician exam includes 90 questions worth 100 points. The minimum passing score is 70%, and the time allowed is 240 minutes. Because the exam is timed, candidates need more than topic familiarity. They need to be able to read quickly, locate code material efficiently, solve calculations carefully, and manage the full testing session without losing time on one difficult question.

## Open Book Test

The Maryland Master Electrician examination is an open book test. Candidates may bring approved reference materials into the examination room, but study guides are not allowed as exam-room references. Reference materials may be indexed and may contain highlighted or underlined text, but they must be unmarked, meaning they may not contain written notes or additional loose or attached papers.

Open-book testing does not mean the exam is easy. Master electrician exams require candidates to locate code material quickly, read the correct section carefully, understand the question being asked, and apply the requirement accurately. ## Licensing Steps

Maryland master electrician licensing is handled through the Maryland State Board of Electricians. A typical path includes meeting the required experience standard, applying to PSI for examination approval, submitting required documentation, receiving approval to schedule, taking and passing the examination, and then applying to the Board for the appropriate license after passing.

To qualify to take the Maryland master electrician examination, an applicant must have been engaged or employed regularly and principally in providing electrical services for all types of electrical equipment and apparatuses for at least seven years under the direction and supervision of a Maryland licensed master electrician or a similarly qualified employee of a governmental unit.

The State Board may allow up to three years of credit toward the required experience with proof that the applicant completed a formal course of study or professional training in electrical installation comparable to the required experience. Candidates using education or training credit should keep documentation organized because review of formal study may require additional processing time.

After the candidate meets the examination requirements, the candidate applies through PSI and uploads the required documentation. Once PSI reviews and approves the examination application, the candidate receives instructions for paying and scheduling the examination. After passing the exam, the candidate applies to the Maryland State Board of Electricians for licensure.

Candidates should keep copies of all important records, including experience certification, formal training documentation, PSI approval, scheduling confirmation, identification information, exam result, and license application materials. Organized paperwork can help reduce delays between exam approval, testing, and final license application.

## State Requirements

The Maryland State Board of Electricians is responsible for licensing master, journeyperson, and apprentice electricians. Before applying for a Maryland master electrician license, candidates must satisfy the applicable experience requirements and pass the required examination unless they qualify through an approved waiver or reciprocity pathway.

Maryland provides waiver and reciprocity options in certain situations. A waiver may be available for an applicant who holds a local master registration and meets the Board’s requirements. Maryland also recognizes certain out-of-state master electrician applicants when they meet equivalent licensing standards and the applicable reciprocity conditions. These pathways are separate from standard examination preparation and are reviewed by the Board.

Maryland also issues apprentice and journeyperson electrician licenses. Journeyperson applicants have separate experience requirements, and apprentice applicants do not take an exam to obtain an apprentice license. Candidates should apply under the license classification that matches their experience, supervision history, and work goals.
