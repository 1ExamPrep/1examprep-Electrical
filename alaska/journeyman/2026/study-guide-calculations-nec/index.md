Before scheduling a test, the department must process and approve the applicant’s packet. ## Licensing Steps

Alaska issues an Electrical Journeyman Certificate of Fitness through the Department of Labor and Workforce Development, Mechanical Inspection section. A candidate begins by submitting an application packet and documentation for review. The department must process and approve the application packet before the candidate can schedule testing.

The application packet must include the current application, verification of the required hours for the license requested, and the required application or testing fee. Experience documentation must be provided on company letterhead or an Experience Verification Form and must be notarized. Self-verification is not accepted.

After the application packet is approved, the candidate may schedule the exam according to the department’s testing procedures. Testing is available by appointment at department testing locations and through approved proctored arrangements. A government-issued picture ID is required at the time of testing.

After testing, exam results are emailed to the candidate. A passing score allows the candidate to continue with the certificate process. If the candidate does not pass, Alaska requires a 30-day waiting period from the date of the last exam before retesting.

A good licensing plan should begin well before the exam date. Candidates should gather employment records, confirm qualifying hours, organize notarized verification forms, review the application instructions, and build a regular study schedule. The strongest preparation plans include trade review, code navigation, and calculation practice rather than relying on last-minute reading.

## State Requirements

The Alaska Electrical Journeyman Certificate of Fitness requires **8,000 hours** of work in the electrical trade. A minimum of **6,000 hours** must be commercial or industrial electrical work. Up to **2,000 hours** may be residential. No maintenance hours apply toward the electrical certificate requirements.

Alaska allows up to **1,000 hours** of trade-related classroom training to be substituted for 1,000 hours of the 6,000 commercial or industrial hour requirement. Candidates using classroom training should provide documentation that satisfies the state’s application requirements.

Experience verification must be documented properly. Alaska’s application materials require notarized verification of qualified hours. The employer verification section is used to document direct knowledge of the applicant’s employment and hours. Applicants should make sure forms are complete, accurate, and properly signed before submitting the packet.

The Electrical Journeyman Certificate of Fitness is separate from Alaska’s Electrical Administrator credential. The journeyman certificate supports individual journeyman-level qualification, while electrical administrator licensing is tied to broader supervisory and contracting responsibilities. Candidates should apply for the credential that matches their current experience and licensing goal.

Alaska electrical certificates are issued for a two-year period. Electrical journeymen are required to complete continuing education for renewal. Renewal rules and fee requirements should be followed according to the state’s current Mechanical Inspection instructions.
