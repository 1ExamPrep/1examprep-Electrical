Exam Details

New Mexico journeyman certification is handled through the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department, Construction Industries Division. PSI conducts the examination approval process and administers the examinations for New Mexico journeyman candidates. Candidates must be preapproved before scheduling an examination, and eligibility must be used within the period stated in the PSI and state instructions.

The EE-98J Journeyman Residential and Commercial Electrical classification includes Part 1 Code, Part 2 Theory, and a practical examination. The Part 1 Code examination is listed with 50 questions, a 75% passing score, and 145 minutes allowed. The Part 2 Theory examination is listed with 50 questions, a 75% passing score, and 120 minutes allowed. The practical examination is listed with 100 points, a 75% passing score, and 120 minutes allowed.

The EE-98J Part 1 Code examination includes General Knowledge and Electrical Installation Requirements, Services, Feeders and Branch Circuits, Overcurrent Protection, Grounding and Bonding, Conductors and Cables, Raceways and Boxes, Hazardous Locations, Special Occupancies and Special Equipment, Lighting, Signs and General-Use Equipment, Motors, Transformers and Generators, and New Mexico State Code.

The EE-98J Part 2 Theory examination includes General Knowledge and Electrical Installation Requirements, Services, Feeders and Branch Circuits, Overcurrent Protection, Grounding and Bonding, Conductors and Cables, Raceways and Boxes, Hazardous Locations, Special Occupancies and Special Equipment, Lighting, Signs and General-Use Equipment, and Low Voltage, including Alarms.

The EE-98J practical examination includes hands-on point categories such as a motor control project, transformer project, doorbell project, lighting project, simple conduit bending, complex conduit bending, and materials identification. Candidates should prepare for both written knowledge and practical trade tasks because the New Mexico journeyman process is not limited to multiple-choice questions.

Open Book Test

The New Mexico EE-98J Part 1 Code examination is open book. The EE-98J Part 2 Theory examination is closed book. The practical examination measures hands-on electrical skills and should be prepared for as a separate practical trade evaluation. Because the exam process includes both reference-based and closed-book testing, students should prepare with two goals: fast code navigation for Part 1 and strong memory-based understanding for Part 2 and practical work.

For the open-book portion, candidates are responsible for bringing approved references to the testing center. Reference materials must be bound and may be highlighted, underlined, or indexed before the exam session. References containing writing are not allowed. Candidates may not write in references during the exam session. Additional papers, whether loose or attached, are not permitted with approved references. Permanent tabs are allowed, but temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not allowed.

Open book does not mean easy. Candidates still need to understand electrical concepts, recognize how questions are written, locate information quickly, apply code rules correctly, and manage time. A student who depends only on searching through references for every answer can lose valuable time during the exam.

For the closed-book theory portion, students need stronger recall of electrical fundamentals, general installation requirements, low-voltage and alarm concepts, grounding and bonding, conductors, raceways, boxes, overcurrent protection, and theory-based trade knowledge. Flash cards are especially helpful for this portion because they help students practice active recall instead of passive reading.

A strong New Mexico strategy includes learning where major NEC topics are located, practicing article navigation, reviewing New Mexico Electrical Code rules, memorizing core theory concepts, and practicing hands-on trade tasks when practical testing is part of the candidate’s exam path. This combo supports that preparation with multiple review methods before test day.

Licensing Steps

New Mexico journeyman certification begins with meeting the qualification requirements for the classification the applicant is pursuing. For the EE-98J Journeyman Residential and Commercial Electrical classification, the common experience requirement is four years, or 8,000 hours, of qualifying electrical experience. Other New Mexico journeyman classifications may require different experience levels, so applicants should apply for the classification that matches their work history and career goal.

A typical New Mexico journeyman path includes completing supervised electrical experience, maintaining accurate work records, gathering verification documents, submitting the required application materials, receiving approval from the Construction Industries Division process, scheduling the required examinations through PSI, passing the required written and practical examinations, and completing the remaining certification steps.

Candidates should keep records of employment dates, job duties, hours worked, supervising personnel, apprenticeship participation when applicable, related classroom training, employer verification, and any documents required by the state or PSI. Accurate records help support the application process and reduce delays when the candidate is ready to seek examination approval.

Once a candidate becomes eligible, the candidate must follow PSI’s scheduling instructions and take the required examinations within the eligibility period. Passing scores are valid for the period stated in the current PSI bulletin. Candidates who fail an examination must follow the retesting rules before attempting the same exam again.

New Mexico contractor licensing is separate from journeyman certification. A journeyman certification supports individual trade qualification, while contractor licensing is tied to business operations, contracting authority, classification requirements, bonding, insurance, and additional examination requirements. Candidates should understand whether their goal is journeyman certification, contractor licensing, or both.
