## Exam Details

New Mexico journeyman examinations are administered through PSI for the New Mexico Construction Industries Division. Candidates must be approved before taking a journeyman examination. The application process requires the applicant to determine the proper certificate classification, submit the required application materials, and provide proof of qualifying experience or approved training for the classification requested.

For many electricians seeking residential and commercial journeyman certification, the relevant classification is **EE-98J Journeyman Residential and Commercial Electrical**. This classification is tied to residential and commercial electrical work and is commonly associated with PSI examination components covering electrical code knowledge, trade application, and practical electrical skills.

The New Mexico EE-98J journeyman residential and commercial electrical code examination is commonly listed with **80 scored questions**, a testing time of **200 minutes**, and a required passing score of **75%**. Exam content areas commonly include general knowledge and electrical installation requirements, services, feeders, branch circuits, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, hazardous locations, special occupancies, special equipment, lighting, signs, general-use equipment, motors, transformers, generators, and New Mexico state code content.

New Mexico’s journeyman residential and commercial electrical practical examination is a separate practical-style component associated with the EE-98J classification. It commonly includes project-based subjects such as motor control, transformer work, doorbell work, lighting, simple conduit bending, complex conduit bending, and materials identification. Candidates should review the current PSI candidate information bulletin for the exact examination components required for their classification.

This exam path requires both electrical understanding and careful test management. ## Licensing Steps

New Mexico journeyman electrician candidates begin by identifying the correct certificate classification for the type of electrical work they intend to perform. For residential and commercial electrical work, many candidates pursue the EE-98J Journeyman Residential and Commercial Electrical classification.

After identifying the proper classification, the candidate gathers documentation showing the required experience or approved training. New Mexico requires applicants for journeyman certification to provide proof of work experience in the classification for which the application is made. Technical training from approved or accredited programs may be considered in place of part of the work experience requirement, subject to state limits.

The candidate submits the journeyman certificate application through the Construction Industries Division or its designee. The application must be complete and include the required forms, experience documentation, and certification fee. Incomplete applications may be rejected and returned with an explanation of the issue.

After the application is reviewed and the candidate is approved, the candidate schedules the required examination through PSI. Candidates should review the PSI candidate information bulletin for the classification they are testing for, including the exam format, examination components, approved references, identification requirements, calculator rules, testing procedures, score reporting, and retake rules.

After passing the required examination components, the candidate completes the remaining certification requirements through the New Mexico Construction Industries Division. Passing the exam is an important step, but the certification process is not complete until all state requirements are satisfied and the journeyman certificate is issued.

## State Requirements

New Mexico requires electrical work to be performed by, or under the supervision of, a certified journeyman for the classification of work being performed. The Construction Industries Division oversees journeyman certification for electrical, mechanical, and plumbing classifications. Electrical candidates should apply for the certificate classification that matches the work they plan to perform.

A journeyman may engage in the trade authorized by the certificate of competence only when employed by a properly licensed entity or an entity holding a valid annual permit authorizing that type of work. This means the journeyman certificate is an individual trade certification, while contractor licensing and business authorization are handled separately.

New Mexico’s journeyman certification process requires applicants to provide proof of required experience within the 10 years immediately before application, unless a recognized alternate or expedited path applies. Training classified as technical training by an accredited college, university, manufacturer’s accredited training program, technical vocational institute, or accredited apprenticeship program may be considered in place of work experience. Each year of equivalent training may be applied as one-half year of experience, but accredited training cannot exceed one-half of the total work experience requirement.

For the EE-98J residential and commercial electrical journeyman path, candidates should be prepared to document substantial electrical experience and training connected to residential and commercial electrical work. Candidates should use the current New Mexico application instructions for the exact documentation required for their classification.

New Mexico also has continuing education requirements for journeyman electrician and journeyman lineman certificate renewal. The state requires continuing education during the three-year period between electrical code updates, including code change instruction and other industry-related instruction. License holders should follow the current Construction Industries Division continuing education rules for their classification.
