Exam Details

New Mexico journeyman certification examinations are administered through PSI after the candidate is approved to test. Candidates must apply for the proper journeyman certificate classification and receive approval before scheduling the examination. Electrical journeyman classifications in New Mexico include classifications tied to the type of work being performed, such as residential and commercial electrical work, residential wiring, electrical distribution systems, and specialty electrical classifications.

The commonly referenced residential and commercial electrical journeyman classification is EE-98J. Candidates preparing for this classification should be ready for NEC-based code questions, electrical installation requirements, electrical theory, calculation work, and practical trade knowledge. New Mexico journeyman electrical testing may include multiple parts depending on the classification, including code-based examination content and practical examination content.

New Mexico journeyman electrical exam preparation should include general electrical knowledge, electrical installation requirements, wiring methods, conductors, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, services, feeders, branch circuits, motors, transformers, lighting, controls, equipment use, safety practices, and calculation work. Candidates should also be familiar with the New Mexico Construction Industries Division process for journeyman certification and the classification that matches the work they intend to perform.

The PSI candidate information process provides examination scheduling instructions, testing rules, exam format, allowed references, and any classification-specific details. Candidates should use the exam bulletin and admission instructions for the specific New Mexico journeyman classification they are taking. Because electrical exams are timed, candidates should study both content and strategy. Code-based questions require the ability to find rules efficiently. Open Book Test

The New Mexico journeyman electrician examination process uses open-book reference rules for applicable examination parts. Candidates must follow PSI and New Mexico Construction Industries Division instructions for approved references, code edition, tab rules, calculator rules, and prohibited materials. The allowed materials can depend on the journeyman classification and the specific examination part being taken.

Open book does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates still need to know how to read the question, identify the subject, locate the correct reference, interpret the code language, and answer within the time limit. A candidate who is familiar with the NEC can spend less time searching and more time solving the question.

Reference rules may address whether tabs must be permanent, whether notes are allowed, whether highlighting is permitted, what calculator may be used, and whether other materials are prohibited. Licensing Steps

New Mexico candidates should begin by determining the journeyman certificate classification required for the work they intend to perform. The classification matters because electrical, mechanical, and plumbing work must be performed by, or under the supervision of, a certified journeyman for the classification of work being performed.

A practical path begins with identifying the correct journeyman electrical classification, documenting the required work experience, completing the journeyman certificate application, submitting the application to the Construction Industries Division, receiving approval to test, scheduling the examination through PSI, preparing with the applicable exam content and references, passing the required examination, and completing the certification process.

For the New Mexico EE-98J residential and commercial electrical journeyman classification, candidates commonly prepare for broad residential and commercial electrical work. Candidates should review the state’s requirements for work experience and classification-specific documentation before applying. The state reviews the application and determines whether the candidate is approved for examination.

After approval, PSI provides the examination scheduling process. Candidates should schedule the correct exam for their classification, arrive with required identification, follow testing rules, and complete the exam according to PSI procedures. Passing the examination is an important milestone, but certification is completed through the state process after all requirements are satisfied.

State Requirements

New Mexico requires journeyman certification for electrical work performed under the applicable classification. The Construction Industries Division administers journeyman certification and contractor licensing programs, while the Electrical Bureau supports electrical code compliance, licensing, permitting, and inspection functions.

Journeyman electrical applicants must qualify under the classification they are pursuing. The residential and commercial electrical journeyman classification is commonly associated with substantial documented work experience in the electrical trade. Candidates should maintain accurate records of employers, dates, duties, supervised work, and classification-related experience so the state can review eligibility.

New Mexico’s journeyman structure is classification-based. This means a candidate should not assume that one journeyman classification covers all types of electrical work. Candidates should review the classification that matches the work they intend to perform and prepare for the exam associated with that classification.

New Mexico also licenses contractors separately from journeyman certificate holders. A contractor license does not replace the need for properly certified journeyman supervision or performance of work in the required classification. Candidates who later plan to operate as contractors should review the contractor licensing path in addition to the journeyman certification path.

Study materials help candidates prepare, but certification decisions are made through the New Mexico Construction Industries Division based on the applicant’s classification, documentation, exam results, and compliance with state requirements.

Grounding and bonding are important areas for journeyman exam preparation. Motors, transformers, controls, and equipment should also be part of the study routine. Practical examination preparation should include understanding how electrical work is performed, not just where the rule appears in the NEC. Candidates should be comfortable with wiring layouts, basic troubleshooting logic, circuit identification, conductor termination concepts, device installation, and safe work practices. Practical knowledge works best when combined with code knowledge and calculation ability.

Flash cards are useful for shorter study sessions. Flash cards work best when paired with deeper reading, calculation drills, and code lookup practice.

A strong study plan should include scheduled reading, NEC navigation, calculation drills, timed practice, and repeated review of weaker topics.
