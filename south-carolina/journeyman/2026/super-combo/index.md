## Exam Details

South Carolina journeyman-level electrician certification is connected to the CAGC SC Trades Certification Program. As of January 1, 2026, the CAGC Foundation oversees the trade certification process that was formerly managed through the Municipal Association of South Carolina. The CAGC SC Trade Certification card confirms that the cardholder has satisfied the experience and written examination qualifications required by the program for the level of certification indicated.

The CAGC trade certification card is not the same as a South Carolina Mechanical Contractors License or a South Carolina Residential Builders Commission Specialty Contractors License. Candidates who plan to perform commercial contracting work, residential specialty contracting work, or business operations should confirm whether they also need a license through the South Carolina Contractors Licensing Board or the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission.

Applicants for a new CAGC SC Trades Certification complete the application process, pay the application fee, and wait for review. Once approved, the applicant receives notification from PROV, the CAGC Foundation’s contracted examination provider. Applicants are responsible for scheduling their own exam date with PROV. Exams may be taken online or in person at an approved testing facility.

South Carolina also has residential electrical licensing through the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission. Residential Specialty Contractors taking the electrical technical examination are tested through the Commission’s examination program. The residential electrical technical examination is listed as a 60-question examination, and applicants must meet the passing score established by the Commission’s examination instructions. Residential candidates may also need to complete the Business Management and Law examination depending on the license path.

Journeyman-level electrical preparation should include general electrical knowledge, services, service equipment, feeders, branch circuits, conductors, wiring methods and materials, raceways, boxes, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, equipment for general use, motors, transformers, control devices, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, safety, and electrical calculations.

## Open Book Test

South Carolina electrical exam reference rules depend on the specific credential, licensing body, and testing provider. Journeyman-level and contractor-style electrical exams are commonly prepared for as open-book, NEC-based exams, but candidates must follow the exact instructions issued for the exam they are taking.

For CAGC SC Trades Certification, approved applicants test through PROV. Candidates should follow PROV’s current exam instructions for allowed references, whether materials may be used during the exam, whether tabs are permitted, whether notes or highlighting are allowed, what calculator may be used, and whether the exam is taken remotely or in person. Remote testing may include additional rules for camera, browser, identification, workspace, and exam security.

For residential electrical licensing through the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission, candidates should follow the Commission and PSI candidate bulletin for the approved reference list, code edition, tab rules, calculator rules, and prohibited materials. Business Management and Law rules may differ from the technical electrical exam rules.

Open book does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates still need to know how to read the question, identify the subject, locate the correct article or table, interpret the code language, and answer within the time limit. A candidate who is comfortable with the NEC can spend less time searching and more time solving the question.

## Licensing Steps

South Carolina candidates should begin by identifying the correct credential for their work goals. A journeyman-level trade certification card, a residential electrical license, and a commercial contractor license are not the same credential. The correct path depends on whether the candidate wants individual trade certification, residential specialty work authority, commercial contractor licensing, business authority, or advancement toward a master-level credential.

For CAGC SC Trades Certification, candidates complete the new certification application in full, submit the application fee, and wait for the application review. After approval, the candidate receives notification from PROV and schedules the examination. The candidate then takes the exam online or in person at an approved testing facility. If the candidate does not pass or allows the exam voucher to expire, the retesting process is handled through the program’s current retest instructions.

For residential electrical licensing, candidates follow the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission process. This may include meeting the Commission’s application, experience, and examination requirements, applying to the state, taking the required technical examination, completing any required business examination, and satisfying the licensing steps required before performing regulated residential electrical work.

For commercial electrical contracting, candidates should follow the South Carolina Contractors Licensing Board process. The contractor licensing path applies to commercial work and business operations that require a contractor license. Contractor applicants may need to meet separate classification, examination, financial, business, and license requirements.

Candidates should keep accurate records of their experience, training, employer information, certification applications, exam approvals, score reports, renewal records, and license or certification documents. South Carolina’s structure makes it important to know the difference between trade certification, residential licensing, and commercial contractor licensing before performing regulated work.

Grounding and bonding are important areas for journeyman exam preparation. Candidates should review hazardous locations, health care facilities, emergency systems, standby systems, swimming pools, signs, mobile homes, RV parks, data processing equipment, fire alarm systems, and low-voltage systems when these areas appear in the applicable exam outline.

Flash cards are useful for shorter study sessions. Flash cards work best when paired with deeper reading, calculation drills, and code lookup practice.
