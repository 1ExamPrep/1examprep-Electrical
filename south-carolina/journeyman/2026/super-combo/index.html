Exam Details

South Carolina journeyman-level electrician certification is connected to the CAGC SC Trades Certification Program. As of January 1, 2026, the CAGC Foundation oversees the trade certification process that was formerly managed through the Municipal Association of South Carolina. The CAGC SC Trade Certification card confirms that the cardholder has satisfied the experience and written examination qualifications required by the program for the level of certification indicated.

The CAGC trade certification card is not the same as a South Carolina Mechanical Contractors License or a South Carolina Residential Builders Commission Specialty Contractors License. Candidates who plan to perform commercial contracting work, residential specialty contracting work, or business operations should confirm whether they also need a license through the South Carolina Contractors Licensing Board or the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission.

Applicants for a new CAGC SC Trades Certification complete the application process, pay the application fee, and wait for review. Once approved, the applicant receives notification from PROV, the CAGC Foundation’s contracted examination provider. Applicants are responsible for scheduling their own exam date with PROV. Exams may be taken online or in person at an approved testing facility.

South Carolina also has residential electrical licensing through the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission. Residential Specialty Contractors taking the electrical technical examination are tested through the Commission’s examination program. The residential electrical technical examination is listed as a 60-question examination, and applicants must meet the passing score established by the Commission’s examination instructions. Residential candidates may also need to complete the Business Management and Law examination depending on the license path.

Journeyman-level electrical preparation should include general electrical knowledge, services, service equipment, feeders, branch circuits, conductors, wiring methods and materials, raceways, boxes, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, equipment for general use, motors, transformers, control devices, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, safety, and electrical calculations.

This Super Combo is based on the 2026 National Electrical Code for candidates who want current-code study materials and strong NEC navigation practice. Candidates should follow the current CAGC, PROV, South Carolina Contractors Licensing Board, or South Carolina Residential Builders Commission instructions for the exact exam they are approved to take, including the adopted code edition, approved materials, scheduling process, and exam rules.

Open Book Test

South Carolina electrical exam reference rules depend on the specific credential, licensing body, and testing provider. Journeyman-level and contractor-style electrical exams are commonly prepared for as open-book, NEC-based exams, but candidates must follow the exact instructions issued for the exam they are taking.

For CAGC SC Trades Certification, approved applicants test through PROV. Candidates should follow PROV’s current exam instructions for allowed references, whether materials may be used during the exam, whether tabs are permitted, whether notes or highlighting are allowed, what calculator may be used, and whether the exam is taken remotely or in person. Remote testing may include additional rules for camera, browser, identification, workspace, and exam security.

For residential electrical licensing through the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission, candidates should follow the Commission and PSI candidate bulletin for the approved reference list, code edition, tab rules, calculator rules, and prohibited materials. Business Management and Law rules may differ from the technical electrical exam rules.

Open book does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates still need to know how to read the question, identify the subject, locate the correct article or table, interpret the code language, and answer within the time limit. A candidate who is comfortable with the NEC can spend less time searching and more time solving the question.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs included in this Super Combo is intended for study and code navigation practice. Tabs help candidates become familiar with major NEC articles, definitions, tables, and commonly used sections. The strongest preparation comes from using the tabs as a navigation aid while also learning how the code is organized and how common exam topics connect to specific NEC articles.

Licensing Steps

South Carolina candidates should begin by identifying the correct credential for their work goals. A journeyman-level trade certification card, a residential electrical license, and a commercial contractor license are not the same credential. The correct path depends on whether the candidate wants individual trade certification, residential specialty work authority, commercial contractor licensing, business authority, or advancement toward a master-level credential.

For CAGC SC Trades Certification, candidates complete the new certification application in full, submit the application fee, and wait for the application review. After approval, the candidate receives notification from PROV and schedules the examination. The candidate then takes the exam online or in person at an approved testing facility. If the candidate does not pass or allows the exam voucher to expire, the retesting process is handled through the program’s current retest instructions.

For residential electrical licensing, candidates follow the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission process. This may include meeting the Commission’s application, experience, and examination requirements, applying to the state, taking the required technical examination, completing any required business examination, and satisfying the licensing steps required before performing regulated residential electrical work.

For commercial electrical contracting, candidates should follow the South Carolina Contractors Licensing Board process. The contractor licensing path applies to commercial work and business operations that require a contractor license. Contractor applicants may need to meet separate classification, examination, financial, business, and license requirements.

Candidates should keep accurate records of their experience, training, employer information, certification applications, exam approvals, score reports, renewal records, and license or certification documents. South Carolina’s structure makes it important to know the difference between trade certification, residential licensing, and commercial contractor licensing before performing regulated work.

State Requirements

South Carolina does not rely on one single credential for every electrician role. The CAGC SC Trades Certification Program handles trade certification cards that confirm experience and written examination qualifications for the certification level. The South Carolina Residential Builders Commission regulates residential specialty contractor licensing, including residential electrical licensing. The South Carolina Contractors Licensing Board regulates commercial contractor licensing.

The CAGC SC Trades Certification Program should not be confused with a contractor license or residential specialty contractor license. A trade certification card can demonstrate competency at a trade level, but business authority, contractor licensing, residential specialty licensing, permitting, and scope of work may require separate approval through the appropriate South Carolina agency.

Residential electrical candidates must meet the requirements established by the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission. The Commission’s examination information identifies the residential specialty contractor electrical technical examination as a 60-question examination. Candidates should follow the Commission’s application and examination process for the current licensing requirements.

Commercial electrical contractor candidates should work through the South Carolina Contractors Licensing Board. Commercial contractor licensing requirements may include classification-specific application steps, examination requirements, financial documentation, business documentation, and compliance with Board rules. Candidates should not assume that a journeyman-level certification automatically authorizes commercial contracting.

For journeyman-level preparation, candidates should be ready to demonstrate knowledge of electrical theory, National Electrical Code application, wiring methods, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors, overcurrent protection, motors, transformers, equipment, special installations, safety practices, and electrical calculations. Study materials help candidates prepare, but licensing and certification decisions are made by the applicable South Carolina program or agency based on the applicant’s qualifications, documentation, examination results, and compliance with requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

The South Carolina Journeyman Electrician Super Combo supports a complete preparation routine by combining structured study, code review, calculation practice, and quick recall. The South Carolina Journeyman Electrician Study Guide helps candidates organize preparation around journeyman-level electrical knowledge. The Electrician Calculations Study Guide strengthens math-based preparation. The NEC paperback with tabs supports code navigation. The Journeyman Electrician Flash Cards reinforce important information through repetition.

Electrical calculations should be practiced consistently before the exam. Candidates should review Ohm’s Law, power formulas, amperage, voltage, resistance, watts, conductor sizing, branch circuit calculations, feeder concepts, service load concepts, box fill, conduit fill, motor calculations, transformer concepts, overcurrent protection, and voltage drop. Calculation questions can take more time than direct code questions, so repeated practice helps improve both speed and accuracy.

Code navigation should also be part of every study plan. Candidates should practice using the NEC table of contents, index, article numbers, definitions, tables, notes, and exceptions. The goal is to identify the topic quickly and move to the correct section without wasting time. A candidate who understands how the NEC is organized is better prepared for open-book electrical testing.

Services, feeders, and branch circuits should receive careful attention. Candidates should review service conductors, service disconnects, grounding at services, feeder sizing, branch circuit ratings, required outlets, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, and load calculations. These topics connect code knowledge with practical installation skill.

Grounding and bonding are important areas for journeyman exam preparation. Candidates should review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, raceway bonding, panel bonding, grounded conductors, separately derived systems, and related safety requirements. Grounding and bonding questions often require careful reading because similar terms can lead to different code requirements.

Wiring methods and materials should be studied consistently. Candidates should review conductors, cables, raceways, boxes, fittings, supports, conductor ampacity, insulation types, flexible wiring methods, underground wiring, and installation conditions. These topics appear frequently in electrical work and are central to NEC-based study.

Motors, transformers, controls, and equipment should also be part of the study routine. Candidates should review motor branch circuits, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, disconnecting means, controllers, transformer sizing, transformer protection, luminaires, receptacles, switches, appliances, panelboards, and related installation requirements.

Special occupancies, special equipment, and special conditions should not be ignored. Candidates should review hazardous locations, health care facilities, emergency systems, standby systems, swimming pools, signs, mobile homes, RV parks, data processing equipment, fire alarm systems, and low-voltage systems when these areas appear in the applicable exam outline.

Flash cards are useful for shorter study sessions. Candidates can use them to review formulas, definitions, electrical theory, safety concepts, NEC topics, and trade terms before work, after work, during breaks, or between longer study blocks. Flash cards work best when paired with deeper reading, calculation drills, and code lookup practice.

A strong study plan should include scheduled reading, NEC navigation, calculation drills, timed practice, and repeated review of weaker topics. Candidates should avoid studying only familiar material. Journeyman electrician preparation rewards steady practice, careful reading, and the ability to apply electrical knowledge under exam conditions.
