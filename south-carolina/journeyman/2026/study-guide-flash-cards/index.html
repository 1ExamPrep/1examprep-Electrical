Exam Details

The South Carolina Journeyman Electrician certification exam is designed to test whether a candidate has the knowledge needed to perform electrical trade work while employed by a master electrician. The exam has historically been administered through the Mechanical Trades Certification Program, with Prov used as the testing provider for certification examinations.

The South Carolina Journeyman Electrician exam is listed as 70 questions with 3 hours allowed. The examination is open book and timed. The required passing score is 70%. The subject areas include Communication Systems, Equipment for General Use, General Electrical Theory and Principles, Motors and Controls, Plan Reading, Special Conditions, Special Equipment, Special Occupancies, Wiring and Protection, and Wiring Methods and Materials.

South Carolina also has a Journeyman Electrician Residential exam. That residential exam is separate from the general journeyman electrician exam and is focused on the planning, installation, repair, alteration, and maintenance of electric conductors, raceways, and equipment for light, heat, and power in one-, two-, and three-family dwellings. Students should choose the exam that matches the credential they are pursuing.

For the general South Carolina Journeyman Electrician exam, the largest listed topic areas are Wiring Methods and Materials and Wiring and Protection. These areas should receive steady study attention because they cover many practical installation rules that electricians use in the field. General Electrical Theory and Principles, Equipment for General Use, Communication Systems, Plan Reading, Motors and Controls, Special Conditions, Special Equipment, and Special Occupancies should also be included in the study plan.

Open Book Test

The South Carolina Journeyman Electrician certification exam is an open-book examination. Open book does not mean easy. Candidates still need to understand electrical concepts, recognize how questions are written, locate information quickly, apply code rules correctly, and manage time. A student who depends only on searching through references for every answer can lose valuable time during the exam.

South Carolina certification testing rules allow approved references in the testing room, but reference materials must follow the current exam rules. Historically permitted preparation methods include highlighting and attaching permanent tabs. Reference books cannot contain Post-it notes, loose papers, photocopied additions, photocopied versions of reference books, or unauthorized inserted material. Test preparation study guides and practice examinations are not approved exam-room references.

Open-book electrical exams reward reference familiarity. Students should practice using the National Electrical Code table of contents, index, article structure, definitions, tables, exceptions, notes, and common code sections before exam day. Students should also practice calculations repeatedly so conductor sizing, load relationships, grounding and bonding rules, motor requirements, box fill, raceway fill, voltage drop concepts, and overcurrent protection requirements become easier to recognize.

A strong open-book strategy includes learning where major NEC topics are located before the exam. Students should practice identifying the correct article, checking definitions first, reading table notes carefully, reviewing exceptions, and confirming whether the question asks for a minimum, maximum, permitted condition, prohibited condition, or calculation result. Flash cards help students recall key concepts faster, while practice exams help build the habit of reading questions carefully and choosing the correct reference path.

Students should use this study guide, the 12 practice exams, the 2 full final exams, and the flash cards before test day. During the official exam, candidates should bring only the references and materials allowed by the current testing instructions for the South Carolina certification exam they are taking.

Licensing Steps

South Carolina journeyman electrician certification begins with selecting the correct trade credential. Candidates should distinguish between journeyman electrician certification, residential journeyman electrician certification, master electrician certification, residential electrical contractor licensing, and mechanical electrical contractor licensing. Each credential has a different purpose and may be tied to a different work scope.

A typical South Carolina journeyman electrician path includes entering the electrical trade, gaining supervised electrical work experience, developing journeyman-level knowledge, submitting the required certification or examination application, receiving approval or registration information, scheduling the examination, passing the required test, and completing any remaining certification steps required by the program administrator.

Journeyman electricians are tested to perform work in the electrical trades while employed by a master electrician. Candidates should build experience in wiring methods, equipment, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, motors, controls, plan reading, communication systems, special occupancies, special equipment, and practical field installation. Hands-on experience and code study work together during journeyman preparation.

South Carolina electrical contractor licensing is a separate path. Contractor licensing can involve the South Carolina Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation, including the appropriate board depending on whether the work is residential or commercial in scope. A journeyman certification supports trade qualification, while contractor licensing is connected to business authority, bidding, contracting, and regulated work scope.
