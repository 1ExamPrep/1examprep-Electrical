## Exam Details

South Carolina electrical licensing and examination requirements depend on the license category, the issuing authority, and the examination path assigned to the candidate. For residential electrical specialty licensing through the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission, candidates must receive examination eligibility before scheduling with PSI. The residential electrical examination is administered by PSI and is part of the Residential Builders and Residential Specialty examination program.

For the South Carolina Residential Electrical state-specific examination, the published examination outline lists 60 questions, a 180-minute time limit, and a passing requirement of 44 correct answers, which equals 73.3%. The exam covers electrical work on residential structures consisting of sixteen units or less and not exceeding three stories in height. South Carolina applicants taking the Residential Electrical exam may also have a choice between the South Carolina state-specific Residential Electrical exam and the NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Residential Electrical Contractors, depending on the licensing route that fits their business needs.

Key residential electrical examination content areas include general electrical knowledge, services, feeders, branch circuits, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, lighting, specialty applications, and safety. Students preparing with this combo should spend time on both trade knowledge and NEC lookup practice. Many electrical questions require more than finding a single article; students often need to understand definitions, follow exceptions, apply tables, and recognize how multiple code rules work together.

The PSI exam process is computer-based. Candidates should arrive early, bring required identification, and follow all testing-center rules. PSI provides a computer tutorial before the examination begins, and scores are typically provided after completion. Candidates approved for testing should schedule according to the instructions provided in their eligibility materials.

## Open Book Test

The South Carolina Residential Builders Commission residential builder and residential specialty examinations administered by PSI are open book when the candidate uses the reference books listed in the applicable bulletin. For the Residential Electrical examination, candidates may bring listed reference books into the testing center, subject to PSI rules.

Open book testing does not mean the exam is simple. It means the candidate must know how to use the reference books correctly and quickly. Electrical candidates should be comfortable locating NEC articles, moving between definitions and installation requirements, reading table headings and footnotes, and identifying exceptions that may change the answer. A candidate who owns the right book but cannot navigate it efficiently may spend too much time searching and too little time answering.

Reference books may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed, but they must otherwise be unmarked. They may not contain additional loose or attached papers. Tabs must be permanent tabs. Temporary tabs, removable notes, and tabs that can be removed without tearing the page are not allowed. This makes code-book preparation an important part of the study process. The tabs included with this combo are intended to help students organize the 2026 NEC and practice moving through the book during study.

Students should use the **National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs** throughout preparation, not just during the final review. Repeated practice with the NEC helps students learn article locations, table structures, key definitions, and common electrical code relationships. The more familiar the code book feels, the easier it becomes to focus on the question instead of struggling to find the right section.

## Licensing Steps

South Carolina candidates should follow the licensing path assigned to the specific electrical credential they are pursuing. For the Residential Electrical specialty route through the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission, applicants must complete the application process with the Commission before scheduling the exam with PSI.

1. **Review the electrical license category.** Determine whether the Residential Electrical state-specific examination or another accepted electrical examination path applies to the license goal.
2. **Meet eligibility requirements.** Residential specialty applicants must have Commission-approved experience under the supervision of a licensee in the trade before being approved for examination.
3. **Submit the application to the Commission.** Applications must be complete before an examination eligibility letter can be issued.
4. **Receive examination eligibility.** Once approved, the candidate receives an eligibility letter with instructions for scheduling.
5. **Schedule the exam with PSI.** PSI administers the examination and collects the applicable examination fee.
6. **Study the exam outline and reference materials.** Candidates should use the published outline, the NEC, and structured practice materials to prepare.
7. **Take the examination.** Candidates must follow PSI identification, security, calculator, and reference-book rules at the testing center.
8. **Submit post-exam licensing materials.** After passing, applicants follow the Commission’s instructions for completing the licensing process, including any required license fee, bond, or documentation that applies to the license type.

## State Requirements

South Carolina electrical licensing requirements are tied to the license classification and the authority responsible for that credential. For the Residential Electrical specialty license path, individuals must apply through the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission. The Commission requires applicants to be approved before scheduling the examination, and applicants must obtain an examination eligibility letter before contacting PSI to schedule.

Residential specialty applicants must have one year of Commission-approved experience under the supervision of a licensee in the trade for which they are applying. The Commission reviews applications for accuracy and completeness. If an application is incomplete or has deficiencies, the applicant may receive a deficiency notice before eligibility is granted.

For Residential Electrical licensing, the candidate must pass the applicable electrical trade examination and the Business Management and Law for Residential Builders examination unless a waiver or alternate accepted examination path applies. The Business Management and Law examination covers business management, licensing laws and rules, estimating and bidding, contracts, project management, insurance and bonding, safety recordkeeping, labor laws, financial management, tax laws, liens, and environmental laws and regulations.

Residential Electrical licenses renew June 30 of every odd-numbered year. Candidates and licensees should keep their licensing records current and follow the renewal requirements for their license classification.
