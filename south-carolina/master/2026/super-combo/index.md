## Exam Details

South Carolina commercial contractor licensing is handled through the South Carolina Contractor’s Licensing Board under the South Carolina Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation. The Board regulates general and mechanical contractors, and the Electrical classification is listed under the mechanical contractor classifications that require technical examination. PSI administers the South Carolina commercial contractor examinations.

The South Carolina Electrician examination for commercial contractor candidates is listed with 80 questions, a required passing score of 56 correct answers, and 5 hours of testing time. The PSI bulletin also states that a small number of experimental questions may be administered during the exam. These experimental items are not scored, but they count against the allowed testing time.

The South Carolina Electrician examination content outline includes General Electrical Knowledge, Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits, Grounding and Bonding, Conductors and Cables, Raceways and Boxes, Special Occupancies and Equipment, Electrical Power, Motors, Low Voltage, Lighting, Illuminated Signs, Fire Detection and Alarm Systems, and Safety Information. South Carolina Electrician commercial contractor candidates are also required to pass the Business Management and Law for Contractors examination. The South Carolina Contractor’s Licensing Board states that applicants must pass the South Carolina Business Management and Law for Commercial Contractors exam before requesting qualifying party certification. Contractor-level preparation should include both technical electrical study and the business and law requirements connected with licensure.

South Carolina Electrician commercial contractor candidates have a choice of trade exams. They may take either the South Carolina Electrician state-specific exam or the NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors, which is accepted by multiple state agencies. The NASCLA electrical contractor exam is listed with 100 questions, a required passing score of 75 correct answers, and 270 minutes of testing time. Candidates should choose the trade exam that fits their licensing and business goals.

## Open Book Test

The South Carolina commercial contractor examinations are identified as open book examinations. Open book testing does not make the exam easy. It means candidates must know how to use approved references efficiently, read code language carefully, understand table notes and exceptions, and apply requirements under time pressure.

South Carolina commercial contractor exam references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. They must otherwise be unmarked, may not be written in, and may not contain additional loose or attached papers. References may be tabbed or indexed with permanent tabs only. Temporary tabs, including removable Post-it style tabs, are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins.

The PSI bulletin lists the National Electrical Code with South Carolina modifications, or the National Electrical Code Handbook, as an Electrician reference, along with other electrical and safety references used for the examination. For code questions, the exam is based only on the listed code edition. Candidates should follow the current PSI bulletin for the specific code edition and approved reference list that applies to the exam date they schedule.

## Licensing Steps

A practical South Carolina licensing path begins by confirming whether the work falls under the South Carolina Contractor’s Licensing Board or the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission. A license issued by the Contractor’s Licensing Board allows an entity to engage in commercial as well as residential construction. If an entity is solely engaged in residential construction, the South Carolina Residential Home Builders Commission handles that residential licensing path.

For the commercial Electrical classification, applicants must designate a Primary Qualifying Party for the classification or subclassification requested. The Primary Qualifying Party is the principal individual responsible for directing or reviewing work performed by the applicant in that classification. Additional qualifying parties may also be added when they meet the same exam requirements.

South Carolina states that Primary Qualifying Parties must complete the exam requirements for certification before the entity or individual submits the license application to the Board. There are no pre-approvals required to schedule the PSI commercial contractor exams. Candidates register for the appropriate PSI technical exam and the required South Carolina Business Management and Law for Commercial Contractors exam.

After passing the required examinations, the applicant submits the licensing materials to the Contractor’s Licensing Board. South Carolina identifies required items that may include the initial application, licensure fee, verification to conduct business in South Carolina from the Secretary of State when applicable, financial statement or surety bond supporting the requested group limitation, qualifying party application materials, PSI technical exam pass sheet, and PSI Business Management and Law exam pass sheet.

Applicants must also submit at least two years of work experience performed within the past five years for the requested license classification or subclassification, except for waiver or reciprocity applicants. Unless otherwise listed, the experience should primarily include commercial work experience. Candidates should prepare documentation carefully so the licensing step after testing is organized and complete.

State licensure is only one part of operating legally. Local rules, fees, and permitting procedures may apply depending on where the work is performed.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong South Carolina electrical contractor study plan should include NEC study, calculation practice, trade knowledge review, business and law review, safety review, reference navigation, and timed question work. Electrical exams can cover a wide range of subjects, so candidates should avoid spending all study time on only one topic. A balanced routine helps build confidence across services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors, cables, raceways, boxes, special occupancies, electrical power, motors, low-voltage systems, lighting, illuminated signs, fire detection, alarm systems, safety, and calculations.

For NEC study, start by learning the structure of the code book. Review article organization, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and cross-references. Practice moving from a question to the correct article and reading the surrounding language carefully. Many electrical exam questions are missed because a candidate finds a related section but overlooks a condition, exception, table note, or specific wording.

For calculations, write out each step. Electrical calculation mistakes often happen when a candidate skips a conversion, uses the wrong table, applies the wrong value, or confuses branch circuit, feeder, service, motor, transformer, raceway fill, box fill, voltage drop, or overcurrent protection requirements. The 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide gives candidates a dedicated way to practice these skills repeatedly.

For business and law preparation, review contractor licensing responsibilities, business management, project management, contracts, state law topics, financial requirements, qualifying party responsibilities, and the practical requirements connected with operating as a licensed contractor in South Carolina. Commercial contractor candidates should treat the Business Management and Law exam as a required part of the licensing process, not as an afterthought.

For open book preparation, use the NEC actively during study. Practice locating sections, reading tables, and confirming answers in the code book. Then practice some questions without immediately opening the code book so you can build recognition. The stronger your recall becomes, the more time you can save during questions that require deeper code lookup.

For flash card study, use short sessions throughout the week. Flash cards can help reinforce formulas, definitions, code concepts, safety topics, and common electrical terms. They are useful before work, during breaks, after a longer study block, or as final review. Flash cards should not replace full study sessions, but they help strengthen recall and recognition.
