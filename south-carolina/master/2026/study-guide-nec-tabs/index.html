Licensing Steps

The South Carolina electrical licensing path begins with identifying the correct license type. Electricians pursuing commercial electrical contracting should review the South Carolina Contractor’s Licensing Board requirements for mechanical contractor electrical classifications. Electricians pursuing residential electrical work should review the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission requirements for residential specialty electrical licensing.

For commercial contractor licensing, the applicant identifies the proper license group, classification, and qualifying party. The business entity applies through the Contractor’s Licensing Board, and the qualifying party must satisfy the required examination, experience, and documentation requirements. Commercial contractor licensing may also require business management and law testing, technical examination results, financial information, bond or financial documentation, and other materials required by the Board.

For residential electrical specialty licensing, applicants follow the Residential Builders Commission process. The process may include application materials, examination requirements, experience documentation, business information, and other state requirements tied to residential specialty contractor licensing. Residential specialty electrical candidates should prepare for NEC-based residential wiring topics and the rules that apply to residential electrical contracting.

After the proper application process is started, the candidate prepares for and schedules the required examination through the applicable testing process. PSI administers examinations connected to South Carolina contractor licensing. Candidates should bring required identification, follow exam rules, and use only approved materials allowed for the scheduled exam.

After passing the required examination and completing the licensing process, the applicant receives the license, certification, or registration issued by the applicable South Carolina board or commission. Licensed contractors must continue to follow renewal requirements, continuing education requirements when applicable, permit rules, inspection requirements, business rules, and the scope of work authorized by the credential held.
