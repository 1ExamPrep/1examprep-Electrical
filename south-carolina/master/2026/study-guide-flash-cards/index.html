Exam Details

The South Carolina Contractor’s Licensing Board contracts with PSI to administer the commercial contractor examination program. Candidates for commercial contractor licensing must meet the Board’s minimum qualifications before registering for the required examination. General and mechanical contractor candidates must document at least 2 years of commercial experience within the last 5 years in the classification for which they seek licensure.

For South Carolina commercial electrical contractor candidates, the PSI bulletin identifies the Electrician - Commercial Contractor examination. This exam contains 80 questions. Candidates must answer 56 questions correctly to pass, which equals a 70% passing score. The time allowed is 5 hours.

The South Carolina Electrician - Commercial Contractor examination content outline includes:

  • General Electrical Knowledge: 9 items
  • Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits: 9 items
  • Grounding and Bonding: 9 items
  • Conductors and Cables: 9 items
  • Raceways and Boxes: 9 items
  • Special Occupancies and Equipment: 2 items
  • Electrical Power: 7 items
  • Motors: 7 items
  • Low Voltage: 2 items
  • Lighting: 5 items
  • Illuminated Signs: 2 items
  • Fire Detection and Alarm Systems: 2 items
  • Safety Information: 8 items

South Carolina Electrician - Commercial Contractor candidates are also required to pass the Business Management and Law for Contractors examination. This business exam covers contractor responsibilities, business structure, licensing, estimating, contracts, project management, insurance, bonding, safety, labor responsibilities, taxes, and related business-law topics.

South Carolina candidates have a choice of trade exam paths. They may take either the South Carolina state-specific Electrician - Commercial Contractor exam or the NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractor, which is accepted by multiple state agencies. The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractor contains 100 questions, requires 75 correct answers to pass, and allows 270 minutes.

Open Book Test

The South Carolina Electrician - Commercial Contractor examination is an open book test with approved reference materials. Candidates must follow PSI rules for allowed materials, calculators, exam security, and reference use. Only approved items may be taken into the examination room.

Open-book electrical exams still require serious preparation. The exam is not simply about having books available. Candidates need to understand how the National Electrical Code is organized, how South Carolina modifications apply, how to identify the topic being tested, how to locate the correct Article or table quickly, and how to apply the rule accurately within the time allowed.

State Requirements

The South Carolina Contractor’s Licensing Board regulates general and mechanical contractors, construction managers, burglar alarm and fire alarm contractors, fire sprinkler contractors, and the boiler safety program. For commercial electrical contracting, candidates follow the Board’s licensing process and PSI examination requirements.

South Carolina commercial contractor candidates must meet minimum qualifications before registering for the examination. For general and mechanical contractor classifications, candidates must have at least 2 years of commercial experience within the last 5 years in the classification for which they seek licensure. This experience must be documented on an experience affidavit submitted with the commercial license application after passing the required examinations.

A license issued by the South Carolina Contractor’s Licensing Board allows an entity to engage in commercial as well as residential construction. If an entity is solely engaged in residential construction, the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission handles that licensing path. Candidates should select the correct path based on the scope of work and business structure involved.

South Carolina commercial electrical contractor candidates must pass the Business Management and Law for Contractors examination in addition to the electrical technical examination. The business-law requirement reflects the responsibilities of licensed contractors, including legal compliance, project management, financial responsibility, contracts, safety, and business operation.

The PSI bulletin also notes that Electrician - Commercial Contractor candidates may choose between the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Electrical Contractors and the South Carolina state-specific electrician exam. Candidates should compare the two exam paths and choose the one that best fits their business needs, license goals, and jurisdictional requirements.
