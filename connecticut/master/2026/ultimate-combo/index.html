Exam Details

Connecticut electrical licensing is handled through the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection. Electricians must be licensed by the Department of Consumer Protection in order to perform electrical work in the state. Connecticut licenses individuals rather than electrical companies. The state uses limited and unlimited electrical license categories, with odd-numbered licenses generally identifying contractor classifications and even-numbered licenses generally identifying journeyperson or employee classifications.

The Connecticut E-1 Unlimited Electrical Contractor examination is administered by PSI. The E-1 trade exam includes 100 questions, requires a 70% passing score, and allows 4 hours of testing time. Candidates taking contractor-level exams must also pass the Business and Law examination required for contractor and dealer technician licenses. The Business and Law exam includes 50 questions, requires a 70% passing score, and allows 2 hours of testing time.

The Connecticut E-1 Unlimited Electrical Contractor content outline includes General Electrical Knowledge, Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits, Grounding and Bonding, Conductors and Cables, Raceways and Boxes, Special Occupancies and Equipment, Electrical Power, Motors, Low Voltage, Lighting, Illuminated Signs, Fire Detection and Alarm Systems, Safety Information, Overcurrent Protection, High Voltage, and Photovoltaics. The examination may also contain questions based on trade knowledge or general industry practices.

PSI score reporting is provided immediately after the examination. Candidates receive an on-screen result after completing the exam. Candidates who do not pass receive diagnostic information by examination type so they can review weaker areas before retesting. Candidates should follow PSI scheduling instructions and Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection licensing instructions throughout the application and exam process.

Open Book Test

The Connecticut E-1 Unlimited Electrical Contractor examination is an open-book exam. The PSI bulletin identifies the approved reference materials allowed in the examination center and states that candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references. Candidates may use a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator in the examination center.

Open book does not mean the exam is simple. Candidates still need to know how to locate information quickly, understand code structure, read questions carefully, and apply the correct rule or calculation method. A candidate who has not practiced using reference materials can lose time searching, misread exceptions, or choose answers based on memory instead of code language.

Connecticut’s PSI rules allow reference materials to be highlighted, underlined, annotated, and indexed before the examination session. References may not be written in during the examination session. Candidates are not permitted to bring additional loose or attached papers with approved references. References may be tabbed or indexed with permanent tabs only. Temporary tabs, such as Post-it notes, are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins. Writing questions and answers in reference materials is prohibited.

Licensing Steps

Connecticut E-1 Unlimited Electrical Contractor candidates must follow the licensing process established by the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection. Candidates should first confirm the correct license classification, then complete the required experience and training documentation, apply through the Department of Consumer Protection, obtain exam eligibility, schedule the required PSI examinations, pass the trade exam and the Business and Law exam, and submit the required licensing materials after passing.

For the E-1 license path, Connecticut identifies 720 classroom hours and 12,000 total hours of on-the-job training as the equivalent experience and training requirement. The listed E-1 training structure includes 8,000 apprentice on-the-job training hours plus 2 years, or 4,000 hours, of licensed on-the-job training. Classroom hours must be equivalent to the required courses for the electrical license type. On-the-job training and work experience are not accepted in place of the classroom-hour requirement.

Connecticut’s electrical licensing rules also identify the E-1 qualification standard as two years as an unlimited licensed journeyperson or at least six years of equivalent experience and training. Candidates relying on equivalent experience and training should be prepared to submit documentation such as transcripts, degrees, diplomas, out-of-state completion documentation when applicable, and notarized employer letters describing dates of employment and detailed work experience.

After passing the required examination, candidates submit the licensing fee indicated on the passing score report with all passing score reports to the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection. Candidates should keep copies of application materials, score reports, training documentation, and correspondence for their records.

State Requirements

Connecticut requires electricians to be licensed by the Department of Consumer Protection in order to perform electrical work. The Department of Consumer Protection licenses individuals, and the state recognizes both limited and unlimited license categories. Contractor licenses generally use odd numbers, such as E-1, while journeyperson or employee licenses generally use even numbers, such as E-2.

The E-1 Unlimited Electrical Contractor license is the broad unlimited contractor-level electrical license in Connecticut. The holder of the E-1 license is permitted to perform all electrical work as defined under Connecticut electrical licensing rules. The E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson license also permits broad electrical work, but only while the license holder is employed by a properly licensed contractor.

Only contractors can obtain permits and sign contracts in Connecticut. Journeypersons and apprentices cannot work directly for consumers unless employed by, or employing, a licensed contractor of record. Candidates planning to operate independently should understand the difference between contractor-level authority and journeyperson-level work authorization before selecting a license path.

Connecticut also has limited electrical license categories for specific scopes of work, including line work, low voltage work, sign work, telecommunications-related work, and solar electric work. Candidates should apply for the license classification that matches the actual scope of work they intend to perform.
