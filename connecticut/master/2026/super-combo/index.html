Exam Details

The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection uses PSI Services LLC for occupational licensing electrical trades examinations. The Connecticut E-1 Unlimited Electrical Contractor examination is the contractor-level unlimited electrical exam associated with Connecticut electrical licensing.

The E-1 Unlimited Electrical Contractor examination contains 100 questions. The required passing score is 70%, and the time allowed is 4 hours. Candidates may use a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator in the examination center.

The official E-1 exam content outline includes the following subject areas: general electrical knowledge, services, feeders, and branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special occupancies and equipment, electrical power, motors, low voltage, lighting, illuminated signs, fire detection and alarm systems, safety information, overcurrent protection, high voltage, and photovoltaics.

The exam covers a wide range of technical knowledge. Candidates should be prepared for direct code questions, trade knowledge questions, practical application questions, safety questions, and calculation-based questions. The content areas are broad enough that preparation should not focus on only one topic. Strong candidates study services and feeders, grounding and bonding, conductors, boxes, raceways, overcurrent protection, motors, and calculations while also reviewing specialty areas such as photovoltaic systems, low voltage, fire alarm systems, lighting, and high-voltage topics.

Because the exam is timed, speed and accuracy both matter. A candidate may understand the trade but still struggle if too much time is spent searching through references or working slowly through calculations. The best preparation routine includes repeated practice, code navigation, review of weak areas, and timed work sessions that build confidence before test day.

Open Book Test

The Connecticut E-1 Unlimited Electrical Contractor examination is open book. The PSI candidate bulletin lists approved reference materials for the exam and states that candidates are responsible for bringing their own references to the examination center.

Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, annotated, and indexed before the examination session. References may not be written in during the examination. Candidates may not bring additional papers, loose materials, or attached notes with their approved references. References may be tabbed or indexed with permanent tabs only. Temporary tabs, such as Post-it notes, are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins.

This open-book format makes reference navigation a major part of exam preparation. Open book does not mean the exam is easy. Candidates still need to know where information is located, how to interpret code language, and how to apply the correct requirement to the question being asked. The included NEC with tabs helps candidates practice code movement and become more comfortable with the structure of the code book during study.

For the official Connecticut E-1 exam reference list, the PSI bulletin currently lists the National Electrical Code or the National Electrical Code Handbook, 2020 or 2023 edition, along with other approved references for the E-1 examination. This product bundle is based on the 2026 National Electrical Code for candidates who want to study with the 2026 NEC edition and supporting 2026 study materials.

Licensing Steps

Connecticut electrical licensing is handled through the Department of Consumer Protection. Candidates pursuing the E-1 Unlimited Electrical Contractor license should follow the application path provided by the state and the examination scheduling process administered by PSI.

A practical licensing path begins by reviewing the Connecticut license classification and confirming the correct license type. For candidates pursuing the contractor-level unlimited electrical license, the E-1 Unlimited Electrical Contractor classification is the relevant exam classification. Applicants should review the state’s experience and credential requirements, submit the appropriate application, receive authorization to test when required, schedule the examination with PSI, and complete the licensing process after passing the exam.

Connecticut contractor applicants are generally expected to have held the respective journeyperson license for the required period before applying under the contractor license path. The state provides separate application routes for applicants applying under equivalent experience and training or equivalent out-of-state licensing. Candidates should use the application path that matches their background and license history.

After passing the required examination, the candidate must complete the state licensing process and submit any licensing fee or documentation required by the Department of Consumer Protection. Passing the exam is an important step, but the state licensing process determines whether the license is issued.

State Requirements

Connecticut electricians must be licensed by the Department of Consumer Protection in order to perform electrical work. Connecticut issues individual electrical licenses and has limited and unlimited license categories. In Connecticut’s license structure, contractor licenses generally use odd-numbered classifications, such as E-1, while journeyperson licenses generally use even-numbered classifications, such as E-2.

The E-1 Unlimited Electrical Contractor license is the contractor-level unlimited electrical classification. Candidates moving toward the E-1 license should understand that this is not simply a test of memorized definitions. The exam measures broad electrical knowledge and the ability to use approved references effectively. The exam outline includes general electrical knowledge, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, services and feeders, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, motors, photovoltaics, high voltage, lighting, low voltage, fire detection, alarm systems, and safety information.

State licensing requirements are controlled by Connecticut law, Department of Consumer Protection procedures, and the Electrical Work Examining Board. Candidates should follow the current application instructions for the exact license classification they are pursuing. Exam preparation materials help candidates study, but licensing approval depends on the applicant’s qualifications, documentation, exam completion, and compliance with state requirements.
