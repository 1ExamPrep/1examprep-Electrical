Exam Details

Connecticut electrical trade examinations are administered by PSI for the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection. State eligibility is determined by the Department of Consumer Protection, and eligible candidates schedule their examinations through PSI. Contractor license candidates are required to pass both the trade portion and the business portion for licensure. Journeyperson candidates are required to pass the trade portion for the applicable license category.

The Connecticut E-1 Unlimited Electrical Contractor examination contains 100 questions, requires a 70 percent passing score, and allows 4 hours of testing time. The examination is computer-based and is administered through PSI’s examination network. Candidates receive score results immediately after completing the exam. Passing candidates must submit the licensing fee indicated on the passing score report with all required passing score reports to the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection.

The E-1 Unlimited Electrical Contractor content outline includes general electrical knowledge, services, feeders and branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special occupancies and equipment, electrical power, motors, low voltage, lighting, illuminated signs, fire detection and alarm systems, safety information, overcurrent protection, high voltage, and photovoltaics. This wide content range makes structured study important because candidates need both NEC familiarity and practical trade understanding.

The Connecticut E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson examination contains 80 questions, requires a 70 percent passing score, and allows 3.5 hours of testing time. While this product is built for advanced “master electrician” and contractor-level study, many of the same NEC topics and calculation skills are useful for journeyperson-level review as well.

The Connecticut Business and Law examination is required for contractor licenses. It contains 50 questions, requires a 70 percent passing score, and allows 2 hours of testing time. The Business and Law content outline includes licensing, estimating and bidding, lien law, financial management, tax laws, labor laws, project management, contracts, business organization, risk management, and environmental and safety topics. This product focuses on electrical trade study and calculations, not business law preparation.

Open Book Test

The Connecticut E-1 Unlimited Electrical Contractor examination is open book. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references to the examination center. Approved reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, annotated, and indexed before the examination session. References may not be written in during the examination session.

Connecticut PSI rules allow permanent tabs in approved references. Temporary tabs, such as removable notes or Post-it style tabs, are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins. Candidates may not bring additional papers, loose notes, attached notes, or unapproved materials into the testing room with their references. Questions and answers may not be written in reference materials.

Candidates may use a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator in the examination center. Because the exam is open book and timed, students should not rely on simply owning the references. The strongest preparation comes from practicing with the NEC, learning the index, reviewing frequently tested articles, and developing a system for finding code answers quickly.

Open-book electrical exams can still be challenging because the clock continues to run while candidates search. A student who is unfamiliar with the NEC may lose valuable time moving between articles, tables, exceptions, and definitions. Licensing Steps

Connecticut electrical licensing begins with selecting the correct license classification. For advanced contractor-level electrical licensing, the E-1 Unlimited Electrical Contractor classification is the primary unlimited contractor license. The E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson license is a journeyperson classification and is separate from the contractor license path. Limited electrical classifications also exist for narrower scopes of work.

To qualify for the E-1 Unlimited Electrical Contractor license examination, Connecticut regulations require two years as an unlimited licensed journeyperson or at least six years of equivalent experience and training. Candidates must meet the state’s eligibility requirements before scheduling the examination. The Department of Consumer Protection determines eligibility for examination.

After state approval, the candidate schedules the required examination through PSI. Contractor candidates must pass both the electrical trade examination and the Business and Law examination. The candidate’s eligibility period is valid for one year from the date the state approves the application. Contractor examination portion scores are valid for two years from the date passed.

On exam day, candidates should arrive early, bring the required identification, bring only approved references and permitted materials, and follow PSI’s security rules. After the exam, score results are issued immediately. Passing candidates submit the licensing fee shown on the passing score report with the required score reports to the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection.

Students should keep licensing requirements and study preparation organized as separate parts of the same goal. The state application process determines eligibility and licensure. Study materials help the candidate prepare for the electrical content, improve calculation accuracy, and build confidence using the NEC under timed testing conditions.

State Requirements

Connecticut electricians must be licensed by the Department of Consumer Protection to perform electrical work. The state licenses individuals, and electrical license types include unlimited and limited categories. Contractor licenses and journeyperson licenses are separated by classification. E-1 is the unlimited electrical contractor classification, while E-2 is the unlimited electrical journeyperson classification.

The E-1 Unlimited Electrical Contractor license permits the holder to do all electrical work as defined under Connecticut’s electrical licensing provisions. The requirements to qualify for the E-1 license examination are two years as an unlimited licensed journeyperson or at least six years of equivalent experience and training. The E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson classification is tied to journeyperson-level electrical work and is not the same as the contractor classification.

Connecticut contractor candidates must pass the required trade examination and the Business and Law examination. The Department of Consumer Protection determines eligibility, while PSI administers the examinations. Candidates should prepare for the exact classification they are pursuing because Connecticut has multiple electrical license categories and each category may have its own examination content and approved references.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Connecticut E-1 study plan should combine NEC reading, trade-topic review, calculation practice, and timed reference navigation. The E-1 examination covers 100 questions across a broad outline, so candidates should avoid studying only one topic. A balanced routine helps build readiness across general electrical knowledge, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding, bonding, conductors, raceways, motors, lighting, fire alarm systems, safety, high voltage, photovoltaics, and overcurrent protection.

Services, feeders, and branch circuits deserve careful attention. Candidates should understand how load requirements, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, equipment ratings, and NEC article structure connect. These topics often require both code lookup and calculation ability, especially when the question involves conductor ampacity, demand factors, service sizing, or branch-circuit requirements.

Grounding and bonding is another major area for advanced electrical preparation. Students should review grounding electrode systems, bonding jumpers, equipment grounding conductors, grounding electrode conductors, service grounding, separately derived systems, and effective ground-fault current paths. These rules can be detailed, and successful exam preparation requires understanding both the concept and the specific NEC requirements.

Raceways, boxes, conductors, and cables require frequent table use. Students should practice conductor ampacity tables, adjustment and correction concepts, raceway fill, box fill, conductor identification, insulation limitations, wiring methods, and installation conditions. These topics often test a candidate’s ability to read the question carefully and identify the rule that applies to the described installation.

Electrical calculations should be practiced repeatedly. Candidates should work on load calculations, conductor sizing, voltage drop concepts, box fill, raceway fill, motor calculations, overcurrent protection, service and feeder sizing, grounding conductor sizing, and equipment rating questions. A good calculation routine starts with identifying the topic, locating the controlling rule or table, setting up the formula, solving the problem, and checking whether the question asks for a minimum, maximum, adjusted value, or final selection.

The 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide supports this part of preparation by helping students build a consistent method for solving electrical math problems. Many calculation mistakes happen because the candidate rushes, uses the wrong table, skips an important condition in the question, or chooses an answer before confirming what the question is asking. Regular practice helps improve both speed and accuracy.

The 2026 Connecticut Master Electrician Study Guide supports broader exam-topic review. Advanced electrical preparation often requires connecting several concepts in one question. A candidate may need to understand grounding, overcurrent protection, conductor sizing, special occupancies, fire alarm systems, or photovoltaic rules in context. Organized study materials help students move through the content with a clearer plan.

The National Electrical Code 2026 paperback supports daily code navigation. Students can use it to practice finding articles, definitions, tables, exceptions, and installation rules.
