Exam Details

The State of Connecticut has contracted with PSI Services LLC to conduct its examination program. PSI provides examinations through a network of computer examination centers in Connecticut, and eligibility for examination is determined by the state. Candidates should receive approval before scheduling and should follow the current PSI and Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection instructions for examination registration.

Connecticut electrical licensing examinations are designed to measure the knowledge needed to work safely and in compliance with applicable electrical standards. The E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson exam is connected to the journeyperson-level electrical license and focuses on the trade knowledge and code application needed for electrical work performed under a properly licensed contractor.

The current Connecticut electrical examination bulletin identifies the E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson exam as an open book examination. The bulletin lists 80 questions, a 70% required passing score, and 3.5 hours allowed for the E-2 examination. Candidates may use a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator in the examination center.

The Connecticut E-2 exam content outline includes general electrical knowledge, services, feeders and branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special occupancies and equipment, electrical power, motors, low voltage, lighting, illuminated signs, fire detection and alarm systems, safety information, overcurrent protection, high voltage, and photovoltaics. These subject areas show why candidates should prepare broadly and practice both electrical concepts and NEC navigation.

The current Connecticut bulletin states that code questions are based only on the edition of the code book listed in the bulletin. This product is based on the 2026 National Electrical Code, and candidates should match their study materials to the NEC edition required for their actual exam date. Open Book Test

The Connecticut E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson examination is an open book test. The current examination bulletin states that candidates are responsible for bringing their own references to the examination center. Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, annotated, or indexed before the exam session, but references may not be written in during the examination session.

An open book electrical exam still requires serious preparation. Having the NEC available does not mean the answer will be easy to find. Candidates must know how the code book is organized, understand electrical terminology, and recognize which article, table, or rule applies to the question. Time management is also important because the exam is timed.

Licensing Steps

The Connecticut journeyperson electrician licensing path begins with meeting the experience and classroom training requirements for the license type being pursued. For the E-2 license, the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection states that proof of 720 classroom hours and 8,000 hours of on-the-job training is required. Candidates may also apply through an equivalent experience and training path if they can prove that their classroom hours, curriculum, and job training meet the state’s requirements.

Many candidates begin by registering as electrical apprentices and completing supervised on-the-job training along with classroom instruction. Apprenticeship provides a structured path for learning electrical installation practices, jobsite safety, code compliance, tools, materials, and trade procedures while gaining the hours needed for journeyperson eligibility.

Once the candidate has completed the required training or equivalent experience, the next step is to submit the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection application materials for the applicable electrical journeyperson license. Candidates using equivalent experience and training should be prepared to submit documentation such as transcripts, degrees, diplomas, and notarized employer letters that describe employment dates and detailed work experience.

After the state determines that the candidate is eligible, the candidate schedules the examination through PSI according to the current examination bulletin. PSI’s procedure checklist directs candidates to use the examination content outlines as the basis for study, schedule after eligibility is received, bring required identification, and arrive before the appointment time.

After the examination, candidates follow the instructions on their passing score report and submit the licensing fee and required materials to the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection. Passing the exam is an important step, but license issuance still depends on meeting the state’s application, documentation, and licensing requirements.

State Requirements

Connecticut regulates electrical licensing through the Department of Consumer Protection. The E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson license permits the holder to do electrical work as defined by Connecticut law, but only while employed by a properly licensed contractor. This distinction is important because the E-2 license is a journeyperson credential, not an independent electrical contractor license.

For the E-2 license, Connecticut identifies 8,000 hours of apprentice on-the-job training and 720 classroom hours. The state also allows an equivalent experience and training application path when the applicant can prove that the required classroom hours, curriculum, and on-the-job training requirements have been met. Documentation must be complete and detailed because the state uses it to determine whether the applicant qualifies for examination.

The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection lists several electrical license types, and each license type has its own scope and training-hour requirements. Candidates should confirm that they are applying for the correct license classification before submitting materials. The E-2 classification is the unlimited electrical journeyperson license, while other Connecticut electrical licenses cover limited scopes such as low voltage, telecommunications, sign work, photovoltaic work, or other specialty areas.

Because the E-2 license is tied to employment under a properly licensed contractor, candidates should also understand the difference between journeyperson and contractor credentials. A journeyperson license supports electrical work within the licensed employment structure. A contractor license is a separate credential with different requirements and responsibilities.

Connecticut electrical candidates should keep organized records throughout training. Useful records include apprenticeship registration, classroom transcripts, employer letters, employment dates, detailed work descriptions, and proof of completed training. Maintaining these records as you go can make the application process smoother when you are ready to apply.

Test Information and Study Materials

Connecticut journeyperson electrician preparation should include a balanced mix of code study, trade review, calculation practice, and repeated self-testing. The National Electrical Code is central to electrical exam preparation because it contains the rules and structure used to support safe electrical installations. Candidates should become comfortable using Article 100 definitions, wiring and protection requirements, wiring methods and materials, equipment rules, special occupancies, special equipment, and tables used for sizing, fill, adjustment, correction, and installation decisions.

When studying with this combo, begin with one topic in the Connecticut Journeyman Electrician Study Guide, then locate related NEC sections in the 2026 code book. This approach helps connect study guide review with actual code language. It also helps candidates practice the same thinking process they will need during an open book exam.

For calculation topics, write out each step. Electrical calculations may require identifying what the question gives you, determining what the question asks for, selecting the correct table or rule, applying adjustments or corrections, and checking whether the answer should be a minimum, maximum, ampacity, rating, load, conductor size, or overcurrent device value. A written process helps improve accuracy.

Timed practice is also valuable. The E-2 exam is timed, so candidates should occasionally study under time limits. Practice using the NEC without rushing the reading. Speed matters, but accurate code application matters more. The goal is to build a steady rhythm: read, identify, locate, verify, and answer.

Review missed questions carefully. When an answer is wrong, return to the study guide and the NEC. Find the rule, read the surrounding language, and understand why the correct answer applies. This helps prepare you for questions that test the same concept in a new way.
