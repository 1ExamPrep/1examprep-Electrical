Exam Details

Connecticut electrical trades examinations are administered by PSI Services LLC for the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection. The PSI Electrical Trades Candidate Information Bulletin lists the E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson exam as an 80-question exam with a 70% required passing score and a 3.5-hour time limit.

Open Book Test

The Connecticut E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson exam is an open book test. The PSI bulletin states that the E-2 examination is open book and that candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references to the examination center.

Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, annotated, and indexed before the exam. References may not be written in during the examination session. Candidates may not bring additional papers, whether loose or attached, with approved references. Permanent tabs are allowed, while temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins.

Open book testing still requires serious preparation. A candidate who is unfamiliar with the National Electrical Code can lose valuable time searching through articles, definitions, tables, exceptions, and related rules. A prepared candidate can move more efficiently through wiring methods, raceway requirements, box rules, grounding and bonding provisions, conductor requirements, service rules, feeder rules, branch-circuit requirements, motor provisions, fire alarm topics, photovoltaic provisions, and safety references.

Licensing Steps

The Connecticut journeyperson electrician licensing path begins with choosing the correct license type. For the unlimited journeyperson electrical classification, candidates typically pursue the E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson license through the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection.

Connecticut’s electrical journeyperson application information states that an E-2 applicant must prove completion of 720 classroom hours and 8,000 hours of on-the-job training. Classroom hours must match the required curriculum standards, and work experience must be documented according to Department requirements.

A practical licensing path begins with completing the required classroom instruction and on-the-job training. The applicant then submits the journeyperson application and supporting documents through the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection process. Required documents may include transcripts, degrees, diplomas, notarized employer letters, and apprenticeship completion documentation when applicable.

After the application is reviewed and the candidate is approved, the candidate schedules the PSI E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson examination. The candidate takes the exam under PSI rules, uses only approved references and allowed items, and follows all testing center procedures.

After passing the examination, the candidate follows the licensing instructions provided on the passing score report and completes the Department’s final licensing process. Candidates should keep their application documents, work records, classroom records, approval notices, and exam communications organized throughout the process.

Preparation should begin before the exam is scheduled. Connecticut E-2 content is broad, and the best preparation usually comes from consistent review over time. State Requirements

Connecticut electrical licensing is regulated by the Department of Consumer Protection. Electrical license classifications include contractor and journeyperson categories, with the E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson license serving as the unlimited journeyperson classification.

For the E-2 license application path based on experience and training, Connecticut lists 8,000 hours of on-the-job training and 720 classroom hours. The Department states that applicants must be able to prove both the classroom hours and on-the-job training hours for the license type being requested.

Connecticut requires classroom hours to be equivalent to the required electrical curriculum. Continuing education certificates and manufacturer certificates do not meet the classroom-hour criteria for initial qualification, and on-the-job training is not accepted in place of the required classroom hours. Employer letters used for experience documentation must include dates of employment and a specific, detailed description of work experience.

For exam preparation, students should understand that state qualification requirements and exam preparation are related but separate. State requirements determine eligibility to sit for the exam and receive a license. Study materials help students prepare for the knowledge portion of the process.
