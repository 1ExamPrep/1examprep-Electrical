Connecticut electrical licensing is administered through the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection. The E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson exam is administered through PSI. Candidates must complete the proper licensing application path and meet the state’s qualification requirements before testing.

The Connecticut E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson exam is listed with 80 questions, a testing time of 3.5 hours, and a required passing score of 70%. The exam is designed to evaluate whether the candidate has the trade knowledge and code understanding required for journeyperson-level electrical work under a licensed contractor.

Open Book Test

The Connecticut E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson exam is listed as an open book exam. Open book testing still requires serious preparation. Candidates must understand electrical concepts, know how the NEC is organized, and move efficiently through reference material while working under a fixed time limit.

Open book electrical exams can be demanding because the answer is not always obvious from a quick lookup. Licensing Steps

Connecticut E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson candidates begin by completing the required training or equivalent experience path. The E-2 license is the journeyperson-level unlimited electrical license and is generally connected to completion of a registered apprenticeship program or approved equivalent experience and training.

After meeting the required training and experience qualifications, the applicant submits the appropriate journeyperson application materials to the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection. The application should include the documentation required for the license type requested. Applicants should make sure work experience, classroom instruction, apprenticeship completion, or equivalent training records are complete and accurate.

Once approved for testing, the candidate schedules the E-2 exam through PSI. The exam is computer-based and is taken according to PSI’s testing procedures. Candidates should review the exam bulletin, scheduling rules, identification requirements, and reference rules before test day.

After passing the examination, the candidate follows the licensing instructions provided through the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection. The license process is not complete until all required licensing steps, fees, and documentation are satisfied. Candidates who do not pass must follow the applicable retesting procedures before another attempt.

A strong licensing plan begins early. Candidates should maintain accurate apprenticeship and employment records, complete required classroom instruction, review the E-2 scope of work, and begin study before the exam is scheduled. Electrical exam preparation is usually strongest when it is built over time through repeated topic review, calculations practice, and code navigation.

State Requirements

Connecticut E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson applicants must meet the state’s training and experience requirements for the license. The E-2 path is commonly tied to 8,000 hours of on-the-job training and 720 hours of classroom instruction, or approved equivalent experience and training accepted for the license classification.

The E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson license allows the holder to do the same electrical work as an E-1 Unlimited Electrical Contractor, but only while employed by a contractor licensed for that work. This distinction matters because the E-2 is a journeyperson credential, while the E-1 is the contractor-level license.

Connecticut recognizes multiple electrical license categories, including unlimited and limited classifications. Students should make sure they are preparing for the correct license type. The E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson license is different from limited journeyperson classifications and from the E-1 Unlimited Electrical Contractor license.

Applicants using equivalent experience or an equivalent out-of-state license must satisfy the Connecticut requirements for the license classification. Equivalent experience and training must match the classroom and on-the-job training requirements for the license type being requested. Documentation is important because the state reviews whether the applicant’s training and experience meet the required standard.

That includes trade understanding, electrical safety, code navigation, calculation ability, and the confidence to apply electrical rules to practical installation questions.

The 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide supports the math side of preparation. Electrical calculations can involve several steps, and students should practice them repeatedly. Useful calculation areas may include Ohm’s law, power formulas, conductor sizing, voltage drop concepts, box fill, raceway fill, service and feeder calculations, motor calculations, overcurrent protection, and load calculation principles.

Calculation study should be active. Students should work problems by hand, write out formulas, identify known values, convert units where needed, and check whether the answer makes sense. Reading a solved example can help introduce a concept, but repeated practice builds speed and confidence. This is especially important for timed exams because calculation questions can take longer than direct knowledge questions.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback should be used throughout the study process. Students should practice locating definitions, article sections, tables, notes, and exceptions. They should also learn how related code topics connect. For example, a conductor question may involve ampacity, adjustment factors, terminal temperature limitations, overcurrent protection, and equipment requirements. A grounding question may involve grounding electrodes, bonding jumpers, equipment grounding conductors, services, and fault-current paths.

A balanced study schedule may rotate between trade review, calculation practice, and NEC navigation. One session might focus on services and feeders. Another might focus on grounding and bonding. Another might involve working voltage drop or conductor sizing problems. Another might involve locating raceway, box, or overcurrent protection rules in the NEC. Rotating study areas helps candidates prepare across the full exam outline.

Timed practice should also be part of preparation. Since the Connecticut E-2 exam is timed and open book, pacing matters. Students should practice answering questions without spending too long on one item. Learning when to look up a rule, when to calculate, and when to move forward can help create a better exam-day rhythm.
