Exam Details

The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection uses PSI Services LLC for the Occupational Licensing Electrical Trades examination program. The Connecticut E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson exam includes 80 questions, requires a 70% passing score, and allows 3.5 hours of testing time.

The E-2 exam content outline includes general electrical knowledge, service, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special occupancies and equipment, electrical power, motors, low voltage, lighting, illuminated signs, fire detection and alarm systems, safety information, overcurrent protection, high voltage, and photovoltaics. Because the exam covers a wide range of topics, candidates should avoid studying only one area of the trade. A complete plan should include code navigation, theory review, calculation practice, safety knowledge, and exam-question practice.

The exam may include questions based on listed reference material, trade knowledge, and general industry practices. Candidates may use a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator in the examination center. For code questions, the exam is based on the specific code edition listed by the exam provider for the scheduled examination, so candidates should pay close attention to the edition required for their exam appointment.

This Ultimate Combo supports preparation with materials based on the 2026 National Electrical Code. Candidates studying for a Connecticut exam should use their study time to strengthen code-navigation habits, improve calculation speed, and review the E-2 subject areas in a balanced way.

Open Book Test

The Connecticut E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson exam is an open book examination. Candidates are responsible for bringing approved reference materials to the examination center. Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, annotated, and indexed before the exam session. References may not be written in during the examination session.

Permanent tabs and indexes are allowed when they are part of the approved reference setup. Temporary tabs, such as Post-it notes, are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins. Candidates are not permitted to bring additional papers, loose notes, attached notes, or unapproved materials with their references. Writing questions and answers in reference materials is strictly prohibited.

This makes preparation with a tabbed NEC especially valuable. The included National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs is useful for building familiarity with code layout, article structure, tables, definitions, wiring methods, grounding and bonding rules, service requirements, overcurrent protection rules, motor provisions, photovoltaic provisions, and other NEC areas that may appear in journeyperson preparation.

Open book does not mean easy. Candidates still need to know where to look, how to interpret code language, and how to apply the correct rule to the question. The best study approach is to practice identifying the subject of each question first, then locating the relevant code article, table, or rule efficiently.

Licensing Steps

Connecticut electrical licensing is handled through the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection. Candidates pursuing the E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson license should follow the state application process, document required training and experience, complete the exam process through PSI, and submit any required licensing materials and fees.

A typical path for an E-2 journeyperson applicant includes completing a registered apprenticeship program or qualifying through equivalent experience and training, gathering proof of classroom and on-the-job training hours, submitting the proper application materials to the Department of Consumer Protection, scheduling and taking the PSI electrical journeyperson examination, and completing licensing steps after receiving a passing score.

State Requirements

The Connecticut E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson license allows the holder to perform electrical work as defined by Connecticut law while employed by a properly licensed contractor. To qualify for the E-2 examination, candidates must complete a registered apprenticeship program or have at least four years of equivalent experience and training.

Connecticut’s E-2 pathway commonly involves 8,000 hours of on-the-job training and 720 classroom hours. Applicants using equivalent experience and training must be prepared to document that their background meets the license requirements for the category they are applying for.

Connecticut uses a license classification system for electrical work. The E-1 license is the unlimited electrical contractor license, while the E-2 license is the unlimited electrical journeyperson license. Limited electrical categories also exist for specialized work, but this product is written for the Connecticut journeyperson electrician path associated with the E-2 Unlimited Electrical Journeyperson examination.

Passing the examination is one part of the licensing process. Candidates must still meet Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection requirements, submit required documents, and complete the state licensing process. Exam preparation should be paired with careful attention to application requirements, experience records, and the specific license classification being pursued.
