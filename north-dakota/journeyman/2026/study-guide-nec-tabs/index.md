## Licensing Steps

The North Dakota journeyman electrician licensing process begins with meeting the qualification requirements set by the North Dakota State Electrical Board. A journeyman electrician must complete the required experience as a registered apprentice electrician under the supervision of a licensed contracting master or master of record in an area where electrical construction work is performed under licensing and inspection rules similar to North Dakota.

The Board states that a journeyman electrician must have completed 8,000 hours of experience, and that this experience may not be obtained in less than three years. The Board also states that the candidate must have successfully completed apprentice electrician training. A graduate of a two-year or longer electrical school accepted by the Board may receive 2,000 hours of credit.

Once the candidate meets the experience and training requirements, the next step is to apply for examination approval through the North Dakota State Electrical Board. Candidates must be approved before signing up for an exam date. After approval, the candidate receives an exam invite with available dates and must test within the required authorization period.

After taking the exam, the candidate must meet the passing requirement and complete any remaining steps required by the Board. A passing score is an important part of the process, but candidates must still follow all licensing instructions and submit any required documentation or fees connected to the license application.

Preparation should begin before the exam date is selected. ## State Requirements

North Dakota electrical licensing is administered by the North Dakota State Electrical Board. For journeyman electrician qualification, the Board requires 8,000 hours of experience, which may not be obtained in less than three years, while registered as an apprentice electrician. The experience must be under the supervision of a contracting master or master of record licensed electrician in an area with electrical construction licensing and inspection rules similar to North Dakota.

The Board’s qualification information also states that journeyman candidates must successfully complete apprentice electrician training. Up to 3,000 hours may apply under the qualifications of a Class B electrician, and a graduate of a two-year or longer electrical school accepted by the Board may receive 2,000 hours of credit. Practical electrical experience includes wiring for and installing electrical wiring, apparatus, and equipment.

The Board also identifies other types of experience credit that may be allowed in limited amounts, including repairing electrical wiring, apparatus, and equipment for light, heat, and power; wiring fire alarm technology circuits or systems; wiring process control circuits or power limited systems; and qualifying electrical construction experience gained in the armed forces of the United States when determined by the Board to be equivalent.

Because licensing requirements involve experience review, training, application approval, examination, and Board rules, candidates should make sure they understand the exact requirements before applying.
