Exam Details

The North Dakota Journeyman Electrician examination is administered through the North Dakota State Electrical Board. Applicants must apply and be approved before signing up for an exam date. After approval, the applicant receives an exam invitation listing available testing dates and must select an exam date through the Board process.

The North Dakota electrical examinations are based on fundamental electricity, North Dakota laws, North Dakota rules, North Dakota wiring standards, and the current edition of the National Electrical Code. The passing grade is 70%. Applicants must test within six months of receiving an exam invite.

The Journeyman Electrician examination includes both closed-book and open-book testing time. The closed-book portion is listed as 1 hour, and the open-book portion is listed as 3.5 hours. The Board provides code books, calculators, and pencils for the examination.

The current North Dakota State Electrical Board exam information refers to the current edition of the National Electrical Code. This Super Combo is based on the 2026 National Electrical Code for candidates who want 2026 NEC-based study materials, current code navigation practice, and journeyman-level review.

Because the exam includes both no-reference and reference-based work, candidates should prepare in two ways. Closed-book preparation should include fundamentals, definitions, formulas, rules, state-specific requirements, and important trade concepts. Open-book preparation should include code navigation, article recognition, table use, calculation setup, and careful reading of NEC language.

Journeyman-level preparation should include services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, boxes, cables, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, motors, transformers, safety, electrical theory, and calculation work. Candidates should also review North Dakota laws, rules, and wiring standards because those subjects are part of the examination basis.

Open Book Test

The North Dakota Journeyman Electrician examination includes an open-book portion. The Board provides code books for the exam, along with calculators and pencils. The open-book portion for the Journeyman examination is listed as 3.5 hours.

The examination also includes a closed-book portion. The closed-book portion for the Journeyman examination is listed as 1 hour. Candidates should prepare for both types of testing. Licensing Steps

North Dakota candidates begin by confirming that the Journeyman Electrician license is the correct license classification. The North Dakota State Electrical Board issues electrical licenses and reviews applicant qualifications before authorizing candidates to take the examination.

A practical licensing path begins with registering and working as an apprentice electrician under the supervision of a contracting master or master of record licensed electrician in a jurisdiction with similar licensing and inspection rules to North Dakota. Candidates should complete the required experience and apprentice electrician training, document qualifying work, submit the examination application and employment verification, receive Board approval, select an available exam date, take the examination, and complete the licensing process after passing.

The Board states that a Journeyman electrician must have completed 8,000 hours of experience, which may not be obtained in less than three years, while registered as an apprentice electrician. Up to 3,000 hours may apply under Class B electrician qualifications. The experience must be under the supervision of a contracting master or master of record licensed electrician in an area with similar licensing and inspection rules to North Dakota, and the applicant must have successfully completed apprentice electrician training.

The Board may grant 2,000 hours of credit for a graduate of a two-year or longer electrical school accepted by the Board. Practical experience consists of wiring for and installing electrical wiring, apparatus, and equipment. Additional experience credit may be allowed in areas such as repair work, fire alarm technology circuits or systems, process control circuits or power limited systems, and qualifying electrical construction experience gained in the armed forces.

After the Board approves an application, the applicant receives an invitation to take the examination. If the examination is not taken in the allotted time, the applicant may be required to complete another application and pay the necessary fee. Passing the examination is an important milestone, but licensing is completed through the Board process.

State Requirements

The North Dakota State Electrical Board regulates electrical licensing and has jurisdiction over electrical installations in the state. The Board examines applicants and issues licenses to those who have the required qualifications and knowledge of electrical laws and electrical codes.

North Dakota license classifications include master electrician, journeyman electrician, Class B electrician, and power limited electrician classifications. The Journeyman Electrician classification is intended for individuals who have completed the required apprentice experience, training, and examination process.

For a journeyman electrician license, North Dakota requires 8,000 hours of qualifying experience, which may not be obtained in less than three years, while registered as an apprentice electrician. The experience must be under proper supervision and in a jurisdiction with similar licensing and inspection rules. The applicant must also successfully complete apprentice electrician training.

North Dakota journeyman licenses expire each year on March 31. The annual journeyman renewal fee is $25. To renew, the licensee must have 8 hours of continuing education on file with the Board, including 4 hours of code. License renewals begin January 1 each year.

Because licensing requirements are controlled by the North Dakota State Electrical Board, candidates should follow the Board’s current forms, exam invitations, renewal requirements, and instructions. Study materials help candidates prepare, but the Board determines eligibility, exam approval, license issuance, renewal compliance, and enforcement of licensing requirements.

Electrical calculations should be practiced consistently before the exam. Candidates should review Ohm’s Law, power formulas, amperage, voltage, resistance, watts, conductor sizing, branch circuit calculations, feeder concepts, service load concepts, box fill, conduit fill, motor calculations, transformer concepts, overcurrent protection, and voltage drop. Calculation questions can take more time than direct code questions, so repeated practice helps improve both speed and accuracy.

Code navigation should also be part of every study plan. Candidates should practice using the NEC table of contents, index, article numbers, definitions, tables, notes, and exceptions. The goal is to identify the topic quickly and move to the correct section without wasting time. A candidate who understands how the NEC is organized is better prepared for the open-book portion of the North Dakota examination.

Fundamental electricity should be reviewed for the closed-book portion. Candidates should study electrical theory, series and parallel circuits, voltage, current, resistance, power, conductor properties, load relationships, grounding concepts, and practical electrical problem-solving. These topics support both the examination and daily electrical work.

Services, feeders, and branch circuits should receive careful attention. Candidates should review service conductors, service disconnects, grounding at services, feeder sizing, branch circuit ratings, required outlets, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, and load calculations. These topics connect code knowledge with practical installation skill.

Grounding and bonding are important areas for journeyman exam preparation. Candidates should review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, raceway bonding, panel bonding, grounded conductors, separately derived systems, and related safety requirements. Grounding and bonding questions often require careful reading because similar terms can lead to different code requirements.

Wiring methods and materials should be studied consistently. Candidates should review conductors, cables, raceways, boxes, fittings, supports, conductor ampacity, insulation types, flexible wiring methods, underground wiring, and installation conditions. These topics appear frequently in electrical work and are central to NEC-based study.

Motors, transformers, controls, and equipment should also be part of the study routine. Candidates should review motor branch circuits, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, disconnecting means, controllers, transformer sizing, transformer protection, luminaires, receptacles, switches, appliances, panelboards, and related installation requirements.

North Dakota laws, rules, and wiring standards should not be ignored. The examination is based on those state requirements in addition to fundamental electricity and the NEC. Candidates should review state-specific requirements as part of their preparation so they are not relying only on general trade knowledge.

Flash cards are useful for shorter study sessions. Candidates can use them to review formulas, definitions, electrical theory, safety concepts, NEC topics, and trade terms before work, after work, during breaks, or between longer study blocks. Flash cards work best when paired with deeper reading, calculation drills, and code lookup practice.

A strong study plan should include scheduled reading, NEC navigation, calculation drills, closed-book review, timed practice, and repeated review of weaker topics. Candidates should avoid studying only familiar material. Journeyman electrician preparation rewards steady practice, careful reading, and the ability to apply electrical knowledge under exam conditions.
