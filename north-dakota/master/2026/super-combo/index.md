## Exam Details

North Dakota electrical licensing is handled through the North Dakota State Electrical Board. The Board examines applicants and issues licenses to individuals who meet the necessary qualifications and demonstrate knowledge in electrical laws and electrical codes. Candidates must apply and be approved before signing up for an exam date.

The North Dakota State Electrical Board states that the examinations are based on fundamental electricity, Laws, Rules, and Wiring Standards of North Dakota, and the current edition of the National Electrical Code. ## Licensing Steps

A practical North Dakota licensing path begins with confirming the correct license category. North Dakota licensing includes master electrician, journeyman electrician, Class B electrician, and related electrical licensing categories. Candidates pursuing the Master Electrician license should confirm that the master classification is the correct credential for the work, supervision, planning, responsibility, and business role they intend to perform.

North Dakota requires the applicant to complete the proper experience before applying. A Master electrician must have completed one year of experience as a licensed journeyman and must have at least 2,000 hours of experience working as a licensed journeyman electrician under the supervision of a contracting master electrician or master of record. The Board identifies three categories of master electricians: contracting master, master of record, and non-contracting master.

After the candidate meets the required qualifications, the next step is to submit the examination application and required documentation to the North Dakota State Electrical Board. Candidates may also need employment verification and other supporting forms. Once the Board gives final approval, the applicant receives an invitation to take the examination. The invitation outlines available testing dates, and the candidate must choose a testing date within the allowed window.

After the exam is completed, the candidate must meet the passing requirement and follow the remaining licensing steps through the Board. Candidates who do not pass must follow the Board’s re-exam process and waiting periods based on score. North Dakota lists no waiting period for scores from 60 to 69, a 3-month waiting period for scores from 50 to 59, and a 6-month waiting period for scores from 0 to 49.

The study portion of the licensing path should be treated as a structured project. Candidates should review the exam subjects, study the 2026 NEC, practice calculations, work through code navigation, review North Dakota laws and wiring standards, and use flash cards for repeated reinforcement. Consistent preparation helps candidates approach North Dakota master electrician study with more organization and confidence.

## State Requirements

North Dakota regulates electrical licensing through the North Dakota State Electrical Board. The Board is responsible for examining applicants and issuing licenses to individuals who have the necessary qualifications and knowledge of electrical laws and electrical codes. The state’s requirements include electrical construction work performed in a jurisdiction with similar licensing and inspection rules to North Dakota and under the supervision of a master electrician.

For the Master Electrician license, North Dakota requires one year of experience as a licensed journeyman and at least 2,000 hours of experience working as a licensed journeyman electrician under the supervision of a contracting master electrician or master of record. This experience requirement reflects the higher responsibility associated with master-level electrical work, including planning, layout, supervision, and compliance with wiring standards.

The three North Dakota master electrician categories are important for candidates to understand. A contracting master is tied to electrical contracting responsibility. A master of record is responsible for adherence to North Dakota wiring standards for a business or organization and must meet insurance-related requirements through that organization. A non-contracting master is responsible for following laws and rules and has the same responsibility as a journeyman electrician, with electrical work performed under the supervision of a contracting master or master of record.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong North Dakota Master Electrician study plan should include NEC study, calculation practice, trade knowledge review, North Dakota laws and rules review, wiring standards review, closed-book recall practice, open-book reference navigation, and timed question work. Electrical exams can cover a wide range of subjects, so candidates should avoid spending all study time on only one topic. A balanced routine helps build confidence across fundamental electricity, code application, state rules, wiring standards, calculations, and practical installation concepts.

For NEC study, start by learning the structure of the code book. Review article organization, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and cross-references. Practice moving from a question to the correct article and reading the surrounding language carefully. Many electrical exam questions are missed because a candidate finds a related section but overlooks a condition, exception, table note, or specific wording.

For North Dakota laws, rules, and wiring standards, study the state material as part of the regular schedule. These subjects can be tested differently from NEC lookup questions because they may require understanding of licensing responsibilities, supervision rules, installation standards, and state electrical requirements. Candidates should not leave state-specific review until the final study session.

For calculations, write out each step. Electrical calculation mistakes often happen when a candidate skips a conversion, uses the wrong table, applies the wrong value, or confuses branch circuit, feeder, service, motor, transformer, raceway fill, or box fill requirements. The 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide gives candidates a dedicated way to practice these skills repeatedly.

For open book preparation, use the NEC actively during study. Practice locating sections, reading tables, and confirming answers in the code book. Then practice some questions without immediately opening the code book so you can build recognition. The stronger your recall becomes, the more time you can save during questions that require deeper code lookup.

For flash card study, use short sessions throughout the week. Flash cards can help reinforce formulas, definitions, code concepts, and common electrical terms. They are useful before work, during breaks, after a longer study block, or as final review. Flash cards should not replace full study sessions, but they help strengthen recall and recognition.
