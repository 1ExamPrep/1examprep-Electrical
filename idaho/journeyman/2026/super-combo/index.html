Exam Details

Idaho electrical licensing is handled through the Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses and the Idaho Electrical Board. DOPL states that applicants seeking initial licensure as an Electrical Journeyman must meet the requirements for licensure and pass the NASCLA examination before submitting an application to DOPL.

Idaho’s electrical exam process is now offered through PSI. DOPL directs applicants to register for the appropriate exam through PSI, select the desired exam, complete the application, and schedule the examination after the application is accepted through the testing system.

DOPL’s electrical exam information lists the minimum passing score for the Journeyman Electrician exam as 70%. The exam is multiple choice, and exam questions are based on approved references, trade knowledge, and the electrical standards connected with the specific examination.

Open Book Test

Idaho electrical exams are open book. DOPL’s electrical exam information states that all electrical license exams are open book and that approved books are listed for the applicable exam. The bulletin also states that code books are not provided and that candidates must bring their own approved reference materials when required by the exam instructions.

DOPL’s reference rules state that candidates may highlight their books, tab different sections, and leave notes in reference material, but loose paper in reference material is not allowed. Candidates should also follow PSI and NASCLA rules for the specific exam, because current approved references and testing procedures are controlled by the active candidate bulletin.

Open book testing still requires serious preparation. A candidate who is unfamiliar with the National Electrical Code can lose valuable time searching through definitions, article sections, tables, exceptions, and related rules. Licensing Steps

The Idaho journeyman electrician licensing path begins with apprentice registration and qualifying electrical work experience. DOPL lists the initial licensure path for Electrical Journeyman applicants as maintaining an active apprentice registration, obtaining the required work experience and schooling, passing the NASCLA examination through PSI, and then applying for licensure through DOPL.

One Idaho journeyman pathway is 8,000 hours of work experience and four years of approved schooling. Idaho also lists an alternate pathway of 16,000 hours of work experience. Applicants should maintain records of their work experience, schooling, apprenticeship registration, and any documentation needed for DOPL review.

After completing the required experience and education path, the candidate registers for and passes the NASCLA examination through PSI. DOPL states that applicants seeking initial licensure as an Electrical Journeyman must pass the NASCLA examination before submitting the licensure application to DOPL.

After passing the examination, the applicant applies for licensure through DOPL. The license application is separate from exam preparation and requires the applicant to satisfy the Board’s current licensure requirements. Candidates should keep exam results, schooling records, work experience documentation, and registration information organized throughout the process.

A practical preparation path includes maintaining apprentice registration, completing required work hours, completing approved schooling when applicable, reviewing the exam bulletin, studying the NEC, practicing calculations, building speed with reference navigation, using flash cards for recall, passing the examination, and completing the DOPL licensure application.

State Requirements

The Idaho Electrical Board regulates electrical licensing through the Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses. Idaho’s electrical program is designed to protect life, health, and property by ensuring that electrical installations comply with current safety codes and electrical standards.

For initial Electrical Journeyman licensure, DOPL lists the required path as 8,000 hours of work experience and four years of approved schooling, or 16,000 hours of work experience, followed by passing the NASCLA examination through PSI and applying for licensure through DOPL.

Idaho administrative rules identify a journeyman applicant pathway based on 8,000 hours of work experience as an apprentice making electrical installations and satisfactory completion of a four-year approved sequence of instruction. The rules also identify an alternate pathway based on 16,000 hours of electrical experience.

For exam preparation, students should understand that state qualification requirements and study preparation are related but separate. State requirements determine whether an applicant may become licensed. Study materials help the candidate prepare for the knowledge and code portion of the process.
