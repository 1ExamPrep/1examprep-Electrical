Exam Details

The Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses oversees electrical licensing through the Idaho Electrical Board. Idaho journeyman electrician applicants must meet the state’s qualification requirements and pass the required examination before applying for initial licensure.

The Idaho Electrical Journeyman exam is listed as a 4-hour examination. Idaho’s electrical exam information identifies the Journeyman Electrician passing score as 70%. The published exam breakdown lists 100 total journeyman exam questions across National Electrical Code subject areas, including introductory material, NEC Chapters 1 through 9, and calculations.

Idaho applicants seeking initial licensure as an Electrical Journeyman must register for and pass the required NASCLA examination through PSI before submitting the licensure application to DOPL. Open Book Test

Idaho electrical license exams are open book. Open book does not mean easy. Candidates still need to understand electrical concepts, recognize how exam questions are written, and move through approved references efficiently. A student who depends only on searching through a book for every answer can lose valuable time during the exam.

Open-book electrical exams reward preparation and reference familiarity. Students should practice using the table of contents, index, article structure, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and common code sections before exam day. The more comfortable a student is with the reference material, the less time is wasted searching during the test.

Idaho testing information states that candidates must bring approved references for open-book exams and that code books are not provided. Reference materials may be highlighted, tabbed, and include notes, but loose paper in reference materials is not allowed. Only approved references are allowed in the testing room.

This product includes flash cards based on the 2026 National Electrical Code for current-code study and repeated review. Students should use the exam-day reference edition and approved books listed for the Idaho exam they are taking. The study guide, practice exams, final exams, and flash cards are designed for preparation before test day.

A strong open-book strategy includes learning how the NEC is organized, practicing with article structure, using definitions carefully, studying tables, reviewing exceptions, and practicing calculations before the exam. Licensing Steps

Idaho journeyman electrician licensing is handled through the Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses and the Idaho Electrical Board. Applicants seeking initial Electrical Journeyman licensure must meet the requirements for licensure and pass the required examination before submitting the application to DOPL.

The standard Idaho journeyman pathway includes maintaining an active apprentice registration, obtaining 8,000 hours of work experience, and completing 4 years of approved schooling. Idaho also recognizes an alternate pathway of 16,000 hours of electrical work experience. Applicants must register for and pass the required NASCLA examination through PSI before applying for licensure through DOPL.

Idaho rules identify a journeyman applicant pathway requiring a minimum of 8,000 hours of work experience as an apprentice making electrical installations and satisfactory completion of a four-year sequence of approved instruction. The alternate experience pathway requires proof of 16,000 hours of electrical experience. Applicants should keep accurate documentation of work experience, schooling, apprentice registration, and exam completion.

After meeting the qualification pathway and passing the required exam, the applicant submits the Electrical Journeyman licensure application through DOPL. The licensing process is separate from study preparation, and applicants must complete all state forms, documentation, fees, and Board requirements for the license to be issued.

This study guide and flash card combo supports the exam preparation stage of the licensing process. Students can use it while completing apprenticeship training, while documenting work hours, before registering for the exam, after scheduling through PSI, or during final review before the scheduled exam date.
