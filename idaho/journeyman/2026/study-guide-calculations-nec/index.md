## Licensing Steps

Idaho journeyman electrician candidates begin by applying for and maintaining an active apprentice registration while working toward the required experience and education path. Apprentice registration is an important part of the Idaho electrical licensing structure because it connects supervised work experience to future journeyman eligibility.

The standard journeyman path includes obtaining **8,000 hours** of work experience and completing **4 years of approved schooling**. Idaho also provides an alternate path of obtaining **16,000 hours** of electrical experience. Candidates should maintain accurate records of work experience, supervised electrical installations, apprenticeship status, and classroom instruction because those records support the licensing process.

After meeting the required experience and education path, the candidate registers for and passes the NASCLA journeyman electrical examination through PSI. The exam should be scheduled according to PSI’s current procedures. Candidates should review the candidate bulletin for approved references, exam rules, testing options, identification requirements, and score procedures.

After passing the examination, the applicant applies for licensure through the Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses. Passing the exam is a major step, but the license process is not complete until DOPL receives and processes the required journeyman application and supporting documentation.

A strong licensing plan begins early. Candidates should keep employment records organized, complete approved schooling on schedule, maintain apprentice registration when required, review the exam blueprint, and build a steady study routine. Journeyman exam preparation is usually strongest when students study over time through trade review, code navigation, and calculation practice instead of waiting until the exam date is close.

## State Requirements

Idaho journeyman electrician applicants must pass the examination designated by the Idaho Electrical Board and satisfy the required experience and education standards. The standard path requires a minimum of **8,000 hours** of work experience as an apprentice making electrical installations, along with satisfactory completion of a **four-year sequence of instruction** approved by the Idaho Division of Career Technical Education.

Idaho also allows a journeyman applicant to qualify by submitting proof of **16,000 hours** of electrical experience in accordance with the requirements of the jurisdiction where the experience was obtained. This alternate path is useful for applicants with extensive electrical experience who are not using the 8,000-hour plus approved-schooling route.

The Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses currently describes the initial journeyman process as maintaining active apprentice registration, obtaining the required experience and schooling or alternate experience, passing the NASCLA examination through PSI, and then applying for licensure through DOPL.

Idaho electrical licensing is separate from electrical contracting requirements. Journeyman licensing allows an individual to work at the journeyman level within the state’s electrical licensing framework. Electrical contracting and master electrician responsibilities have separate requirements. Students should make sure they are preparing for the correct license type and following the application path for the credential they want to obtain.
