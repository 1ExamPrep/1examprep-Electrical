Exam Details

Idaho electrician licensing is handled through the Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses and the Idaho Electrical Board. The Electrical Program exists to protect life, health, and property by ensuring electrical installations in Idaho comply with current safety codes and electrical standards. The licensing unit helps ensure that contractors and journeyman electricians performing work in Idaho are properly qualified and possess current knowledge and skills.

Applicants seeking initial licensure as an Idaho Electrical Master must have held an active Journeyman license for 4 years and pass the required examination before applying for licensure through DOPL. Idaho’s current electrical exam process directs applicants to PSI for examination registration and scheduling. Candidates register for the appropriate examination through PSI, complete the exam, and then apply for licensure through DOPL after achieving a passing score.

The Idaho electrical master exam path is connected to the NASCLA examination process through PSI. The exam is designed to measure advanced electrical knowledge and the ability to apply code requirements to real-world electrical work. Candidates should be prepared for questions involving electrical theory, code application, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, materials, raceways, conductors, electrical equipment, special occupancies, special conditions, motors, controls, communication systems, safety, and calculations.

Idaho’s exam information identifies electrical licensing exams as computer-based and open book. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved reference materials to the examination center. The testing process requires proper identification, compliance with PSI security rules, and use of only approved items during the exam session. Candidates should schedule the exam under the same legal name that appears on their government-issued identification.

After passing the required exam, the candidate must apply for licensure through the Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses. Passing the examination is an important step, but the license is issued only after DOPL receives and processes the required licensing application and confirms that the applicant meets the applicable requirements.

Open Book Test

The Idaho electrical licensing exams are open book. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved reference materials to the examination center. Approved references must follow the testing rules, and no unauthorized materials may be used during the exam.

Open book does not mean the exam is easy. It means candidates must know how to use their references efficiently. The exam clock continues while candidates search the code book, review tables, compare definitions, check exceptions, and work calculations. A candidate who has not practiced with the NEC can lose valuable time even when the correct reference is available.

Idaho’s exam rules allow candidates to prepare reference materials before the examination with highlighting, underlining, and permanent indexing. Additional papers and temporary markers, including Post-it notes, are not allowed. No writing is allowed during the examination session. Candidates should prepare their references carefully before test day and make sure their materials comply with the current PSI and Idaho testing rules.

The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs included in this Ultimate Combo is a practical study tool for open-book exam preparation. Tabs help candidates become more familiar with NEC organization during study, including definitions, general requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, communication systems, tables, and calculation references.

Strong open-book preparation includes repeated reference navigation. Candidates should practice using the table of contents, index, article structure, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and cross-references. The goal is not to memorize the entire NEC. The goal is to know how the NEC is organized, recognize where common topics are located, and confirm answers efficiently under exam conditions.

Licensing Steps

Idaho Master Electrician candidates begin by confirming that they meet the state’s experience requirement. Applicants seeking initial licensure as an Electrical Master must have held an active Journeyman license for 4 years. This requirement reflects the advanced nature of the Master Electrician credential and the expectation that candidates already have substantial journeyman-level experience before applying for master licensure.

The current Idaho process requires applicants to pass the appropriate examination before submitting the license application to DOPL. Candidates register for and pass the required examination through PSI. Online registration is available through PSI, where candidates create an account, select the appropriate Idaho examination, complete the application process, and schedule the exam.

After registering, candidates should prepare their approved reference materials, study the applicable code content, review electrical calculations, and become familiar with testing center rules. Candidates are responsible for bringing approved references to the exam center. Personal items, electronic devices, unauthorized notes, and unapproved materials are not allowed in the examination room.

After passing the examination, the candidate applies for licensure through the Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses. The candidate creates or uses an online DOPL account, submits the license application, provides any required documentation, and follows DOPL instructions for processing. The license is issued after the application is reviewed and the requirements are satisfied.

Candidates should keep copies of exam registration information, score reports, identification records, experience documentation, and licensing correspondence. Organized records can make the licensing process smoother and help avoid delays caused by incomplete information.

State Requirements

Idaho regulates electrical work through the Idaho Electrical Board under the Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses. The state licenses and regulates electrical contractors, limited electrical contractors, master electricians, journeyman electricians, and apprentice electricians. Idaho’s electrical rules apply to those who make electrical installations, including licensed contractors and the master, journeyman, and apprentice electricians employed by them.

The Master Electrician license is an advanced individual license. A person holding a current master license is not required to hold a journeyman license. The master license is separate from the electrical contractor license. Electricians who plan to operate a business or contract for electrical work should review the contractor licensing requirements that apply to their business activity.

Idaho’s licensing structure is built around safety, experience, examination, and compliance with electrical standards. Candidates should choose the correct license path based on their work history, current credential, and professional goals. Journeyman applicants, master applicants, limited installer applicants, and contractor applicants follow different licensing requirements and examination paths.

Because Idaho’s electrical licensing process uses current DOPL and PSI procedures, candidates should prepare for both the exam and the post-exam licensing application. Passing the exam is not the same as holding the license. The candidate must still complete the DOPL application process and receive the license before representing themselves as licensed at that level.
