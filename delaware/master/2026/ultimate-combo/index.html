Exam Details

Delaware electrical licensing is handled through the Delaware Division of Professional Regulation and the Delaware Board of Electrical Examiners. The Board must approve a candidate before the candidate may sit for the required examination. Delaware uses ProV as the external testing vendor for required electrical licensing examinations.

The Delaware Master Electrician exam is a computer-based, open-book examination. The ProV candidate information bulletin lists the Delaware Master Electrician exam with 80 questions and 3 hours of testing time. The minimum passing score required by Delaware is 75%.

The Delaware Master Electrician exam content outline includes General Electrical Knowledge, Plan Reading, Equipment for General Use, Wiring and Protection, Wiring Methods and Materials, Motors and Controls, Special Conditions, Special Equipment, Special Occupancies, and Communication Systems. These subject areas cover a broad range of electrical work, so candidates should build a balanced study plan instead of focusing only on one or two familiar topics.

Delaware defines a Master Electrician as a person licensed by the Board to plan, estimate, layout, perform, or supervise the installation, erection, or repair of electrical conductors, ducts, raceways, conduits, machinery, apparatus, devices, or fixtures for lighting, heating, or power in a structure. That scope makes the Master Electrician exam a broad trade examination that requires both code knowledge and practical electrical understanding.

The Delaware Division of Professional Regulation states that all electrical examinations are based on the most up-to-date National Electrical Code adopted in Delaware. This product is based on the 2026 National Electrical Code for candidates preparing for the 2026 code cycle.

Open Book Test

The Delaware Master Electrician exam is an open-book, timed exam. Candidates are allowed to use authorized references during testing, and those references are checked by the ProV test administrator before the candidate is permitted into the testing room. Open-book testing still requires strong preparation because candidates must know how to locate information quickly, apply rules correctly, and avoid wasting time searching through unfamiliar material.

The ProV testing rules allow approved reference materials to be prepared with highlighting, underlining in pen only, and permanent tabs. Handwritten notes are not allowed in any portion of a reference book. Moveable tabs are not permitted, Post-it notes are not permitted, and photocopied documents cannot be added to a reference book. Candidates may bring a simple four-function calculator, but special calculators, tablets, phones, and similar devices are not permitted.

Open book does not mean the answer will be obvious. Candidates still need to understand the question, identify the correct code topic, search efficiently, read exceptions carefully, and confirm the answer. A strong study routine should include repeated code-book practice, timed question sets, calculations review, and review of weak topics. Licensing Steps

Delaware Master Electrician candidates begin by applying through DELPROS, the Delaware Professional Regulation Online Services portal. A candidate must create a DELPROS user account, begin the correct license application, submit the required application materials, and receive approval from the Board of Electrical Examiners before sitting for the examination.

For examination applicants, Delaware requires proof of experience. For the Master Electrician license, Delaware lists six years of full-time experience under the supervision of a licensed Master Electrician, or four years of full-time experience under a Master Electrician plus an official transcript showing two years of technical training. Candidates who completed a registered or approved apprenticeship program may submit the required certificate of completion as part of the application process.

Experience documentation is important. Delaware requires a Verification of Employment form from each employer when experience must be documented. For Delaware experience obtained after June 30, 2012, the candidate must have been licensed as an Apprentice Electrician or Journeyperson Electrician at the time for that experience to be credited.

After the Board reviews and approves the application, the candidate receives notice of approval to sit for the exam. ProV then provides the examination registration information. Candidates schedule their exam through ProV and may test in person at available testing locations or through approved online testing when eligible. Candidates should follow all scheduling, identification, reference-material, and test-day rules listed by ProV.

After passing the examination, Master Electrician applicants must submit proof of general liability insurance of at least $300,000. The applicant’s name must appear on the insurance document, even when coverage is provided through an employer’s policy. Once the Board office receives the passing score and confirms that the applicant has met all licensing requirements, the license may be issued.

State Requirements

Delaware requires a professional electrician license to perform electrical services in the state. Delaware defines electrical services or electrical work as activity covered by the National Electrical Code as adopted by the Delaware State Fire Commission. The Delaware Board of Electrical Examiners recognizes several license categories, including Master Electrician, Limited Electrician, Master Electrician Special, Limited Electrician Special, Journeyperson Electrician, Residential Electrician, and Apprentice Electrician.

The Master Electrician license is the broad statewide license associated with planning, estimating, laying out, performing, or supervising electrical installation and repair work for lighting, heating, or power in structures. Candidates who do not qualify for the full Master Electrician license but have knowledge in a specialty area may apply for a Master Electrician Special license in approved specialty areas such as electric signs, elevators, HVAC, primary distribution systems, pools, or refrigeration.

Delaware also allows application by reciprocity for applicants who hold a current license of the same type from another state, United States territory, or the District of Columbia. Reciprocity requirements depend on whether the Board has determined that the other jurisdiction’s licensing standards are substantially similar to Delaware’s standards. Applicants applying by reciprocity may need to submit license verifications and, in some cases, proof of experience.

Master and Limited Electrician applicants must provide proof of general liability insurance of at least $300,000 before the license can be issued. Applicants may take the exam before submitting proof of insurance, but the license cannot be issued until the Board receives acceptable proof. Electricians should also monitor renewal and continuing education requirements after licensure.
