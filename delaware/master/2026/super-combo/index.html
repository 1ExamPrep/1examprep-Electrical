Exam Details

The Delaware Board of Electrical Examiners must approve candidates to sit for the electrical licensing examination. Delaware uses ProV as the external testing vendor for required electrical licensure examinations. After the Board approves a candidate to sit for the exam, the candidate receives examination registration information from ProV.

The Delaware Master Electrician examination has 80 questions and a 3-hour time limit. A minimum score of 75% is required to pass. The test is computer-based and may be taken by scheduled appointment through available testing options identified by ProV after approval.

The Delaware Master Electrician exam content outline includes general electrical knowledge, plan reading, equipment for general use, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, motors and controls, special conditions, special equipment, special occupancies, and communication systems. These areas reflect a broad master-level electrical knowledge base. Candidates should prepare for code lookup, trade application, calculations, plan-related questions, and practical installation concepts.

The official exam breakdown lists 19 questions on general electrical knowledge, 4 questions on plan reading, 5 questions on equipment for general use, 15 questions on wiring and protection, 15 questions on wiring methods and materials, 7 questions on motors and controls, 4 questions on special conditions, 4 questions on special equipment, 4 questions on special occupancies, and 3 questions on communication systems.

Because the exam includes a wide range of subjects, candidates should avoid focusing only on one area of the NEC. Services, branch circuits, raceways, conductors, grounding, bonding, boxes, overcurrent protection, motors, equipment, communication systems, and special installations can all affect exam performance. A strong preparation plan should include both content review and timed practice.

Open Book Test

The Delaware electrical licensing examinations are open book and timed. Open book does not mean the exam is simple. Candidates still need to understand the question, know which reference applies, locate the correct section, interpret the rule, and answer within the time limit.

ProV’s open-book reference rules allow exact book titles listed for the exam. Older or newer editions are permitted, and substitutes are allowed only when listed in the exam details. Permanent tabs, such as gummed tabs, self-adhesive tabs with printable inserts, or tab dividers for three-ring binders, are permitted. Moveable items such as repositionable tabs or Post-it notes are not permitted in reference books.

Books may have highlighting and underlining in pen, but candidates may not mark in books during the test. Handwritten notes are not allowed in any portion of a reference book unless authorized by the licensing or certifying jurisdiction. Other documents, formula sheets, stickers, or additional materials may not be added, glued, or taped to a reference book unless permitted.

This makes organized study especially important. Candidates should practice using the NEC before test day, learn how to move through articles and tables, and build the habit of reading code language carefully. The National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs included in this Super Combo supports that kind of preparation by helping candidates become more comfortable with code structure and navigation.

Licensing Steps

Delaware Master Electrician applicants begin through the Delaware Professional Regulation Online Services system, known as DELPROS. The Board of Electrical Examiners reviews applications and must approve candidates before they sit for the required exam.

A typical pathway includes creating or logging into a DELPROS account, applying for the proper electrician license classification, submitting required documentation, waiting for Board approval to test, receiving registration information from ProV, scheduling the examination, and completing the exam. After passing, the Board office receives the official score and validates that all licensing requirements have been met before issuing the license.

Master and Limited Electrician applicants must also submit proof of liability insurance of at least $300,000 when required by the state process. Passing the exam is an important step, but the license is issued only after the Board confirms that the applicant has satisfied the applicable requirements.

Candidates applying by examination should follow the Delaware application instructions for the Master Electrician classification. Candidates who hold a current electrical license of the same type from another state, U.S. territory, or the District of Columbia may apply through the reciprocity path when applicable. The application route determines what documentation the applicant must submit.

State Requirements

Delaware requires a professional license to perform electrical services in the state. Electrical services or electrical work are tied to activities covered by the National Electrical Code as adopted by the Delaware State Fire Commission. The Delaware Board of Electrical Examiners regulates electrician licensure through the Division of Professional Regulation.

Delaware electrician license types include Master, Master Special, Limited, Limited Special, Journeyperson, Residential, and Apprentice classifications. The Master Electrician classification applies to a person licensed by the Board to plan, estimate, lay out, perform, or supervise the installation, erection, or repair of electrical conductors, ducts, raceways, conduits, machinery, apparatus, devices, or fixtures for lighting, heating, or power in any structure.

Applicants may apply by examination or by reciprocity. The examination path is used when the applicant does not hold a current electrical license of the same type from another state, U.S. territory, or the District of Columbia. The reciprocity path is used when the applicant holds a current license of the same type from another qualifying jurisdiction.

For examination applicants, Delaware requires proof of experience. The experience documentation depends on the type of license being requested and whether the applicant has completed a registered or approved apprenticeship program. Applicants who completed a registered or approved apprenticeship program and are applying for Master, Limited, or Special licensure submit a certificate of completion. Applicants who have not completed a registered or approved apprenticeship program submit verification of employment from each employer showing qualifying full-time experience under the supervision of an appropriate licensed electrician.
