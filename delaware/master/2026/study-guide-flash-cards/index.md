## Exam Details

The Delaware Master Electrician examination is administered through Prov for licensing through the Delaware Division of Professional Regulation. The Delaware Board of Electrical Examiners must approve a candidate to sit for the examination. After Board approval, the testing vendor provides registration information and the candidate schedules the examination through the approved testing process.

The Delaware Master Electrician exam has 80 questions and allows 3 hours of testing time. Delaware electrician examinations require a minimum passing score of 75%. The exam is computer-based and is designed to test the knowledge a qualified master-level electrician should understand during regular electrical work operations.

## Open Book Test

The Delaware Master Electrician exam is open book and timed. Candidates may use authorized references during the exam according to Prov and Delaware Division of Professional Regulation rules. Open book does not mean the exam is simple. It means the candidate must know how to use approved materials quickly and accurately while managing the 3-hour testing window.

Reference materials are checked by the test administrator before the candidate is allowed into the testing room. Approved references may be prepared with highlighting, underlining in pen only, and permanent tabs. Handwritten notes are not allowed in any portion of a reference book. Moveable tabs are not allowed. Post-it notes are not allowed. Photocopied reference books are not allowed, and photocopied documents may not be added to a reference book.

Test preparation study guides and practice examinations are not approved exam-room references. This means the study guide, 12 practice exams, 2 full final exams, and flash cards are for preparation before exam day. During the actual test, candidates should bring only the approved reference materials allowed for the examination.

Because Delaware’s exam is open book, reference navigation should be part of your study plan. Learn the structure of the NEC, practice using the table of contents and index, become comfortable with article layout, and understand where common topics are located. When you work through practice questions, identify the subject first, then locate the supporting code rule or trade concept. This habit helps reduce wasted time during the exam.

## Licensing Steps

Delaware Master Electrician licensing begins with the Division of Professional Regulation online licensing system. Applicants create or use a DELPROS account and submit the appropriate application for licensure by examination. The Delaware Board of Electrical Examiners reviews the application and determines whether the applicant is approved to sit for the required exam.

After the Board approves the candidate for testing, the candidate receives notification and examination registration information from Prov. The testing vendor administers the exam and provides scheduling options. Delaware offers in-person testing through approved testing locations and also offers online testing through the approved remote testing system when available and when the candidate’s computer system meets the technical requirements.

After passing the exam, the candidate’s results are reported to the Division of Professional Regulation. Master and Limited Electrician applicants must also submit proof of liability insurance of at least $300,000. Once the Board office receives the passing score and validates that the applicant has met all licensure requirements, the license is issued through the state licensing process.

Applicants should keep application materials, experience documents, exam approval notices, testing information, insurance documentation, and score reports organized throughout the process. A successful licensing plan includes both exam preparation and careful attention to state application requirements.

## State Requirements

The Delaware Board of Electrical Examiners regulates electrician licensing under the Division of Professional Regulation. Delaware requires a professional electrician license to perform electrical services. Electrical services and electrical work are tied to activities covered by the National Electrical Code as adopted in Delaware.

Delaware electrician license types include Master, Master Special, Limited, Limited Special, Journeyperson, Residential, and Apprentice. The Master Electrician license is the broad master-level license for planning, estimating, laying out, performing, or supervising electrical work within the scope of the license.

Applicants may apply by examination or by reciprocity. Examination applicants use the Delaware application process when they do not hold a current electrical license of the same type from another state, United States territory, or the District of Columbia. Reciprocity applicants follow the state’s reciprocity process when they hold a current license of the same type from a jurisdiction recognized through Delaware’s licensing standards.

Delaware requires applicants to provide proof of education and experience. Applicants who completed a registered or approved apprenticeship program may submit the certificate of completion. Applicants who have not completed a registered or approved apprenticeship program submit verification of employment forms showing full-time experience under the supervision of a licensed master or limited electrician, based on the license type being pursued.

This exam prep combo does not replace Delaware’s application process, experience review, Board approval, liability insurance requirement, license issuance, renewal requirements, or any decision made by the Delaware Board of Electrical Examiners or Division of Professional Regulation.

## Test Information and Study Materials

Effective Delaware Master Electrician exam preparation should combine NEC review, trade knowledge, reference navigation, calculation practice, timed question work, and repeated recall. The exam has 80 questions and allows 3 hours, so candidates must keep a steady pace. Some questions can be answered from knowledge, while others may require reference lookup. The best preparation builds both skills.

Start with general electrical knowledge. This area supports the rest of the exam because master-level electrical work requires a strong foundation in theory, installation practices, calculations, equipment use, and safety. Flash cards are useful for formulas, terminology, and fast recall, while practice exams help you apply knowledge in question form.

Wiring and protection should receive major attention. Study overcurrent protection, conductor sizing, branch circuits, feeders, services, grounding and bonding, protection methods, disconnecting means, and common NEC rules. These topics often overlap, and many exam questions require candidates to apply several related rules at once.

Wiring methods and materials are also important. Review raceways, conductors, cables, boxes, fittings, enclosures, supports, installation methods, conductor fill, box fill, and practical installation requirements. These are common field topics, but exam questions often require precise code application rather than general familiarity.

Plan reading should be included in your study routine. Master electricians are expected to understand layouts, interpret project information, evaluate installation requirements, and connect plan details to real jobsite decisions. Practice questions can help you become more comfortable identifying what a plan-based question is asking.

Motors and controls, equipment for general use, special conditions, special equipment, special occupancies, and communication systems should receive steady review. These categories may include fewer questions than the largest subject areas, but each question matters. A complete study plan gives every content area attention.

Use the 12 practice exams as your main repetition tool. After each practice exam, review every missed question. Identify the topic, return to the NEC-based material or trade reference, and understand why the correct answer is best. Do not only memorize the answer. Learn the concept behind it.

Use the 2 full final exams near the end of your preparation. Treat each final exam like a real testing session. Work in a quiet space, manage your time, answer every question, and review your results carefully. The final exams help build testing stamina and reveal remaining weak areas before exam day.

Use the flash cards for short, repeated review sessions. Flash cards are helpful before work, after a job, during lunch, or during final review.
