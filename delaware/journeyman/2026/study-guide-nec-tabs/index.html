Exam Details

The Delaware Division of Professional Regulation utilizes Prov, Inc. to administer required licensure examinations for the electrical trade. The Board of Electrical Examiners must approve candidates before they sit for the examination. After Board approval, candidates receive exam registration information and may schedule their examination through Prov.

The Delaware Candidate Information Bulletin identifies the Journeyperson Electrician exam as a computer-based, open book, timed examination. The current bulletin lists the Journeyperson Electrician exam as 70 questions with 3 hours allowed. Delaware electrical examinations require a minimum passing score of 75%.

The Journeyperson Electrician exam content outline includes General Electrical Theory and Principles, Plan Reading, Equipment for General Use, Motors and Controls, Wiring and Protection, Wiring Methods and Materials, Communication Systems, Special Conditions, Special Equipment, and Special Occupancies. These subject areas show why candidates should prepare broadly instead of focusing on only one part of the trade.

Delaware’s examination information states that electrical examinations are based on the most up-to-date National Electrical Code adopted in Delaware. The current Candidate Information Bulletin lists the National Electrical Code as an exam reference for the Journeyperson Electrician examination. Open Book Test

The Delaware Journeyperson Electrician examination is an open book test. The Candidate Information Bulletin states that Delaware examinations are open book and timed. It also encourages candidates to prepare reference materials by highlighting text or placing permanent tabs on important pages. Handwritten notes, moveable tabs, Post-it notes, photocopied documents added to a reference book, and other unapproved materials are not permitted.

An open book electrical exam still requires serious preparation. Having the NEC available does not mean every answer will be obvious. Candidates must understand the structure of the code book, know the meaning of electrical terms, and recognize which article, table, exception, or requirement applies to the question. Time pressure can make code lookup difficult for candidates who have not practiced before exam day.

Licensing Steps

The Delaware journeyperson electrician licensing path begins by choosing the correct application type. Delaware provides several ways to apply for a journeyperson electrician license, including by examination, by certificate, or by reciprocity. The correct application path depends on the applicant’s background, completed apprenticeship documentation, current licenses, and work experience.

Applicants who qualify by certificate may apply if they hold a certificate of completion from a state-approved, bona fide, registered apprenticeship program, such as a program approved by the Department of Labor or a Board-approved program. Applicants who qualify by certificate do not have to pass the journeyperson examination.

Applicants who apply by examination must pass the journeyperson examination. Examination applicants must submit the required application and supporting documentation in DELPROS, the Delaware Professional Regulation Online Services portal. Delaware requires a Verification of Employment form or forms showing at least 8,000 hours of full-time experience performing electrical work under the supervision of a licensed master electrician, master electrician special, limited electrician, or limited electrician special.

Applicants who apply by reciprocity must hold a current journeyperson license issued by another state, United States territory, or the District of Columbia. The Board reviews the licensing requirements from the other jurisdiction to determine whether they are substantially similar to Delaware’s requirements. When substantial similarity is not shown, Delaware may require five years of full-time supervised experience for reciprocity eligibility, or the applicant may need to pass the journeyperson examination.

After the Board reviews and approves an examination application, the candidate receives notification of approval to sit for the exam. The candidate then receives registration information from Prov and schedules the test. After passing the exam and after the Board validates that the applicant has met the requirements for licensure, the license is issued through the state process.

State Requirements

Delaware requires a professional electrician license to perform electrical services. The Board defines electrical services or electrical work as activity covered by the National Electrical Code as adopted by the Delaware State Fire Commission. This makes NEC knowledge a central part of both licensing and safe electrical practice in the state.

The Delaware Journeyperson Electrician license allows the holder to perform electrical services under supervision while gaining more experience and developing the skills needed to pursue master, limited, master special, or limited special electrician licensure. This license is an important step for electrical workers who are progressing through the state licensing structure.

For examination applicants, Delaware requires at least 8,000 hours of full-time experience performing electrical work under proper supervision. Acceptable supervision may include a licensed master electrician, master electrician special, limited electrician, or limited electrician special. Applicants use employment verification forms to document qualifying work experience. When an employer verification form cannot be obtained for a period of employment, Delaware allows substitute documentation such as W-2 forms along with an explanation and detailed outline of experience.

Delaware also recognizes apprenticeship completion as a separate path. A certificate of completion from a state-approved, bona fide, registered apprenticeship program can support licensure by certificate. Candidates coming through an apprenticeship program should keep copies of their completion documents, classroom records, employer information, and training records.

Candidates applying through DELPROS should gather documents before starting the application. The state’s online system may require applicants to upload documents directly or acknowledge that third-party documentation will be sent to the Board office. Examples include employment verifications, license verifications, and supervisor-completed forms. Complete documentation helps the Board evaluate the application and reduces delays.

Test Information and Study Materials

Delaware journeyperson electrician preparation should include a balanced mix of code study, trade review, plan reading, calculation practice, and repeated self-testing. Candidates should review general electrical theory, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment for general use, motors and controls, communication systems, special equipment, special occupancies, special conditions, and practical installation rules.

The National Electrical Code is central to electrical exam preparation because it contains the rules and structure used to support safe electrical installations. Candidates should become comfortable using Article 100 definitions, wiring and protection requirements, wiring methods and materials, equipment rules, special occupancies, special equipment, communication system provisions, and tables used for sizing, fill, adjustment, correction, and installation decisions.

When studying with this combo, begin with one topic in the Delaware Journeyman Electrician Study Guide, then locate related NEC sections in the 2026 code book. This approach helps connect study guide review with actual code language. It also helps candidates practice the same thinking process they will need during an open book exam.

For calculation topics, write out each step. Electrical calculations may require identifying what the question gives you, determining what the question asks for, selecting the correct table or rule, applying adjustments or corrections, and checking whether the answer should be a minimum, maximum, ampacity, rating, load, conductor size, or overcurrent device value. A written process helps improve accuracy.

Plan reading should also be part of preparation. Delaware’s journeyperson exam outline includes plan reading, which means candidates should be comfortable interpreting electrical symbols, installation details, layout information, and job conditions that may affect the answer. Combine plan reading review with NEC lookup so that you can connect drawings and specifications to code requirements.

Timed practice is valuable because the Delaware journeyperson exam is timed. Candidates should occasionally study under time limits while using the NEC. The goal is to build a steady rhythm: read the question, identify the topic, locate the rule, verify the language, and answer with confidence.

Review missed questions carefully. When an answer is wrong, return to the study guide and the NEC. Find the rule, read the surrounding language, and understand why the correct answer applies. This helps prepare for questions that test the same concept in a new way.
