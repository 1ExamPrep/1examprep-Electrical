Exam Details

The Delaware Journeyperson Electrician examination is administered by Prov for licensing through the Delaware Division of Professional Regulation and the Board of Electrical Examiners. Candidates must receive Board approval before scheduling the examination. After approval, registration information is forwarded to Prov, and candidates schedule testing according to the instructions in the candidate information bulletin.

The Delaware Journeyperson Electrician exam is computer-based, open book, and timed. The exam contains 70 questions and allows 3 hours of testing time. The passing score for Delaware electrician examinations is 75%. Exam results are provided at the testing center, and the Delaware Division of Professional Regulation is notified of the candidate’s results.

The Delaware Journeyperson Electrician exam content outline includes 9 questions on General Electrical Theory and Principles, 4 questions on Plan Reading, 6 questions on Equipment for General Use, 3 questions on Motors and Controls, 14 questions on Wiring and Protection, 22 questions on Wiring Methods and Materials, 4 questions on Communication Systems, 3 questions on Special Conditions, 3 questions on Special Equipment, and 2 questions on Special Occupancies.

This exam outline shows why students should study across the full range of electrical topics. Wiring methods and materials carry a large portion of the exam, but smaller categories can still affect the final score. A candidate may need to answer questions involving code organization, conductor installation, raceways, boxes, equipment rules, communication systems, motors, wiring protection, special equipment, and plan reading. Preparation should include both broad topic review and focused work on weaker areas.

Because the test is timed, candidates should study with speed and accuracy in mind. Open book testing rewards students who can identify the subject of a question, locate the proper reference material, read the rule carefully, and apply it without spending too much time on one item. Open Book Test

The Delaware Journeyperson Electrician exam is an open book test. Delaware’s Prov candidate information lists all examinations as open book and timed. Open book testing gives candidates access to authorized references, but it does not remove the need for serious preparation. Students still need to understand electrical concepts, know how reference material is organized, and use the code book efficiently under time pressure.

For electrical candidates, open book study should include repeated NEC navigation practice. Students should become familiar with the structure of the code book, including definitions, chapters, articles, tables, notes, exceptions, and the index. A question may require a direct code lookup, but many questions require the student to understand the topic first and then locate the correct rule or table.

Licensing Steps

Delaware journeyperson electrician candidates apply through DELPROS, the Delaware Professional Regulation Online Services portal. All applications are submitted through the online system. The application pathway depends on whether the applicant is applying by examination, certification, or reciprocity.

A candidate may apply by certification after completing a Department of Labor or Board-approved registered apprenticeship program and receiving a certificate of completion. Applicants who qualify by certificate do not have to pass the journeyperson examination. The certificate of completion is submitted as part of the journeyperson licensure process.

A candidate applies by examination when the candidate does not hold a current journeyperson license from another state, territory, or the District of Columbia and does not hold a qualifying certificate of completion from an approved apprenticeship program. Examination applicants must submit the required application, fee, supporting documentation, and Verification of Employment forms showing at least 8,000 hours of full-time experience performing electrical work under the required licensed supervision.

After the Board reviews and approves the examination application, the candidate receives authorization for testing. Prov then provides examination scheduling information. The candidate schedules the computer-based exam, follows the test-day rules, and completes the journeyperson examination.

After testing, candidates receive score information at the test center. The Delaware Division of Professional Regulation receives the results from Prov. A passing candidate continues through the licensing process. A candidate who does not pass may retake the examination after waiting at least seven days from the previous test attempt.

A strong licensing plan starts before the exam is scheduled. Candidates should keep accurate employment records, complete required apprenticeship-related technical instruction when applicable, organize verification forms, review the exam outline, and build a study routine that includes trade topics, calculation practice, and NEC navigation.

State Requirements

Delaware requires a professional electrician license to perform electrical services. Electrical services or electrical work are defined as activities covered by the National Electrical Code as adopted by the Delaware State Fire Commission. Delaware electrician license types include Master, Master Special, Limited, Limited Special, Journeyperson, Residential, and Apprentice.

The Delaware Journeyperson Electrician license allows the holder to perform electrical services under supervision while gaining more experience and mastering the skills needed for Master, Limited, Master Special, or Limited Special Electrician licensure. A journeyperson license is not the same as a master electrician license, but it is an important credential for electricians advancing through the trade.

For examination applicants, Delaware requires Verification of Employment forms showing at least 8,000 hours of full-time experience performing electrical work under the supervision of a licensed master electrician, master electrician special, limited electrician, or limited electrician special. If a Verification of Employment form cannot be obtained for a period of employment, tax W-2 forms may be used with an explanatory letter and detailed outline of experience.

Delaware’s journeyperson pathway also recognizes completion of a state-approved, bona fide, registered apprenticeship program. A person who completes a registered apprenticeship program may apply for journeyperson licensure by certification and does not have to sit for the journeyperson examination. A person who completes an unregistered or not-sponsored apprenticeship path may pursue licensure by examination and must meet the required documentation standards.

Apprentice licensing is important in Delaware. A licensed apprentice electrician may legally perform the hours of electrical work needed to complete apprenticeship requirements. Apprentices and examination applicants should maintain accurate records of work experience, supervision, technical instruction, and program completion because those records support future journeyperson licensure.

A wiring methods question may involve conductor type, raceway selection, box requirements, installation location, or protection from physical damage. A wiring protection question may involve overcurrent devices, branch circuit rules, service equipment, or conductor ampacity.

A balanced study schedule may rotate between trade review, NEC navigation, and calculation practice. One session might focus on wiring methods and materials. Another might focus on motors and controls. Another might involve plan reading. Another might involve calculation practice or locating equipment rules in the NEC. This rotation helps students prepare across the full exam outline instead of becoming comfortable with only one subject area.

Students should also include timed practice. Since the Delaware Journeyperson Electrician exam is timed and open book, pacing matters.
