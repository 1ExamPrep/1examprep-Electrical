Exam Details

The Delaware Division of Professional Regulation and the Delaware Board of Electrical Examiners oversee electrical licensure in Delaware. The Division uses ProV to facilitate required licensure examinations for the electrical trade. Candidates must receive Board approval before registering for the required examination.

The Delaware Journeyperson Electrician exam is a timed, open-book examination. The ProV candidate information bulletin lists the Journeyperson Electrician exam with 70 questions and 3 hours allowed for testing. The exam tests a journey-level electrician’s ability to perform work in the electrical trade while under the supervision of a Master Electrician.

The Delaware Journeyperson Electrician exam content outline includes General Electrical Theory and Principles, Plan Reading, Equipment for General Use, Motors and Controls, Wiring and Protection, Wiring Methods and Materials, Communication Systems, Special Conditions, Special Equipment, and Special Occupancies. Wiring methods and materials make up a large part of the published exam outline, so candidates should spend steady time reviewing conductor use, raceways, boxes, installation methods, and material-related code requirements.

The Delaware Division of Professional Regulation states that all electrical examinations are based on the most up-to-date National Electrical Code adopted in Delaware. This product includes 2026 NEC-based flash cards for current-code study and repeated review. Candidates should use the reference materials allowed for the exam they are approved to take and follow the current ProV testing instructions for exam-day materials.

The Delaware exam page lists a minimum passing score of 75%. Test results are processed through the testing provider and the Board’s licensing process. After passing and meeting all licensing requirements, the license is issued through the Delaware Division of Professional Regulation.

Licensing Steps

Delaware journeyperson electrician licensure is handled through the Delaware Division of Professional Regulation and the Board of Electrical Examiners. Delaware requires a professional electrician license to perform electrical services in the state. Electrical services are defined around work covered by the National Electrical Code as adopted by the Delaware State Fire Commission.

A Delaware Journeyperson Electrician license allows the license holder to perform electrical services under supervision while gaining additional experience and developing the skills needed for higher electrical licensure classifications. Delaware provides several application pathways for journeyperson licensure, including examination, reciprocity, and certification.

Candidates applying by examination must first create a DELPROS account and submit the appropriate licensure application through the Delaware online portal. The Board reviews the application before the candidate is approved to sit for the exam. After approval, the candidate receives exam registration information from ProV.

Once approved, the candidate schedules the examination through ProV. Delaware offers traditional in-person testing at Delaware testing locations and online testing through ProV’s Examroom service when available. Testing is arranged by scheduled appointment.

After passing the examination, the candidate must complete the remaining licensing requirements through the Division of Professional Regulation. The Board office receives the official examination score, validates the licensing requirements, and issues the license through the state’s licensing system.

This study guide and flash card combo supports the exam preparation stage of the licensing process. Students can use it while completing apprenticeship requirements, while preparing the DELPROS application, after receiving Board approval, or during final review before the scheduled exam date.

State Requirements

The Delaware Board of Electrical Examiners regulates electrical licensure in the state. Delaware electrician license types include Apprentice, Journeyperson, Residential, Master, Master Special, Limited, and Limited Special classifications. This product is focused on the Delaware Journeyperson Electrician exam and journeyperson-level electrical preparation.

Delaware rules identify journeyperson electrician experience requirements connected to full-time electrical work and apprenticeship experience. State regulations address 8,000 hours of full-time experience for journeyperson licensure, including requirements related to commercial and industrial electrical applications. At least 4,000 of the 8,000 hours must be under the supervision of a licensed master electrician, and experience under a limited electrician may be applied within the limits set by Delaware rules.

Applicants should follow current Delaware Division of Professional Regulation instructions for application type, experience documentation, apprenticeship completion, reciprocity, certification, examination approval, testing, retesting, renewal, and license status. DELPROS is used for applying, managing applications, checking status, and completing licensing steps.
