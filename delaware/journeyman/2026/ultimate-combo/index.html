Exam Details

The Delaware Journeyperson Electrician exam is administered by Prov, Inc. for licensing through the Delaware Division of Professional Regulation. The exam is computer-based, open book, and timed. The Delaware journeyperson electrician examination includes 70 questions and allows 3 hours of testing time.

The passing score for Delaware electrician examinations is 75%. Exam results are provided after completion, and results are also reported to the Delaware Division of Professional Regulation. Candidates who need to retake the exam may do so after waiting at least seven days from the previous test attempt.

The Delaware Journeyperson Electrician exam content outline includes general electrical theory and principles, plan reading, equipment for general use, motors and controls, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, communication systems, special conditions, special equipment, and special occupancies. Wiring methods and materials is the largest listed content area, followed by wiring and protection and general electrical theory and principles, so candidates should build a study plan that gives enough attention to code application, installation rules, and electrical fundamentals.

The exam tests the knowledge needed by a journey-level electrician working in the electrical trades while under the supervision of a Master Electrician. Candidates should prepare for questions that may require code lookup, rule interpretation, trade understanding, calculation skills, and familiarity with electrical installation terminology.

Open Book Test

The Delaware Journeyperson Electrician exam is an open book test. Candidates may use approved reference books during testing, but all reference materials must follow the exam provider’s rules. The candidate information bulletin states that exams are open book and timed, and it also lists reference materials that may be used during testing.

Reference-book preparation is important because open book does not mean the answers will be easy to find. Candidates need to know where common topics are located, how code articles are organized, how tables are used, and how to move through the NEC efficiently under timed conditions. The included National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs is intended to help candidates build that navigation skill during study.

Prov’s reference material rules prohibit handwritten notes in reference books. Moveable tabs and Post-it notes are not permitted, photocopied reference books are not permitted, and test preparation study guides are not approved references in the testing center. Candidates may not highlight, underline, mark, or write information in reference materials during the exam. Permanent tabs and clean, approved references should be prepared before test day according to the exam provider’s rules.

Because this product is based on the 2026 NEC, candidates should use the 2026 National Electrical Code during study sessions to build familiarity with the code cycle used for this preparation package. Code-navigation practice should include definitions, general requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods, equipment rules, motors, special occupancies, special equipment, communication systems, and calculation-related tables.

Licensing Steps

Delaware journeyperson electrician licensing is handled through the Delaware Division of Professional Regulation’s DELPROS online system. Candidates may apply through different pathways, including examination, reciprocity, or certification, depending on their background and documentation.

A candidate applying by examination must submit the required application materials through DELPROS and pass the journeyperson examination. Delaware also provides a certificate pathway for applicants who hold a certificate of completion issued by the Department of Labor or an approved apprenticeship program in Delaware, another state, a U.S. territory, or the District of Columbia. Reciprocity may be available for applicants who hold a current journeyperson license from another state, U.S. territory, or the District of Columbia, depending on the Board’s review of substantially similar licensing requirements and experience.

A typical examination pathway includes creating or logging into a DELPROS account, selecting the correct journeyperson electrician application pathway, submitting the required application and fee, uploading or arranging required documentation, waiting for authorization or exam information, scheduling the exam through Prov, taking the Delaware Journeyperson Electrician exam, and completing any remaining licensing requirements through the Division of Professional Regulation.

State Requirements

Delaware requires individuals who perform electrical services in the state to hold the proper Delaware professional electrician license. The Board defines electrical services or electrical work as activity covered by the National Electrical Code as adopted by the Delaware State Fire Commission.

A Delaware Journeyperson Electrician license allows the holder to perform electrical services under supervision while gaining additional experience and developing the skills needed for higher-level electrician license classifications. Delaware’s electrician license types include apprentice, residential, journeyperson, master, master special, limited, and limited special classifications.

For examination applicants, Delaware requires Verification of Employment forms showing at least 8,000 hours of full-time experience performing electrical work under the supervision of a licensed master electrician, master electrician special, limited electrician, or limited electrician special. If a candidate cannot obtain an employment verification form for a period of employment, the state allows W-2 forms for those periods along with an explanation and detailed outline of experience.

All journeyperson electrician applications are submitted through DELPROS. The Delaware system allows candidates to save an incomplete application and return to it later, but an application must be submitted within six months after it is started, or the candidate must restart the online application. Candidates should gather license verifications, employment forms, apprenticeship certificates when applicable, and other required documents before beginning the application process.
