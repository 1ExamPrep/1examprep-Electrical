## Exam Details

Mississippi does not issue one statewide individual master electrician license in the same way some states do. Individual electrician licensing is commonly handled by local municipalities or counties. Local jurisdictions may set their own master electrician application requirements, experience documentation, exam rules, approved references, fees, insurance requirements, and renewal procedures.

Electrical contractor licensing is handled through the Mississippi State Board of Contractors. Contractors performing work that falls within the Board’s licensing thresholds and classifications must follow the MSBOC application and examination process. Electrical contractor applicants may be required to pass the applicable trade examination and the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination unless a waiver or reciprocity pathway applies.

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors uses PSI for contractor examinations. Candidates become eligible by completing the appropriate commercial or residential contractor application and submitting it to the Board. After the Board determines eligibility, PSI sends the candidate a confirmation notice with examination scheduling instructions. Exam eligibility is valid for a limited period, and candidates must schedule and test within the period provided by the Board and PSI.

Electrical trade exam content generally focuses on NEC application, electrical theory, installation requirements, services, feeders, branch circuits, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, conductors, raceways, boxes, motors, transformers, lighting, signs, low-voltage systems, alarms, communication systems, safety, and practical electrical trade knowledge. ## Licensing Steps

The Mississippi licensing path begins with identifying which credential is needed. An individual electrician may need a local journeyman or master electrician license through a city or county. A person or business performing electrical contracting work may also need a contractor license through the Mississippi State Board of Contractors, depending on the type, value, and scope of work.

For local master electrician licensing, the candidate should contact the local building department, permitting office, or electrical board where the work will be performed. Local requirements may include documented experience, prior journeyman licensing, proof of insurance, examination, license fees, renewal requirements, and compliance with local electrical ordinances.

For contractor licensing through the Mississippi State Board of Contractors, the applicant submits the appropriate commercial or residential contractor application. The Board reviews the application and determines which examinations are required. Once the candidate is approved for testing, PSI sends a confirmation notice and the candidate schedules the required exam or exams.

Electrical contractor applicants may be required to pass both a trade examination and the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination. The trade exam measures electrical knowledge and code application, while the business and law exam addresses contractor business practices, legal responsibilities, and project management topics. After passing the required examinations, the applicant completes any remaining licensing steps required by the Board.

Students should begin preparing before the application process is complete. NEC study, calculation practice, and reference navigation take time. ## State Requirements

Mississippi uses a combination of local electrician licensing and statewide contractor licensing. Individual journeyman and master electrician licenses are generally issued by local jurisdictions. The Mississippi State Board of Contractors licenses contractors and contractor classifications for work that falls under the Board’s requirements.

Because individual electrician licensing is local, a credential issued by one city or county may not automatically authorize work in another jurisdiction. The Mississippi State Board of Contractors regulates contractor licensing for commercial and residential contracting work within its authority. Electrical contractor applicants follow the Board’s application process, classification requirements, testing requirements, and licensing procedures. The Board determines which examinations are required for each applicant and classification.

Business owners and qualifying parties should understand the difference between an individual master electrician credential and a contractor license. A local master electrician license may demonstrate individual trade qualification, while a contractor license may be required for the business to offer or perform regulated electrical contracting work. The exact requirements depend on the work, location, and licensing authority.
