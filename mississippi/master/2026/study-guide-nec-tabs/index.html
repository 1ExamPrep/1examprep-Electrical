Exam Details

Mississippi does not issue one statewide individual master electrician license in the same way some states do. Individual electrician licensing is commonly handled by local municipalities or counties. Local jurisdictions may set their own master electrician application requirements, experience documentation, exam rules, approved references, fees, insurance requirements, and renewal procedures.

Electrical contractor licensing is handled through the Mississippi State Board of Contractors. Contractors performing work that falls within the Board’s licensing thresholds and classifications must follow the MSBOC application and examination process. Electrical contractor applicants may be required to pass the applicable trade examination and the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination unless a waiver or reciprocity pathway applies.

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors uses PSI for contractor examinations. Candidates become eligible by completing the appropriate commercial or residential contractor application and submitting it to the Board. After the Board determines eligibility, PSI sends the candidate a confirmation notice with examination scheduling instructions. Exam eligibility is valid for a limited period, and candidates must schedule and test within the period provided by the Board and PSI.

Electrical trade exam content generally focuses on NEC application, electrical theory, installation requirements, services, feeders, branch circuits, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, conductors, raceways, boxes, motors, transformers, lighting, signs, low-voltage systems, alarms, communication systems, safety, and practical electrical trade knowledge. Local master electrician exams may follow similar NEC-based subject areas, but the exact format and reference list depend on the issuing city or county.

Open Book Test

Mississippi electrical contractor and many local electrician examinations are commonly administered as open book exams using approved references. For contractor testing through PSI, candidates are responsible for following the current candidate bulletin and bringing only the materials allowed for their scheduled examination. Local master electrician exams may have their own reference rules, so candidates should follow the exact instructions issued by the local city, county, or testing provider.

Open book testing does not make an electrical exam easy. The NEC is a large technical document, and candidates who have not practiced with the book may spend too much time searching. Licensing Steps

The Mississippi licensing path begins with identifying which credential is needed. An individual electrician may need a local journeyman or master electrician license through a city or county. A person or business performing electrical contracting work may also need a contractor license through the Mississippi State Board of Contractors, depending on the type, value, and scope of work.

For local master electrician licensing, the candidate should contact the local building department, permitting office, or electrical board where the work will be performed. Local requirements may include documented experience, prior journeyman licensing, proof of insurance, examination, license fees, renewal requirements, and compliance with local electrical ordinances.

For contractor licensing through the Mississippi State Board of Contractors, the applicant submits the appropriate commercial or residential contractor application. The Board reviews the application and determines which examinations are required. Once the candidate is approved for testing, PSI sends a confirmation notice and the candidate schedules the required exam or exams.

Electrical contractor applicants may be required to pass both a trade examination and the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination. The trade exam measures electrical knowledge and code application, while the business and law exam addresses contractor business practices, legal responsibilities, and project management topics. After passing the required examinations, the applicant completes any remaining licensing steps required by the Board.

Students should begin preparing before the application process is complete. NEC study, calculation practice, and reference navigation take time. The 2026 Mississippi Master Electrician Study Guide helps direct the technical review, while the tabbed NEC helps students practice locating and applying code rules more efficiently.

State Requirements

Mississippi uses a combination of local electrician licensing and statewide contractor licensing. Individual journeyman and master electrician licenses are generally issued by local jurisdictions. The Mississippi State Board of Contractors licenses contractors and contractor classifications for work that falls under the Board’s requirements.

Because individual electrician licensing is local, a credential issued by one city or county may not automatically authorize work in another jurisdiction. Electricians should confirm licensing, registration, permit, and inspection requirements for each area where they plan to work. Local jurisdictions may also adopt specific NEC editions or local amendments.

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors regulates contractor licensing for commercial and residential contracting work within its authority. Electrical contractor applicants follow the Board’s application process, classification requirements, testing requirements, and licensing procedures. The Board determines which examinations are required for each applicant and classification.

Business owners and qualifying parties should understand the difference between an individual master electrician credential and a contractor license. A local master electrician license may demonstrate individual trade qualification, while a contractor license may be required for the business to offer or perform regulated electrical contracting work. The exact requirements depend on the work, location, and licensing authority.

Test Information and Study Materials

Mississippi master-level electrical preparation should begin with core NEC knowledge. Students should review Article 90, Article 100 definitions, Article 110 general requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, communication systems, and Chapter 9 tables.

Services, feeders, and branch circuits should receive focused attention. Students should practice service calculations, feeder sizing, branch-circuit rules, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, service disconnecting means, panelboard rules, and load calculation concepts. These subjects often appear in both local master electrician exams and contractor-level electrical exams.

Grounding and bonding should be studied carefully. Important topics include grounding electrode systems, grounding electrode conductor sizing, bonding jumpers, equipment grounding conductors, service bonding, separately derived systems, and special grounding rules. These questions often depend on precise wording and careful code interpretation.

Wiring methods and materials are also important. Students should review raceways, cables, boxes, cabinets, fittings, conduit fill, box fill, conductor protection, support rules, wet-location requirements, underground installations, pull boxes, junction boxes, and Chapter 9 table use. Many electrical exam questions require candidates to combine article requirements with table information.

Motors, transformers, lighting, signs, special occupancies, and low-voltage systems should also be part of the study plan. Students should practice motor branch-circuit conductor sizing, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, disconnecting means, transformer conductor rules, lighting outlet requirements, swimming pool rules, fire alarm systems, and communication circuit basics.

Calculation practice is essential for master-level preparation. Students should work through Ohm’s Law, Watt’s Law, service load calculations, feeder calculations, branch-circuit calculations, conductor ampacity, adjustment and correction factors, conduit fill, box fill, voltage drop concepts, motor calculations, transformer calculations, grounding electrode conductor sizing, and overcurrent protection sizing. Each calculation should be practiced step by step so the student understands the process behind the answer.
